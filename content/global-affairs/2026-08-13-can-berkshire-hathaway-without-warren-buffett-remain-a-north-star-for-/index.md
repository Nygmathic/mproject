## A Legacy of Value

Berkshire Hathaway's new leader, Greg Abel, has ended a 14-quarter net-selling streak, injecting fresh momentum into the conglomerate's operations. Yet, a question lingers in the minds of Chinese investors: can the company continue to serve as a beacon for value-driven investors without Warren Buffett at the helm?

For decades, thousands of Chinese investors made the pilgrimage to Omaha, Nebraska, to attend Berkshire Hathaway's shareholder meetings. These gatherings were less about corporate updates and more about seeking wisdom from the legendary investor. Buffett's presence was a magnet, drawing in enthusiasts who revered his value-investing philosophy. His approach, rooted in buying assets for less than their true worth, based on fundamentals rather than short-term trends, captivated Chinese investors. It was a "north star" that guided their investment decisions.

Yang Delong, chief economist at First Seafront Fund Management, has been a regular attendee at these shareholder meetings for a decade. He believes that Buffett's investment philosophy is universal and remains relevant, regardless of his retirement. "Whether he is retired or not, his principles remain worthy of everyone's attention," Delong said. The economist noted that Abel's approach so far has been consistent with Buffett's long-standing strategy, suggesting that Chinese investors' attention towards Berkshire Hathaway will not diminish.

Kevin Chen Kaifeng, chief economist at Horizon Financial in New York, concurred with Delong's assessment. Chen attributed Buffett's enduring influence to the fact that his principles were introduced to China in the 1990s, long before he retired. "Buffett's philosophy has served as a 'north star for Chinese investors' ever since," Chen said, highlighting the timelessness of Buffett's value-investing approach.

However, not everyone is convinced that Berkshire Hathaway can maintain its allure without Buffett's presence. The company's ability to adapt to changing market conditions and navigate the complexities of modern investing will be crucial in determining its continued relevance. Analysts suggest that Berkshire may need to refine its tactics to stay ahead of the curve.

As the company navigates this transition, it is essential to recognize the significance of Buffett's legacy. His impact on value investing extends beyond his own firm, influencing a generation of investors worldwide. The question now is whether Berkshire Hathaway can continue to inspire devotion and serve as a guiding light for Chinese investors, even without Warren Buffett's iconic presence.

## A New Era of Leadership

Greg Abel's appointment as CEO has marked a significant shift in Berkshire Hathaway's leadership. While Abel has been part of the company since 1999 and has run its non-insurance businesses since 2018, his tenure as CEO is a departure from Buffett's 60-year reign. As Abel charts a new course for the company, investors will be watching closely to see how he adapts to the ever-changing investment landscape.

Abel's initial steps have been encouraging, with the company ending a 14-quarter net-selling streak. This development has sparked renewed interest in Berkshire Hathaway, particularly among value investors. However, the company still faces challenges in maintaining its position as a leading value investor. Abel will need to demonstrate his ability to navigate the complexities of modern investing and adapt to changing market conditions.
