## Tariffs as a Tool of Trade Policy: The Uncharted Territory of Presidential Overreach

As the clock struck midnight on Friday, a new chapter in the United States' trade policy saga began. Donald Trump's administration unveiled a fresh round of tariffs on more than 80 countries, including the United Kingdom, Mexico, Canada, Australia, India, China, and the 27 countries that comprise the European Union. This move marks a significant escalation in Trump's aggressive trade policies, drawing widespread protests from US allies and major trading partners.

In a daring attempt to circumvent the limitations imposed by the US supreme court, Trump's administration has invoked section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, aimed at countries engaging in forced labor practices. The tariffs, ranging from 10% to 12.5%, will replace the blanket 10% tariff that was due to expire. The move is seen as a brazen attempt to assert unprecedented authority, as the constitution explicitly entrusts Congress with the power to levy taxes.

Trump's tariff policies have been a contentious issue for years, with critics arguing that they are a tool of protectionism rather than a legitimate means of addressing trade imbalances. The latest round of tariffs has sparked a chorus of disapproval from trading partners, with Australia and Brazil describing them as unjustified and vowing to seek their removal. Norway's foreign minister has also expressed skepticism, stating that there is no basis for the new tariffs.

The European Union has taken a more measured approach, with foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas seeking clarification from Washington. The bloc views the new tariffs as a shock, particularly given their commitment to a transatlantic trade agreement reached last year. Canada, one of America's largest trading partners, has responded by asserting its commitment to combating forced labor practices, while urging a coordinated approach through a multilateral mechanism.

## The Fallout from Trump's Tariffs: A Domestic Conundrum

The move has also sparked a domestic debate, with American consumers bearing the brunt of the effects. A recent Harris Poll survey found that seven in 10 Americans believe they have paid higher prices due to Trump's tariffs, with 72% viewing them as having a negative impact on consumers. This sentiment persists even among Republican voters, underscoring the growing unease among Americans about the effectiveness of Trump's trade policies.

The tariffs have also coincided with a surge in energy costs and gas prices, driven in part by the ongoing conflict in the Middle East. This has led to a rise in inflation, which jumped to a three-year high in May. Trump and his administration, however, remain adamant that their policies have improved the lives of American consumers, despite evidence to the contrary.

The implications of Trump's tariffs are far-reaching, with analysts warning of the potential for a trade war that could have devastating consequences for the global economy. As the stakes continue to rise, it remains to be seen how this latest development will play out, and whether Trump's administration will ultimately be able to navigate the treacherous waters of trade policy.

## The Road Ahead: A Test of Presidential Authority

As the dust settles, the question on everyone's mind is: what happens next? The US supreme court's ruling in February, which declared that many of Trump's earlier tariffs were illegal, has left the administration scrambling to find a new justification for their actions. The invocation of section 301 has raised fresh concerns about the limits of presidential authority, with critics arguing that Trump is overstepping his bounds.

The Brookings Institution's analysis of the law has highlighted the potential for further courtroom challenges, with senior fellow Alan Wolff warning that the latest tariffs "again raise the question of whether the president has the legal authority to determine and implement US tariff policy – an authority that the constitution vests in Congress." If these tariffs are challenged in court, the supreme court may ultimately be forced to intervene, further limiting Trump's ability to impose tariffs without congressional approval.

As the global economy continues to grapple with the fallout from Trump's tariffs, one thing is clear: the stakes have never been higher. As the US administration navigates the treacherous waters of trade policy, the world holds its breath, waiting to see how this latest development will play out.
