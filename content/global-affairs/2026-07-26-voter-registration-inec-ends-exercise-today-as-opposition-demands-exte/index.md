## Voter Registration Deadline Looms: INEC Stands Firm Amid Opposition Demands

With the clock ticking on the Continuous Voter Registration exercise, the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has made it clear that the deadline will not be extended, despite mounting pressure from opposition parties and civil society organizations. As the exercise comes to a close, concerns are growing that millions of eligible Nigerians may be excluded from the 2027 general elections.

The opposition parties and civil society organizations have been vocal in their demands for an extension, citing concerns that millions of citizens could be left out of the electoral process. However, INEC has insisted that the exercise must end today, Sunday, July 26, as stipulated in the Electoral Act. According to the law, INEC is required to stop voter registration, updating, and revision of the voters' register 90 days before an election to allow for data clean-up, the printing of Permanent Voter Cards, and other logistical preparations.

The INEC National Commissioner, Mohammed Haruna, has emphasized that the commission cannot extend the deadline, as it must draw a line to allow for the processing of the data already collected. "By midnight on Sunday, voter registration ends. There won't be any extension. There are so many things that we need to do at the back end," Haruna explained. The commission's Information Technology team and the Voter Registration Department will begin processing the data immediately after the closure of the registration window.

The Automated Biometric Identification System will be deployed to detect and remove multiple registrations before the provisional voters' register is displayed for public scrutiny. According to Haruna, the final number of newly registered voters will only be known after the biometric verification and the claims and objections process. "So, it's after all that has been done that we will have a final figure, which we will now add to the existing 93 million or so. That will then become the voters' register," he said.

The controversy surrounding the deadline has sparked concerns over the number of Nigerians who may be excluded from the 2027 electoral process. However, INEC has maintained that it can only account for eligible citizens who presented themselves for registration. The commission has recorded registrations through its online platform, physical registration centres, and INEC offices across the 774 local government areas.

In response to the opposition's demands, the Chief Press Secretary to the INEC Chairman, Dayo Oketola, has stated that the decision to end the Continuous Voter Registration exercise is in line with constitutional and legal provisions and necessary to pave the way for preparations ahead of the 2027 general elections. Oketola has dismissed concerns that the closure of the registration exercise would disenfranchise eligible Nigerians, noting that the exercise had already been extended to ensure all interested voters had ample opportunity to register.

The controversy surrounding the deadline is a complex one, with multiple stakeholders vying for attention. While INEC has maintained that the deadline will not be extended, opposition parties and civil society organizations continue to push for an extension. As the clock ticks down on the registration deadline, one thing is clear: the fate of millions of Nigerians hangs in the balance.
