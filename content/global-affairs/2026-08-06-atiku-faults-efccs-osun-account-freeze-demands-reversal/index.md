## A High-Stakes Showdown: Atiku Challenges EFCC's Osun Account Freeze

Presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has launched a scathing attack on the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission's (EFCC) decision to freeze the Osun State Government's statutory allocation account, labeling it a "dangerous assault on democracy." The move, which has sparked intense controversy ahead of the August 15 governorship election in the state, has raised serious concerns about the neutrality of institutions expected to uphold the rule of law and protect the integrity of the electoral process.

Atiku, the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress, argues that the timing of the EFCC's action is capable of eroding public confidence in the credibility of the forthcoming governorship election. "It is impossible to ignore the implications of freezing a state's principal operational account on the eve of a governorship election," he stated. "Such an action could disrupt governance, delay salary payments, impede essential public services, and create an atmosphere of intimidation that has no place in a democratic society."

The former Vice President, who served from 1999 to 2007, also alleged that the EFCC's decision is part of a growing pattern of selective deployment of federal institutions against opposition-controlled states. He pointed to the prolonged withholding of the state's local government allocations as an example of this trend, arguing that it reinforces the perception that state institutions are being weaponized for political purposes rather than serving the public interest.

## A Threat to Democratic Governance

Atiku's assertion that the EFCC's action is a threat to democratic governance is echoed by the Osun State Governor, Ademola Adeleke, who has accused the anti-graft agency of attempting to cripple the state's operations on the eve of the election. Adeleke has alleged that the affected account is used for the payment of workers' salaries, and that the EFCC's decision is an attempt to undermine the state's ability to function effectively.

The EFCC, however, has maintained that the restriction on the Osun State Government's account is part of an ongoing investigation into the alleged diversion of about ₦11 billion in public funds. The agency has insisted that the action is unrelated to the August 15 governorship election, but has sparked widespread criticism from opposition figures and civil society groups.

## A Growing Concern: Selective Deployment of Institutions

Atiku's charge that the EFCC's decision is part of a growing pattern of selective deployment of federal institutions against opposition-controlled states is a concern that has been echoed by other opposition figures. The ADC presidential candidate has called on the EFCC to immediately lift the restriction on the Osun State Government's allocation account, warning that ordinary residents, rather than political office holders, stand to bear the consequences of the action.

"This is not merely an attack on the Osun State government; it is a direct assault on the people of Osun State, whose salaries, essential public services, and daily governance stand to suffer," Atiku argued. "The anti-corruption mandate of the EFCC must never be distorted into a political weapon against opposition governments."

## Implications and Reactions

The controversy surrounding the EFCC's decision to freeze the Osun State Government's allocation account has sparked intense debate in Nigeria, with many calling for caution and restraint in the run-up to the governorship election. The Osun State Governor, Ademola Adeleke, has vowed to take legal action against the EFCC, while opposition figures have accused the anti-graft agency of attempting to undermine the democratic process.
