## Regional Tensions Escalate as US Launches 'Heavy' Strikes on Iran

US military forces have launched a new wave of strikes against Iran, following a days-long pause in violence that had fueled fears of a return to all-out war in the Middle East. The strikes, described by the US military as a "heavy wave of strikes," targeted dozens of Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets, including military command centers.

The attacks, which were launched in response to Iranian missile strikes targeting American bases in Jordan, have further exacerbated the already strained relations between the two nations. In a statement, the US Central Command confirmed that the strikes had been carried out, stating that they had targeted "dozens of IRGC targets."

The Iranian military, in turn, has reported strikes on several cities in the south-western Khuzestan province, as well as on Qeshm island in the Gulf. The Iranian state news agency, Irna, cited provincial authorities as saying that areas around the cities of Abadan, Shadegan, Arvandkenar, and Ahvaz had been struck.

Jordan's military has also reported foiling an Iranian attempt to target the kingdom with five missiles, with no casualties reported. The incident has raised concerns about the potential for further escalation in the region, with the risk of a wider conflict.

The escalation of tensions has also had implications for regional players. Pakistan, which has been attempting to broker a peace deal between the US and Iran, has stated that negotiations between the two parties are "ongoing." In a press briefing, Pakistan's foreign ministry spokesperson, Tahir Andrabi, said that Islamabad was doing its "utmost" to bring the warring parties back to the negotiating table under the memorandum of understanding (MoU) signed by the US and Iran in June.

However, not all regional players are taking a neutral stance. Saudi Arabia, which has partnered with the US in striking Iranian-backed militias in Iraq, has reported two days of drone attacks targeting crude facilities. The attacks have pushed the region into "uncharted territory," according to Saudi officials.

The conflict has also had economic implications, with oil prices experiencing a slight dip despite the escalating attacks in the Gulf. Brent futures fell 1.06% to $89.78 a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate crude fell 0.76% to $83.82 a barrel.
