## Grassroots Football Development Holds Key to Nigeria's Revival

Former member of the Ondo State House of Assembly, Gbenga Omole, has emphasized the importance of investing in grassroots football development to revive Nigeria's football fortunes. Speaking at the grand finale of the Akogun U-20 Annual Football Competition in Oka Akoko, Omole urged the Federal and Ondo State governments, as well as other stakeholders, to provide greater support for grassroots football initiatives.

Omole, who organized the competition, highlighted the significance of identifying and nurturing promising young players at an early age. "This would provide a wider pool of talents for professional clubs and the national teams, and reduce the risk of losing talented footballers who lack the opportunity to develop their abilities," he explained. His passion for football and desire to empower young people inspired him to organize the competition in 2018, which has since become a platform for discovering, nurturing, and promoting promising football talents from the grassroots.

The competition has already produced several players who have progressed to professional football, with one of its products currently playing in Europe. Omole revealed that about 10 promising players had been identified from this year's competition, with four or five of them set to be taken to an academy in Abuja through a partnership with Inspiration Academy for further development. The organiser also cited the examples of prominent Nigerian footballers, including Finidi George, Joseph Yobo, and Sunday Oliseh, who all began their careers from similar grassroots environments.

The Ondo State Governor, Lucky Aiyedatiwa, represented by his Special Adviser on Special Duties and Strategy, Gani Mohammed, commended the organizers for the impressive turnout, organization, and quality of football skills displayed by the young players. The governor acknowledged the enormous potential of young people in the area and stressed that improved sporting facilities could help the community produce football stars capable of earning global recognition. He disclosed that the state government is currently working on a world-standard stadium in Akure and plans to develop more sporting facilities across the state.

Former Super Eagles goalkeeper Aloy Agu also commended the organizers, describing the final as a highly competitive encounter that demonstrated the determination and resilience required in football. Agu identified good skills, dribbling ability, anticipation, positioning, and decision-making as essential qualities talent scouts look for in grassroots players. The competition's grand finale saw Ore-Ofe FC emerge as winners, receiving N500,000, while Owase FC and Dogo FC received N300,000 and N200,000 respectively as first and second runners-up.

Omole's advocacy for grassroots football development resonates with the broader context of Nigeria's football landscape. Despite billions spent on football development, the country continues to struggle with a lack of homegrown talent. The reliance on imported players and the inability to consistently produce world-class footballers have contributed to the decline of Nigeria's football fortunes. By investing in grassroots football development, the Federal and Ondo State governments can help address this issue and provide a sustainable solution to the country's football woes.
