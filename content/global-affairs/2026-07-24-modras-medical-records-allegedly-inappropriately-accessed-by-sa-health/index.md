## A System in Question: South Australia's Ongoing Battle with Patient Record Security

Tony Modra, the former Australian Rules footballer, has been at the centre of a disturbing revelation in South Australia: three staff members at SA Health allegedly inappropriately accessed his private medical records. The incident has sparked a renewed debate about patient record security and the need for greater accountability within the health system.

Modra, who suffered serious injuries in a truck crash last month, has been undergoing treatment at a hospital in the Fleurieu Peninsula region. The alleged breach of his medical records has caused distress for his family, and SA Health has issued a formal apology. However, the incident is the latest in a series of high-profile cases highlighting the vulnerability of patient records in South Australia.

In 2016, 13 hospital staff were sanctioned for accessing the medical records of Cy Walsh, who was charged with the stabbing murder of his father, Phil Walsh, the then-coach of the Adelaide Crows. Two years ago, an SA Health investigation found that 18 staff members engaged in misconduct or inappropriately accessed the medical records of Charlie Stevens, the late son of SA Police Commissioner Grant Stevens.

These incidents have raised concerns about the culture within SA Health and the government's response to breaches. Opposition spokesperson Ben Hood has called for an inquiry into patient record privacy breaches, arguing that "the standard you walk past is the standard you accept." Hood's statement highlights the need for greater accountability and a more robust system for detecting and preventing unauthorized access to patient records.

Premier Peter Malinauskas has acknowledged the disappointment of the latest breach but emphasized that the "overall majority" of the 50,000-strong SA Health workforce acts with integrity. However, the repeated incidents suggest that more needs to be done to address the root causes of these breaches and restore trust in the system.

The incident has also sparked concerns from medical professionals, with Bernadette Mulholland, the chief industrial officer of the South Australian Salaried Medical Officers Association, stating that such cases erode trust in the health system. Mulholland emphasized the importance of maintaining confidentiality and ensuring that only authorized personnel have access to patient records.

SA Health has implemented measures to track and monitor access to patient records, but the latest breach suggests that more needs to be done to prevent unauthorized access. Acting Health Minister Chris Picton has committed to taking disciplinary action against staff members who have accessed patient records inappropriately, including termination of employment.

As the investigation into the alleged breach continues, the incident serves as a reminder of the need for greater vigilance and accountability within the health system. The repeated incidents highlight the importance of robust systems for detecting and preventing unauthorized access to patient records and the need for a culture of transparency and accountability within SA Health.
