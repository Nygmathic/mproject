## A Tumultuous Week in Kyiv: Zelensky's Gamble on Military Reform

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has made a bold move, dismissing the country's army commander-in-chief, Oleksandr Syrskyi, in a bid to quell growing unrest and bolster his administration's credibility. The sudden removal of Syrskyi, a 60-year-old veteran of the Soviet-school military, has sparked widespread protests and raised questions about the future of Ukraine's armed forces.

The decision to axe Syrskyi is the latest in a series of high-profile changes to Ukraine's military leadership. Just days ago, Defence Minister Mykhailo Fedorov, a 35-year-old dynamo who had energised the ministry and driven efforts to root out corruption, was ousted in a move that triggered street protests across the country. Initial whispers of a conflict between Fedorov and Syrskyi were soon confirmed, and the latter's departure has been seen as a key factor in calming tensions.

Syrskyi's replacement, Mykhailo Drapatyi, a 43-year-old battle-hardened general, has been hailed as a breath of fresh air. Drapatyi, a close friend of Fedorov's, has pledged to work tirelessly to strengthen the Ukrainian army and has been praised by many for his innovative approach to military leadership.

The ousting of Syrskyi and Fedorov's subsequent removal have sparked intense debate about the direction of Ukraine's military reform efforts. Many have questioned the wisdom of axing Fedorov, who was widely admired for his data-driven approach to military strategy and his successful efforts to mobilise the Ukrainian public in support of the war effort. His departure has left a power vacuum that many believe will be difficult to fill.

The protests that have erupted in response to Syrskyi's dismissal are not just about the removal of a senior military leader; they reflect deeper concerns about the future of Ukraine's armed forces and the government's commitment to reform. Zelensky's administration has been under growing pressure to address long-standing issues with military procurement, mobilisation, and contracting, all of which have contributed to the sense of crisis that has gripped Ukraine in recent weeks.

As the dust settles on this tumultuous week in Kyiv, it remains to be seen whether Zelensky's gamble on military reform will pay off. The appointment of Drapatyi has been welcomed by many, but the road ahead will be fraught with challenges. The Ukrainian president has asked Drapatyi and acting Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara to develop a new defence strategy, one that addresses key issues such as reforming the military corps system, improving weapons supply, and expanding the use of drones.

For now, the focus remains on the protests and the fallout from Syrskyi's dismissal. Many protesters will want to see Fedorov restored to a position of real authority, where he can continue to drive forward innovation in Ukraine's battle with Russia. If Zelensky can deliver on this promise, he may yet be able to repair the immediate damage. But much will depend on the government's willingness to listen to the concerns of its people and to implement meaningful reforms.

As Ukraine navigates this uncertain terrain, the international community is watching with interest.
