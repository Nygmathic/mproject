## Rapprochement in the Making: Pentagon Official Hails Strengthened Ties with PLA

A rare moment of optimism in the fraught US-China relationship emerged yesterday as a senior Pentagon official hailed "stronger" military ties with the People's Liberation Army (PLA) at a reception marking the 99th anniversary of the Chinese military's founding. Deputy Assistant Defence Secretary Alvaro Smith's remarks, delivered at the Chinese embassy in Washington, highlighted a gradual improvement in communication between the two militaries, a development that underscores the complexities of their strategic rivalry.

Smith's comments, which emphasized "clear, steady progress" in repairing a previously strained military-to-military channel, marked a significant departure from the tense rhetoric that has characterized much of the bilateral relationship in recent years. The official's statement acknowledged the work of Secretary Pete Hegseth, who has been instrumental in shaping a new approach to US-China military relations. This approach, as described by Smith, prioritizes "candid, respectful, clear conversations" aimed at de-escalation and the advancement of US national priorities.

The reception at the Chinese embassy, which was attended by a senior Pentagon official for the first time in several years, serves as a testament to the growing recognition of the importance of direct communication between the two militaries. Smith's presence at the event underscored the US Department of Defence's commitment to fostering a more collaborative relationship with the PLA, despite ongoing differences on a range of issues.

While the US and China have historically maintained a delicate balance of power, their military relationships have been subject to intense scrutiny in recent years. The Biden administration's efforts to reorient US foreign policy towards Asia, combined with China's aggressive military modernization, have created an environment of heightened tension. Nevertheless, Smith's remarks suggest that Washington is willing to engage with Beijing on a range of issues, from non-proliferation to maritime security.

The implications of this rapprochement extend beyond the realm of military-to-military relations. A strengthened US-China relationship could have far-reaching consequences for regional stability, as well as the global balance of power. As the world's two largest economies continue to navigate the complexities of their strategic rivalry, the emergence of a more collaborative military relationship offers a rare glimmer of hope for a more peaceful and stable future.

## The New Paradigm: A Shift in US-China Military Relations

The notion that the US and China are seeking to reorient their military relationships is not new. However, the speed and scope of this transformation are noteworthy. In recent years, Washington has made concerted efforts to engage with Beijing on a range of issues, from counter-terrorism to disaster relief. These initiatives have been accompanied by a significant increase in high-level exchanges between the two militaries, including the establishment of a hotline between the US and Chinese defence ministries.

The US Department of Defence's new approach to US-China military relations has been shaped by Secretary Hegseth's vision for a more collaborative and respectful relationship. This vision, as articulated by Smith, emphasizes the importance of candid and respectful dialogue between the two militaries. By prioritizing de-escalation and the advancement of US national priorities, the US is seeking to create a more stable and predictable environment for military-to-military relations.

## Regional Perspectives: A Mixed Reaction

The implications of a strengthened US-China military relationship are far from uniform. While some regional actors have welcomed the development, others have expressed concern about the potential consequences. In Tokyo, officials have echoed Washington's commitment to a more collaborative relationship with Beijing, while in Seoul, there is growing anxiety about the potential implications for regional security.

In Beijing, the response to Smith's remarks has been more cautious. While officials have acknowledged the importance of military-to-military relations, they have also emphasized the need for greater caution and prudence in the face of ongoing US aggression. As one Chinese diplomat noted, "We welcome the US's willingness to engage with us on military issues, but we cannot simply ignore the broader strategic context of our relationship."
