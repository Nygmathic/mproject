## A Shift in Military Strategy: Trump Orders Cuts to South Korea Joint Drills

US President Donald Trump has decided to scale back joint US military drills with South Korea, citing a "very good relationship" with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un. However, military officials from both countries have warned that Kim's regime is acquiring new battlefield tactics from the Ukraine war, rendering the drills less effective than previously thought.

The drills, part of the Ulchi Freedom Shield exercise, were set to begin on Monday and run until 27 August, involving approximately 18,000 South Korean troops alongside US forces and personnel from 11 member states of the United Nations Command. However, Trump has ordered the defence secretary, Pete Hegseth, to "substantially reduce" the drills, reportedly due to their cost and perceived hostility towards North Korea, which Trump asserts has been "unthreatening and respectful" during his presidency.

This move is a departure from the stance of US military officials, who have emphasized the importance of incorporating lessons learned from North Korean troops' experience in Ukraine into this year's drills. In a recent briefing, Col Ryan Donald, public affairs director for the US-led commands in South Korea, stated that the exercises would reflect "drones, GPS disruption, and cyber attacks" – threats directly linked to North Korea's military support for Russia in Ukraine. Donald noted that the drills have evolved over the past five years to account for the increased use of these technologies and North Korea's growing battlefield experience.

South Korea's defence ministry has maintained that the joint drills will proceed as scheduled, contradicting Trump's assertion that the exercises are unnecessary. The ministry's stance is echoed by Lt Gen Scott Winter, deputy commander of the United Nations Command, who emphasized the significance of countering the threats posed by North Korea's contemporary battlefield experience. Winter highlighted that this year's drills would be "bigger and better than ever," underscoring the importance of preparedness in the face of evolving North Korean capabilities.

The decision to scale back the drills comes as North Korea deepens its military ties with Russia, with more than 14,000 North Korean troops having fought alongside Russian forces in Ukraine. The construction of a road bridge over the Tumen river, which analysts believe functions as a military logistics corridor, is also nearing completion. These developments have raised concerns among military experts, who warn that North Korea's increasing battlefield experience and military cooperation with Russia pose a significant threat to regional stability.

Chun In-bum, a retired lieutenant general who commanded South Korea's special forces, underscored the importance of timely preparation for emerging challenges, stating that North Korea's capabilities have undergone a "serious improvement." Chun's warning highlights the need for continued vigilance and adaptation in the face of evolving threats.

The implications of Trump's decision are far-reaching, with analysts suggesting that the US president's move may embolden North Korea to further escalate its military activities. The reduced scale of the drills also raises questions about the effectiveness of the US-South Korea alliance in deterring North Korean aggression. As the situation continues to unfold, one thing is clear: the landscape of regional security has shifted, and the international community will be watching closely to see how this development plays out.
