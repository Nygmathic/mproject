## Laying Bare the Fallout

Colombia is confronting a humanitarian crisis of unprecedented proportions in the aftermath of a devastating earthquake that tore through its communities, leaving a trail of destruction and unprecedented loss of life. The death toll now stands at 300, with almost 13,000 homes irreparably damaged. Amidst this turmoil, Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella has taken a critical step, appealing to his counterpart, US President Donald Trump, to provide the nation with a vital respite from the crippling economic impact of the quake.

At the heart of this plea is a contentious trade issue that has been ongoing since late July. In a bid to bolster the US economy, Trump had implemented a tariff increase on various Colombian products, which rose from 10 per cent to 12.5 per cent. While some sectors were exempt, such as coffee and oil, the tariffs have had a disproportionate effect on Colombian businesses, exacerbating their already dire circumstances following the earthquake.

De la Espriella's appeal to Trump is a pragmatic acknowledgment of the urgent need for relief. The Colombian leader has requested that his counterpart temporarily suspend the tariffs, citing the need to ease the burden on Colombian business owners who are struggling to cope with the aftermath of the disaster. In a statement posted on social media, De la Espriella emphasized the gravity of the situation, highlighting the difficulties faced by entrepreneurs who are working tirelessly to provide aid and support to those affected by the earthquake.
