## China's AI Governance: A Growing Divide

China's World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organisation (Waico) has been established with the stated aim of advancing "artificial intelligence for good and for all." However, the new international body has been met with skepticism and concerns that it could exacerbate the growing divide between China and the United States over AI governance.

The emergence of Waico, with 29 initial members primarily from the Global South, has been seen by some as a strategic move by Beijing to counter Washington's Pax Silica partnership. Formed last December, Pax Silica comprises 24 members with a focus on building "supply chain security" and is widely perceived as a tech bloc designed to exclude China.

Analysts have warned that China must avoid creating a binary divide between its own AI governance framework and that of the United States. This could lead to a situation where other countries are forced to choose between the two, undermining global cooperation and exacerbating existing tensions.

Waico's initial membership from the Global South has raised questions about whether China is attempting to create a rival bloc. However, officials insist that the organisation is not designed to be an alternative to Pax Silica, but rather a complementary initiative aimed at advancing AI for the greater good.

The establishment of both Waico and Pax Silica has also sparked concerns about the polarisation of the global AI landscape. As the two superpowers continue to invest in their respective AI governance frameworks, the risk of a technological cold war grows, with other countries potentially being forced to choose between the two.

The implications of this growing divide extend beyond the realm of technology. A binary approach to AI governance could have far-reaching consequences for global economic and political stability, as well as the future of international cooperation.
