## Constitutionally Challenged

A French court has dealt a significant blow to President Emmanuel Macron's plans to restrict social media access for minors, ruling that the proposed ban infringes upon freedom of expression and privacy. The constitutional council's decision has sparked widespread interest as governments around the world weigh the pros and cons of restricting children's use of social media.

The contested law, approved by parliament in July, aimed to bar under-15s from opening social media accounts from September 1, with tech platforms required to close existing accounts within four months and implement age-verification tools. However, the council found that the legislation failed to specify the necessary conditions and limits for age verification, effectively rendering the restrictions unconstitutional.

This setback is a significant blow to Macron's efforts to regulate social media use among minors, particularly in light of growing concerns about the impact of digital platforms on children's mental health and well-being. France's top court has sent a clear message that any such restrictions must be carefully calibrated to balance the need to protect young users with the importance of safeguarding their fundamental rights.

The move has significant implications for the broader debate on social media regulation, with several countries following Australia's lead in introducing age restrictions. Indonesia and Malaysia have implemented restrictions for under-16s on certain platforms, while the UK is preparing to block leading apps for the same age group next year. The European Commission has also signaled its intention to seek stronger protections for children from harmful social media features.

However, social media companies have generally opposed blanket bans, arguing that they already have measures in place to protect younger users, including a minimum age requirement of 13. This stance has been echoed by Ursula von der Leyen, the president of the European Commission, who has advocated for age-appropriate restrictions rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

Macron's administration has vowed to rework the legislation to address the constitutional council's concerns, with the president remaining committed to implementing the reform before France's presidential election in spring 2027. The Élysée has emphasized that Macron remains determined to push through the necessary changes, despite the setback.

As the debate on social media regulation continues to unfold, it is clear that the issue requires a nuanced and multi-faceted approach. The decision by the French constitutional council has set a high bar for any future attempts to restrict social media access for minors, underscoring the need for careful consideration and consultation with all stakeholders involved.
