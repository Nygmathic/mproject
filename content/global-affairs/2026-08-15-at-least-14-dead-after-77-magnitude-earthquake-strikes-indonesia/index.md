## Devastating Quake Strikes Indonesian Island, Kills At Least 14

A powerful 7.7-magnitude earthquake struck the Indonesian island of Flores early on Saturday, leaving at least 14 people dead and dozens injured in its wake. The quake, which occurred at a depth of 15km, has caused widespread destruction and triggered a series of aftershocks that have further exacerbated the situation.

The disaster mitigation agency, BPBD, has confirmed that at least 14 people have lost their lives in the earthquake, while local rescuer Emanuel Melkiades Laka Lena reported that up to 20 people may have been killed. The region has been hit by dozens of aftershocks, including a magnitude 6.1 tremor, which has left buildings severely damaged and thousands of people displaced.

One of the most striking images from the disaster has come from the port in Maumere, where large chunks of concrete were seen breaking off the terminal building and crashing onto passengers waiting to board an inter-island ferry. The situation has been further complicated by the receding of the sea, which has left residents in a state of panic and prompted widespread evacuations.

The quake struck the island of Flores shortly before 05:00 local time on Saturday, with officials warning that the figures for casualties and damage are preliminary and subject to change. BPBD has cautioned that the full extent of the destruction will only become clear once search and rescue operations are complete.

The Indonesian island of Flores is no stranger to earthquakes, lying as it does along the meeting point of three major tectonic plates. The country's geography makes it prone to seismic activity, and the disaster highlights the need for robust disaster preparedness and response mechanisms.

## Island's Residents Left to Face the Consequences

The impact of the earthquake on the island's residents has been severe, with many left to face the consequences of the disaster. Arnold Welianto, a villager from Talibura, described the experience of being awoken by the quake: "I was startled awake and immediately went to my child's room. I was very scared and my heart was racing."

Welianto's account is echoed by Yulian Juita Ekalia, a university lecturer from Ruteng, who said that she had dragged her stove outside and was cooking there for fear of further tremors. "I have never felt an earthquake this big," she said. "It felt like we were on a trampoline - it was really scary."

The situation on the ground remains chaotic, with thousands of people forced to evacuate their homes and seek shelter elsewhere. The Indonesian authorities have advised people to remain calm and to stay away from coastal areas and structures showing signs of damage.

## Assessing the Full Extent of the Destruction

The full extent of the destruction caused by the earthquake will only become clear once search and rescue operations are complete. Preliminary reports indicate that 54 houses have been severely damaged, as well as five healthcare facilities, three places of worship, six government buildings, an educational institution, and a warehouse.

Manggarai province is among the most affected areas, with BNPB stating that three fatalities had been recorded in Sikka province, six in Manggarai province, and five in East Manggarai province. The province's remote and mountainous terrain has made it difficult to access the affected areas, with officials warning that it may take time to assess the full extent of the damage.
