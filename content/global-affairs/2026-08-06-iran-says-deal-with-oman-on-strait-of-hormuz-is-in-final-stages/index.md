## Deal in Final Stages: Iran's Route through the Strait of Hormuz

Iran has confirmed that a deal with Oman on a route for shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is in its final stages. According to Foreign Ministry Spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei, the agreement has been reached on the geographical coordinates of the route, marking a significant development in efforts to reopen the key shipping lane.

Baqaei emphasized, however, that the deal with Oman would not guarantee safe navigation through the strait, as security remains impacted by the US blockade of Iran's ports. Iran has maintained that the blockade is a major obstacle to resolving the crisis, and has accused the US of exacerbating the situation through aggressive and threatening actions against the country.

The US and Israel attacked Iran in February, precipitating a sharp decline in traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, which is crucial for global energy supplies. Since then, Iran has largely blocked the strait, citing concerns over the safety of shipping in the face of the US naval blockade. The move has had a significant impact on energy prices, which have fluctuated wildly during the conflict.

The proposed deal with Oman would see Iran and Oman agree on a temporary route for shipping through the strait, which could remain open for between two to four months. Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi confirmed that the new route would be temporary, but provided few details on the specifics of the agreement.

The deal has been met with caution from the US, which has maintained a naval blockade of Iranian ports in the region. The US has also expressed concerns over Iran's plans to impose a fee on vessels wishing to cross the strait, with a senior Iranian official cited by Reuters suggesting that Iran is seeking fees of between 5% and 7% of the price of cargoes.

In contrast, Oman is reportedly discussing fees of around 3%, while Washington wants no fees at all. The proposed deal would also give Tehran control over ships entering the Gulf through the Strait of Hormuz, a move that has raised concerns over the implications for regional security.

The US and Oman have yet to comment on the proposal, but the development has been welcomed by energy markets, with oil prices edging lower on Thursday following the news. The proposed deal is seen as a significant step towards resolving the crisis, but its success remains uncertain given the complex web of issues at play.

The US has maintained a hardline stance on Iran, with President Trump warning that Iran would be "hit very hard" if the strait did not open "very soon". The threat has been met with a defiant response from Tehran, which has maintained that it is not negotiating with the US and has no plans to do so.

As the situation remains fluid, the international community is closely watching developments on the ground. The proposed deal with Oman represents a key opportunity for Iran and the US to find common ground, but its success will depend on a range of factors, including the willingness of all parties to compromise.
