## A Brewing Storm Over Academic Freedom

A scathing critique from Chinese state media has emerged in response to a recent report by the Washington-based Centre for Immigration Studies (CIS), which hailed a significant drop in US student visas issued to Chinese and Indian nationals as a boon for US students and national security. The report, which analysed US State Department data, revealed a 46% decline in F-1 student visas issued to Chinese nationals and a 60% drop in those issued to Indian nationals compared with average levels from previous years.

The CIS report's assertion that fewer Chinese students in the US would reduce the risks associated with technology transfer and espionage has sparked a backlash in China, with Chinese media accusing the organisation of recycling unfounded claims about the security risks posed by Chinese students. The Global Times tabloid, a prominent nationalistic publication, has taken particular issue with the report, arguing that it failed to provide evidence to support its claims and that the student visa policy has become a deeply politicised issue in the US.

According to the report, the sharp drop in visas issued to Chinese students is not a result of any concrete evidence of espionage or technology theft, but rather a consequence of a series of US political manoeuvres aimed at enhancing scrutiny of students from specific countries. These measures include suspending interviews, conducting comprehensive social media screening, and implementing layered background checks. The Global Times has characterised these actions as "a series of US political manoeuvres" that have led to the decline in visas.

The debate over the CIS report's findings has implications that extend far beyond the realm of education and security, touching on issues of academic freedom and the role of international students in fostering cross-cultural understanding. As the world becomes increasingly interconnected, the exchange of ideas and expertise between nations has become an essential component of global cooperation and development. However, the increasingly politicised nature of this exchange raises concerns about the erosion of academic freedom and the potential for xenophobia to undermine the very principles of internationalisation.

In recent years, the US has become a leading destination for international students, with China and India among the top sending countries. The influx of international students has brought significant benefits to the US, including economic gains, cultural enrichment, and a more diverse talent pool. However, the CIS report's framing of the decline in Chinese and Indian student visas as a benefit for US students and national security has been met with widespread criticism, with many arguing that it perpetuates a flawed narrative about the security risks posed by international students.

## The Politics of Education

The controversy surrounding the CIS report highlights the complex and often fraught nature of international relations, where even seemingly innocuous issues can become highly politicised. The use of education as a battleground in the US-China rivalry is a stark reminder of the ways in which academic freedom and cultural exchange can be manipulated for strategic purposes.

The CIS report's reliance on data from the US State Department has been called into question by some experts, who argue that the decline in student visas is a symptom of a broader trend of increased scrutiny and restrictions on international students. This trend has been exacerbated by the growing tensions between the US and China, with both nations engaging in a series of reciprocal measures aimed at limiting the other's access to education and other areas of cooperation.

As the debate over the CIS report continues to unfold, it remains to be seen how this controversy will impact the future of international student mobility and academic freedom. Will the US continue to pursue a more restrictive approach to international students, or will there be a pushback against the politicisation of education? The answer to this question will have far-reaching implications for the global community, highlighting the need for a more nuanced and evidence-based approach to international relations.
