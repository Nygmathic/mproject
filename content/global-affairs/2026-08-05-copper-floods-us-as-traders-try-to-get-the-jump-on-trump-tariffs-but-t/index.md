## Copper Rush: Traders Flock to US in Anticipation of Tariffs

Copper shipments to the United States have surged to a 12-year high, with over 200,000 tonnes arriving in American ports in July. This influx is largely a result of traders attempting to beat the anticipated imposition of tariffs on cathode imports by US President Donald Trump. As a consequence, copper prices in the US are being driven up, but analysts warn that a sudden reversal of Trump's policy could lead to a sharp decline in prices, reminiscent of the "Taco" scenario in which the president's threats are ultimately downplayed.

The sheer volume of copper pouring into the US has pushed the combined inventories of the New York Commodity Exchange (Comex) and the London Metal Exchange (LME) above 740,000 tonnes. Furthermore, an additional 110,860 tonnes are being held in storage at US ports, according to the LME. This trend is not limited to LME facilities, as data suggests that Trump's tariff threat is pulling copper out of the Chinese mainland. The bonded-warehouse hub of Lingang, located in Shanghai, has seen a similar trend unfold since the start of the year.

The rush to stockpile copper in the US is a reflection of the uncertainty surrounding Trump's trade policies. His proposed tariffs have created a sense of urgency among traders, who are seeking to front-load their copper shipments in anticipation of the levies. This move is seen as a calculated risk, as a reversal of Trump's policy could lead to a surplus of copper in the US, driving prices down and rendering the early shipments unprofitable.

Analysts are warning of the potential consequences of a sudden policy reversal. Should the "Taco" scenario play out, copper prices in the US would likely fall in the short term, as a glut of the metal would flood the market. This would be a significant blow to the copper market, which has been impacted by the ongoing trade tensions between the US and its trading partners.

The outflow of copper from China, particularly from bonded-warehouse hubs like Lingang, is a significant development in the global copper market. China is the world's largest consumer of copper, and any disruption to its supply chain has the potential to send shockwaves through the industry. The fact that copper is being pulled out of China in anticipation of Trump's tariffs highlights the complex and interconnected nature of global trade.

The implications of this trend are far-reaching and have significant implications for the global economy. As the world's largest consumers of copper continue to grapple with the consequences of Trump's trade policies, the copper market is likely to remain volatile in the short term. Traders will be closely watching the developments in the US, as any policy reversal or clarification on the tariffs could have a significant impact on the market.
