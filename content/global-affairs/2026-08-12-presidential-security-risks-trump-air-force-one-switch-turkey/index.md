## Presidential Deception: US Military and Secret Service Insisted on Air Force One Decoy in Turkey

US President Donald Trump has confirmed that he covertly switched planes upon his departure from [Turkey](/tags/turkey/), amidst reports of an Iranian assassination threat against him. The elaborate manoeuvre, first revealed by The Washington Post, involved deceiving journalists, White House staff members, and other government officials aboard [Air Force One](/tags/air-force-one/), who were led to believe the president was flying with them back to the US from a Nato summit in Ankara.

Trump explained that the [Secret Service](/tags/secret-service/) and military insisted on the switch, citing unspecified safety threats. "They wanted me to go on a different flight, a different plane, equal safety, but they wanted me to do it, so I do it. I do what they say," he said, deflecting criticisms about the decision. The deception was carried out as Trump departed from Ankara, leaving others to assume he was still on the original aircraft.

The US president acknowledged that he was aware of a threat, but declined to elaborate on the nature of the threat or the specific reasons behind the military's decision to switch planes. "I guess there was a threat out there, I didn’t really ask too much about it. I get a lot of threats," he said, downplaying the significance of the threat.

The incident has raised questions about the level of secrecy surrounding presidential security, and the extent to which military and intelligence agencies are willing to go to protect the President. The use of a decoy plane, while not unprecedented, is a rare and extraordinary measure that underscores the gravity of the threat.
