## Gas Shortfalls and Regulatory Overhaul in Nigeria

Nigeria's domestic gas supply has fallen short of its target for the first six months of 2026, with a compliance level of just 65% against the 7C1 Domestic Gas Delivery Obligation allocation of 3.16 billion cubic feet per day. According to the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission (NUPRC), the country's daily gas supply averaged 2.05 billion cubic feet, a significant decline of 35% from the target.

This persistent gap between allocated and delivered gas has led the NUPRC to introduce a Gas Swap Framework aimed at improving compliance with the Domestic Gas Delivery Obligation. The framework seeks to address logistical and infrastructure constraints preventing some producers from meeting their obligations. By allowing operators with stranded or inaccessible gas to partner with those possessing the necessary infrastructure, the initiative aims to turn obligation into actual supply, make better use of existing assets, and strengthen Nigeria's gas value chain.

The introduction of the Gas Swap Framework is a critical step towards ensuring that Nigeria's vast gas reserves are leveraged to boost economic diversification, deepen industrialization, and improve energy security. However, industry stakeholders have consistently identified infrastructure limitations, evacuation constraints, and commercial challenges as key factors affecting full compliance with the obligation. The NUPRC's Chief Executive, Oritsemiyewa Eyesan, emphasized that collaboration between producers, transporters, and regulators will be essential in improving domestic gas availability and strengthening Nigeria's gas market.

The Domestic Gas Delivery Obligation, introduced as part of Nigeria's gas policy, requires upstream companies to reserve a specified portion of their gas production for domestic consumption. This initiative is a key component of the Federal Government's drive to leverage the country's gas reserves and promote economic growth. However, the current compliance level of 65% highlights the need for innovative and market-responsive solutions to address the delivery gap.

Experts have long emphasized the importance of leveraging Nigeria's gas reserves to drive economic development. The country's vast gas resources have the potential to support industrialization, stimulate economic growth, and improve energy security. However, the persistent gap between allocated and delivered gas underscores the need for a more comprehensive approach to address the logistical and infrastructure constraints hindering compliance with the Domestic Gas Delivery Obligation.

The Gas Swap Framework is a significant step towards bridging this gap. By allowing operators to partner and share resources, the initiative aims to make better use of existing assets and improve gas-to-power delivery. As Nigeria seeks to strengthen its gas value chain and promote economic diversification, the success of the Gas Swap Framework will be closely watched by stakeholders and analysts. The framework's ability to turn obligation into actual supply will be crucial in realizing the country's full potential in the gas sector.

The NUPRC's Chief Executive, Oritsemiyewa Eyesan, has urged industry stakeholders to support the initiative, emphasizing the importance of collaboration in improving domestic gas availability.

However, some experts have expressed concerns that the framework may not be sufficient to address the deeper structural issues hindering compliance with the Domestic Gas Delivery Obligation.
