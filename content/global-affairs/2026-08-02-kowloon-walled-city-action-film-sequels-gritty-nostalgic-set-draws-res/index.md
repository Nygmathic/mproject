## Echoes of a Bygone Era

A cluster of cluttered flats at Yuen Long InnoPark, a hi-tech industrial estate in the outskirts of Hong Kong, has been transformed into a gritty, nostalgic set for the sequel to the 2024 blockbuster film Twilight of the Warriors: Walled In. The set, which recreates the iconic Kowloon Walled City, has drawn the attention of residents who have been watching its transformation with increasing curiosity.

The production for Twilight of the Warriors: The Final Chapter coincides with Hong Kong authorities' recent bid to convert film sets and props into tourist attractions. This initiative is part of a broader effort to preserve the city's cultural heritage and stimulate local tourism. The authorities' focus on leveraging film sets as tourist attractions highlights the growing recognition of the importance of cinematic heritage in shaping urban identity.

The set itself is a highly detailed replica of the Kowloon Walled City, a notorious slum that was demolished in the late 1990s. The film's director, Soi Cheang, has stated that the sequel will capture the era leading up to the demolition, offering a glimpse into the lives of those who called the city home. The original film, released in 2024, was a revenge drama that centered on brotherhood among the triad gangs that ruled the crime-ridden estate during the 1980s.

## A Glimpse into the Past

For Ben Kock, a worker at the industrial estate, the transformation of the site has been a fascinating spectacle. Kock, who frequently jogs past the site during his lunch breaks, had not initially realized it was the set for the film sequel. "I just thought it was another construction project," he said. "But as I got closer, I realized it was something much bigger." Kock's observations are a testament to the power of film to capture the imagination and recreate the past.

The choice of location for the set is also significant. Yuen Long InnoPark, a former industrial site, has been transformed into a hub for creative industries, including film production. This shift reflects the city's efforts to diversify its economy and capitalize on its cultural heritage.
