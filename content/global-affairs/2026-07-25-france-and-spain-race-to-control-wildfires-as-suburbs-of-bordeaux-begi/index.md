## Fierce Blaze

Racing winds and unrelenting heat have brought France and Spain to the brink of disaster, as raging wildfires ravage the countryside. Thousands of residents have been forced to flee their homes, with officials warning that the situation could worsen in the coming hours.

French authorities have been evacuating suburbs near Bordeaux, a city in the southwest of the country, where wildfires have shown no signs of abating. More than 141,000 people have been evacuated from the Gironde and Landes regions, according to French Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu. The French army has been drafted in to help, and Lecornu has warned that the fires are at a level that has never been seen before.

Across the border in Spain, the situation is equally dire. A national emergency has been declared, with over 63,000 people evacuated or confined to their homes. Madrid's regional government has requested assistance, describing the situation as one of "extreme gravity." Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez is due to visit the affected area on Saturday, and has promised to deploy all available resources to combat the blaze.

The Madrid regional government's emergency management chief, Carlos Novillo, has warned that the fire is "at its peak and ... currently beyond the capacity of firefighters to contain." Francisco Martín, the government delegate in Madrid, has also sounded a cautionary note, warning that stronger winds later today could hamper efforts and intensify the fire.

France's President Emmanuel Macron has activated the EU's civil protection mechanism, seeking assistance from Croatia, Portugal, the Czech Republic, and Slovakia. Macron has said that reinforcements will soon be available, including two Croatian aircraft, two Portuguese planes, and two heavy helicopters from the Czech Republic and Slovakia. Meanwhile, France's interior minister, Laurent Nuñez, has reported that 32 wildfires are burning, with 50,000 hectares scorched already this year – nearly four times the total by the same date last year.
