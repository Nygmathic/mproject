## A Global Gold Rush

Tensions simmer in the corridors of power as the once-staid world of mining fundraising bursts into the global spotlight, driven by a perfect storm of geopolitics, commodity prices, and the energy transition. In the midst of this turmoil, Hong Kong is emerging as a key player, with the city's stock exchange and financial institutions at the forefront of the action. As the stakes rise, one thing is clear: this is not just a domestic market issue, but a global phenomenon with far-reaching implications.

## A Commodities Supercycle Takes Shape

The past year has seen a seismic shift in the mining sector, with commodity prices experiencing a meteoric rise. The trigger for this surge was the global energy transition, as governments and corporations scrambled to reduce their reliance on fossil fuels and switch to cleaner, more sustainable alternatives. The resulting demand for metals such as copper, lithium, and cobalt has been nothing short of astonishing, with prices skyrocketing in response. For mining and metals companies, this has been a game-changer, as they seek to capitalize on the "supercycle" and raise the capital needed to meet the unprecedented demand.

At the heart of this phenomenon is Hong Kong, a city that has long been a hub for international trade and finance. The Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing (HKEX), the territory's main stock exchange, has been at the forefront of the action, with its head of global issuer services, Johnson Chui, hailing 2025 as an inflection point. "Mining and metals companies have become a key driver in the capital market," Chui noted, "as rising commodity prices, triggered by the energy transition and increasing demand, have created a perfect storm of opportunity for companies to raise capital and invest in new projects." With the HKEX at the center of this maelstrom, the city is poised to become a global leader in mining fundraising, with a plethora of international companies and investors clamoring for a piece of the action.

## The Geopolitics of Mining Fundraising

As the stakes rise, however, concerns over national security are beginning to complicate dealmaking. Governments around the world are taking a closer look at the ownership and control of mining companies, with some countries imposing strict rules and regulations to ensure that their strategic resources are not falling into the wrong hands. For companies seeking to raise capital in Hong Kong, this has created a complex and often fraught environment, as they navigate the treacherous waters of geopolitics and regulatory compliance.

One of the most significant challenges facing companies is the increasing scrutiny of foreign ownership. In the past, it was not uncommon for international companies to list in Hong Kong and raise capital from a diverse range of investors. However, with the rise of nationalism and protectionism, governments are becoming increasingly wary of foreign ownership and control. China, in particular, has been at the forefront of this trend, with its government imposing strict rules and regulations on foreign investors and companies operating in the country. For companies seeking to raise capital in Hong Kong, this has created a complex and often fraught environment, as they navigate the treacherous waters of geopolitics and regulatory compliance.

## The Rise of Emerging Markets

As the global mining sector continues to evolve, one trend is clear: emerging markets are on the rise. Companies from Africa, Asia, and Latin America are increasingly taking center stage, as they seek to capitalize on the opportunities presented by the commodities supercycle. In particular, countries such as South Africa, Chile, and Peru are emerging as major players, with their rich mineral resources and favorable investment climates making them attractive destinations for international investors.

At the forefront of this trend is the African continent, where a new generation of mining companies is emerging to take advantage of the opportunities presented by the energy transition. Companies such as Anglo American, Glencore, and Vedanta are all investing heavily in the region, as they seek to tap into the rich mineral resources and growing demand for commodities. For Hong Kong, this presents a significant opportunity, as the city seeks to establish itself as a global leader in mining fundraising.
