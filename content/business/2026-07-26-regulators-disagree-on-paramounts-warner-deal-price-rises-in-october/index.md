## Regulators at Odds: Paramount's Warner Deal Hangs in the Balance

Regulators Disagree On Paramount’s Warner Deal. Price Rises In October

A proposed acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery by Paramount Skydance Corporation has reached an impasse, with four regulatory regimes in the United States, the United Kingdom, and Europe offering starkly different assessments of the deal's implications. The transaction, valued at over $45 billion, has been cleared by the U.S. Justice Department but is under scrutiny from a California court, the European Union, and the British government.

At the heart of the dispute lies a fundamental disagreement over the nature of media power and its impact on the market. While the Justice Department found the deal "not likely to result in harm to competition or American consumers" in three key markets – streaming video on demand, linear television, and film production – a California federal court has reached the opposite conclusion on the theatrical distribution front. The court, citing Paramount's anticipated 27% share of wide-release theatrical distribution, has paused the deal pending further review.

Meanwhile, the European Union has approved the deal subject to conditions, requiring Paramount to exit a European distribution venture, United International Pictures. In the United Kingdom, Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy has signaled her intention to intervene under the Enterprise Act 2002, citing concerns over news plurality and the impact of the merger on on-demand programming services. However, the British parliament's calendar is at odds with the American merger timetable, with the Commons set to return on September 1 and rise again for recess in the middle of the month.

The deal's timeline is now inextricably linked to the regulatory process. Paramount has agreed to delay closing until June 2027, with additional consideration accruing daily at a rate equivalent to 25 cents per share every 90 days. This would translate to roughly $650 million per quarter, or $7 million per day, adding approximately $1.7 billion to the purchase price if the deal closes. In the event of regulatory failure, Paramount could owe Warner Bros. Discovery a staggering $7 billion termination fee, guaranteed by Larry Ellison and his revocable trust.

The structure of the deal, which converts every additional month of regulatory argument into a higher price for Warner Bros. Discovery shareholders and a guaranteed payment if the transaction collapses, has significant implications for the parties involved. Paramount has forcefully argued that the states' challenge is disconnected from the modern media market and that delay serves large technology platforms rather than consumers. Warner Bros. Discovery, on the other hand, has been relatively quiet, its position written into the contract.

As the regulatory review process continues to unfold, the clock is ticking against a deal that has already been delayed. The difficulty lies in the fact that four authorities are reviewing the transaction, but they are not measuring the same source of media power. The outcome remains uncertain, with the British government's planned introduction of secondary legislation to address the current legislative gap adding further complexity to an already intricate picture.
