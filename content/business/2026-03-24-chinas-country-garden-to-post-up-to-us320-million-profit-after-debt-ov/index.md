## China's Debt Odyssey: Country Garden's Road to Recovery

As the sun set over the gleaming skyscrapers of Shenzhen, China's southern technology hub, whispers of a once-faltering giant's resurgence began to circulate among financial circles. Country Garden Holdings, one of the world's largest property developers, is poised to post a profit of up to US$320 million for the year 2025, marking a remarkable turnaround from its US$35.15 billion loss just a year prior. The Hong Kong-listed company's filings with the Hong Kong stock exchange on Monday night revealed a non-cash gain of approximately 1 billion yuan to 2.2 billion yuan, bolstered by the completion of its debt restructuring plan.

The stakes of Country Garden's recovery are immense, with far-reaching implications for China's beleaguered property sector and the global economy at large. As the world's second-largest economy navigates a delicate balance of growth and debt management, the fortunes of Country Garden serve as a barometer for the country's ability to contain its debt crisis. China's property market, which accounts for nearly 30% of its GDP, has been a major contributor to the nation's economic woes, with many developers struggling to service their massive debt obligations. Country Garden's debt restructuring, which involved the issuance of new bonds and the consolidation of debt, is seen as a model for other struggling developers to follow.

Country Garden's journey to recovery is a story of resilience in the face of adversity. Founded in 1992 by Yang Guoqiang, the company rose to become one of China's largest property developers, with a portfolio spanning from luxury apartments to sprawling urban complexes. However, as China's economic growth slowed and the property market began to cool, Country Garden found itself struggling to meet its debt obligations. The company's woes were exacerbated by a 2022 liquidity crisis, which saw it default on a US$545 million bond repayment, sending shockwaves through the global financial markets.

The debt restructuring plan, which was initiated in 2023, has been a crucial factor in Country Garden's turnaround. By issuing new bonds and consolidating debt, the company has managed to reduce its debt burden and improve its liquidity position. The completion of the restructuring plan has also enabled Country Garden to unlock significant non-cash gains, which have contributed to its improved financial performance. According to industry analysts, the company's new debt profile is more sustainable, with a lower debt-to-equity ratio and a more manageable debt service burden.
