## Devastating Flooding Brings Akwa Ibom to a Standstill

Hours of torrential rain have left communities in Uyo, the Akwa Ibom State capital, submerged in floodwaters, prompting residents to stage a protest and blockade a major interstate highway in anger. The flooding, which began on Wednesday evening, has destroyed homes, household property, and livestock, and has become a recurring nightmare for residents who claim they have been neglected by the state government.

Several communities, including Nkemba, Abak Road, Ukana Offot, and Port Harcourt Street, have been severely affected, with residents forced to abandon their homes and livelihoods. The rain, which eased briefly overnight, resumed with increased intensity on Thursday morning, catching residents off guard and leaving them helpless.

The protesters, who blocked the busy Ikot Ekpene Road, disrupting traffic on the highway linking Akwa Ibom with Abia and Cross River states, are demanding answers from the state government. They claim that the prolonged delay in completing the Tabernacle Road project has worsened flooding in surrounding communities, leaving them to suffer in silence.

Udeme Ubong, a resident of Tabernacle Road, told PREMIUM TIMES that repeated flooding has become unbearable after years of abandoned road construction. "For eight years now, we have suffered like slaves in our own state. We park our vehicles elsewhere because of flooding. Anytime it rains heavily, water enters our homes and destroys our property," he said.

The situation in Nkemba is equally dire, with residents alleging that poor drainage maintenance has led to the death of one resident and hospitalization of two others. They claimed that the government had agreed to evacuate accumulated silt from the drainage channel annually but had failed to honor that commitment. According to them, runoff from surrounding communities empties into the Nkemba drainage channel, but years of accumulated silt have significantly reduced its capacity.

The flooding has also highlighted the need for effective flood control measures in Akwa Ibom. In June, PREMIUM TIMES observed that substantial sections of the drainage corridor around Nkemba were heavily silted ahead of the peak rainy season. The lack of maintenance and upkeep has left the community vulnerable to flooding, and residents are demanding action from the government.

As the situation in Akwa Ibom continues to deteriorate, residents are calling on the state government to take immediate action to address the flooding and provide relief to affected communities. The protest, which has brought traffic to a standstill, is a clear indication of the frustration and anger felt by residents who have been neglected for far too long.

## The Road to Nowhere: A History of Neglect

The Tabernacle Road project, which was initially scheduled for completion in 2018, has been plagued by delays and neglect. The project, which was budgeted for over N2 billion, has been stalled for years, with residents bearing the brunt of the consequences. The lack of progress on the project has led to repeated flooding in surrounding communities, causing damage to property and livelihoods.
