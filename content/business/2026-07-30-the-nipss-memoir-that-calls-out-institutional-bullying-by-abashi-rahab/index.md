## Institutional Bullying: A Memoir Exposes the Dark Side of Power

Yushau A. Shuaib's memoir, MNI versus MNIPR in NIPSS: Tackling Institutional Bullying, has left a lasting impression on those who have read it. The book's unflinching portrayal of the darker aspects of institutional power has raised critical questions about the way adults are treated in supposedly meritocratic environments.

Shuaib's experiences, spanning over three decades in crisis communication and public relations, provide a unique perspective on the inner workings of power. His confrontations with high-ranking officials and institutions, including the Federal Civil Service and Access Bank, have given him a sharp insight into the dynamics of power and coercion. This background lends credibility to his meticulously assembled case file, which examines his own experience with institutional bullying.

At its core, the memoir is not a rant or a lament, but a thoughtful analysis of the ways in which institutions can strip adults of their dignity. Shuaib's writing is characterized by its nuance and self-awareness, as he grapples with the complexities of power and leadership. The book is a call to action, urging institutions to reexamine their treatment of adults and to prioritize dialogue, disagreement, and independent judgment.

One of the most striking aspects of the memoir is its portrayal of the petty cruelties and bureaucratic red tape that can suffocate individuals in institutional settings. The story of a participant who was reprimanded for standing with his hand in his pocket is a telling example of how even small acts of conformity can be enforced. Shuaib's documentation of these incidents paints a picture of a system that values obedience over autonomy and creativity.

The book also highlights the emotional costs of institutional bullying. Shuaib recalls reading apology letters that seemed to have been written by schoolchildren begging for forgiveness, rather than by accomplished professionals. These moments are not just trivial or humorous; they are a manifestation of the dehumanizing effects of institutional power.

Shuaib's memoir has sparked a necessary conversation about the importance of dignity and autonomy in adult life. His writing is a reminder that institutions have a responsibility to nurture excellence, rather than to stifle it. By exposing the darker aspects of institutional power, Shuaib's memoir offers a powerful critique of the ways in which we treat adults in supposedly meritocratic environments.

## The Institutional Culture of Fear

The memoir sheds light on the institutional culture of fear that pervades many organizations. Shuaib's experiences demonstrate how institutions can create an atmosphere of anxiety and compliance, where individuals are reluctant to speak out or challenge the status quo. This culture of fear is perpetuated by the use of arbitrary rules and regulations, which can be used to intimidate and control individuals.

One of the most striking aspects of the memoir is its portrayal of the ways in which institutions can strip adults of their professional identity. Shuaib recalls being instructed not to use professional titles or post-nominal letters, and being forced to rewrite responses to official queries in a prescribed NIPSS style. These moments are a manifestation of the ways in which institutions can dehumanize individuals and reduce them to mere cogs in a bureaucratic machine.
