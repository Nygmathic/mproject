## Philanthropist and Author MacKenzie Scott Embarks on Literary Venture

Billionaire philanthropist MacKenzie Scott has announced plans to self-publish her third novel, "Last Days in Two Nations (A History in Eighteen Minds)," on Substack throughout the month of August. The book, an 80,000-word narrative, will be released serially, with Scott providing scant details about its content beyond a title page, table of contents, character list, and epigraphs.

This literary endeavor marks a departure from Scott's relatively low profile in recent years. The philanthropist and author has been largely absent from the public eye since her 2019 divorce from Amazon founder Jeff Bezos, during which she received a 4% stake in the online retailer. Since then, Scott has given away upwards of $26 billion to over 2,500 organizations, including historically Black colleges and universities, environmental groups, and economic equity nonprofits.

Scott's literary career began with the publication of her debut novel, "The Testing of Luther Albright," in 2005. The book received widespread critical acclaim, earning an American Book Award and a spot on the Los Angeles Times Best of the Year list. Her subsequent novel, "Traps," published in 2013, was also met with positive reviews, although Scott's subsequent literary endeavors have been limited to rare posts on her blog and website.

Notably, Scott's literary pursuits have been shaped by her academic background in creative writing. Under the tutelage of Nobel Prize-winning author Toni Morrison at Princeton University, Scott completed a 168-page thesis project, "The Fathering Water," which Morrison supervised. The two authors reportedly maintained a close relationship after Scott graduated in 1992.

Scott's financial circumstances have also undergone significant changes since her divorce. Her estimated net worth now stands at $33.8 billion, a staggering figure that underscores her status as one of the world's wealthiest individuals. Despite her vast resources, Scott has chosen to publish her new novel on Substack, a platform that allows authors to connect directly with readers and maintain creative control.

The decision to release "Last Days in Two Nations" on Substack reflects Scott's commitment to autonomy and artistic expression. By forgoing traditional publishing channels, Scott can present her work to a dedicated audience without the constraints of commercial considerations. As she embarks on this new literary venture, Scott's followers are left to ponder the themes and ideas that will shape her latest narrative.
