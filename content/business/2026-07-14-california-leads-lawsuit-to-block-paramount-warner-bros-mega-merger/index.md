## A Billion-Dollar Merger in Jeopardy: California Leads the Charge Against Paramount Warner Bros

The $110bn merger between Warner Bros and Paramount, set to create the largest media conglomerate in Hollywood history, has hit a major hurdle. A coalition of 12 US states, led by California, has filed a lawsuit to block the deal, claiming it would stifle competition and raise consumer prices. At the heart of the contentious issue lies the fate of the American entertainment industry, where a handful of giants – Disney, Universal, Sony, Warner Bros, and Paramount – control a staggering 86% of major film releases.

The stakes are high, and the consequences of this merger would be far-reaching. According to California Attorney General Rob Bonta, the combination of Warner Bros and Paramount would end up harming "audiences on every sofa and movie theater seat in the US" by leading to higher prices, lower quality, and less content for film and television. Bonta's concerns are shared by the coalition of attorney generals, who argue that the merger would strip movie theaters and television networks of vital bargaining power, leaving them to face higher fees that would eventually hit consumers.

The lawsuit, filed in response to the US Department of Justice's approval of the merger in June, seeks to halt the transaction pending judicial review. If approved, the combined entity would control nearly a third of the US theatrical motion picture market and basic cable programming, further consolidating the industry's already oligopolistic structure. The regulatory challenge marks a significant hurdle for the entertainment giants, as they attempt to merge operations amidst a rapidly changing media landscape.

Paramount, the controlling stakeholder led by David Ellison, has been at the center of the merger talks. However, the company's decision to seek a merger has not been without its challenges. Bonta has explicitly stated that he is aware of reports that Ellison has been urged by advisers to move the company's operations out of California, where Paramount has been based for over 100 years. While Paramount declined to comment on the matter, Bonta's response was unequivocal: "It didn't work. It won't work. It doesn't work."

The merger would not only have far-reaching consequences for the entertainment industry but also mark the end of a century-long rivalry between two of Hollywood's biggest hitmakers. Warner Bros and Paramount have produced iconic franchises such as Harry Potter, Batman, Mission: Impossible, and Top Gun, as well as TV channels CNN, MTV, and Nickelodeon. The combined entity would own an unprecedented share of these franchises, further cementing its control over the industry.

Supporters of the deal argue that the traditional media world is in crisis, with cable TV audiences shrinking rapidly and cinema attendance facing intense pressure from tech giants and streaming platforms. Scale, they argue, is an economic necessity in this rapidly changing landscape. However, the lawsuit's focus on major cinema releases, massive blockbusters, and cable TV channels suggests that the stakes are higher than ever.

As the lawsuit unfolds, the entertainment industry watches with bated breath. The consequences of this merger will be far-reaching, and the outcome will have significant implications for the future of American entertainment. Paramount has described the lawsuit as "fundamentally flawed" and "wrong," adding that it would "vigorously defend the transaction." The company's statement echoes the sentiments of many in the industry, who see the merger as a necessary step in the face of a rapidly changing media landscape.

However, the coalition of attorney generals remains resolute in its opposition to the deal. Bonta's statement to the BBC World Service – "It didn't work. It won't work. It doesn't work" – serves as a stark reminder of the challenges ahead for the entertainment giants. As the regulatory challenge unfolds, one thing is clear: the fate of the American entertainment industry hangs in the balance.
