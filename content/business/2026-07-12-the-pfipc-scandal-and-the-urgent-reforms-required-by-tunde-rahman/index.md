## A Systemic Failure: Unpacking the PFIPC Scandal

In a shocking expose of systemic rot, the PFIPC scandal has laid bare the vulnerabilities of Nigeria's governance structure. At the heart of this scandal is Prince Adeniyi Adeyemi Matthew, who allegedly gamed the system to secure an office, signage, and a semblance of government legitimacy for a non-existent agency. But this is not a story about one man's audacity; it's a story about the failure of due diligence, appointment verification, and banking regulations.

The PFIPC scandal is a stress test that has revealed the weaknesses in Nigeria's budget process, appointment procedures, and banking oversight. The fact that a ghost agency was able to secure ₦1.3 billion in the 2026 budget, without any legal backing or establishment by the Federal Government, is a worrying indictment of the system. Public finance analysts and commentators have pointed out several systemic breaches, including the failure of due diligence in budgeting, the lack of appointment verification, and the absence of banking regulations.

The 2026 Appropriation Act contained ₦1.3 billion for the "Presidential Economic Advisory Council/PFIPC," but there is no legal backing for this agency. Top sources in the National Assembly claim that the allocation entered "through a backdoor arrangement" without budget defence. This is a clear example of why the budget process has been criticised for "insertions" and "undue inflation" over the years. A ghost agency should not be able to get real money, and if the Budget Office, House of Representatives, and Senate all missed this, our first line of fiscal defence needs a long, hard look.

The issue of appointment and office verification is also a major concern. Adeyemi allegedly used a forged appointment letter bearing Chief of Staff Femi Gbajabiamila's purported signature and counterfeit presidential letterhead to present himself as DG. Gbajabiamila's lawyers insist he has "never had any contact whatsoever with Adeyemi," yet somehow, that letter was "accepted at the civil service headquarters without adequate verification." This is a disturbing example of how easily fake documents can be used to secure government positions and resources.

The PFIPC scandal has also highlighted the lack of banking regulations. Adeyemi allegedly operated multiple accounts for the non-existent agency, which raises serious questions about the oversight of government funds. The fact that the PFIPC was able to secure office space in the heart of government, host top government functionaries from Nigeria and abroad, and allegedly operate multiple accounts without any checks and balances is a clear indication of a systemic failure.

Nigeria's governance structure has been plagued by a legacy of weaknesses, including a lack of transparency, accountability, and due diligence. A ghost agency should not be able to get real money, and if the Budget Office, House of Representatives, and Senate all missed this, our first line of fiscal defence needs a long, hard look.

The PFIPC scandal has sparked a heated debate about the need for urgent reforms. The PFIPC scandal is a wake-up call for Nigeria's governance structure.
