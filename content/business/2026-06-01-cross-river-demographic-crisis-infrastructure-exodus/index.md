## Cross River on the Brink: A Looming Demographic Haemorrhage

Tensions simmer in Cross River state, where nearly 9 in 10 residents are now considering leaving due to the deteriorating state of infrastructure, healthcare, and other essential services. A recent report by SBM Intelligence highlights the gravity of the situation, warning that Nigeria's southern state risks hemorrhaging its population if swift reforms are not implemented. The alarming figures are a stark reminder of the consequences of neglecting the fundamental needs of its citizens, casting a shadow over the region's economic prospects and long-term sustainability.

The report, citing extensive consultations with residents, businesses, and civil society groups, underscores the extent to which Cross River has fallen short of providing basic services. From crumbling roads and bridges to inadequate healthcare facilities, and woefully underfunded education systems, the cumulative effect is a sense of disillusionment and disenchantment with the state government's ability to deliver on its promises. "We're witnessing a crisis of confidence that threatens to unravel the social fabric of Cross River," a source close to the report notes. "People are losing faith in the system, and the consequences will be far-reaching if we don't act now."

The stakes are clear: Cross River's population is projected to decline significantly, potentially by as much as 20% over the next decade if current trends continue. This would not only have devastating social implications but also pose grave economic risks, as a shrinking workforce and dwindling tax base threaten to undermine the state's economic viability. With Nigeria's national economy already fragile, the loss of Cross River's human capital would exacerbate the country's existing development challenges.

To understand the scale of the crisis, it's essential to consider the broader historical context. Nigeria's post-independence years were marked by rapid urbanization and migration, as people flocked to cities in search of economic opportunities. However, the resulting strain on infrastructure and public services has long been a source of contention. In Cross River, the situation is particularly acute, given the state's relative isolation and limited access to resources. The legacy of years of underinvestment in basic services has created a perfect storm, where the lack of trust in institutions is compounded by the perception of corruption and mismanagement.

The situation is further complicated by the influx of displaced persons from neighboring states, such as Akwa Ibom and Ebonyi, who have been driven by conflict and insecurity. This influx has put additional pressure on Cross River's already strained resources, exacerbating the sense of urgency around reform. "We're facing a perfect storm of demographic and economic challenges," notes a senior government official, who wished to remain anonymous. "We need to act swiftly to prevent a catastrophic decline in our population."

The reactions to the report have been swift and varied. Civil society groups have called for the immediate resignation of the state governor, citing a complete failure of leadership. Business leaders, meanwhile, are urging the government to prioritize investment in infrastructure and human development, arguing that a more stable and secure environment is essential for economic growth. International development partners have pledged support for Cross River's recovery efforts, but only if the state government demonstrates a genuine commitment to reform.

As the situation in Cross River continues to deteriorate, the international community is holding its breath. Will the state government finally acknowledge the gravity of the situation and take concrete steps to address the underlying issues? Or will the status quo prevail, condemning Cross River to a fate of decline and stagnation? The coming weeks and months will be critical in determining the course of events. As the world watches, one thing is clear: the future of Cross River hangs in the balance, and the choices made in the next few months will have far-reaching consequences for generations to come.
