## The New PM's Conundrum: Balancing Ambition with Fiscal Reality

Andy Burnham's ascension to the Prime Minister's office has sparked a sense of optimism in the UK, with his promise to introduce a 10-year plan for the country and the economy. However, beneath the surface of grand announcements lies a complex web of fiscal realities that threaten to constrain his ambitions. As he navigates the treacherous waters of high national office, Burnham's decisions will have far-reaching consequences for the UK's economy, markets, and citizens.

Burnham's recent comments on cost of living support have hinted at a willingness to implement tax rises or spending cuts to fund his proposals. The markets have already taken notice, with the UK government's 10-year borrowing rising above 5% on Monday, a development not seen in other European economies. This development serves as a stark reminder of the sensitivities at play, particularly as Burnham overhauls his cabinet and grapples with the trade-offs involved in implementing his policies.

One of the most contentious proposals on the table is the unfreezing of the income tax threshold, which would cost at least £4bn depending on the rate of inflation. This idea has gained traction among some big unions, who see it as a vital step in addressing the cost of living crisis. However, others have raised concerns about the potential costs, particularly if the thresholds are rolled back to their pre-freeze levels. This would be a significant undertaking, with the freeze having been in place since 2022.

Burnham's 'Manchesterism' has been credited with helping him secure the top job, but its viability as a national policy remains to be seen. His plans for decentralisation, devolution, and rebuilding Britain are ambitious, but they will require significant investment. The Defence Investment Plan, a multi-billion, multi-year spending commitment for the UK military, adds to the pressure on Burnham to find a way to fund his proposals. His decision to release funds to address rough sleeping from within the housing budget is a step in the right direction, but it remains to be seen whether this will be enough to make a meaningful impact.

Welfare reform is another critical test for Burnham, who has promised to deliver sustainable reform through the work of local mayors. His reliance on Alan Milburn's review, which is likely to recommend significant job support funds and mental health assistance for young workers, raises questions about the feasibility of his plans. The recent rapid turnover of prime ministers and chancellors serves as a reminder that even thinking a few years ahead might feel like a luxury in the current climate.

As Burnham navigates the complexities of high office, he will be forced to confront the harsh realities of fiscal policy. His decisions will have far-reaching consequences for the UK's economy, markets, and citizens, and it remains to be seen whether he will be able to balance his ambition with the constraints of fiscal reality. The stakes are high, and the markets are watching with bated breath as Burnham attempts to put his vision for the UK into practice.
