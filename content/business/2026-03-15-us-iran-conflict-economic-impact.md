This surge in energy costs is likely to have a disproportionate impact on emerging markets, where fuel subsidies are already a significant burden on government finances. However, the effects of the conflict are likely to be felt far beyond the energy sector, with potential disruptions to global supply chains, increased costs for businesses, and reduced consumer spending. In the United States, the conflict is also likely to have significant implications for the country's trade deficit, which could widen further as a result of higher oil prices and reduced exports. In both cases, the economic consequences of the conflict were far-reaching and devastating, highlighting the need for a swift and decisive resolution to the current crisis.
