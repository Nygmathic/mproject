## Reimagining Nigeria's Judicial Vocations

The parlous state of Nigeria's legal and judicial institutions is a far cry from the dignified and professional vocations they once represented. Chief Justice of Nigeria, Olukayode Ariwoola, recently issued a circular addressing the issue of dress code and prefix usage among lawyers and judges, but the move has been met with skepticism by many. The real issue, however, goes far beyond mere trappings of professionalism – it is about the lost mission and ethos of these institutions.

In Kenya, the first Chief Justice under the 2010 Constitution, Willy Mutunga, took a bold step to address similar issues in his country's judicial system. Upon assuming office in 2011, Mutunga launched a four-year long Judiciary Transformation Framework, aimed at imbuing the institutions of the law and judiciary with the ethos of the new constitution. The framework provided a stark diagnosis of an antiquated system, out of touch with its people and resistant to change.

In a passage that could be applied with ease to Nigeria's judicial institutions, the Transformation Framework highlighted the need to address the "founding history of dominance, power, prestige and remoteness" that had taken hold in these institutions. It noted that the "architecture, rules, dress code and other rituals" had become disconnected from social reality, leading to a legal system perceived as "alien and insensitive."

Mutunga's approach was consultative, involving magistrates, judges, judicial workers, the public, political leaders, the media, and communities in the transformation process. This collaborative effort aimed to reorient the institutions of the law towards a more service-oriented and inclusive approach.

In contrast, the recent circular by Nigeria's Chief Justice has been criticized for its lack of substance and failure to address the root issues plaguing the judiciary. By focusing solely on dress code and prefix usage, the circular has been seen as a mere tinkering with the symptoms, rather than a genuine attempt to address the systemic problems.

It is high time for Nigeria's judicial institutions to embark on a similar transformation journey, one that prioritizes service, equality, and social relevance. This requires a fundamental shift in the way lawyers and judges perceive their roles and responsibilities, as well as a willingness to engage with the public and other stakeholders in the process.

The Chief Justice of Nigeria has the opportunity to learn from the Kenyan example and embark on a more comprehensive transformation of the judiciary. By doing so, she can help restore the dignity and professionalism of the legal and judicial vocations, and ensure that these institutions serve the needs of the Nigerian people.

## A Long History of Decline

Nigeria's judicial institutions have a long history of decline, dating back to the colonial era. The legacy of colonialism, with its emphasis on power, prestige, and remoteness, has left an indelible mark on the judiciary. This has resulted in a system that is often seen as alien and insensitive to the needs of the people.

The current state of the judiciary is a far cry from the ideals of the 1999 Constitution, which enshrines the principles of justice, equality, and social justice. The Constitution's Preamble emphasizes the need for a just and equitable society, where the rights of all citizens are respected and protected.

Despite this, the judiciary has failed to live up to these ideals, with a system that is often characterized by delays, inefficiency, and a lack of transparency. The result is a public perception that the judiciary is disconnected from the people and more concerned with maintaining its own power and prestige.

## A Call to Action

The recent circular by the Chief Justice of Nigeria has been met with skepticism, but it also presents an opportunity for reform. The Judiciary Transformation Framework launched by Willy Mutunga in Kenya provides a model for Nigeria's judiciary to follow.

This framework prioritizes service, equality, and social relevance, and involves a collaborative approach to transformation. It is a comprehensive approach that addresses the systemic problems plaguing the judiciary, rather than just tinkering with the symptoms.

The Chief Justice of Nigeria has the opportunity to learn from the Kenyan example and embark on a more comprehensive transformation of the judiciary. By doing so, she can help restore the dignity and professionalism of the legal and judicial vocations, and ensure that these institutions serve the needs of the Nigerian people.

## A New Path Forward

The path forward requires a fundamental shift in the way lawyers and judges perceive their roles and responsibilities. It requires a willingness to engage with the public and other stakeholders in the transformation process, and a commitment to prioritizing service, equality, and social relevance.

The Judiciary Transformation Framework provides a model for this approach, and one that has been successful in Kenya. By following this model, Nigeria's judiciary can embark on a transformation journey that prioritizes the needs of the people, rather than just maintaining its own power and prestige.

The time for change is now. The Chief Justice of Nigeria has the opportunity to lead this transformation and restore the dignity and professionalism of the legal and judicial vocations. The Nigerian people are watching, and it is time for the judiciary to serve them.
