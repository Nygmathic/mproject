## The Strait of Hormuz: A New Flashpoint in the Middle East

[Saudi Arabia](/tags/saudi-arabia/)'s decision to rely on an alternative route for exporting oil following the Iran war has been dealt a significant blow as the [Houthis](/tags/houthis/), a Yemeni militia backed by Iran, have begun to threaten the [Bab-el-Mandeb](/tags/bab-el-mandeb/) Strait.

The Bab-el-Mandeb, a narrow waterway connecting the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden, is a crucial chokepoint for global oil trade. With Saudi Arabia already diverting its oil exports from the Strait of Hormuz to the Bab-el-Mandeb, the threat posed by the Houthis has sent shockwaves through the global energy market.

According to reports, the Houthis have been increasing their military presence in the Bab-el-Mandeb, with the group's naval forces reportedly patrolling the waters in the vicinity of the strait. While the Houthis have not explicitly stated their intentions, their actions have been seen as a clear warning to Saudi Arabia and other regional powers.

The stakes are high, as the Bab-el-Mandeb is a critical route for oil exports from the Middle East to Europe and Asia. The strait's importance cannot be overstated, with over 3.8 million barrels of oil passing through it every day. Any disruption to this route would have far-reaching consequences, exacerbating tensions in the region and potentially leading to a significant spike in oil prices.

Regional analysts have long warned that the Bab-el-Mandeb was at risk of becoming a new flashpoint in the Middle East conflict. With the Houthis' recent actions, those concerns have been vindicated. Saudi Arabia, in particular, is vulnerable to disruptions in the Bab-el-Mandeb, as its oil exports are heavily reliant on the route.

The Iran war has already had a profound impact on global oil markets, with the conflict contributing to a significant increase in oil prices. The threat to the Bab-el-Mandeb has added a new layer of complexity to the region's already fragile dynamics. As tensions continue to escalate, it remains to be seen how the international community will respond to the growing crisis.

The Bab-el-Mandeb has a long history of conflict, with several major powers vying for control of the strait over the centuries. In recent years, the strait has become a focal point in the conflict between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis, with both sides accusing each other of aggression.

The Houthis, a Zaidi Shia militia, have been backed by Iran since the start of the conflict in Yemen in 2015. The group's military capabilities have been significantly enhanced by Iranian support, with the Houthis possessing advanced naval and air capabilities.

Saudi Arabia, which has been leading a coalition of regional powers in the conflict against the Houthis, has been keen to protect its oil exports through the Bab-el-Mandeb. The kingdom has invested heavily in its naval capabilities, with a fleet of warships and patrol boats deployed in the strait to protect its oil tankers.
