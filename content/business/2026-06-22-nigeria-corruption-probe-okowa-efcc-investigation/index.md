## Former Delta Governor at EFCC Office Amid Corruption Probe: What's at Stake

Former Delta Governor Ifeanyi Okowa's visit to the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) office in Lagos on Monday is a stark reminder of the ongoing corruption probe into his administration's handling of derivation funds between 2015 and 2023. The investigation, which has been ongoing since late 2024, has sparked intense public interest and raised questions about the accountability of Nigeria's leaders.

The 13 per cent derivation allocation from the Federation Account, which has been a contentious issue in the Niger Delta region, is a significant source of revenue for oil-producing states. The alleged diversion of these funds by Okowa's administration has sparked outrage among residents of Delta State and beyond. The EFCC's probe has remained one of the agency's most closely watched corruption cases involving a former governor, with no charges filed against Okowa despite the ongoing investigation.

Okowa's visit to the EFCC office on Monday was reportedly linked to his efforts to secure the release of his international passport to enable him to travel abroad for medical treatment. However, it remains unclear whether the commission has approved his request. The development has sparked intense speculation about the outcome of the probe and the potential consequences for Okowa's political career.

As the investigation continues, it is essential to examine the broader implications of the probe for Nigeria's governance and accountability. The EFCC's actions have sent a strong signal that no one, including former governors, is above the law. However, the agency's effectiveness in tackling corruption remains a subject of debate, with many calling for more transparency and accountability in its operations.
