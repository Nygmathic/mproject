## Crisis Averted: Keyamo Directs Airlines, Unions to Agree on Repayment Plans Over TSC Debt

Nigeria's Aviation Minister, Festus Keyamo, has intervened in a long-standing dispute between airlines and unions, directing the Nigeria Civil Aviation Authority (NCAA) and other aviation agencies to work with airlines on repayment plans for outstanding statutory charges. The development comes two days after aviation unions picketed Air Peace operations in Lagos and Abuja, disrupting flights and leaving passengers stranded.

The crisis was triggered by allegations that airlines were withholding statutory charges collected from passengers, with the unions accusing them of failing to remit the statutory five per cent Ticket Sales Charge (TSC). The unions also alleged that some airlines were restricting workers from joining trade unions. However, airline operators maintained that they had been engaging with the NCAA over the obligations and complying with directives issued by the regulator on repayment.

In a bid to resolve the dispute, Keyamo convened an emergency meeting with airline operators, aviation unions, and heads of aviation agencies. The meeting resolved that the agencies should obtain payment schedules from airlines, taking into account the airlines' operating costs and current economic realities. The repayment schedules are to be spread over a "reasonable timeframe" with consideration given to the cost of airline operations and the prevailing economic situation.

The Directors of Finance and Accounts of the aviation agencies are also expected to meet individually with the affected airlines and agree on realistic repayment plans within one week. The resolution is aimed at addressing the central issues in the dispute between aviation unions and airline operators. The unions have accused airlines of withholding statutory charges already collected from passengers, arguing that the funds are needed to finance aviation agencies and support workers' conditions of service.

Air Peace, which was at the centre of Tuesday's picketing, had also rejected allegations that it owed ₦25 billion in statutory dues, saying it had been complying with NCAA directives on the applicable five per cent TSC. The meeting also reached a resolution on the dispute over workers' unionisation. Keyamo recognised both the right of workers to belong to unions and their right to decide not to belong to one. However, the minister said the decision should be made directly by the workers rather than through airline management.

As part of the resolution, the NCAA is to immediately ensure that aviation unions have direct access to workers and are not restricted from joining trade unions. The move is expected to bring an end to the industrial action, which had disrupted flight operations and left passengers stranded. The development is a significant step towards resolving the long-standing dispute between airlines and unions, and is expected to have a positive impact on the aviation sector.
