## Genetic Guardianship: Nigeria's Unwavering Commitment to Livestock Conservation

As the sun rises over the verdant savannahs of Nigeria, a quiet revolution is unfolding. Behind the scenes, a team of scientists, policymakers, and livestock experts has been working tirelessly to safeguard the country's precious genetic resources. The federal government has just validated a set of harmonised national protocols aimed at strengthening the conservation, characterisation, and monitoring of Nigeria's Animal Genetic Resources (AnGR). This landmark decision marks a significant turning point in the nation's efforts to protect its livestock diversity, ensuring the long-term sustainability of its agricultural sector and the livelihoods of millions of people who depend on it.

The stakes are high, as Nigeria's agricultural sector is a crucial component of its economy, accounting for over 25% of the country's GDP and employing a significant portion of the workforce. However, the sector is also vulnerable to the impacts of climate change, disease outbreaks, and the degradation of natural resources. By prioritising the conservation of its AnGR, the government is not only ensuring the resilience of its livestock populations but also enhancing the country's food security and its ability to compete in the global market. The newly validated protocols will enable Nigeria to better characterise, conserve, and monitor its AnGR, thereby reducing the risk of genetic erosion and promoting the efficient use of these valuable resources.

The development of the harmonised national protocols has been a collaborative effort, involving input from various stakeholders, including researchers, policymakers, livestock producers, and international partners. The process has been guided by the principles of the International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture, which recognises the importance of conserving and using genetic resources sustainably. Nigeria's commitment to AnGR conservation is also reflected in its ratification of the Convention on Biological Diversity, which aims to conserve and sustainably use biological diversity.

The importance of AnGR conservation cannot be overstated. Livestock are a critical component of Nigeria's agricultural sector, providing meat, dairy products, and draught power for millions of people. However, the country's livestock populations are facing numerous challenges, including the spread of diseases such as brucellosis and trypanosomiasis, which can have devastating impacts on productivity and livelihoods. By conserving and characterising its AnGR, Nigeria can better understand the genetic diversity of its livestock populations and develop more effective strategies for disease control and management.

Nigeria's efforts to conserve its AnGR are also being guided by historical parallels. In the 1970s and 1980s, the country's livestock sector was severely impacted by the introduction of exotic breeds, which led to the decline of native breeds and the loss of valuable genetic material. This experience has informed the development of the harmonised national protocols, which prioritise the conservation and use of indigenous breeds and the development of more effective breeding strategies.

The validation of the national protocols has sparked a wave of reactions from various stakeholders. The Minister of Agriculture and Rural Development has hailed the decision as a major breakthrough, noting that it will enable Nigeria to better harness the potential of its AnGR and promote the sustainable development of its agricultural sector. Livestock producers have also welcomed the decision, citing the need for more effective strategies to manage disease outbreaks and promote productivity. However, some critics have raised concerns about the lack of resources and infrastructure to support the implementation of the protocols, warning that the initiative may not yield the desired outcomes without sufficient investment.

As Nigeria embarks on this new chapter in its AnGR conservation journey, the international community is taking notice. The Food and Agriculture Organisation of the United Nations has offered its support to the initiative, providing technical assistance and expertise to help Nigeria implement the harmonised national protocols. The European Union has also expressed its commitment to supporting Nigeria's efforts to conserve its AnGR, citing the importance of promoting sustainable agriculture and reducing the impacts of climate change.

As the sun sets over the Nigerian savannah, the future looks brighter for the country's livestock sector. With its harmonised national protocols now in place, Nigeria is poised to take a major leap forward in its efforts to conserve and use its AnGR sustainably. The implications of this decision will be far-reaching, with potential benefits for the country's agricultural sector, its food security, and its competitiveness in the global market. As the world watches, Nigeria's commitment to AnGR conservation serves as a beacon of hope for the continent, highlighting the importance of sustainable agriculture and the need to protect the genetic resources that underpin it.
