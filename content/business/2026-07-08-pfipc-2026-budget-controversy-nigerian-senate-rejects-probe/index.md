## Senate Rejects Motion to Probe ₦1.3 Billion Vote for PFIPC in 2026 Budget

The Nigerian Senate has made a decisive move, rejecting a motion to investigate the inclusion of the so-called Presidential Foreign Intervention Promotion Council (PFIPC) in the 2026 budget, which allocated ₦1.3 billion to the entity. This decision, reached on Wednesday, has sparked intense debate and raised questions about the credibility of the National Assembly's oversight functions.

The motion, sponsored by Kawu Sumaila (APC, Kano South), sought to probe the circumstances surrounding the PFIPC's inclusion in the budget. However, the Senate ultimately decided against a legislative inquiry, citing the Presidency's directive to the Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission (ICPC) to investigate the matter. The Senate will now await the ICPC's findings before deciding on further action.

The controversy surrounding PFIPC has been ongoing, with allegations of administrative lapses and forgery of official documents. Adeniyi Adeyemi, declared wanted by authorities, has been accused of presenting himself as the director-general of PFIPC and the Presidential Executive Advisory Council (PEAC), both of which the Presidency maintains do not exist. Mr Adeyemi has denied the allegations, describing them as an attempt to discredit him.

In a statement, Senator Sumaila warned that the inclusion of a purported non-existent or unauthorized entity in the national budget undermines the credibility of the appropriation process, exposes weaknesses in international budgetary scrutiny, erodes public confidence in the National Assembly, and subjects the federal government to avoidable domestic and international criticism regarding transparency, accountability, and fiscal governance.

The decision to reject the motion has been met with mixed reactions. While some analysts see it as a sign of the Senate's willingness to cooperate with the Presidency, others believe it undermines the National Assembly's oversight functions. The House of Representatives has recently adopted a motion to investigate the allocation of ₦1.3 billion to the same agency, adding to the controversy.

The Nigerian Senate's decision to await the ICPC's findings raises questions about the effectiveness of the National Assembly's oversight functions. As the country grapples with issues of transparency and accountability, the PFIPC controversy serves as a reminder of the need for robust scrutiny of government agencies and institutions.
