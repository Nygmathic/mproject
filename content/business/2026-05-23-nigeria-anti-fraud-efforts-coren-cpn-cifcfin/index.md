## Fraud Eradication Takes Center Stage: Forensic Institute Admits COREN, CPN into Governing Council

For years, Nigeria's economy has grappled with the crippling effects of fraud, with billions of naira lost to cybercrimes and other forms of financial malfeasance. The country's financial sector has long been plagued by a culture of corruption and deceit, with many perpetrators evading justice due to inadequate forensic capabilities. However, a significant shift in the fight against financial crimes has taken place with the recent admission of the Council for the Regulation of Engineering in Nigeria (COREN) and the Computer Professionals Registration Council of Nigeria (CPN) into the governing council of the Chartered Institute of Forensic and Certified Fraud Examiners (CIFCFIN).

The move, announced by CIFCFIN's Registrar/Chief Executive, Isa Salifu, in a statement on Friday, is expected to bolster the institute's capacity to address the growing threat of cybercrimes, which have drained billions of naira from the country's economy. Salifu emphasized that the inclusion of CPN, a regulatory body responsible for the registration and regulation of computer professionals in Nigeria, will significantly enhance the institute's ability to combat cybercrimes and other forms of financial malfeasance. "This partnership will enable us to tackle the menace of cybercrimes head-on, and I have no doubt that it will mark a significant milestone in our efforts to eradicate financial fraud in Nigeria," Salifu said.

This development comes at a time when Nigeria's economy is facing mounting pressure from a range of factors, including a decline in oil prices and a significant increase in the country's fiscal deficit. The country's financial sector has been further destabilized by a series of high-profile cases of financial malfeasance, including the infamous $2.1 billion arms deal scandal. In this context, the admission of COREN and CPN into the governing council of CIFCFIN is seen as a significant step towards strengthening the country's anti-corruption infrastructure.

CIFCFIN, established in 2018, is a professional body dedicated to promoting the highest standards of forensic and fraud examination practices in Nigeria. The institute's governing council, which now includes representatives from COREN and CPN, will play a critical role in shaping the country's approach to financial crime investigation and prosecution. The inclusion of these two regulatory bodies will bring a wealth of expertise and experience to the governing council, particularly in the areas of engineering and computer science.

The admission of COREN and CPN into CIFCFIN's governing council is also significant from a historical perspective. In the past, Nigeria's engineering and computer science sectors have been plagued by a lack of regulation and oversight, which has enabled the proliferation of substandard and unqualified professionals. The inclusion of these two regulatory bodies will help to address these issues and ensure that only qualified professionals are registered and licensed to practice.

Reactions to the development have been mixed, with some stakeholders hailing it as a major breakthrough in the fight against financial crimes. "This is a major step forward in our efforts to combat financial malfeasance in Nigeria," said a spokesperson for the Nigerian Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC). "We look forward to working closely with CIFCFIN and its governing council to ensure that those responsible for financial crimes are brought to justice." Others have expressed concerns about the potential impact on the country's financial sector, particularly in terms of the costs associated with implementing new regulations and standards.

As the country embarks on this new journey towards eradicating financial fraud, one thing is clear: the stakes are high, and the challenges are many. However, with the admission of COREN and CPN into CIFCFIN's governing council, Nigeria has taken a significant step towards strengthening its anti-corruption infrastructure and promoting a culture of transparency and accountability in its financial sector. As the country moves forward, it will be essential to continue to build on this momentum and ensure that the new regulatory framework is implemented effectively and efficiently. One thing that Nigeria's citizens, investors, and the international community will be watching closely is the impact of this development on the country's financial sector and the effectiveness of its anti-corruption efforts.
