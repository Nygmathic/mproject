## A New Era of Environmental Stewardship in Kaduna State

In a long-awaited bid to address the devastating environmental degradation and gully erosion crisis that has plagued Rigasa and adjoining communities in Kaduna State for over three decades, Governor Uba Sani has announced a N34 billion intervention project to reclaim degraded lands, restore livelihoods, and create a safer and healthier environment for residents.

The crisis, which has claimed lives, destroyed livelihoods, and exposed thousands of residents to severe environmental and safety risks without adequate intervention over the years, has been a major concern for the governor and the people of Kaduna State. Governor Sani, during an inspection of the affected areas, described the environmental challenge as a major threat that has persisted for far too long.

"The challenge has persisted for over 30 years, causing immense hardship for communities along the corridor," Governor Sani said. "Our administration is determined to bring lasting solutions to the affected areas." The governor's commitment to addressing this crisis is a welcome respite for the communities affected by the erosion, which have been struggling to cope with the devastating consequences of environmental degradation.

The project, which has an approved contract sum of N34 billion, will cover a 17-kilometre stretch across Igabi and Kaduna South Local Government Areas, including Rigasa and several neighbouring communities. The beneficiary communities are predominantly urban-poor settlements that contribute significantly to Kaduna State's economic growth and development. By addressing the environmental degradation and gully erosion crisis, the Kaduna State Government is not only mitigating the risks associated with erosion, flooding, and land degradation in these communities but also creating opportunities for economic growth and development.

The project is expected to generate direct and indirect employment opportunities, stimulate economic activities, and improve living conditions across the affected communities. Upon completion, the project is expected to directly benefit more than two million people living and working within the corridor and surrounding areas. The initiative is one of the largest environmental remediation projects undertaken by the Kaduna State Government, and its success will set a precedent for other states in the country to follow.

The Kaduna State Government has also shown a commitment to ensuring that the affected residents are not left behind during the implementation of the project. The governor has earmarked over N2 billion for compensation and resettlement support for affected residents, ensuring that their lives are not disrupted during the project's implementation.

Groundbreaking for the project is expected within the next two weeks, and completion is projected within 18 months. With the project's commencement, the people of Kaduna State can look forward to a safer, healthier, and more prosperous future. The Kaduna State Government's commitment to addressing the environmental degradation and gully erosion crisis in Rigasa and adjoining communities is a testament to its dedication to the well-being of its citizens and a significant step towards creating a more sustainable and environmentally conscious society.
