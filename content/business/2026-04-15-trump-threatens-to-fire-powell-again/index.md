Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell's phone rang again, the caller a familiar voice on the other end. This time, it was President Donald Trump, and once more, the President was threatening to fire the Fed chief. The call came as news emerged of a Senate confirmation hearing for Kevin Warsh, Trump's latest pick to lead the US central bank. The prospect of Warsh taking over at the Fed has sent shockwaves through financial markets, with investors scrambling to assess the implications of a potential change in leadership.

## Stakes High as Senate Prepares to Grill Warsh

The decision to nominate Warsh, a former Fed board member and Goldman Sachs executive, marks the third time Trump has sought to replace Powell. The stakes are high, given the critical role the Fed plays in shaping monetary policy and stabilizing the US economy. Powell, a widely respected central banker, has been a thorn in Trump's side, repeatedly clashing with the President over issues such as interest rates and fiscal policy. Warsh, on the other hand, is seen as a more malleable choice, with some analysts speculating that he may be more willing to accommodate Trump's demands. The Senate confirmation hearing will be closely watched, as lawmakers grill Warsh on his views on monetary policy, banking regulation, and the Fed's role in shaping the US economy.

## A Historical Paradox: The Power Struggle between Presidents and Federal Reserve Chairs

The power struggle between the President and the Fed chair is a long-standing one, with each side seeking to assert its influence over monetary policy. In 1913, when the Fed was first established, the central bank's independence was enshrined in law. However, this did not prevent Presidents from attempting to exert their influence over the Fed's decision-making process. Franklin D. Roosevelt, for example, tried to pressure the Fed into relaxing its monetary policies during the Great Depression, while Richard Nixon famously imposed a 90-day freeze on wage and price increases in 1971, forcing the Fed to cut interest rates. More recently, Trump's attempts to pressure the Fed into cutting interest rates have been a subject of controversy, with many arguing that such interference undermines the Fed's independence and erodes trust in the financial system.
