## Banking Sector Stands Tall Amidst Regulatory Storm

Nigeria's banking sector has defied the odds by meeting a crucial recapitalisation deadline, a move that has been hailed as a testament to the industry's resilience in the face of stringent regulatory requirements. The Association of Corporate Affairs Managers of Banks (ACAMB) has lauded the banking sector for achieving an impressive 96% compliance rate, a feat that underscores the industry's ability to adapt to changing regulatory landscapes.

At the heart of the recapitalisation exercise is the Central Bank of Nigeria's (CBN) efforts to strengthen the banking sector and improve its ability to withstand economic shocks. The CBN has consistently emphasized the need for a robust and resilient banking sector, and the recapitalisation exercise is a critical step in achieving this goal. The industry's 96% compliance rate is a significant achievement, considering the magnitude of the undertaking and the resources required to meet the new capital requirements.

The recapitalisation exercise, which was launched in December 2020, has been a long and arduous process. It has required banks to inject significant amounts of capital into their balance sheets, a move that has been necessitated by the evolving regulatory environment in Nigeria. The CBN has been at the forefront of this exercise, providing guidance and oversight to ensure that banks meet the new capital requirements. The association's praise for the banking sector's disciplined execution of the exercise is a testament to the industry's ability to work collaboratively with regulatory bodies to achieve common goals.

The recapitalisation exercise has significant implications for the Nigerian economy, and the banking sector's ability to meet the deadline is seen as a major boost for the sector. The exercise has helped to improve the capital adequacy ratio of banks, which is a critical measure of a bank's ability to withstand economic shocks. Improved capital adequacy ratios will enable banks to provide more credit to the real sector, thereby supporting economic growth and development. This is particularly significant in Nigeria, where the banking sector plays a critical role in supporting economic growth and development.

However, the recapitalisation exercise has not been without its challenges. Some banks have struggled to meet the new capital requirements, and the exercise has been criticized for being overly burdensome and restrictive. Some have argued that the exercise has been driven more by regulatory ambition than by the need to strengthen the banking sector. Others have questioned the CBN's decision to insist on a uniform capital requirement for all banks, arguing that this has resulted in some banks being over-capitalized while others have been unable to meet the requirements.

Despite these challenges, the banking sector's ability to meet the recapitalisation deadline is a testament to its resilience and adaptability. The industry has demonstrated its ability to work collaboratively with regulatory bodies to achieve common goals, and the exercise has helped to improve the overall stability and resilience of the banking sector. This is a significant achievement, particularly in a country where the banking sector plays a critical role in supporting economic growth and development.
