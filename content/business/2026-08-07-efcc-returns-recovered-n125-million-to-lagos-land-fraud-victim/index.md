## Restitution Restores Faith in Nigeria's Anti-Graft Agency

In a significant development, the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) has handed over N125 million recovered from an alleged land fraud suspect to a Lagos businessman, Akinsewa Akiode. This restitution marks the latest in a string of efforts by the anti-graft agency to compensate victims of economic and financial crimes.

According to a statement by EFCC spokesperson Dele Oyewale, the money was presented to Mr Akiode on Thursday by the commission's Lagos Zonal Directorate 2 in Ikoyi after an investigation into a petition he filed over the disputed property transaction. The investigation concluded that Mr Akiode had been defrauded by Olufemi Fashehun, who allegedly collected N125 million from him for the sale of a 550-square-metre parcel of land at No. 10, Kudirat Abiola Way, Oregun, Lagos. However, Mr Fashehun had failed to disclose that the property had been the subject of litigation since 2011, a fact that ultimately led to Mr Akiode losing both the property and the structures he had begun putting up on the land.

The EFCC's recovery of the full N125 million from the suspect and its subsequent return to the petitioner through Sterling Bank drafts are a testament to the agency's commitment to investigating economic and financial crimes, recovering illicit proceeds, and ensuring victims receive restitution where possible. This handover is part of a broader effort by the EFCC to restore faith in the institution and demonstrate its capacity to hold perpetrators accountable for their actions.

The EFCC's actions in this case have significant implications for Nigeria's business and economic landscape. It highlights the need for greater transparency and accountability in property transactions, particularly in the context of ongoing litigation. Furthermore, it underscores the importance of the EFCC's role in protecting the rights of victims and promoting a culture of integrity in the business community.

The EFCC's restitution efforts have not gone unnoticed, with the agency having returned N108 million recovered during an investigation into an alleged fraud involving two former employees of Greenwich Planet Sun King Nigeria Limited just over two weeks ago. In October 2025, it also handed over three houses, two vehicles, and N1.1 million recovered from a convicted fraudster to one of his victims in Ibadan after a Federal High Court ordered the assets forfeited.

Speaking at Thursday's ceremony, the Acting Zonal Director of the EFCC's Lagos Zonal Directorate 2, Bawa Kaltungo, reiterated the commission's commitment to continuing its investigations into economic and financial crimes and ensuring that victims receive restitution where possible. While the EFCC did not state whether criminal charges have been filed against Mr Fashehun over the alleged fraud, the agency's actions in this case demonstrate a clear determination to hold perpetrators accountable for their actions.

The EFCC's restitution efforts are a crucial step towards rebuilding trust in Nigeria's institutions and promoting a culture of integrity in the business community. As the agency continues to investigate economic and financial crimes, recover illicit proceeds, and ensure victims receive restitution, it is clear that the EFCC is committed to playing a critical role in shaping Nigeria's economic and business landscape.
