## Breadwinner's Dilemma

Darkness has descended over the once-thriving streets of Yasnogorsk, a small town located just 50 kilometers north of Moscow. The war in Ukraine has left deep scars on this Russian town, forcing many to confront the harsh realities of a dwindling economy. Amidst this turmoil, the story of a small bakery stands as a stark reminder of the dire consequences of the tax hike that has left many entrepreneurs on the brink of collapse. 

At the heart of this crisis is Elena Ivanova, a determined entrepreneur who has spent years building her small bakery into a staple of Yasnogorsk's community. Her daily routine starts before dawn, as she carefully crafts traditional Russian bread and pastries for the town's residents. However, Ivanova's success is now under threat due to the Russian government's decision to impose a staggering 3,500% tax increase on small businesses such as hers. The impact of this sudden and drastic hike is evident in Ivanova's exhausted expression, as she navigates the precarious landscape of rising costs, dwindling profits, and dwindling customer base.

The stakes are high for Ivanova and her bakery, which is not just a source of income for her family but also a vital part of the town's social fabric. The bakery has been a gathering place for the community, where neighbors and friends come together to share news, laughter, and warm bread. As the tax hike looms, Ivanova is forced to confront the reality of potential closure, which would not only devastate her family but also leave a void in the community's social structure.

## A Perfect Storm

To understand the severity of the situation, it is essential to examine the broader economic context in which Ivanova's bakery operates. Russia's war in Ukraine has led to a sharp decline in foreign investment, a significant drop in oil prices, and a crippling sanctions regime imposed by the West. These factors have collectively taken a devastating toll on the Russian economy, which is already reeling from the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. As the government scrambles to find ways to stimulate growth and reduce the burden on the population, the tax hike on small businesses has been introduced as a short-term solution. However, this decision has only served to exacerbate the problem, pushing many entrepreneurs to the edge of financial ruin.

The situation is further complicated by the fact that many small businesses in Russia, including Ivanova's bakery, operate on thin margins. A 3,500% tax increase is equivalent to a 35% tax rate, which is unsustainable for most entrepreneurs. Moreover, the tax hike has been imposed without any corresponding measures to support small businesses, making it even more challenging for them to adapt to the new reality. As a result, many entrepreneurs are being forced to make difficult decisions, such as reducing staff, cutting production, or even shutting down operations altogether.

## A Historical Parallel

This crisis has eerie parallels with the 1998 Russian financial crisis, which led to a significant devaluation of the ruble and a devastating economic downturn. At that time, the government's decision to devalue the currency and raise interest rates led to a sharp decline in economic activity, forcing many small businesses to close their doors. The current crisis, however, is distinct in that it is driven by a combination of external factors, including the war in Ukraine and the sanctions regime, as well as internal policies, such as the tax hike.

The parallels with the 1998 crisis are also evident in the government's response to the crisis. Just as then, the current administration is relying on a mix of rhetoric and short-term measures to address the economic woes. However, this approach has been criticized by many economists and experts, who argue that it will only serve to exacerbate the problem in the long term. As the crisis deepens, the government's ability to respond effectively will be put to the test.

## Reactions and Implications

The reactions to the tax hike have been mixed, with some entrepreneurs and business leaders calling for more support from the government, while others are urging caution and restraint. The Russian Federation of Trade Unions has condemned the tax hike, arguing that it will only serve to deepen the economic crisis and exacerbate the suffering of working-class families. Meanwhile, the Ministry of Finance has defended the decision, citing the need to stimulate economic growth and reduce the burden on the population.

As the situation continues to unfold, the implications of the tax hike are becoming increasingly clear. The small businesses that are forced to close will not only lose their livelihoods but also contribute to a decline in economic activity, which will have far-reaching consequences for the entire economy. The government's ability to respond effectively to this crisis will be a test of its commitment to supporting small businesses and promoting economic growth. The fate of Elena Ivanova's bakery, and that of countless others like it, hangs in the balance.
