## A Legacy of Oil and Denial

Lee Raymond, the architect of Exxon Mobil, the world's largest publicly traded oil and gas company, has passed away at the age of 87. The company he helmed for nearly a decade, from 1999 to 2005, has become synonymous with the extraction and sale of fossil fuels, shaping the global energy landscape in ways both profound and contentious. As the news of his demise trickles in, the world cannot help but reflect on the indelible mark Raymond left on the oil industry and the world's efforts to combat climate change.

## The Stakes of a Legacy

Under Raymond's leadership, Exxon Mobil embarked on an aggressive expansion drive, culminating in the merger with Mobil in 1999. This deal created a behemoth with an unparalleled presence in the global energy market. The acquisition marked a pivotal moment in the consolidation of the oil industry, with Exxon Mobil emerging as a dominant force, capable of exerting significant influence over global energy policy. Raymond's relentless pursuit of cost-cutting and efficiency enabled the company to maintain its position at the forefront of the industry, even as competitors faltered.

However, Raymond's legacy is not without controversy. As a vocal skeptic of climate change, he publicly disputed the scientific consensus, questioning the human role in global warming. This stance has become a defining characteristic of Exxon Mobil's approach to the issue, with the company being accused of deliberately obscuring the risks associated with fossil fuel consumption. Raymond's views on climate change have been cited as a prime example of the fossil fuel industry's efforts to delay and discredit climate action, with many arguing that this has contributed to the world's failure to address the crisis with sufficient urgency.

## A Historical Context

Exxon Mobil's ascent to the pinnacle of the oil industry was not achieved in a vacuum. The company's success was, in part, a product of the neoliberal economic policies that dominated the late 20th century. The deregulation of the oil industry, coupled with the decline of state-led economies, created an environment in which companies like Exxon Mobil could thrive. The collapse of the Soviet Union and the subsequent shift in global geopolitics further solidified Exxon Mobil's position as a major player in the global energy market.

The company's expansion into emerging markets, particularly in Africa and Asia, has been a key factor in its growth. Exxon Mobil has invested heavily in these regions, partnering with local governments and energy companies to develop new resources and infrastructure. However, this expansion has also been accompanied by controversy, with Exxon Mobil facing criticism for its environmental and social practices in countries such as Nigeria and Papua New Guinea.

## Perspectives from the Global South

The passing of Lee Raymond has sparked a range of reactions across the Global South, where countries are increasingly turning to renewable energy sources to meet their development needs. In Africa, for example, where energy poverty remains a pressing challenge, many governments are embracing solar and wind power as a means of driving economic growth and reducing dependence on fossil fuels. In this context, Exxon Mobil's continued focus on oil and gas extraction has been seen as a relic of the past, out of step with the changing global energy landscape.

In Asia, where countries like China and India are driving the growth of renewable energy, Exxon Mobil's legacy has been viewed as a cautionary tale. The company's efforts to delay climate action and obscure the risks associated with fossil fuel consumption have been seen as a prime example of the need for greater cooperation and transparency in the global energy sector.
