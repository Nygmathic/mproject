## Inflation's Double-Edged Sword

The latest inflation figures from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics reveal a modest easing in price rises, with the annual inflation rate falling to 3.5% in June, down from 4.2% in May. This decline is largely attributed to a 9.7% drop in gasoline prices, although the average cost of a gallon remains significantly higher than a year ago. However, as the global oil market reacts to the ongoing conflict in the Middle East, the respite from inflationary pressures may be short-lived.

The renewed military strikes on Iran by the US have sent the price of Brent crude soaring to $87, an increase of nearly $10 in just 24 hours. This spike has analysts predicting a rise in inflation in the coming months, with interest rate cuts increasingly unlikely. "Gasoline prices are already back above June levels, meaning the next inflation report will heat up again," warned Ipek Ozkardeskaya, senior analyst at Swissquote Bank.

The Federal Reserve, led by its newly appointed chairman Kevin Warsh, will be closely monitoring the inflation figures. Warsh has expressed his committee's "no tolerance to persistently elevated inflation," and emphasized their commitment to restoring price stability. While some analysts suggest that rates could be raised in the coming months, Warsh's predecessor, Jerome Powell, was pressured by President Trump to cut interest rates, and it remains to be seen whether Warsh will prioritize appeasing the President or maintaining a conservative outlook.

The easing of inflationary pressures is a welcome respite for small business owners, who have been grappling with the impact of rising prices. According to the National Federation of Independent Business, more than a fifth of small business owners reported that inflation was their "single most important" problem, the highest amount for almost two years. However, the benefits of lower inflation may be tempered by the ongoing conflict in the Middle East, which threatens to disrupt global trade and further exacerbate price pressures.

## A Delicate Balance

The Federal Reserve's decision to hold interest rates between 3.5% and 3.75% at Warsh's first meeting in June reflects their cautious approach to tackling inflation. By keeping rates steady, policymakers aim to strike a balance between curbing price rises and avoiding a slowdown in economic growth. However, this balancing act is becoming increasingly challenging, as the global economic environment becomes increasingly uncertain.

The theory behind increasing interest rates to tackle inflation is that by making borrowing more expensive, more people will cut back on spending, leading to a decrease in demand for goods and a subsequent easing of price rises. However, this approach can have unintended consequences, as high interest rates can harm the economy by discouraging businesses from investing in production and jobs. Interest rate cuts, on the other hand, can boost the economy by lowering borrowing costs and encouraging spending and investment.

## A Complex Picture

The inflation figures reveal a complex picture, with some prices falling while others continue to rise. While energy prices decreased 5.7% last month, food price inflation increased further, with the cost of meat, poultry, fish, and eggs going up, along with dairy products and cereals. Eating out remains more expensive, with meals on average costing 3.7% more than they did last year.

So-called core inflation, which strips out food and energy prices seen as volatile, remained unchanged in June at 2.6%. This figure will be closely monitored by the Fed when deciding whether to cut, hold, or raise interest rates. Stephen Brown, chief North America economist at Capital Economics, suggested that the latest figure would "all but rule out an interest rate hike this month, but we still see reason to expect the Fed to hike interest rates later this year."

## A Waiting Game

As the global economic environment becomes increasingly uncertain, policymakers and analysts alike are left waiting for the next inflation report. Will the renewed conflict in the Middle East lead to a further spike in oil prices, exacerbating price pressures? Or will the Federal Reserve's cautious approach to tackling inflation pay off, allowing the economy to continue growing while keeping price rises in check? Only time will tell, but one thing is certain – the Federal Reserve will be closely monitoring the inflation figures, ready to adjust their monetary policy accordingly.

## Watching the Fed

As the Federal Reserve prepares to meet in a fortnight, investors and small business owners alike will be watching with bated breath. Will Kevin Warsh's approach to tackling inflation be more aggressive, or will he prioritize a conservative outlook? Will interest rates be cut, or will they remain steady? The answers to these questions will have far-reaching implications for the US economy, and the global economy at large. As the world waits for the next inflation report, one thing is certain – the stakes are high, and the outcome is far from certain.
