## Tackling the Cost of Living Crisis
Prime Minister Andy Burnham has pledged to alleviate the burden of the cost of living on British households, announcing a review of the tax-free personal allowance and promising to bring essentials under public control. The implications of these measures are far-reaching, affecting not just individual finances but also the broader economy.

Burnham's promise to give people "breathing space" to help with the cost of living is a clear acknowledgment of the strain that the economic environment has placed on households. Many individuals, he notes, are unable to enjoy simple pleasures like going out for a pint or taking their children on holiday due to the pressure on their finances. The cost of living has dominated people's lives in recent years, and easing that pressure hasn't been easy.

The incoming prime minister's focus on the cost of living is not new; Labour's leader, Sir Keir Starmer, and his chancellor, Rachel Reeves, also prioritized this issue. However, their efforts were met with challenges, including the impact of global events like the US-Israeli strikes on Iran, which led to a surge in energy bills and other costs. "A more volatile world is a more expensive world," observes Adam French of Moneyfacts, highlighting the unpredictability of the global economic landscape.

Burnham's review of the tax-free personal allowance is a key aspect of his plan to alleviate the cost of living. Under existing government policy, income tax and National Insurance thresholds have been frozen until April 2031 in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland, meaning that as individuals earn more, a greater proportion of their income is taxed. Reversing this policy would require significant changes to government revenue, potentially through borrowing or finding alternative sources of funding.

The prime minister has also hinted at increasing taxes on some individuals, while sticking to Labour's manifesto promise not to raise the main three taxes: income tax, National Insurance, and VAT. However, other taxes, such as inheritance tax and capital gains tax, can be altered to bring in more revenue. This could involve replacing stamp duty and council tax with an alternative property tax or introducing higher rates on capital gains tax.

The challenge of implementing these changes is significant, particularly given the need to balance competing priorities and manage the trade-offs involved. Burnham and his chancellor will face tough decisions on funding and will need to navigate the complexities of the economy to achieve their goals. Opinion is divided on whether the government's self-imposed fiscal rules are a sensible framework for economic decision-making or a "dysfunctional" straitjacket.

## A Record High in Energy Debts
One of the most pressing concerns for households is the record-high amount of unpaid energy debt. According to debt charity Citizens Advice, the amount of money owed to energy suppliers by customers is at £4.79bn, a 15% increase from the previous year. This has led to a situation where households are forced to choose between heating and eating, with many struggling to afford basic necessities.

The issue of energy debt is closely linked to the cost of living crisis, as households struggle to pay their energy bills, which have remained high despite a temporary reduction in April. The Labour Party's general election promise to cut household energy bills by £300 by 2030 remains under close scrutiny, with Burnham pledging to "bring essentials under public control" to make them more affordable.

## Transport Fares: A Priority in Manchester
As the former mayor of Greater Manchester, Burnham has a track record of making changes to the bus network, which has earned him significant political capital. Announcements on bus fares are expected this week, but the prime minister's ability to control transport fares is limited to England, outside of London. The £3 cap on bus fares in England is set to expire in March next year, and it remains to be seen whether Burnham will be able to implement changes to make transport more affordable for households.
