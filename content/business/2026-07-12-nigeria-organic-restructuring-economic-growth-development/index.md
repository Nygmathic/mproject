## A Nation Reborn: The Quest for Organic Restructuring in Nigeria

In a country where ethnicity and regionalism often take precedence over national identity, the notion of a pan-Nigerian consensus is slowly gaining traction. Majeed Dahiru, a renowned commentator, argues that by putting our Nigerian citizenship above our individual ethno-geographic indigeneship, a new path to economic growth and development can be forged. The concept of organic restructuring, a process of systematic assimilation and integration of Nigerians from plural backgrounds into geo-economic zones, holds the key to unlocking the nation's full potential.

The current state of affairs in Nigeria is one of fragmentation, where localised interests often override the needs of the broader collective. The country is a collection of micro-ethnic nationalities, with each group prioritizing its own agenda over the national good. This has led to a lack of unity, social cohesion, and structural stability, hindering the nation's economic progress. As Dahiru astutely observes, "The Nigerian populace are also more of 'indigenes' of these micro-ethnic nationalities than 'citizens' of Nigeria."

One of the primary arguments against Nigeria's unity is the notion that the country is an amalgam of culturally incompatible groups, a narrative that has become an entrenched trope. However, this argument is built on a flawed premise. Dahiru argues that diversity, in the context of Nigeria, refers to the racial mix within a given geographic space, not the ethnic composition within a broad racial categorisation. As such, Nigeria, a mono-racial (black) country, does not qualify as a diverse nation on account of ethnic differences.

This distinction is crucial, as it highlights the similarities between Nigeria and other mono-racial nations, such as China and the United Kingdom. Like these countries, Nigeria's ethnic diversity is not a source of division, but rather a reflection of a shared cultural and ancestral heritage. The various ethnic groups in Nigeria have a common source, as evident in the close similarities in culture, language, and interwoven ancestral history.

The concept of organic restructuring offers a compelling solution to Nigeria's challenges. By integrating Nigerians from plural backgrounds into geo-economic zones, the country can move beyond its current fragmentation and towards a more unified national identity. This process would allow time-tested economic models to work for the nation, rather than being hindered by localised interests.

The implications of this process are far-reaching. Commercial cities like Aba and Onitsha, once enclaves of Igbo traders, would become hubs of Nigerian entrepreneurship. Cattle breeding, currently dominated by the Fulani, would become a Nigerian industry. This transition would not only promote economic growth but also foster social cohesion and structural stability.

As Nigeria embarks on this journey of organic restructuring, it is essential to acknowledge the historical context that has shaped the country. The implosion of the Kwararafa confederacy several centuries ago, which ignited a wave of southward migrations from the fringes of the old Kanem Borno Empire into the Benue valley, is a testament to the nation's complex history. The Kwararafa, a confederacy of groups who are regarded as "Banza bokwoi," shared a common patriarchy with the original seven Hausa kingdoms, highlighting the interwoven nature of Nigeria's ethnic groups.

In conclusion, the quest for organic restructuring in Nigeria offers a beacon of hope for a more unified and prosperous nation. By prioritizing Nigerian citizenship over individual ethno-geographic indigeneship, the country can overcome its current fragmentation and unlock its full potential. As Dahiru argues, "The Nigerian federation would have undergone a process of organic restructuring, and the commercial cities of Aba and Onitsha will no longer be enclaves of Igbo traders but those of Nigerian entrepreneurs." This vision is not only a promise of economic growth but also a testament to the nation's ability to overcome its challenges and emerge stronger.
