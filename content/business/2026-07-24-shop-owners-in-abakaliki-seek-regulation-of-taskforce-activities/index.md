## Taskforce Tensions: Abakaliki Shop Owners Seek Regulation Amid Harassment and Extortion

In the commercial hub of Abakaliki, a city in the southeastern Nigerian state of Ebonyi, a simmering crisis is brewing. Shop owners and traders, weary of the constant harassment and extortion at the hands of taskforce officials, have appealed to the state government to introduce regulation and oversight. The calls for reform come as business owners struggle to make ends meet amidst a complex web of levies, taxes, and fees that have become unsustainable.

The traders, who spoke to the News Agency of Nigeria on Thursday, described a pattern of harassment and intimidation by taskforce officials. Chika Uzu, a recharge card dealer and POS operator, alleged that the officials damaged some of their property and collected money without issuing official government receipts. Mr Uzu claimed that the task force visits increased after a dispute between two neighbouring traders, subjecting traders to frequent extortion.

Onyemachi Ngboke, a fashion designer, echoed the sentiments, stating that traders were burdened by multiple charges, including internally generated revenue, local government levies, and other payments imposed by different task forces. He lamented that the levies, ranging from N10,000 to N20,000 at irregular intervals, had become unsustainable for many small business owners. Ngozi Nwafor, a provision store owner, also alleged that the activities of task force officials had increased her financial hardship, appealing to the state government to urgently check what she described as the excesses of the taskforces.

The traders' pleas for reform have been met with a mixed response from the authorities. Christopher Omo-Isu, Chairman of the Ebonyi State Internal Revenue Service, acknowledged the existence of a demand notice shared with business owners in the state. He revealed that the official government task force commenced operations in October each year and that the government-approved payment for business owners in the state is N14,000 annually. While condemning the activities of the task force, Mr Isu promised to use government apparatus to deal with the perpetrators of the crime.

However, the traders remain skeptical, citing a lack of transparency and accountability in the revenue collection process. They urge the government to introduce a uniform annual levy with proper documentation, streamline revenue collection processes, and ensure that only authorised officials collect approved levies to protect businesses and improve the state's investment climate.

The crisis in Abakaliki highlights the need for effective regulation and oversight in the enforcement of tax and revenue collection policies. If left unaddressed, the situation could lead to further disruptions in business activities, ultimately affecting the state's economic growth. As the debate continues, one thing is clear: the traders of Abakaliki are calling for change, and the government must listen.

## The Human Cost of Taskforce Excesses

The stories of harassment and extortion at the hands of taskforce officials are a stark reminder of the human cost of unchecked power and corruption. For many small business owners, the levies and fees imposed by the task forces have become a significant burden, threatening their very existence. The traders' appeals for regulation and oversight are not just a call for reform; they are a cry for help, a plea to the government to intervene and protect their livelihoods.

As the government grapples with the issue, it is essential to consider the long-term implications of unregulated taskforce activities. The loss of confidence in the government's ability to enforce tax and revenue collection policies could have far-reaching consequences, including a decline in investment, a decrease in economic growth, and a deterioration in the overall business climate.

## A Call to Action

The traders of Abakaliki are not alone in their struggle. Across Nigeria, small business owners face similar challenges, including harassment, extortion, and corruption. The government must take immediate action to address these issues, introducing measures to regulate and oversee taskforce activities, ensure transparency and accountability, and protect the rights of business owners.

The crisis in Abakaliki serves as a wake-up call, a reminder of the need for effective governance and regulation. As the government moves forward, it must prioritise the concerns of small business owners, listening to their pleas for reform and taking concrete steps to address their needs. Only then can Nigeria's economy truly thrive, and the rights of business owners be protected.
