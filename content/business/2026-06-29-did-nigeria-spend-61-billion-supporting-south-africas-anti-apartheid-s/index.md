## The Unsettled Question of Nigeria's $61 Billion Pledge to Anti-Apartheid South Africa

Amidst the backdrop of renewed xenophobic attacks against Nigerians in South Africa, a long-standing claim about Nigeria's contribution to the country's anti-apartheid struggle has resurfaced, sparking heated debates and queries on social media. At the heart of this controversy lies a staggering figure: $61 billion. The claim, made by an X user, asserts that Nigeria spent this considerable sum supporting South Africa's fight against apartheid. But is this assertion rooted in fact, or is it a product of exaggeration and misinformation?

## Unpacking the Claim

The claim, which has garnered significant attention online, suggests that Nigeria's contribution to the anti-apartheid struggle was not only substantial but also unparalleled. The figure of $61 billion is cited as a testament to Nigeria's unwavering support for South Africa's liberation. However, as PREMIUM TIMES noted, this claim has been met with skepticism by some users, who question the feasibility of such a massive financial outlay by a country with a relatively modest GDP at the time.

One of the key questions raised is whether Nigeria had the financial capacity to support South Africa's liberation struggle to the tune of $61 billion. As @MaMhizzy pointed out, Nigeria's GDP in the 1960s was a mere $4 billion, making it challenging to comprehend where such an enormous sum would have come from. Similarly, @Tygerzs noted that Nigeria's GDP in 1993 was $56.7 billion, leaving many wondering where the additional funds would have been sourced from.

## Historical Records and Academic Sources

To shed light on this controversy, PREMIUM TIMES conducted a fact-check by tracing the widely cited $61 billion estimate to a publication by the South African Institute of International Affairs (SAIIA) titled "Giants of Africa Limp Along – South Africa and Nigeria." The publication, which provides a detailed analysis of the economic, diplomatic, and financial contributions of Nigeria to the anti-apartheid struggle, estimates that by the end of apartheid in 1994, Nigeria had contributed approximately $61 billion to the anti-apartheid effort. However, it is essential to note that this estimate represents Nigeria's cumulative financial, economic, and diplomatic support over several decades, rather than a single cash payment.

## Mandela's Acknowledgment

Former South African President Nelson Mandela also acknowledged Nigeria's contributions to the anti-apartheid movement. In a video of his 1990 visit to Nigeria published on the official YouTube channel of the Lagos State government, Mandela thanked the Nigerian government and its people for their financial and material support to the liberation struggle. Mandela specifically noted that Nigeria "have been among the most generous donors" to the anti-apartheid movement and recognized the country's scholarships for South African students, as well as the deployment of teachers, doctors, and architects.

## The Significance of Nigeria's Contribution

Nigeria's contribution to the anti-apartheid struggle was not merely a financial one. The country's support extended to diplomatic efforts, economic aid, and the provision of scholarships for South African students. This multifaceted approach not only demonstrated Nigeria's commitment to the cause but also underscored its role as a leader in the African continent. As the SAIIA publication highlights, Nigeria's contributions to the anti-apartheid struggle were instrumental in the eventual collapse of the apartheid regime.
