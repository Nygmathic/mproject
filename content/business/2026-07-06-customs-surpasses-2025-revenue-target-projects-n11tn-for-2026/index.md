## Customs Surpasses Revenue Target, Eyes N11tn for 2026

Bashir Adewale Adeniyi, Comptroller General of Customs, has revealed that the Nigeria Customs Service (NCS) exceeded its 2025 revenue target by ₦674 billion, generating ₦7.277 trillion, a 10.24 per cent increase over the projected ₦6.584 trillion.

The Comptroller-General made this disclosure on Monday while presenting the agency's 2026 budget proposal before the Senate Committee on Customs. According to Adeniyi, the service initially projected ₦6.584 trillion in revenue for 2025, but generated ₦7.277 trillion, representing a 10.24 per cent increase over the target.

Adeniyi attributed the shortfall in revenue to the non-collection of excise duties on telecommunications services, import duty waivers on Compressed Natural Gas (CNG) equipment, and the non-collection of import duties on military equipment, which accounted for about 40.52 per cent of the forgone revenue. The Russia-Ukraine war also had a significant impact on revenue generation, particularly in the import of wheat and other commodities.

Despite these challenges, the NCS is confident of achieving its 2026 revenue target of ₦11.04 trillion. According to Adeniyi, ₦5.542 trillion of the projected revenue will accrue to the Federation Account, ₦1.491 trillion to non-Federation accounts, ₦2.773 trillion to Import VAT, while ₦1.26 trillion will come from Free-on-Board (FOB) collections.

The Comptroller-General also expressed optimism that easing geopolitical tensions and improving global trade conditions would further boost revenue in the second half of the year. He stated that June recorded the highest monthly revenue so far in 2026, and the service expects this momentum to continue.

The Senate Committee on Customs commended the agency for surpassing its 2025 revenue target and urged them to sustain the momentum and exceed the proposed 2026 target. The committee chairman, Isah Jibrin, praised the CG and his management team, urging them to work harder and actualise the projected revenue figures for 2026.

## Boosting Revenue Through Ongoing Reforms

The NCS has been implementing various reforms to boost revenue generation and improve trade facilitation. The deployment of the Indigenous Unified Customs Information System (UCIS) has significantly improved automation across the agency's commands, strengthening revenue collection and trade facilitation. Enhanced post-clearance audits and intelligence-driven anti-smuggling operations have also been effective in identifying and blocking revenue leakage.

The Comptroller-General disclosed that the service had already generated ₦4.043 trillion between January and June 2026, expressing confidence that it would meet its annual revenue target before the end of the year. The agency is hopeful that easing geopolitical tensions and improving global trade conditions will further boost revenue in the second half of the year.

## Meeting the Challenge

The NCS faces significant challenges in meeting its revenue targets, including the ongoing Russia-Ukraine war, which has disrupted international trade and impacted revenue generation. However, the agency is confident of achieving its 2026 revenue target through ongoing reforms and improving global trade conditions.

The Comptroller-General has urged the agency to work harder and sustain the momentum to exceed the proposed 2026 target. The Senate Committee on Customs has also commended the agency for surpassing its 2025 revenue target and urged them to continue to work hard to meet the revenue targets.
