Nigeria's National Biosafety Management Agency, the government body responsible for regulating genetically modified organisms, is facing intense scrutiny from experts and civil society groups who accuse it of lacking transparency in its decision-making processes. At the heart of the controversy is the agency's handling of genetically modified crops, which have been increasingly introduced into the country's agricultural sector. The criticism has sparked a heated debate about the safety and efficacy of these crops, with many Nigerians expressing concerns about the potential risks to human health and the environment. As the use of genetically modified organisms continues to expand in Nigeria, the lack of transparency and accountability has become a major stumbling block, eroding trust in the regulatory system and fueling public skepticism.

The stakes are high, with Nigeria's agricultural sector accounting for a significant portion of the country's economy and employing millions of people. The introduction of genetically modified crops has been touted as a solution to the country's food security challenges, with proponents arguing that they can increase crop yields and improve resistance to pests and diseases. However, critics argue that the benefits of genetically modified crops have been exaggerated, and that the risks to human health and the environment have not been adequately assessed. The joint statement issued by Nnimmo Bassey, a prominent environmental activist, and Joyce Brown, a public health scientist, highlights the growing concerns among Nigerians about the lack of transparency and independent scientific evidence supporting the safety of genetically modified organisms approved for use in the country. The statement argues that the National Biosafety Management Agency has failed to provide adequate information about the genetic modification process, the potential risks and benefits, and the measures in place to mitigate any adverse effects.

## The Regulatory Framework
The National Biosafety Management Agency was established in 2015 to regulate the use of genetically modified organisms in Nigeria. The agency is responsible for evaluating the safety and efficacy of genetically modified crops, as well as ensuring that they are handled and disposed of in a responsible manner. However, critics argue that the agency's regulatory framework is flawed, with inadequate provisions for public participation, transparency, and accountability. The agency's decision-making processes are often opaque, with little information made available to the public about the evaluation and approval of genetically modified crops. This lack of transparency has created an environment of mistrust, with many Nigerians questioning the agency's independence and its ability to protect the public interest.

The controversy surrounding genetically modified organisms in Nigeria is not new, with debates raging for years about the safety and efficacy of these crops. However, the lack of transparency and accountability has become a major point of contention, with many arguing that the National Biosafety Management Agency has failed to provide adequate assurances about the safety of genetically modified crops. The agency's reliance on industry-funded research has also raised concerns, with critics arguing that this creates a conflict of interest and undermines the agency's independence. Furthermore, the agency's failure to conduct independent scientific research and to consult with a broad range of stakeholders has eroded trust in the regulatory system, fueling public skepticism and mistrust.
