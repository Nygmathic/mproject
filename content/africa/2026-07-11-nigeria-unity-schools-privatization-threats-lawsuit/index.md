## Unity Schools in Jeopardy: ASCSN Threatens Lawsuit Over Alleged Concession to Private Investors

The Association of Senior Civil Servants of Nigeria (ASCSN) has sounded the alarm over a reported plan to cede the country's 120 Federal Government Colleges, popularly known as Unity Schools, to private investors. The move, if implemented, would have far-reaching consequences for the education sector, threatening the livelihoods of thousands of workers and the accessibility of quality education for children from low-income families.

In a press conference convened by National Vice President Olubunmi Fajobi on Wednesday, the ASCSN vehemently opposed the alleged plan, warning that it would lead to higher tuition fees and displacement of education officers and other employees. Fajobi emphasized that the union would engage the government in constructive dialogue to resolve the matter, but would not hesitate to explore other options, including seeking court intervention, if the government fails to reconsider its stance.

The ASCSN's opposition comes on the heels of the King's College Old Boys' Association's announcement that the Nigerian government has conceded the school to them. However, when contacted, the spokesperson for the Ministry of Education, Folasade Boriowo, referred PREMIUM TIMES to Ikharo Attah, the spokesperson to the Minister of Education, Tunji Alausa, who has yet to respond to inquiries seeking clarification on the matter.

The ASCSN has a long history of resistance against privatization attempts in the education sector. In 2005, during the administration of former President Olusegun Obasanjo, the government allegedly sought to concession the Unity Schools under a Public-Private Partnership arrangement. The ASCSN, in collaboration with other labour unions, student groups, parents, civil society organizations, religious leaders, and traditional rulers, mobilized against the proposal, embarking on industrial action and litigation before the plan was ultimately abandoned. Stability returned to the Unity School system in 2010 after then-President Goodluck Jonathan ordered the restoration of junior secondary school sections that had earlier been separated from the colleges.

The ASCSN's stance on the current proposal is clear: Federal Government Colleges should remain public institutions serving Nigerian children rather than becoming privately controlled establishments. Fajobi questioned the template the old boys association has designed to manage the school, emphasizing that the union would not allow the interests of a select few to supersede the needs of the broader Nigerian public.
