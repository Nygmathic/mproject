## A Bittersweet Victory

The sun has barely set over Gaborone, casting a golden glow over the city's vibrant nightlife. Yet, amidst the hum of music and laughter, a sense of unease lingers. For weeks, the city's nightlife industry has been embroiled in a bitter dispute with the government over proposed 24-hour liquor trading hours. But in a surprise move, nightclub and bar owners have put their petition on hold, citing a need for further consultation.

At the heart of the controversy lies a proposed amendment to the Liquor Act, which would allow licensed premises to operate 24 hours a day, seven days a week. Proponents argue that the move would boost the economy, create jobs, and provide a much-needed boost to the city's nightlife. Opponents, however, claim that the move would lead to increased crime, disorder, and exploitation of vulnerable populations. For Gilbert Seagile, president of the Botswana Nightlife Association, the decision to suspend the petition is a calculated move. "We're not opposed to the idea of longer liquor hours in principle," he explains. "However, we need to ensure that any changes are implemented in a way that prioritizes the safety and well-being of our patrons, staff, and the broader community."

The proposed amendment has sparked a heated debate, with some arguing that it would be a major blow to the country's efforts to reduce alcohol-related harm. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), Botswana has one of the highest per capita consumption rates of alcohol in the world. The country's Ministry of Health and Wellness has long advocated for stricter liquor laws to mitigate the impact of excessive drinking on public health. In a statement, the ministry expressed its concerns about the proposed amendment, citing the need for a more nuanced approach to addressing the complex issues surrounding alcohol consumption.

The controversy over the Liquor Act amendment has also highlighted deeper tensions between the government and the private sector. For some, the proposed changes represent a classic case of regulatory capture, where the interests of powerful business lobbyists are prioritized over those of the broader public. Others, however, see it as a much-needed reform that would unlock the potential of the nightlife industry. "The proposed changes would level the playing field for small businesses, allowing them to compete with larger chains and create more opportunities for entrepreneurs," argues a local business owner, who wishes to remain anonymous.

The debate over the Liquor Act amendment has also been characterized by a series of high-profile interventions from the country's leadership. In a recent statement, President Mokgweetsi Masisi expressed his support for the proposed changes, citing the need for a more dynamic and adaptable regulatory environment. However, his remarks were swiftly countered by opposition parties, who accused the government of prioritizing the interests of big business over those of ordinary citizens. As the debate rages on, many are left wondering whether the proposed changes would truly benefit the broader public or simply enrich a select few.

## A Complex History of Liquor Laws

The Liquor Act has been a contentious issue in Botswana for decades. In the 1960s and 1970s, the country's colonial-era laws prohibited the sale of liquor outside of designated areas, sparking a series of protests and demonstrations from local residents. Following independence in 1966, the government began to relax these restrictions, allowing licensed premises to operate more freely. However, this shift was short-lived, as concerns about excessive drinking and public disorder led to a series of crackdowns on the nightlife industry.

In the 1990s and 2000s, the country's liquor laws underwent a series of reforms aimed at reducing the impact of excessive drinking on public health. These changes included stricter licensing requirements, increased penalties for drunk driving, and enhanced support services for problem drinkers. However, these efforts were often hampered by a lack of coordination between different government agencies and a shortage of resources.
