The National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) has dealt a major blow to transnational and domestic narcotics syndicates after intercepting massive shipments of cannabis and codeine-based cough syrup valued at more than N5 billion across Nigeria’s primary maritime gateways. The high-value interdictions, executed at the Apapa and Tincan Island ports in Lagos State as well as the Onne Port in Rivers State, underscore the sustained efforts of Nigerian authorities to prevent the country's commercial corridors from being exploited by illicit trafficking rings. Disclosing the details of the coordinated operations in an official statement on Sunday, NDLEA Director of Media and Advocacy, Femi Babafemi, highlighted that the border clampdowns formed part of an expansive enforcement push that also dismantled multi-ton cultivation sites and transit networks across several states.

## Maritime Interdictions at Tincan, Apapa, and Onne Ports

The largest single valuation among the maritime seizures occurred at the Tincan Island Port in Lagos, where intelligence-led operations yielded significant consignments of synthetic cannabis imported from North America. Acting on actionable intelligence, agency operatives searched a container originating from Montreal, Canada, on 29 September. Within the cargo, officers uncovered 2,595 parcels of Canadian Loud weighing a total of 1,297.5 kilogrammes. The illicit consignment, valued by the agency at more than N3.8 billion, was intricately concealed inside two vehicles—a Toyota Sienna and a Hyundai Elantra—alongside 24 blue drums and six jumbo bags. This significant find followed an earlier operation conducted weeks prior at the very same port, during which NDLEA personnel intercepted 1,200 parcels of Canadian Loud weighing 603.75 kilogrammes, valued at N1.81 billion.

Parallel operations at the Apapa Port in Lagos exposed further reliance on maritime trade routes for high-potency cannabis smuggling originating from Southeast Asia. Working in close collaboration with the Nigeria Customs Service and other sister security agencies, NDLEA operatives conducted a thorough search of two targeted containers that arrived from Bangkok, Thailand. The physical examination confirmed the presence of 161 parcels of Thai Loud weighing 124 kilogrammes, with an estimated street value of N372 million. Demonstrating the sophisticated concealment strategies employed by the trafficking networks, the illicit substances had been carefully packed and stowed inside two refrigerators and two separate cartons to evade initial screening.

At the Onne Port in Rivers State, operatives focused their enforcement on vessels of interest by tracking and searching two containers that had been formally placed on the agency’s watchlist. The operation resulted in the recovery of 40 packs of Loud weighing 22 kilogrammes, discovered inside a travelling bag alongside three imported vehicles—a Toyota Matrix, a Toyota Corolla, and a Hyundai Elantra. A subsequent inspection of the second watchlist container yielded 109,600 bottles of codeine-based cough syrup with an aggregate weight of 16,440 kilogrammes. According to the NDLEA, the narcotics originated in India and were initially shipped to Saudi Arabia, with shipping documentation and route details allegedly manipulated in a bid to obscure the trail and bypass surveillance. The agency valued the combined seizures at Onne Port at N833.2 million, adding to the facility's recent record of enforcement following an April ruling by the Federal High Court in Port Harcourt, which granted an interim forfeiture order over 17 containers holding millions of opioid pills and nearly 2.5 million bottles of codeine-based syrup worth N33.69 billion.

## Cross-Border Smuggling Foiled at Lagos Airport

Beyond maritime borders, the agency maintained heightened scrutiny across international aviation terminals, intercepting outbound trafficking attempts aimed at European destinations. At the Murtala Muhammed International Airport in Lagos, operatives apprehended a 38-year-old businessman, Philip Tunde, on 27 September as he attempted to board a Royal Air Maroc flight bound for Milan, Italy. The arrest occurred at the joint screening point of Terminal 2, where officers uncovered 4.8 kilogrammes of confirmed cocaine hidden within his personal luggage and footwear. The search revealed 16 parcels of the drug concealed inside the soles of eight pairs of trainers and canvas shoes, while an additional 195 wraps were embedded within the mechanical railings of four travelling bags.

During interrogation by the anti-narcotics agency, the suspect provided detailed insight into the logistics of his aborted enterprise. Mr Tunde stated to investigators that he had expended N16 million in cash to acquire the cocaine secreted within the soles of the shoes, while maintaining that the secondary consignment embedded in the luggage railings was intended for delivery to his partner based in Italy. The suspect further stated that the capital used to finance the purchase was sourced directly from financial compensation he had previously received following an industrial workplace accident at a plastic manufacturing facility in Italy, an incident that had resulted in the physical loss of the tips of two of his fingers.
