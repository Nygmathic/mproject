## A Nation's Turmoil Revisited Through Verse

In a dimly lit Lagos auditorium, the air is electric with anticipation as poet and playwright, Akpan Udofia Wariboko, takes stage. Tonight, he is to recite a new collection of poems, one that delves deep into the complexities of Nigeria's economic history. The subject: the Naira crisis of 2016, a period marked by crippling inflation, currency devaluation, and widespread hardship. Wariboko's words weave a narrative that is both a searing indictment of the era's leadership and a testament to the resilience of the Nigerian people.

As the poet begins to speak, the audience is transported to a tumultuous time when the Naira plummeted in value, rendering the nation's currency nearly worthless. It was a moment of unprecedented economic distress, leaving millions to struggle with the basics: food, shelter, and employment. Wariboko's verses paint a vivid picture of a nation on the brink, where the haves and have-nots were starkly divided by the ravages of economic collapse. His words are laced with a sense of urgency, a call to action that echoes through the years, a reminder that the choices made then continue to shape the present.

Wariboko's poetry is more than a nostalgic reflection of a bygone era; it is a commentary on the enduring legacy of that crisis. The poet highlights the systemic failures that contributed to the economic downturn, from a lack of transparency in governance to a woefully inadequate economic framework. His words also underscore the human cost of the crisis, where families were torn apart by the struggle to survive, and communities were left to grapple with the fallout of a faltering economy. The poet's vision is not limited to the past, however; he also casts a hopeful and thought-provoking gaze upon Nigeria's future.

## A Legacy of Economic Woes

To fully appreciate the context of Wariboko's poetry, it is essential to understand the historical roots of Nigeria's economic struggles. The nation's economic woes can be traced back to the early decades of independence, when a reliance on a single crop, oil, led to a volatile economy. The 1970s, often referred to as the "oil boom" era, saw a surge in oil revenues, which was squandered on consumption and corruption rather than invested in the nation's development. This pattern of mismanagement would repeat itself in the 1980s and 1990s, with the country's economy teetering on the brink of collapse. The 2016 Naira crisis was merely the latest manifestation of this long-standing issue.

Wariboko's poetry serves as a reminder that Nigeria's economic struggles are not unique to the current administration. The nation's economic woes are a product of decades of poor governance, corruption, and a lack of visionary leadership. His words also highlight the need for a fundamental transformation in the way the nation approaches economic development. This requires a shift from a focus on consumption and patronage to a more sustainable model that prioritizes investment in human capital, infrastructure, and innovation.

## A Vision for the Future

As Wariboko's poetry weaves its way through the audience's hearts and minds, it is clear that the poet is not simply recounting a history but imagining a future. A future where Nigeria is led by a strong and visionary female president, a leader who can harness the nation's vast potential and guide it towards a brighter tomorrow. This vision is not a pipe dream but a testament to the resilience and determination of the Nigerian people. Wariboko's words are a call to action, urging his audience to reclaim their nation's narrative, to reimagine a future that is inclusive, equitable, and just.

The poet's vision is not without its challenges, however. Nigeria's patriarchal society remains a significant barrier to the advancement of women in politics. The nation's history is dotted with examples of women leaders who have been marginalized, excluded, or even silenced. Yet, Wariboko's poetry is a beacon of hope, a reminder that the tide is turning. The rise of women in politics, business, and civil society is a testament to the nation's growing recognition of the importance of women's participation in shaping the future.
