## Oil Production on the Rise as Middle East Tensions Ease

The Middle East, a region long plagued by conflict and instability, is slowly exhaling a sigh of relief as the Strait of Hormuz, a critical oil shipping route, reopens for business. This development has set the stage for a possible increase in oil production quotas, as seven OPEC+ members are likely to raise their output on Sunday.

The Organisation of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC+) is set to meet online to discuss quotas for August, with Saudi Arabia, Russia, and five other members expected to continue unwinding production cuts at the same pace as in previous months. According to Giovanni Staunovo, a commodity analyst at UBS, this could translate to an increase of 188,000 barrels per day. However, Staunovo cautions that production is still below the group's targets, and it remains to be seen whether the proposed increase will be enough to meet demand.

The Strait of Hormuz, which connects the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman, has been a focal point of tensions between Iran and the US in recent months. The near-paralysis of the waterway, orchestrated by Iran, led to a significant decline in oil exports from Gulf countries. However, with the signing of a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) between Iran and the US on June 17, shipping in the region has begun to recover, and oil prices have plummeted as a result.

## The Economics of Recovery

The impact of the Strait's reopening on oil production and prices cannot be overstated. The Strait is responsible for approximately 20% of the world's oil exports, and its closure had a ripple effect on global oil markets. The subsequent increase in oil prices had far-reaching consequences, including inflationary pressures and economic strain on oil-importing countries.

As the Middle East calms, oil-producing countries are poised to capitalize on the trend. Saudi Arabia, the world's largest oil exporter, has been at the forefront of efforts to increase production, and the proposed increase in quotas is likely to boost the kingdom's oil exports. Russia, another major player in OPEC+, is also expected to benefit from the move.

## The Politics of Oil

The proposed increase in oil production quotas has significant implications for the global oil market and the politics of the Middle East. The move is likely to be seen as a victory for Saudi Arabia and its allies, who have long advocated for increased oil production. Iran, on the other hand, may view the development as a setback, given its efforts to exert control over the Strait of Hormuz.

The international community will be watching the developments closely, as the global oil market continues to navigate the complexities of geopolitics and economics. The United States, in particular, has a vested interest in the outcome, given its significant oil imports from the Middle East.

## Reactions and Implications

Reactions to the proposed increase in oil production quotas are mixed, with some analysts welcoming the move as a positive step towards global economic recovery. However, others caution that the move may lead to a surge in oil production, which could have negative consequences for the environment and oil-importing countries.

The implications of the move are far-reaching, with the global oil market, economies, and the politics of the Middle East all set to be impacted. As the Middle East continues to calm, the world waits with bated breath to see what the future holds for oil production and prices.
