## Strategic Failure in the Making

As the ceasefire between Israel and Iran holds, the Israeli government is grappling with the weight of its own strategic failure. Critics from the opposition are leading the charge, accusing the administration of failing to translate military successes into tangible results. The criticism comes at a time when the international community is closely watching the developments in the region, with Iran's nuclear program and ballistic missile capabilities remaining a pressing concern.

At the heart of the criticism lies Israel's failure to secure concessions from Iran on its nuclear program, ballistic missiles, and regional activities. Critics argue that the ceasefire, brokered through backchannel negotiations, has given Iran a much-needed respite to regroup and reassess its strategy. Yair Golan, leader of the Democratic Party, described the ceasefire as "one of the worst strategic failures" in Israeli history, emphasizing that while the military achieved significant successes, the government failed to capitalize on them. Golan's remarks reflect the growing unease among opposition leaders, who see the ceasefire as a missed opportunity to exert pressure on Iran.

## A History of Deterrence

The current situation is not the first time Israel has found itself in a similar predicament. In 2015, Israel's then-Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had to navigate a fragile ceasefire with Hamas in Gaza, which was largely seen as a temporary reprieve. The experience served as a reminder that deterrence, while a key aspect of Israel's national security strategy, is not a foolproof solution to resolving conflicts with its neighbors. Critics of the current ceasefire warn that a similar pattern is unfolding, with Iran using the truce to regroup and rearm, setting the stage for a more costly conflict down the line.

Avigdor Lieberman, leader of the Yisrael Beiteinu party and a former Finance Minister, has been vocal in his criticism of the ceasefire. He argues that any agreement that fails to address Iran's nuclear program, ballistic missiles, and regional activities will ultimately lead to another conflict. Lieberman's views reflect the concerns of many within the Israeli security establishment, who see the current ceasefire as a temporary fix rather than a lasting solution. The opposition's criticism has also highlighted the need for greater transparency and accountability in the Israeli government's decision-making process.
