## Vigilantism and the Rule of Law: A Delicate Balance in South Africa

The streets of South Africa have become a battleground for those pushing the boundaries of immigration law. While the government continues to grapple with the complexities of managing migration, isolated groups have taken it upon themselves to conduct identity checks on foreign nationals, igniting fears of vigilantism and the erosion of the rule of law.

The Inter-Ministerial Committee (IMC) on Migration has sounded a stern warning to these groups, emphasizing that their actions are not only unlawful but also threaten the very fabric of South African society. Speaking at a media briefing in Pretoria, Justice Minister Mmamoloko Kubayi emphasized that the management of immigration, border management, deportation, and facilitated repatriation is the exclusive responsibility of the State. "Government reiterates that no individual or group has the authority to take the law into their own hands or to intimidate, threaten, or unlawfully remove any person from any community," she stated firmly.

The IMC's stance is a response to reports of groups conducting searches in homes and businesses suspected of housing undocumented migrants. These actions have sparked widespread concern, with many questioning the motivations behind such vigilantism. While South Africans have every right to protest against irregular migration, the IMC is clear that criminality will not be tolerated.

The consequences of these actions have been severe, with law enforcement authorities having opened 205 cases and arresting 350 suspected individuals as of 8 July 2026. Of these cases, 69 were pending investigation, and 112 were on the court roll in relation to incidents linked to unlawful conduct, intimidation, incitement, and related offenses. The IMC has made it clear that law enforcement will not hesitate to act against those who continue to conduct these unlawful searches and identity checks.

Despite the IMC's warnings, the issue of migration remains a pressing concern for South Africans. The government has committed to facilitating the return of foreign nationals to their home countries, guided by the Five-Point Plan on managing migration. This plan is designed to balance the concerns of South Africans with the principles of human rights and dignity. To date, a total of 53,449 foreign nationals have been processed for deportation and repatriation, with the majority being Malawians, followed by Zimbabweans and Mozambicans.

In addition to these efforts, the normal immigration deportation processes have continued. For the month of June 2026, a total of 4,898 individuals were deported, with the majority being from Malawi, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, and Lesotho. The period between 14 June 2026 and 8 July 2026 saw a further 2,801 individuals being deported, with similar nationalities represented.

As the government continues to grapple with the complexities of migration, it is clear that the IMC's warnings are not merely a warning but a call to action. The rule of law must be upheld, and vigilantism must be condemned. The government's commitment to facilitating the return of foreign nationals and upholding human rights is a crucial step in addressing the concerns of South Africans while also respecting the dignity of all individuals.

The reactions to the IMC's stance have been varied, with some hailing it as a necessary measure to maintain the rule of law, while others have expressed concern that it may be seen as overly heavy-handed. As the situation continues to unfold, one thing is clear: the delicate balance between vigilantism and the rule of law must be maintained. The IMC's warnings serve as a reminder that the government will not tolerate actions that undermine the law and threaten the very fabric of South African society.
