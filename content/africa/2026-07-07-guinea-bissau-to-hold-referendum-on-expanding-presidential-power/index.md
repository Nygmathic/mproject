## A Presidential Power Grab in Guinea-Bissau

Guinea-Bissau, a nation with a history of turmoil and military interventions, is poised to hold a referendum on a constitutional amendment that would significantly expand the president's power. The country's military junta, which seized power in November, has announced that the referendum will take place on August 30, just weeks before new elections are scheduled to restore civilian rule.

The proposed change would abandon the parliamentary system and introduce a presidential one, allowing the president to appoint the prime minister and members of government, as well as dissolve parliament. This move has sparked widespread concern among opposition groups and civil society organizations, who argue that it would undermine the country's fragile democratic institutions.

Guinea-Bissau has a long history of military interventions and coups, with five takeovers and several attempted coups since it gained independence from Portugal in 1974. The country's last president, Umaro Sissoco Embalo, was ousted in 2025 after ruling by decree and dissolving the opposition-dominated parliament in December 2023. Embalo's removal was seen as a significant setback for democracy in Guinea-Bissau, and the current military junta's plans to expand presidential power have only exacerbated concerns about the country's democratic trajectory.

The opposition in Guinea-Bissau has long argued that the country's parliamentary system has led to difficult periods of coalition governments and has hindered the effective functioning of the government. However, critics of the proposed constitutional amendment argue that it would concentrate power in the hands of the president, undermining the country's already fragile democratic institutions.

The referendum is likely to be a contentious issue in Guinea-Bissau, with opposition groups and civil society organizations planning to mobilize against the amendment. The country's main opposition party has urged dialogue with the military rulers, but it remains to be seen whether this will be enough to prevent the amendment from passing.

Guinea-Bissau's history of military interventions and coups has had a profound impact on the country's development and stability.
