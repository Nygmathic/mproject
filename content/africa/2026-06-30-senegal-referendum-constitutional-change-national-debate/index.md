## Senegal's Constitutional Crossroads: A National Referendum Looms

As the sun sets over the Senegalese capital, Dakar, the country's politics are ablaze with controversy. The National Assembly's passage of a bill revising the balance of power between the executive and parliament has sparked a heated debate, culminating in the announcement of a national referendum on the matter. This sudden turn of events has left many wondering what lies ahead for Senegal, a country with a long history of democratic stability.

The bill, which was passed by an overwhelming majority in a tense atmosphere, seeks to strengthen the powers of the National Assembly and the prime minister while effectively curtailing those of the president. According to the Pastef party, which holds 130 of the Assembly's 165 seats, the reform aims for a "better rebalancing of powers" among the executive, legislative, and judicial branches in Senegal. This move has been met with criticism from the opposition and various civil society organisations, who claim that the president is being weakened.

The opposition has long been wary of President Bassirou Diomaye Faye's actions, and the latest development has only added fuel to the fire. "Parliament is being used to weaken the president," said Presidential coalition leader Aminata Toure during a press briefing. "The proposals appear aimed at limiting the influence of the president of the republic by increasing the powers of the head of the National Assembly." These allegations have been echoed by other opposition parties, who see the move as an attempt to consolidate power at the expense of the presidency.

The National Assembly's decision to push through the bill despite opposition has sparked controversy, with some arguing that the process was rushed and opaque. The debate on the bill was marked by heated exchanges, with one opposition MP being forcibly removed from the chamber by gendarmes after refusing to leave the podium. This incident, along with the subsequent walkout by opposition MPs, suggests that tensions are running high.

The announcement of a national referendum has raised questions about the legitimacy of the process. While some see it as an opportunity for the people to have their say, others believe it is a ploy to bypass the opposition and consolidate power. "The president has decided to inform the speaker of the National Assembly that he has decided... to put the adopted text to a referendum," Justice Minister Moussa Sarr said ahead of the bill's adoption. This decision has been met with skepticism by many, who argue that it is an attempt to circumvent the opposition.

As the country prepares for the referendum, many are left wondering what the implications will be. Will the people of Senegal approve the constitutional change, or will they reject it? The stakes are high, and the outcome could have far-reaching consequences for the country's politics and governance. As the referendum approaches, it is clear that Senegal is at a crossroads, and the future is uncertain.
