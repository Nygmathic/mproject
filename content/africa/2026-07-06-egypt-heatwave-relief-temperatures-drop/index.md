## Brief Respite on the Horizon

A sweltering heatwave that has gripped Egypt for days is finally set to subside by Tuesday, July 7th, according to forecasts from the Egyptian Meteorological Authority (EMA). While temperatures across the country will drop, the EMA warns that high humidity levels will continue to make the air feel oppressive, with the perceived temperature increasing by one to three degrees.

The scorching heat has been particularly intense in Upper Egypt, where temperatures have reached as high as 44C in some areas. In contrast, temperatures in Greater Cairo and Lower Egypt have ranged between 34-35C, while the North Coast has seen temperatures between 29-31C. By the end of the week, temperatures in Upper Egypt are expected to drop to between 36-37C, still unbearably hot by any standard.

The EMA has urged citizens to remain vigilant, advising them to avoid direct sun exposure during peak hours and to drink plenty of water to stay hydrated. The authority has also cautioned drivers to exercise caution during the early morning hours, when mist is expected to form on some roads leading to and from northern Egypt, including Greater Cairo, the Suez Canal cities, central Sinai, and northern Upper Egypt.

While the easing of the heatwave is welcome news, it is essential to remember that the high humidity levels will continue to make the air feel stifling. The EMA's warnings serve as a reminder of the importance of taking precautions to protect oneself from the harsh climate. As the country breathes a collective sigh of relief, it is crucial that citizens remain vigilant and take necessary measures to stay safe.

## The Human Impact of Extreme Weather

The effects of extreme weather on human health cannot be overstated. Heatwaves, in particular, can have devastating consequences, especially for vulnerable populations such as the elderly, young children, and those with pre-existing medical conditions. Prolonged exposure to high temperatures can lead to heat exhaustion, heat stroke, and even death.

In Egypt, where temperatures often soar during the summer months, the heatwave has been particularly grueling. The high temperatures have not only posed a threat to human health but have also disrupted daily life, with many people forced to stay indoors to avoid the heat. The economic impact of the heatwave cannot be ignored, with businesses and industries affected by the reduced productivity and increased costs associated with maintaining air conditioning and other cooling systems.
