## Crisis of Confidence: The Afcon Organising Committee's Tightrope Walk

The Local Organising Committee for the African Cup of Nations (Afcon) is facing a mounting crisis of confidence, with critics questioning its ability to deliver the 2023 tournament on time and within budget. Tensions are running high as the committee scrambles to address concerns over infrastructure, logistics, and financial management, with just months to go before the competition kicks off. The pressure is mounting, and the committee's credibility hangs in the balance.

At the heart of the controversy is a proposed increase in the budget for the tournament, which has sparked outrage among stakeholders. The Local Organising Committee has requested additional funding to cover unforeseen expenses, including the construction of new stadia and upgrading of existing facilities. However, many are questioning the committee's decision-making process and the need for such a significant increase. Critics argue that the committee has been slow to address long-standing issues with infrastructure and logistics, and that the proposed budget increase is a Band-Aid solution to a more complex problem.

The stakes are high for the committee, which has a reputation to uphold as the host nation prepares to welcome the continent's top football teams. The tournament is a major showcase for African football, and any delays or cost overruns will have far-reaching consequences for the sport on the continent. Moreover, the Afcon is a critical event for the host nation's economy, generating significant revenue and promoting local tourism. A successful tournament will not only boost the country's international profile but also create jobs and stimulate economic growth.

To understand the current crisis, it is essential to consider the broader context of African football development. The continent has made significant strides in recent years, with the introduction of the African Football Confederation's (CAF) new vision and strategy aimed at promoting the growth of the sport. However, the implementation of these reforms has been patchy, and many countries continue to struggle with basic issues such as infrastructure, coaching, and player development. The Local Organising Committee's struggles to deliver the Afcon on time and within budget are, therefore, a symptom of a deeper problem – a lack of investment in the sport's underlying infrastructure.

The proposed budget increase has also sparked a heated debate about the role of international bodies in supporting African football development. While many experts argue that external funding is essential for the growth of the sport, others contend that African countries should take greater responsibility for their own development. The CAF has a significant role to play in promoting good governance and financial management within its member associations, but some critics argue that the organisation has been too slow to address these issues.

The Local Organising Committee's response to the controversy has been characterised by defensiveness and a lack of transparency. While committee officials have acknowledged the need for greater accountability and communication, many stakeholders remain unconvinced. The committee's credibility has been eroded by a series of mistakes and missteps, including the delayed completion of key infrastructure projects and the failure to engage with local communities and stakeholders.

As the clock ticks down to the tournament's opening match, the Local Organising Committee must provide clear justification for the resources it seeks. This will involve a more transparent and inclusive decision-making process, as well as a more nuanced understanding of the complex issues facing African football development. The committee's ability to deliver the Afcon on time and within budget will have far-reaching consequences for the sport on the continent, and for the country's reputation as a host nation.

In the coming weeks and months, the committee will face intense scrutiny from stakeholders, media, and the public. Its ability to respond to criticism and demonstrate a commitment to transparency and accountability will be crucial in restoring confidence in the tournament. If the committee fails to deliver, the consequences will be severe – not just for the tournament itself but for the future of African football development. The clock is ticking, and the Local Organising Committee must act swiftly to address the crisis of confidence that threatens to derail the Afcon.
