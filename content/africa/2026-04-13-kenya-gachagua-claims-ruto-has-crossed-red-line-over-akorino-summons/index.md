At the heart of the controversy lies a little-known Christian denomination, the Akorino Church, whose members have been summoned to State House, the official residence of President William Ruto. The move has sparked widespread outrage, with former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua accusing the head of state of "crossing the red line." The implications are far-reaching, with many questioning the motivations behind Ruto's actions and the consequences for Kenya's already fragile democracy.

Gachagua, a key ally of the President's predecessor, Uhuru Kenyatta, has been vocal in his condemnation of Ruto's moves, warning that the President's actions will have far-reaching consequences for the nation.

The controversy is not without historical precedent. Kenya's complex history of church-state relations has been marked by numerous instances of governments seeking to exploit religious institutions for their own gain. In the 1970s, the Kanu government, led by Jomo Kenyatta, famously co-opted the Catholic Church in its efforts to crush opposition to its rule. The move backfired spectacularly, as the Church's influential membership rallied behind the opposition, leading to widespread protests and a significant erosion of the government's legitimacy. Ruto's actions, many argue, risk repeating this pattern, as he seeks to harness the Akorino Church's influence in pursuit of his own ambitions.

The stakes are high, with many fearing that Ruto's actions could precipitate a constitutional crisis of unprecedented proportions.
