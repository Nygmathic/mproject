## A Shared Blueprint for Peace in Jeopardy: Can Africa Meet the SDG Deadline?

The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) have been hailed as a "shared blueprint for peace" by the United Nations, but a new report warns that the 2030 deadline is slipping away, and challenges remain daunting. The 2026 SDG Progress Report highlights significant gains, yet progress remains far too slow, leaving billions of people in Africa and around the world vulnerable to poverty, hunger, and climate-related disasters.

As the High-Level Political Forum on Sustainable Development (HLPF) convenes in New York, the stakes are clear: governments must urgently accelerate action to meet the ambitious targets set out in the SDGs. The report notes that nearly one billion people have gained access to safe drinking water, 1.2 billion have gained access to safely managed sanitation, and new HIV infections have fallen by 30 per cent between 2015 and 2024. Electricity now reaches 92 per cent of the global population, and internet access has surged from 40 to 74 per cent. Social protection now covers more than half the global population.

However, these hard-won gains mask a more disturbing reality. One in 10 people still live in extreme poverty, food insecurity affects 2.3 billion people, and maternal mortality remains nearly three times the global target. The report also highlights the devastating impact of climate change, with global temperatures reaching 1.43°Celsius above pre-industrial levels in 2025, and 273 million children and young people remaining out of school.

Escalating conflicts, climate change, slowing economic growth, rising debt, and a record decline in official development assistance have all contributed to the slow progress toward the SDGs. As Deputy Secretary-General Amina Mohammed noted at the UN Headquarters, "Many countries are being asked to deliver on promises without the tools to keep them." Mohammed called for reforms that would allow international development banks to provide debt relief and longer-term financing to initiatives that would advance the SDGs.

The HLPF, which runs until 15 July, will bring together government ministers, top UN officials, civil society representatives, and other stakeholders to exchange knowledge and ideas on sustainable development. ECOSOC President Lok Bahadur Thapa urged countries to "work differently" to achieve the SDGs, pointing to widening disparities in areas ranging from clean energy to water security. "The 2030 Agenda remains our shared promise - to people, to the planet, and to future generations," Thapa said. "The years ahead will not ask whether our challenges were difficult. They will ask whether we were equal to them."

As Africa's population continues to grow, the region's governments are under pressure to deliver on the SDGs. With the 2030 deadline looming, this year's HLPF focuses on "transformative, equitable, innovative, and coordinated actions." Each year, Member States conduct in-depth reviews of a subset of the goals rather than all 17. SDG 17 (partnerships for the goals) is a permanent fixture, and in addition to SDG 17, States will analyze challenges and solutions surrounding SDG 6 (clean water and sanitation), SDG 7 (affordable and clean energy), SDG 9 (industry, innovation, and infrastructure), and SDG 11 (sustainable cities and communities).

As the HLPF unfolds, African governments, civil society, and the private sector will come together to share knowledge, ideas, and best practices. However, the pace of progress remains slow, and the challenges daunting. The SDGs are not just a set of targets; they are a shared blueprint for peace, and Africa's future depends on its ability to meet the deadline. The question now is whether governments, international partners, and civil society will work together to accelerate action and ensure that the SDGs are achieved by 2030.

## A Decade of Progress, but Much Work Remains

The SDGs were adopted in 2015, and since then, hard-won gains have been made. However, progress remains far too slow, and the challenges are increasingly complex. The report highlights the need for sustained investment and international cooperation to accelerate progress toward the SDGs. With the global refugee population having more than doubled in the past decade, and 273 million children and young people remaining out of school, the stakes are clear: governments must urgently accelerate action to meet the SDG deadline.
