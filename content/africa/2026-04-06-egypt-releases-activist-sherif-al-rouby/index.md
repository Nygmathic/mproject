## A Long-Term Prisoner Walks Free

Sherif Al-Rouby, a key figure in Egypt's 2011 uprising and leading member of the pro-democracy April 6th movement, was released from prison in the early hours of yesterday morning after spending nearly four years behind bars. The 35-year-old activist, who had been held without trial since September 2011, walked out of the notorious Wadi el-Natroun prison in the governorate of Beheira, a few hours north of Cairo, accompanied by his family and a handful of supporters.

The release of Al-Rouby marks a significant development in Egypt's ongoing struggle for democratic rights and freedoms, which has seen numerous activists, politicians, and civil society figures detained, tried, or imprisoned in recent years. The April 6th movement, which played a pivotal role in mobilizing protests against former President Hosni Mubarak's regime in 2011, has been a vocal advocate for democratic reforms and greater accountability in Egypt. Al-Rouby's detention and subsequent release serve as a stark reminder of the challenges faced by Egypt's pro-democracy movement in its continued quest for justice, equality, and human rights.

Al-Rouby's story is one of many that highlight the Egyptian government's ongoing crackdown on dissenting voices and opposition groups. The 2011 uprising, which brought an end to Mubarak's 30-year rule, was followed by a brief period of relative freedom and liberalization under the interim military-led government that succeeded him. However, as the Muslim Brotherhood's Mohamed Morsi took office in 2012, the situation rapidly deteriorated, with the ousting of Morsi in 2013 by the military, led by then-Defense Minister Abdel Fattah el-Sisi, marking a significant turning point in Egypt's democratic trajectory.

The subsequent years have seen a relentless assault on the country's civil society, with numerous activists, journalists, and politicians detained, tried, or imprisoned on charges of terrorism, defamation, or other trumped-up offenses. The government's tactics have been criticized by human rights groups and international organizations, who have highlighted the erosion of due process, the use of torture, and the arbitrary detention of suspects.
