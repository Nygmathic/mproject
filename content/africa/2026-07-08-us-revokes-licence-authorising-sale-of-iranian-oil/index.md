## US Tightens Noose on Iran, Revoking Licence for Oil Sales

The US Treasury Department's decision to revoke the general licence authorising the sale of Iranian oil has sent shockwaves through the global energy landscape. The move, announced in a statement issued by the Office of Foreign Assets Control, effectively shuts down a channel that had allowed Iran to export oil to the US. This reversal comes amidst rising tensions in the Strait of Hormuz, where three tankers were reportedly attacked in the last 24 hours, prompting a stern warning from US officials.

The revoked general licence, General License X, was introduced just a month ago on June 21, 2026, as part of a broader effort to ease sanctions on Iranian oil exports. The decision to partially lift sanctions followed what the US described as "encouraging" talks aimed at ending the conflict in the region. However, the latest developments have put a damper on these efforts, with the US Treasury Department now superseding General License X with a new licence, General License X1, which becomes effective on July 7, 2026.

This sudden change in policy is a significant escalation in the US's approach to Iran's oil exports. The 60-day sanctions waiver issued in June had created a window of opportunity for the production, delivery, and sale of Iranian oil to the US. However, with the revocation of General License X, this channel is now closed, leaving Iran's oil exports in limbo. The move is likely to have far-reaching consequences for the global energy market, particularly for countries that rely heavily on Iranian oil imports.

The US Treasury Department's decision has been met with a stern warning from Iranian officials, who have vowed to take "consequences" against the US. Reuters quoted unnamed US officials as saying Iran's actions in the Strait of Hormuz were "wholly unacceptable to the United States and will be met with consequences." The statement suggests that the US is preparing for a more aggressive stance on Iran's activities in the region.

The Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping lane that connects the Persian Gulf to the Arabian Sea, has been a flashpoint in the Iran-US conflict.
