**Xenophobia: Two more Nigerians killed in South Africa**

**A Pattern of Violence**

The death of two more Nigerians in South Africa has brought the total number of Nigerians murdered in the country since the latest wave of xenophobic attacks began this year to four. Emeka Iroegbu and Musa Joe, both Nigerian nationals, lost their lives under different circumstances last Sunday, 28 June. Their deaths serve as a stark reminder of the violence and harassment faced by black foreigners in South Africa.

According to the Nigerian Consulate in Johannesburg, Mr Joe was killed by "suspected criminals" in Witbank city, Mpumalanga province. In a separate incident, security operatives murdered Mr Iroegbu in Sunnyside, Pretoria. The Nigerian consulate noted that Mr Iroegbu was subjected to gruesome torture techniques at the hands of the Tshwane Metro Police, which led to his death. The pattern of these deaths mirrors that of the two Nigerians murdered earlier in the country, Amaramiro Emmanuel and Ekpenyong Andrew.

## **Xenophobic Attacks: A Long-Term Issue**

The recent wave of xenophobic attacks in South Africa began in April, with footage of citizens attacking African foreigners circulating online. The arbitrary 30 June deadline announced by anti-immigrant South Africans for African foreigners to leave the country has further intensified tension. Since then, the lives of at least five Mozambicans and five Ethiopians have also been lost to xenophobic violence in the country. Hundreds of African foreigners have been displaced, and shops have been looted and properties destroyed.
