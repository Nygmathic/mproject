## Turkey's Fighter Jet Engine Ambitions: A Glimmer of Hope at NATO Summit

As leaders from NATO's 32 member states converge on Ankara for the July 7-8 summit, whispers of a potential green light for Turkey's acquisition of fighter jet engines have sent ripples of optimism through the nation's defence establishment. The development, which could see Turkey secure dozens of F110 GE engines for its KAAN fighter plane, has analysts predicting a significant boost to Turkey's defence capabilities.

The KAAN, a twin-engine stealth fighter being developed by Turkey to replace its ageing F-16 fleet, has been a flagship project for Ankara's ambitions to join the exclusive club of nations producing fifth-generation combat aircraft. With a domestically-produced engine still in the preliminary design phase, the F110s – which lack stealth capability – represent a crucial stepping stone towards the KAAN's eventual rollout. According to Sinan Ulgen, director of the Istanbul-based Edam think tank, Turkey has already received a first batch of 10 F110s in September, with ongoing talks with the US government to acquire 80 more.

However, the path to securing these engines has been fraught with obstacles. Turkey's 2017 acquisition of a Russian S-400 missile defence system triggered a chain reaction of sanctions and diplomatic fallout with Washington. In 2019, Turkey was expelled from the F-35 fighter jet programme, while a year later, the US imposed CAATSA sanctions, hampering Turkish defence projects and straining ties. The lingering impact of these sanctions has yet to be fully alleviated, casting a shadow over Turkey's ability to secure the F110s.

The July summit, hosted by US President Donald Trump, may provide a crucial window of opportunity for Turkey to break through the logjam. In a recent exchange with the press, Trump promised to make Erdogan "very happy" when asked about Turkey's bid to secure F110 jet engines and rejoin the F-35 programme. While the outcome remains uncertain, analysts believe that the green light for the F110s is a distinct possibility.

## A Complex Web of Interests and Alliances

Turkey's pursuit of fighter jet engines is just one thread in a complex tapestry of interests and alliances that has been woven over decades. The country's strategic location at the crossroads of Europe and the Middle East has made it a prized partner for NATO, while its ambitions to join the exclusive club of nations producing fifth-generation combat aircraft have sparked intense competition with its Western counterparts.

The F-35 programme, in particular, has been a source of tension between Turkey and the US. Ankara's decision to pursue the S-400 missile defence system, despite warnings from Washington, set off a chain reaction of events that ultimately led to its expulsion from the programme. The consequences of this decision have been far-reaching, with Turkey's defence industry facing significant challenges in the face of CAATSA sanctions.
