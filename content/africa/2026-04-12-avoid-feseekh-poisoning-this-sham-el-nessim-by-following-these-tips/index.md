As the scent of salted fish wafts through the streets of Cairo, signaling the start of Sham El-Nessim celebrations, many Egyptians will be tempted to indulge in a traditional dish of feseekh and herring. However, the stakes are high, and the consequences of food poisoning can be severe.

The risks associated with consuming salted fish like feseekh and herring are well-documented. If not handled and stored properly, these fish can harbor pathogens like Clostridium botulinum, which can cause botulism, a potentially life-threatening illness. The Ministry of Health and Population is urging consumers to take precautions to minimize the risk of poisoning. According to officials, the first step to avoiding poisoning is to choose reliable sources when purchasing feseekh or herring. This means selecting vendors who have a good reputation for handling and storing their products safely.
