## Tensions Rise Across Borders

Ghana's Foreign Minister on Thursday summoned South Africa's High Commissioner to Accra, a move seen as a sharp escalation in tensions between the two nations following a series of xenophobic incidents that have shocked many on the continent. The summoning of the envoy, a rare and serious diplomatic move, comes after a disturbing incident in which a Ghanaian migrant, a law-abiding resident of South Africa, was allegedly told to leave the country and "fix his own home," a phrase that has sparked outrage and calls for greater protection of African migrants in South Africa.

The incident, which took place in the port city of Durban, is part of a broader pattern of xenophobic attacks and intimidation reported in South Africa in recent months. Many of the victims have been migrant workers from countries such as Ghana, Nigeria, and Zimbabwe, who have traveled to South Africa in search of better economic opportunities. The Ghanaian government's decision to summon the South African envoy is a clear indication of its growing frustration with the situation and its concerns about the safety and well-being of its citizens living in South Africa.
