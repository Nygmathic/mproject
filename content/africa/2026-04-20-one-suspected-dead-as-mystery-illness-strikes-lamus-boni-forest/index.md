The stakes are high in this unfolding drama. ## A History of Displacement and Exploitation

The Boni people have long been the subject of displacement and economic marginalization. In the 1960s, the Kenyan government forcibly relocated the Boni from their ancestral lands to make way for the construction of the Lamu-Southern Sudan Railway. Decades later, the discovery of oil reserves in the region exacerbated tensions, as local communities found themselves caught in the crossfire between international energy corporations and the Kenyan government. The Boni people's traditional way of life has been all but erased, their customs and practices dismissed as "backward" and "unsophisticated" by outsiders.

The legacy of colonialism and post-colonial exploitation continues to shape the region's fortunes. The Lamu archipelago, which includes the Boni Forest, has long been a coveted prize for local and international investors. The region's strategic location, rich natural resources, and picturesque coastline have made it a magnet for developers, entrepreneurs, and policymakers. However, this influx of outside capital and influence has come at a steep price for local communities, who have been forced to navigate a complex web of competing interests and agendas.
