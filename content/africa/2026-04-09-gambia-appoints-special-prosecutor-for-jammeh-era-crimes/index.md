## "The Shadow of the Past"

As Abubacarr Tambadou stepped into the conference room of the Gambian Ministry of Justice, a sense of anticipation hung in the air. The country's Attorney General had been tasked with a weighty responsibility: appointing a special prosecutor to investigate and prosecute crimes committed under the regime of Yahya Jammeh, the former strongman who ruled the Gambia with an iron fist for over two decades. The decision, announced on Wednesday, marks a significant milestone in the country's quest for justice and accountability.

The stakes are high, not just for the Gambia, but for the entire region. The Jammeh era was marked by widespread human rights abuses, extrajudicial killings, and disappearances. Many Gambians continue to live in fear, haunted by the memories of loved ones who vanished or were brutally murdered by Jammeh's security forces. The appointment of a special prosecutor signals that the country is finally prepared to confront the dark legacy of its past and hold those responsible accountable.

The creation of the post was a landmark moment in the Gambia's transition to democracy. In 2017, after Jammeh's ouster, the government established a Truth, Reconciliation and Reparations Commission to investigate atrocities committed during his rule. However, many victims and their families have expressed frustration with the commission's slow pace and lack of progress. The appointment of a special prosecutor is seen as a crucial step towards ensuring that justice is served and that those responsible for crimes are brought to book.

The context of the Jammeh era is complex and multifaceted. The former dictator's rule was marked by a tight grip on power, and opposition to his regime was frequently met with violence and intimidation. The country's security forces were notorious for their brutal tactics, including extrajudicial killings and torture. Many Gambians were forced into exile, and those who remained were often subjected to arbitrary arrests, detention, and harassment.

The international community has been watching the Gambia's transition with interest. The African Union, in particular, has been a vocal advocate for justice and accountability in the country. In 2018, the AU's Peace and Security Council called on the Gambia to establish a special tribunal to prosecute crimes committed during Jammeh's rule. The appointment of a special prosecutor is a significant step towards fulfilling this commitment.

However, the road ahead will be challenging. The special prosecutor will face significant obstacles in investigating and prosecuting crimes committed under Jammeh's regime. Many of those responsible have gone into hiding, and some have even been granted impunity by the government. The prosecutor will also need to navigate the complex web of allegiances and rivalries within the security forces, where many former Jammeh loyalists still hold positions of power.
