As the sun set over the desert horizon, a sudden explosion rocked the East-West pipeline in eastern Saudi Arabia, sending shockwaves through the global energy market. The blast, attributed to Iranian strikes, has left the kingdom reeling, with officials confirming the loss of 700,000 barrels per day (bpd) of oil export capacity – a staggering 10 percent of Saudi Arabia's current exports. The attack, which also targeted a critical pumping station on the pipeline, has sent ripples across the international community, as leaders scramble to respond to the unfolding crisis.

The scale of the damage is nothing short of catastrophic for Saudi Arabia, which relies heavily on oil exports to fuel its economy. The kingdom's energy ministry has confirmed that the East-West pipeline, which spans over 1,200 kilometers, is the main route for supplying global markets during this period. The loss of 700,000 bpd is a significant blow, with implications for the global economy that are yet to be fully assessed. The impact on oil prices, already volatile, is expected to be substantial, with analysts predicting a sharp increase in the coming weeks.
