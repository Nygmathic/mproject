## Urban Renewal on the Horizon

As Kigali's residents throng the city's main bus terminals, a cacophony of horns, chatter, and diesel fumes fills the air. The city's bus network, once a relatively straightforward affair, has grown into a complex web of routes and schedules, with passengers clamouring for more frequent services and wider coverage. Behind the scenes, city officials are grappling with the implications of a transportation boom that shows no signs of slowing. Rwanda's capital is on the cusp of a major expansion of its bus network, with far-reaching consequences for residents, commuters, and the city's urban planning.

The surge in demand for Kigali's bus services is a testament to the city's remarkable growth. Once a small, rural town, Kigali has evolved into a thriving metropolis, drawing in people from across the country in search of economic opportunities. The city's population has swelled to over 1.2 million, with many newcomers settling in informal settlements on the outskirts of town. As the city's economy continues to expand, so too does the need for reliable, affordable public transportation. For residents like Aline Uwase, a 30-year-old market vendor, the bus network is a lifeline. "I've been using public transport for years," she says. "It's convenient, it's cheap, and it gets me to my destination quickly. Without it, I'd be lost."

However, as the demand for bus services grows, so too do the challenges facing city officials. The existing network is often plagued by congestion, particularly during peak hours, while routes in underserved areas are frequently overcrowded and underfunded. To address these issues, the city is considering a major expansion of its bus network, with new routes and services aimed at connecting residents in all parts of the city. This would involve a significant investment in new infrastructure, including bus lanes, terminals, and maintenance facilities. "We're looking at ways to optimize our network, to make it more efficient and accessible," says Jean-Baptiste Nsengiyumva, the city's director of transportation. "We want to ensure that every resident has access to reliable public transport, regardless of where they live."

The expansion of Kigali's bus network is also being driven by a broader urban planning agenda. The city's mayor, Pudence Ndahimana, has made clear his commitment to creating a more sustainable, equitable urban environment. This includes not only investing in public transportation but also promoting the development of walkable, bikeable neighbourhoods and reducing the city's reliance on private vehicles. "We want to create a city that works for everyone," says Ndahimana. "One that's green, one that's inclusive, and one that's prosperous for all its residents." For Kigali's residents, the expansion of the bus network is not just about getting from point A to point B – it's about building a more cohesive, more connected community.

The city's plans to expand its bus network have also sparked lively debate among residents and experts. Some have raised concerns about the potential impact on the city's traffic congestion, while others have questioned the need for new infrastructure. "We need to be careful about how we invest in public transportation," says Kigali-based urban planner, Dr. Marie-Claire Uwizeyimana. "We should focus on improving the efficiency of our existing network before adding new routes and services." Others have highlighted the need for more investment in non-motorized transportation, such as cycling and walking infrastructure. "We need to create a more balanced transportation system," says Kigali's deputy mayor, Marie-Chantal Rugwabiza. "One that prioritizes the needs of all residents, not just those who own cars."

Reactions to the city's plans to expand its bus network have been mixed. Some residents have welcomed the news, seeing it as a long-overdue investment in the city's transportation infrastructure. Others have expressed concerns about the potential impact on traffic congestion and the city's urban character. As the city moves forward with its plans, residents will be watching closely to see how the expansion of the bus network shapes the city's future. "We're excited about the possibilities," says Uwase. "We just want to make sure that the city gets it right."

As Kigali embarks on this major urban renewal project, the stakes are high. The decision on whether to expand the city's bus network will have far-reaching consequences for residents, commuters, and the city's urban planning. One thing is clear, however: the future of Kigali's transportation infrastructure will be shaped by the city's residents, not just its officials. As the city continues to grow and evolve, its residents will be at the forefront of shaping the urban environment that they call home.
