## A Call to Care: Egypt's Foreign Minister Promises Support for Egyptians Abroad

As the sun sets over the sprawling metropolis of Kuwait City, a sense of unease lingers among the Egyptian expatriate community. For months, they have been struggling to make ends meet, weathering the economic storm that has swept across the region. But on Wednesday, April 8, 2026, a glimmer of hope emerged as Egypt's Minister of Foreign Affairs, International Cooperation, and Egyptian Expatriates, Badr Abdelatty, met with members of the Egyptian community in Kuwait to check on their conditions.

The stakes are high for the estimated 6 million Egyptians living abroad, many of whom have faced a perfect storm of economic hardship, pandemic-related disruptions, and bureaucratic red tape. As Abdelatty's remarks underscored, the Egyptian government has issued a clear directive: to provide full support and care for its citizens abroad. The question on everyone's mind is: will it be enough?

## The Context of Caring

Egyptians living abroad have long been a source of pride for the country, contributing significantly to the national economy through remittances and entrepreneurial spirit. However, as the COVID-19 pandemic took hold, many found themselves caught in a web of uncertainty, facing job losses, reduced income, and mounting debt. The situation has only worsened with the economic downturn, leaving some Egyptians abroad struggling to access basic necessities like food, healthcare, and housing.

Abdelatty's meeting with the Egyptian community in Kuwait was a poignant reminder of the government's commitment to its citizens abroad. The Minister emphasized that the Egyptian leadership is fully aware of the challenges faced by expatriates and is working tirelessly to address their concerns. In particular, he highlighted the government's efforts to facilitate the return of Egyptians who have lost their jobs or face financial difficulties, as well as its initiatives to support entrepreneurs and small business owners.

## A History of Diasporic Engagement

Egypt's engagement with its diaspora is not new. In the 1970s and 1980s, the government launched a series of initiatives to promote economic development and social welfare among Egyptians living abroad. These efforts included the creation of specialized agencies, such as the Egyptian Expatriates' Affairs Agency, to provide assistance and support to Egyptians abroad. However, it was the 2011 revolution that marked a turning point in Egypt's relations with its diaspora. As Egyptians from around the world flocked to Cairo to participate in the protests, the government recognized the importance of harnessing the energy and skills of its diaspora to drive national development.

Since then, Egypt has made significant strides in engaging its diaspora, establishing a network of diplomatic missions and cultural centers that provide a range of services, from visa facilitation to language training and cultural events. The government has also launched several initiatives to promote entrepreneurship and innovation among Egyptians abroad, including the establishment of a diaspora-led business incubator.

## Regional Perspectives

The Egyptian government's commitment to supporting its citizens abroad has not gone unnoticed in the region. Many Arab countries, which have their own significant diaspora populations, are taking note of Egypt's efforts and are beginning to follow suit. In Saudi Arabia, for example, the government has launched a series of initiatives to support its citizens living abroad, including a dedicated agency to provide assistance and support.

In the Gulf states, where many Egyptians have found work as migrant laborers, the government's efforts to support its citizens abroad are seen as a welcome development. "We welcome any efforts by the Egyptian government to support its citizens abroad," said a spokesperson for the Kuwaiti Ministry of Foreign Affairs. "As neighbors and friends, we believe that the well-being of the Egyptian people is crucial to regional stability and prosperity."

## Reactions and Implications

Abdelatty's meeting with the Egyptian community in Kuwait has sparked a mix of reactions among expatriates. While some have expressed gratitude for the government's attention and support, others have raised concerns about the pace and effectiveness of these initiatives. "We need more than just promises," said one Egyptian expatriate in Kuwait. "We need concrete action and support on the ground."

The Egyptian government's commitment to supporting its citizens abroad has significant implications for regional politics and diplomacy. As Egypt continues to engage with its diaspora, it is likely to strengthen its position as a leader in regional affairs. The government's efforts to promote entrepreneurship and innovation among Egyptians abroad may also have a positive impact on regional economic development.

## Looking Ahead

As the Egyptian government continues to implement its directives to support Egyptians abroad, one thing is clear: the stakes are high, and the challenges are many. However, with a renewed commitment to caring for its citizens abroad, Egypt is poised to take a major step forward in its relations with its diaspora. As the Minister of Foreign Affairs, Badr Abdelatty, emphasized, "The support and care of our citizens abroad is a top priority for the Egyptian government. We will continue to work tirelessly to address their concerns and provide them with the assistance they need to thrive."
