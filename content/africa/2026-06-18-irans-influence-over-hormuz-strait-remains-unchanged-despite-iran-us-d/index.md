## Hormuz Waters Remain a Sensitive Point

The Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman, remains a contentious issue despite the recent deal to ease tensions between the United States and Iran. Iranian state television has made it clear that the transit of vessels through this critical shipping lane still requires coordination with the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) navy.

The move of vessels through the strait has resumed following the signing of a deal aimed at ending the US war on Iran. The deal's specifics are still unclear, but its implications on the region's delicate balance of power are already being felt. For Iran, its position as a major player in the region remains unchanged, with the IRGC navy continuing to exert significant influence over the strategic waterway.

The IRGC navy's role in the Strait of Hormuz is a legacy of the 1979 revolution, when the Iranian government, under the leadership of Ayatollah Khomeini, nationalized the country's oil industry and established a powerful military force to protect its interests. Since then, the IRGC has grown into a formidable force, with its navy playing a crucial role in maintaining Iran's dominance over the Persian Gulf.

The importance of the Strait of Hormuz cannot be overstated. It accounts for approximately 20% of the world's total oil exports, with many major oil producers relying on the waterway to transport their crude to refineries around the world. The strait's significance extends beyond oil exports, as it is also a critical route for international trade and commerce.

The recent deal, while welcome news for those seeking to ease tensions in the region, has done little to change the underlying dynamics at play in the Strait of Hormuz. The IRGC navy remains in control, and vessels passing through the waterway must still coordinate with the Iranian military. This arrangement has significant implications for regional stability, as it underscores the importance of Iran's position in the region and the country's continued willingness to assert its influence over critical shipping lanes.

As the international community continues to grapple with the implications of the recent deal, one thing is clear: the Strait of Hormuz remains a sensitive point in the region's complex web of alliances and rivalries. The ongoing presence of the IRGC navy, combined with the country's continued influence over the waterway, serves as a potent reminder of Iran's status as a major player in the region.
