## UTME 2026: A Crucial Test of Nigeria's Education System

As the country's education system teeters on the brink of a major overhaul, the UTME 2026 exam is poised to be a make-or-break moment for thousands of students, teachers, and policymakers alike. On the eve of this critical test, the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) has announced that candidates can now print their mock slips, a move that has sent a wave of relief and anticipation across the nation. The mock exam, scheduled for Saturday, 28 March, is a trial run of the Computer-Based Test (CBT) system, designed to test its efficacy and identify any technical glitches before the real UTME takes place.

The stakes are high, as the UTME 2026 exam will determine the fate of over 1.5 million candidates vying for admission into Nigeria's tertiary institutions. The exam is not only a crucial milestone for individual students but also a reflection of the country's broader effort to reform its education system. For years, Nigeria has struggled with issues of inadequate infrastructure, poor teacher training, and a curriculum that is often out of touch with the needs of the 21st century. The UTME 2026 exam is an opportunity for the country to put its education system to the test, to identify areas of improvement, and to lay the groundwork for a more effective and inclusive system.

## A History of Challenges: Understanding the Context

The UTME 2026 exam is the latest iteration of a system that has been plagued by controversy and dysfunction for decades. In the past, the exam has been marred by allegations of malpractice, widespread cheating, and a lack of transparency. These issues have led to a crisis of confidence in the education system, with many Nigerians questioning the fairness and validity of the exam. In recent years, the JAMB has made efforts to address these concerns, introducing new technologies and reforms aimed at making the exam more secure and efficient. However, much work remains to be done, and the UTME 2026 exam is a critical test of the JAMB's ability to deliver on its promises.

One of the key challenges facing the JAMB is the issue of infrastructure. Many centres across the country lack the necessary technology and resources to administer the CBT system effectively. In some cases, candidates have been forced to sit for exams in makeshift centres, with inadequate seating, lighting, and ventilation. These conditions not only compromise the integrity of the exam but also raise serious concerns about the health and well-being of the candidates. The JAMB has acknowledged these challenges and has promised to address them, but it remains to be seen whether they will be able to deliver on their commitments.
