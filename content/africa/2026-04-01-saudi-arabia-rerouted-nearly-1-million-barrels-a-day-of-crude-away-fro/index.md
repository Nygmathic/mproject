## Oil in Transit: A New Route for Saudi Crude

These vessels, each carrying hundreds of thousands of barrels of crude oil, are the manifestation of a strategic decision by Saudi Arabia to reroute nearly a million barrels a day of its oil exports away from the Hormuz Strait. This move, aimed at mitigating the economic impact of the Strait's closure, has sent shockwaves throughout the global oil market.

The stakes are high, as Saudi Arabia, the world's biggest oil exporter, seeks to maintain its market share and cushion the blow of the Hormuz Strait's disruption. The Strait of Hormuz, a mere 50 miles wide at its narrowest point, is the only passage for oil tankers between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman, making it a strategic lifeline for the global economy. The closure of this waterway would have far-reaching consequences, including a significant increase in oil prices and potential economic instability.
