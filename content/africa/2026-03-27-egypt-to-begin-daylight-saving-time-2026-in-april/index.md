## Egypt Embarks on a New Era of Timekeeping

As the sun rises over the pyramids of Giza this morning, Egyptians are bracing themselves for a change that will have a profound impact on their daily lives. At midnight on Friday, April 24, 2026, the clocks will spring forward by 60 minutes, ushering in a new era of Daylight Saving Time (DST) for the North African nation. This shift, aimed at maximizing daylight hours and rationalizing national energy consumption, marks a significant departure from Egypt's traditional timekeeping practices. The move, which has been in the works since the 2023 passage of Law No. 24, has sparked a mix of excitement and trepidation among the population.

The stakes are high, with the government touting the potential benefits of DST as a means of reducing energy consumption, promoting economic growth, and enhancing the overall quality of life for Egyptians. With a population projected to reach 104 million by 2027, Egypt's energy demands are expected to skyrocket, making every effort to optimize energy usage a pressing concern. According to government estimates, the implementation of DST is expected to result in a 2-3% reduction in energy consumption, a modest but significant gain in a country where energy demand has grown by an average of 5% annually over the past decade. Additionally, proponents argue that DST will enable Egyptians to make the most of the longer summer days, fostering a more active and engaged society.

However, not everyone is convinced of the merits of DST. Critics argue that the benefits are overstated, and that the negative consequences – including disruptions to traditional work schedules, potential health impacts, and increased energy consumption in the short term – outweigh any potential gains. Some have also raised concerns about the economic burden of implementing DST, citing the costs associated with updating infrastructure, including public transportation systems, and ensuring that businesses and households are equipped to adapt to the new time regime. As one Egyptian business owner noted, "We're still struggling to recover from the economic fallout of the 2020 pandemic. Implementing DST now just adds another layer of complexity and expense to our already strained operations."

The debate surrounding DST is a longstanding one, with some nations embracing the practice and others rejecting it outright. In Africa, only a handful of countries – including South Africa, Namibia, and Botswana – have adopted DST, while many others, such as Egypt's neighbors in the Middle East and North Africa, remain steadfast in their commitment to traditional timekeeping practices. The decision to introduce DST in Egypt is, therefore, a significant departure from the status quo and a reflection of the country's growing ambition to assert its position on the global stage.

## A Brief History of Timekeeping in Egypt

The decision to adopt DST marks a significant departure from Egypt's rich history of timekeeping, which dates back to the pharaonic era. In ancient times, the Egyptians divided the day into 24 equal periods, with the sun rising at noon and setting at midnight. This system, known as the "decans," was based on the 36 decans or lunar cycles that governed the movement of the stars and the passage of time. With the advent of the Julian calendar in the 4th century BC, Egypt adopted a more conventional system of timekeeping, dividing the day into 24 equal hours, with the sun rising at dawn and setting at dusk. The introduction of DST, therefore, represents a major shift in Egypt's traditional timekeeping practices, one that reflects the country's growing engagement with modernity and its desire to participate fully in the global economy.

## The Road to Implementation

The process of implementing DST has been a long and arduous one, involving extensive consultations with stakeholders, including government officials, business leaders, and civil society organizations. In 2023, the Egyptian government passed Law No. 24, which established the framework for the introduction of DST. Since then, a team of experts has been working tirelessly to develop a comprehensive plan for the transition, including the updating of infrastructure, the retraining of personnel, and the dissemination of information to the general public. The government has also established a dedicated task force to oversee the implementation process, ensuring that the transition is smooth and minimizes disruptions to daily life.

As the clock ticks closer to the April 24 deadline, Egyptians are bracing themselves for the changes that lie ahead. While some have expressed concerns about the potential disruptions and challenges associated with DST, others see the move as a necessary step towards a more sustainable and efficient future. As one Egyptian engineer noted, "We're finally catching up with the rest of the world. It's about time we started thinking about our energy consumption and our impact on the environment." Whatever the outcome, one thing is certain – the implementation of DST marks a significant moment in Egypt's history, one that will have far-reaching implications for the country and its people.
