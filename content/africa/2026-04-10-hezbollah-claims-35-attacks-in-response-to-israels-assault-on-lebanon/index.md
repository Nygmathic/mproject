## Escalating Violence Rocks the Levant

Rockets streaked across the night sky, illuminating the darkening landscape as the sound of explosions echoed through the valleys of southern Lebanon. The air was thick with tension as the people of this ravaged country huddled in fear, their lives forever changed by the unrelenting cycle of violence that has gripped the region for weeks. Hezbollah, the Lebanese Shia militant group, has been at the forefront of the resistance against Israel's assault on the country, and in a bold move, it has now claimed responsibility for 35 attacks targeting Israeli settlements, forces, vehicles, and military sites.

The scale of these attacks has been staggering, with Hezbollah's military wing, the Islamic Resistance, unleashing a wave of precision-guided missiles and rockets on Israeli positions in recent days. According to estimates, since March 2, Hezbollah has waged over 1,500 attacks on Israel, a figure that is likely to continue rising in the coming days. Meanwhile, Israel has responded with brutal force, killing nearly 1,900 people across the country in the same time period. The humanitarian crisis unfolding in Lebanon is dire, with widespread destruction, displacement, and a growing sense of desperation among the population.

## A Long and Bloody History

The roots of this conflict run deep, with the Israeli-Palestinian dispute dating back to the early 20th century. For decades, the region has been plagued by tensions between Israelis and Palestinians, with Lebanon's complex sectarian landscape adding an extra layer of complexity to the conflict. Hezbollah's rise to prominence in the 1980s and 1990s was fueled by its opposition to Israel's occupation of southern Lebanon, which lasted from 1978 to 2000. Since then, the group has maintained a delicate balance between its military wing and its political wing, all while navigating the treacherous waters of regional politics.

In recent years, the conflict has taken on a new dimension, with the involvement of powerful external actors. The United States, Iran, and Saudi Arabia have all been drawn into the fray, with each side seeking to further its own interests in the region. The Gulf states, in particular, have been vocal in their support for Israel, viewing the Jewish state as a bulwark against the spread of Iranian influence in the region. Meanwhile, Iran has seen Hezbollah as a key ally in its regional struggle, providing the group with financial and military support in exchange for its loyalty.

## A Web of Interests and Alliances

At the heart of this conflict lies a complex web of interests and alliances, with each side seeking to outmaneuver the other in a game of regional geopolitics. Hezbollah's claim of 35 attacks on Israel is just one manifestation of this larger struggle, with the group seeking to assert its dominance in the region while simultaneously protecting its interests in Lebanon and beyond. Israel, for its part, is determined to maintain its military superiority in the region, viewing Hezbollah as a key threat to its national security.

The international community has been largely silent in the face of this escalating violence, with Western powers hesitant to intervene in a conflict that is so deeply entrenched in regional politics. The United Nations has issued several calls for calm, but its efforts have been largely ineffective in stopping the violence. As the situation continues to deteriorate, the people of Lebanon and beyond are left to wonder when, if ever, the cycle of violence will be broken.
