## A Decade of Dominance: Djibouti's Guelleh Secures Sixth Term

It is a chilly Tuesday morning in Djibouti's capital, and the news is already rippling through the city's narrow streets. Ismaïl Omar Guelleh has been reelected for a sixth term as President of the East African nation, securing a commanding 97.8 percent of the vote, according to the country's constitutional council. The announcement, made public on Tuesday, marks a milestone in Guelleh's unyielding grip on power, which now spans over two decades. As the news spreads, residents of Djibouti's bustling markets, cafes, and government buildings are met with a mix of emotions: relief, resignation, and a hint of trepidation.

Guelleh's landslide victory is a testament to his party's, the People's Rally for Progress (RPP), dominance in Djibouti's politics. The RPP has been in power since the country's independence from France in 1977, and under Guelleh's leadership, it has successfully navigated the complexities of regional politics and secured strategic economic partnerships with international powers. His re-election is also a reflection of the country's fragile democratic landscape. Critics have long accused the Guelleh regime of authoritarian tendencies, suppressing dissent, and stifling opposition. The opposition parties, who fielded a total of 12 candidates in the election, have already begun to express concerns over the legitimacy of the vote.

Djibouti's electoral landscape is a complex web of alliances, rivalries, and competing interests. Guelleh's RPP has skillfully managed to outmaneuver opposition parties, often through a combination of co-optation, intimidation, and strategic alliances with powerful interest groups. The country's opposition parties, on the other hand, have struggled to present a unified front, often divided by internal power struggles and ideological differences. The 2024 election has seen a slight improvement in the opposition's organization, but their efforts have ultimately been overshadowed by Guelleh's entrenched power base.

## The Guelleh Legacy: A Balance of Power and Economic Growth

Guelleh's presidency has been marked by a delicate balance between maintaining power and promoting economic growth. Djibouti has emerged as a vital strategic location for international powers seeking to project influence in the region. The country's geographical location, situated at the southern entrance of the Red Sea, has made it an attractive hub for naval and military bases. China, the United States, France, and Japan have all established military bases in Djibouti, generating significant revenue for the government. These strategic partnerships have facilitated significant economic growth, with Djibouti's GDP per capita increasing from $1,000 in 2000 to over $2,000 in 2020.

However, Guelleh's regime has also been criticized for its human rights record, particularly in the treatment of opposition figures and minority groups. The country's prisons are notorious for their poor conditions, and reports of torture and arbitrary detention are common. The regime's heavy-handed approach to dissent has stifled opposition voices, making it challenging for alternative perspectives to emerge. As Djibouti continues to navigate its complex regional dynamics, it is imperative that the international community holds the government accountable for its human rights obligations.
