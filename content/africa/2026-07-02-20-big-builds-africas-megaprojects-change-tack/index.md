## Building a New Africa: 20 Megaprojects Shift Gears

Africa's transformation is on the horizon, but it's not a straightforward journey. Behind the scenes, the continent's most ambitious projects are undergoing a significant overhaul. The 20 megaprojects selected for this dossier, each with substantial investment and transformative potential, are being re-evaluated to ensure they meet the evolving needs of Africa's rapidly changing landscape.

These projects, ranging from gas pipelines and nuclear power plants to deep-water ports and submarine cables, were chosen based on their economic impact, investment potential, and geographical diversity. At the forefront of this transformation are key players such as Dangote, Otedola, and Sanwo-Olu, who are quietly shaping the continent's future through strategic partnerships and investments.

One of the most notable shifts is the increased focus on diversification. Dangote, the Nigerian billionaire, is expanding his billion-dollar empire beyond cement in Tanzania and Congo. This move reflects a broader trend of African businesses seeking to reduce their dependence on single markets and sectors. In Ghana, the commercialisation of informal settlements is gaining momentum, with slums being transformed into thriving assets.

However, these developments are not without their challenges. In South Africa, the shadow of former President Zuma still looms large, contributing to ongoing anti-immigrant protests. Meanwhile, in Ghana, the rich and powerful are building private cities, leaving the state to play catch-up. This trend raises important questions about the role of the state in Africa's development and the consequences of private sector dominance.

In the realm of technology, Namibia and South Africa are leading the resistance against Starlink, a satellite internet service that has sparked concerns about data sovereignty and regulation. The stakes are high, with Africa's growing digital economy at risk of being compromised by external interests. This is a critical moment for the continent to assert its authority and chart its own course in the digital age.

The $5 billion insurance market in the Democratic Republic of Congo is another hotly contested prize, with Equity, Britam, and Jubilee vying for control. This battle is not just about market share; it's about shaping the future of Africa's financial sector and ensuring that the continent's growth is sustainable and equitable.

Ethiopia's debt restructuring test is a closely watched affair, with Addis Ababa's reserves under scrutiny. The country's commitment to debt restructuring is a critical step in maintaining investor confidence and preventing a debt crisis. The outcome will have far-reaching implications for Africa's economic landscape and the continent's ability to navigate the complex web of international finance.

As these megaprojects shift gears, Africa's top leaders are paying close attention. The stakes are high, and the consequences of failure are significant. But with each project, a new chapter in Africa's transformation is being written. The question on everyone's mind is: what does the future hold for these ambitious initiatives, and how will they shape the continent's destiny?
