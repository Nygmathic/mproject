## Scorching Heat Waves Claim Thousands of Chickens

The withering heat waves that have gripped France in recent weeks have left a devastating trail in their wake, with thousands of chickens succumbing to the unrelenting temperatures. The birds, particularly vulnerable to high temperatures, have been unable to cope with the extreme heat, resulting in a significant loss of poultry across the country. According to official data, the torrid June has seen a substantial number of chickens perish, with many farms struggling to keep their flocks safe.

The heat waves, characterized by temperatures soaring above 35 degrees Celsius, have made it increasingly difficult for farmers to provide adequate conditions for their animals. With many chicken coops lacking adequate ventilation and insulation, the birds have been exposed to the full force of the heat, leading to a significant number of deaths and illnesses. The situation has been exacerbated by the fact that chickens, unlike some other poultry, are unable to sweat effectively, making them even more susceptible to heat-related stress.

The economic impact of the heat waves on the poultry industry has been substantial, with many farmers reporting significant losses. The French poultry industry, which is a major player in the global market, is expected to feel the pinch of the heat waves, with some estimates suggesting that the losses could be in the hundreds of millions of euros. The industry's reliance on traditional farming practices, which often prioritize short-term productivity over environmental sustainability, has been highlighted as a major factor in the industry's vulnerability to climate-related disruptions.

The impact of the heat waves on chicken welfare has also sparked concerns among animal welfare groups and environmental organizations. "Chickens are sentient beings that feel pain and stress, just like humans," said a spokesperson for the French animal welfare organization, L214. "The fact that thousands of chickens have died due to the heat waves is a stark reminder of the need for more humane and sustainable farming practices." The organization has called on the French government to implement stricter regulations on animal welfare and to provide support to farmers who are struggling to adapt to the changing climate.

The heat waves have also raised questions about the role of climate change in exacerbating the vulnerability of chickens to heat. According to scientists, the increased frequency and severity of heat waves are a direct result of climate change, which is expected to continue to have a profound impact on global food systems. "The heat waves are a symptom of a larger problem," said a climate scientist at the French National Center for Scientific Research. "As the planet continues to warm, we can expect to see more frequent and severe heat waves, which will have significant consequences for food production and animal welfare."

In response to the heat waves, the French government has announced plans to provide support to farmers who are struggling to adapt to the changing climate. The plan includes measures to improve animal welfare, such as providing additional funding for ventilation and insulation systems in chicken coops. The government has also pledged to increase its focus on sustainable farming practices, which prioritize environmental sustainability and animal welfare. While the plan has been welcomed by some, others have criticized it for not going far enough to address the root causes of the problem.

As the French poultry industry continues to reel from the impact of the heat waves, many are left wondering what the future holds for the industry. With climate change expected to continue to have a profound impact on global food systems, the industry will need to adapt quickly to avoid further losses. "The heat waves are a wake-up call for the poultry industry," said a spokesperson for the French poultry association. "We need to work together to find more sustainable and humane ways of farming, or risk losing even more chickens in the future."
