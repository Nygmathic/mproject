## UK Temperatures Soar as Fifth Heatwave Looms, Drought Expected to Spread

Temperature forecasts indicate that the UK is on the cusp of its fifth heatwave this summer, with the possibility of temperatures reaching 36C in some areas. As the country bristles in anticipation of this latest extreme weather event, the National Drought Group is set to convene on Monday to assess the situation and declare drought in additional areas of England.

Half of England and all of Wales are already officially in drought, with approximately 40% of the population of England subject to a hosepipe ban. The UK Centre for Ecology & Hydrology has highlighted the unprecedented dry spell, stating that last month was the driest July since records began nearly 200 years ago. River flows have plummeted across the UK, with only catchments in the far north of Scotland remaining above normal levels.

Groundwater levels are expected to be normal to below normal in the chalk and Jurassic limestone aquifers of England, while the carboniferous limestone in south Wales is likely to experience notably low levels. This dire situation has prompted the Environment Agency to warn that several areas are likely to see dry weather and drought status changes this week.

As the UK struggles to cope with the consequences of climate change, scientists attribute the intensification of heatwaves and extreme dry weather to the fossil fuel-driven climate crisis. The June heatwave that affected much of western Europe was deemed "only possible due to the climate crisis." The Green party leader, Zack Polanski, has accused Labour of "gross negligence" for its failure to act on the heatwaves and stated that this summer's heat is the "greatest crisis this country has faced since the second world war."

Downing Street has defended the government's response to the heatwaves and wildfires this summer, citing the changing climate and the increasing frequency of droughts. The prime minister's official spokesperson emphasized the focus on both the immediate response and long-term resilience.

The human toll of this extreme weather is stark. England is on track to record its highest ever number of heat-related deaths this summer, with an estimated 2,877 people having died in the May and June heatwaves. The UK Health Security Agency has updated heat health alerts for nine regions in England, warning that rising temperatures may increase health risks, particularly for vulnerable groups.

Amber alerts will be in place for Yorkshire and the Humber, West Midlands, East Midlands, east of England, south-east, London, south-west, and north-west regions from Tuesday to Friday, while a yellow alert will be issued for the north-east. These alerts signify an increased likelihood of health impacts for those aged 65 and older and people with pre-existing conditions.

As the UK navigates this treacherous weather landscape, concerns are mounting about the long-term implications of climate change. River Action's head of campaigns, Amy Fairman, has called for an urgent review of abstraction licences, which permit water to be extracted from the environment. The organisation's analysis has revealed that there are 14,128 commercial abstraction licences across England and Wales, with privately owned water companies abstracting more than 6 trillion litres annually for public supply.

As the UK struggles to respond to the immediate needs of its citizens, it is clear that a more concerted effort is required to mitigate the effects of climate change. The UK must adopt a comprehensive approach to address the root causes of this crisis and work towards a more resilient future.
