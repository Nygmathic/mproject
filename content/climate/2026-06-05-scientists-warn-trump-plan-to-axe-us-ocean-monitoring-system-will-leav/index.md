## The Fragile Threads of Global Climate Understanding

Climate scientists and oceanographers are sounding the alarm over a little-known American system that has been quietly tracking the health of the world's oceans for decades. The US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration's (NOAA) National Data Buoy Center, a network of floating and moored buoys that have been monitoring ocean temperatures, currents, and other vital signs since the 1960s, is set to be dismantled as part of the Trump administration's proposed budget cuts. The implications of this decision are far-reaching, with experts warning that it would severely degrade the accuracy of weather predictions and El Niño forecasts, with devastating consequences for global food security and economies.

## A Critical Component of Global Climate Understanding

The NOAA buoys are a vital component of a global ocean observation network that includes buoys and monitoring stations operated by other nations, including the European Union, Australia, and Japan. Together, these systems provide critical data on ocean currents, temperatures, and chemistry, which are essential for predicting weather patterns, understanding the impacts of climate change, and managing marine ecosystems. The buoys also play a key role in tracking the global heat budget, which is essential for monitoring the rate of ocean warming and its impacts on sea levels, ocean acidification, and marine biodiversity. According to research published last month, decommissioning the US buoys would lead to a massive increase in error in the annual estimates of ocean heating rates, which would have significant implications for global climate models and predictions.

## A Legacy of Climate Research

The NOAA buoys have a long and storied history in climate research, dating back to the 1960s when the first buoy was deployed in the Gulf of Mexico. Since then, the network has grown to include over 300 buoys operating in the world's oceans, providing critical data on ocean currents, temperatures, and chemistry. The buoys have also played a key role in some of the most significant climate research breakthroughs of the past few decades, including the discovery of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) and the development of the global climate model that has become the standard for predicting weather patterns and climate change impacts. The buoys have also provided critical data on ocean acidification, sea level rise, and marine biodiversity, all of which are essential for understanding the impacts of climate change on global food security and economies.

## A Global Response to a Global Problem

The potential loss of the NOAA buoys has sparked a global response from scientists, policymakers, and economists who are concerned about the implications for global climate understanding and food security. The European Union has already pledged to increase its investment in ocean observation systems, while Australia and Japan have announced plans to expand their own buoy networks. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) has also weighed in, warning that the loss of the NOAA buoys would "severely degrade" the accuracy of climate models and predictions. Meanwhile, the US Congress has been urged to reconsider its decision to dismantle the NOAA buoys, with lawmakers from both parties expressing concerns about the implications for global food security and economies.

## A Human Impact on a Global Scale

The potential loss of the NOAA buoys would have far-reaching implications for global food security and economies. According to the World Food Programme, climate-related disasters such as droughts, floods, and heatwaves are already impacting food production and availability, with millions of people around the world at risk of hunger and malnutrition. The loss of the NOAA buoys would only exacerbate these problems, making it more difficult for farmers, fishermen, and policymakers to predict and prepare for climate-related disasters. The economic impacts would also be significant, with estimates suggesting that the loss of the NOAA buoys could cost the global economy tens of billions of dollars annually.

## A Call to Action for a Global Response

As the world grapples with the challenges of climate change, the potential loss of the NOAA buoys serves as a stark reminder of the importance of global cooperation and investment in climate research and observation. The international community must come together to ensure that the global ocean observation network remains intact, with continued investment in buoy systems and other critical infrastructure. This will require a sustained commitment to global cooperation and collaboration, as well as a willingness to invest in the research and technology needed to support climate modeling and prediction. The stakes are high, but the rewards are worth it – a more accurate understanding of the world's oceans and climate systems will be essential for building a more sustainable and resilient future for all.
