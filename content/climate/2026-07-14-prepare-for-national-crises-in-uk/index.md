## Britain's Pragmatic Approach to National Resilience

As the UK grapples with the multifaceted challenges posed by climate change, rising cyber threats, and escalating global tensions, the government has issued a stark warning to citizens to take "small but important steps" to prepare for potential national crises. This call to action comes as the UK's national risk register has been updated to reflect a growing array of threats, including foreign interference in democracy, cyber-attacks on critical infrastructure, and a heightened risk of severe weather events.

Darren Jones, the chief secretary to the prime minister, underscored the gravity of the situation, cautioning that "the risks we face from climate change cannot be underestimated." He highlighted the potential for "significant and prolonged disruption to essential services" as a result of extreme weather events, which could be exacerbated by the increasing sophistication of artificial intelligence. The government's concerns are further amplified by the ongoing conflict in the Middle East and Russia's war in Ukraine, which could enable hostile cyber-attacks against businesses and critical infrastructure.

The National Preparedness Commission has privately warned that Britain's vital supply chains are unprepared for the prospect of a major shock, such as a war with Russia. Research suggests that the UK is lagging behind other European countries in stockpiling essential supplies, including critical medicines. The government has responded by launching a national public awareness campaign to help people prepare for emergencies, including severe weather events and cyber-attacks.

Scientists have sounded the alarm on the devastating impact of human actions on the environment, with data suggesting that the heatwave across England and Wales in June killed approximately 440 people per day during its three-day peak. The UK's history is replete with examples of overcoming challenges, from plagues and pandemics to war and wet weather. As Jones noted, "it is right that we consistently evaluate the risks we could face and plan for what may come."

The government's efforts to bolster national resilience are also reflected in the planned "national home defence exercise" in 2027, which will feature hundreds of officials wargaming Britain's preparedness for hybrid attacks. This exercise will ensure that the UK is aligned with its Nato allies in the face of rising threats. The armed forces minister, Louise Sandher-Jones, has emphasized that Russia poses a direct threat to the UK homeland and that the exercises will help prepare the country to meet that threat.

The UK's response to these challenges is characterized by a pragmatic approach, one that acknowledges the complexity of the issues and the need for a multifaceted response. By urging citizens to take "small steps" to prepare for emergencies and updating the national risk register to reflect new threats, the government is demonstrating a commitment to national resilience. As the country navigates the uncertain landscape of climate change, cyber threats, and global tensions, this approach will be crucial in ensuring the UK's preparedness for the challenges ahead.
