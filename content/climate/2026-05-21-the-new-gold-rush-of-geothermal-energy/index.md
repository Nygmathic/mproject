## Unearthing a Climate Solution

Deep beneath the scorching deserts of Djibouti, a new frontier is emerging. Here, a clutch of ambitious start-ups are attempting to harness the hidden power of the Earth's core – geothermal energy. This clean, emissions-free fuel source has been touted as a game-changer in the fight against climate change, but the industry is still in its infancy, and the challenges are numerous.

The stakes are high. As the world's most vulnerable regions – from drought-stricken Africa to flood-prone Bangladesh – struggle to adapt to the impacts of climate change, the need for affordable, reliable energy has never been more pressing. Geothermal energy, which harnesses the heat from the Earth's core to generate electricity, offers a tantalizing prospect. No longer reliant on finite resources or fossil fuels, it could be the key to powering the transition to a low-carbon economy.

Yet, despite its promise, the geothermal industry remains beset by significant hurdles. One of the most pressing is access to suitable locations. The ideal conditions for geothermal energy – hot, porous rock formations and a reliable heat source – are rare and often buried deep beneath the Earth's surface. For many African countries, this means that geothermal potential is frequently concentrated in areas of high biodiversity or cultural significance. The dilemma is acute: can the pursuit of clean energy be squared with the need to protect the environment?

The history of geothermal energy is one of fits and starts. The first commercial geothermal power plant was opened in Larderello, Italy, in 1904, but it wasn't until the 1970s that the industry began to gain momentum. In the following decades, the geothermal sector expanded rapidly, with countries like the Philippines and Indonesia becoming major players. However, this growth was often accompanied by a lack of regulation and oversight, leading to concerns about the environmental and social impacts of large-scale geothermal development.

In recent years, a new wave of start-ups has sought to reboot the geothermal sector, driven by advances in technology and a growing recognition of the industry's potential. Companies like Baseload Capital, a Swedish firm that has invested heavily in geothermal projects in Africa and Latin America, are pushing the boundaries of what is possible. By combining cutting-edge drilling techniques with innovative financing models, these entrepreneurs aim to unlock the geothermal potential of regions that were previously inaccessible.

However, not everyone is convinced that the geothermal industry is ready for prime time. Critics argue that the costs of geothermal development remain prohibitively high, making it difficult for many countries to justify the investment. Moreover, the environmental impacts of large-scale geothermal development – from induced seismicity to land subsidence – are still not fully understood.

Reactions to the geothermal industry's growth have been mixed. In Djibouti, where several start-ups are currently exploring geothermal potential, the government has welcomed the investment, seeing it as a key component of its strategy to become a hub for renewable energy in the Horn of Africa. In contrast, environmental groups have expressed concerns about the potential impacts of large-scale geothermal development on the region's fragile ecosystem.

As the geothermal industry continues to evolve, one thing is clear: the clock is ticking. With the world's most vulnerable regions facing an existential threat from climate change, the need for affordable, reliable energy has never been more pressing. While the challenges ahead are significant, the potential benefits of geothermal energy are undeniable. As the industry navigates its way through the complexities of access, costs, and environmental impacts, one thing is certain: the future of geothermal energy will be shaped by a delicate balance between economic imperatives and environmental concerns.

What happens next will be crucial. As the geothermal sector continues to expand, it will be essential to establish robust regulations and oversight mechanisms to ensure that the industry is developed in a responsible and sustainable manner. Only by striking this balance can the world unlock the full potential of geothermal energy, and begin to harness the power of the Earth's core to fuel a low-carbon future.
