Climate change is not just a distant threat; it is a harsh reality that is already being felt on the ground. Scientists have long warned of the dangers of rising temperatures and changing precipitation patterns, and the data is clear: the past few decades have seen a marked increase in the frequency and severity of wildfires. The 2020 wildfire season in the United States, for example, saw a record 10.3 million hectares of land burned, with the majority of those fires occurring in the western states of California, Arizona, and Oregon. The smoke and ash from these fires are having a profound effect on human health, with particulate matter and other pollutants causing respiratory problems and other health issues for millions of people. The effects are particularly pronounced in marginalized communities, where access to clean air and water is already scarce.
