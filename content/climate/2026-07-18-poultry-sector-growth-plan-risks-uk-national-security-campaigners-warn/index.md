## The Poultry Sector's Double-Edged Sword: National Security and Climate Consequences

Campaigners have sounded the alarm on the UK government's proposed poultry sector growth plan, warning that it poses a significant threat to national security. The initiative, aimed at enhancing food security, has been met with criticism from environmental groups and experts who argue that intensive poultry farming is unsustainable and vulnerable to supply chain shocks.

At the heart of the controversy lies the government's reliance on animal feed imports, with soy from South America accounting for 18% of the UK's animal feed production. This reliance not only contributes to the destruction of the Amazon rainforest but also leaves the country exposed to potential supply chain disruptions. Maya Pardo, campaign lead at Communities Against Factory Farming (CAFF), emphasizes that the government's own national security assessment highlights the risks associated with this approach.

"The government's own assessment warns that animal farming at current levels is unsustainable without imports, and this vulnerability to supply chain shocks is a national security issue," Pardo stated. "By relying on imports of animal feed, we are putting our food security at risk, and this is a consequence we cannot afford to ignore."

The UK's food security concerns extend beyond the poultry sector, with the government's farming roadmap warning of the potential for "severe food price shocks" and reduced availability of certain foods due to geopolitical instability, the climate crisis, and environmental degradation. This echoes the sentiments of the UK's top national security officials, who have warned that the climate crisis poses a severe threat to national security.

The government's push for growth in the poultry sector has also raised concerns about water pollution, air quality, and local opposition. Ruth Westcott, campaign manager at Sustain, argues that intensive poultry farming is a resource-intensive and inefficient approach to food security. "Intensive poultry farming can never be a solution to food security; it's a recipe for disaster," Westcott said.

In contrast, experts like Harriet Bell, regenerative farming lead at Riverford, advocate for a more sustainable approach to food production. Bell welcomes planning reform that supports farmers in investing in reservoirs, renewable energy, and polytunnels, but emphasizes that this must not come at the expense of healthy water systems, biodiversity, or animal welfare. "If ministers are serious about food security, the forthcoming organic action plan for England should play an important role in turning that ambition into reality," Bell said.

As the UK grapples with the challenges of food security, experts like Tim Benton, professor of population ecology at the University of Leeds, caution that the government must adapt to a new world of increased volatility. "We're in a new world where events are happening all of the time and will continue to happen," Benton said. "The government needs to move away from having a risk register that focuses on pinpointing particular events and instead recognize the complexity of the challenges we face."

The controversy surrounding the poultry sector growth plan highlights the need for a more nuanced approach to food security. As the UK navigates the complexities of climate change, supply chain disruptions, and environmental degradation, it is imperative that policymakers prioritize sustainable and resilient food production systems. The government's forthcoming organic action plan for England offers a critical opportunity to turn the tide towards a more sustainable future, but only if it is guided by the principles of regenerative agriculture and a deep understanding of the interconnectedness of food security, climate, and national security.

## The Economic and Environmental Consequences of Intensive Poultry Farming

The UK's reliance on intensive poultry farming has significant economic and environmental consequences. The production of animal feed, particularly soy, contributes to the destruction of the Amazon rainforest, with devastating impacts on biodiversity and ecosystems. The reliance on imports also exposes the UK to potential supply chain disruptions, which could have severe consequences for food security and national security.

In contrast, regenerative agriculture offers a more sustainable approach to food production. By prioritizing soil health, biodiversity, and ecosystem services, farmers can produce high-quality food while reducing their environmental footprint. This approach not only benefits the environment but also provides economic benefits for farmers and rural communities.
