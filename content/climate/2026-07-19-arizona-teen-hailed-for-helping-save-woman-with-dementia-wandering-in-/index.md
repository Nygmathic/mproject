## A Hero's Act in the Face of Scorching Heat

As the sun beats down relentlessly on the parched earth, a stark reminder of the climate crisis-driven heatwaves that have ravaged parts of the US this summer, a remarkable story of heroism has unfolded in the Arizona town of Gilbert. In the midst of 103F (39.4C) temperatures, a 14-year-old boy, Royal Cothrun, has been hailed for his selfless act in saving an elderly woman with dementia who had wandered miles off course, her life hanging precariously in the balance.

The incident occurred in June, when Cothrun was out riding his bicycle. It was then that he encountered Theresa Morgan, 76, who had just been diagnosed with dementia. Disoriented and struggling to walk, Morgan was carrying a grocery bag and a purse, her sweat-drenched clothes a testament to the potentially lethal temperatures. Cothrun's quick thinking and compassion kicked in as he slowed down to speak to Morgan, sensing that something was amiss. "She just looked like something was wrong," he told local news outlet KNXV.

Cothrun's words were a harbinger of the gravity of Morgan's situation. Being in heat over 100F for an extended time can cause life-threatening illness, including heatstroke, which can be fatal if not addressed promptly. The climate crisis-driven heatwave that scorched parts of the US in June through early July had already claimed the lives of at least two dozen people, according to officials.

As Cothrun spoke to Morgan, he realized the gravity of her situation and took matters into his own hands. He accompanied her to some shade, where he helped her remember her son's telephone number. With his smart glasses capturing the moment, Cothrun then called Morgan's son, Jeff, and let him know about the situation. The boy stayed with Theresa until Jeff and Gilbert's fire and rescue department arrived to take over and get her to safety.

Jeff Morgan, in a report first published on 9 July, expressed his gratitude and amazement at Cothrun's kindness. "It's incredible, really, what he did," he told KNXV. "Just so lucky, so fortunate, that he ran across her and he cared enough to stay with her and to take care of her." The sentiment was echoed by the Gilbert fire and rescue department, which plans to honor Cothrun at an 11 August town council meeting.

The department's statement highlighted the dangers posed by Arizona's extreme heat and the importance of staying alert and looking out for one another, especially during the summer months when heat-related illnesses can become life-threatening. The agency's battalion chief, Ryan McHugh, praised Cothrun's heroism and quick thinking, stating, "Royal's actions remind us all to stay alert and look out for one another." McHugh added, "His compassion not only protected a vulnerable member of our community – but also highlighted the real dangers posed by Arizona's extreme heat."
