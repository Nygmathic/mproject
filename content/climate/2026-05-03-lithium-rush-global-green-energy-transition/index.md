## Rush to the Future: Uncovering the Lithium Rush in the Americas

A faint dust storm billows up from the dry, cracked earth of the Atacama Desert in northern Chile, as a fleet of excavators and trucks rumble across the barren landscape. This is the epicenter of the global lithium rush, a frenzied pursuit of the critical metal that's fueling the green-energy transition. The Atacama Desert is already home to some of the world's largest lithium mines, but the real action is now shifting to North America, where a new wave of lithium projects is taking shape.

The stakes are high: lithium, a key component in electric vehicle batteries and renewable energy systems, is expected to power the world's transition to a low-carbon economy. Demand for the metal is projected to skyrocket in the coming years, with some estimates suggesting it could reach 2.5 million metric tons by 2025, up from just 300,000 metric tons in 2020. The scramble for lithium is not just about meeting this growing demand; it's also about securing the supply chain and ensuring that the world's most vulnerable communities are not left behind in the process.

We embarked on an in-depth investigation to track the lithium rush, working with local communities, industry insiders, and environmental experts to collect and analyze data on new lithium projects across the Americas. Our findings paint a complex picture of a rapidly evolving industry, marked by both opportunity and risk. In the United States, for example, we identified over 20 new lithium projects in development, with many more in the planning stages. These projects are scattered across the country, from the vast expanses of the Mojave Desert in California to the rugged terrain of Nevada's Silver State.

The lithium rush in North America is being driven by a perfect storm of factors, including the growing demand for electric vehicles and renewable energy, as well as the discovery of new lithium deposits in the United States. The country's geology is particularly well-suited to lithium extraction, with deposits found in various states, including Nevada, California, and Oregon. However, the rush to develop these deposits is also raising concerns about the environmental impact, particularly in regions where communities are already vulnerable to the effects of climate change.

One of the most significant challenges facing the lithium industry is the issue of water usage. Lithium extraction requires large amounts of water, which can strain local resources and exacerbate existing water scarcity problems. In Chile, for example, lithium mining has already led to concerns about water depletion in the Atacama Desert, where the mining industry is competing with local communities for access to this precious resource. Similarly, in the United States, the lithium rush is putting a strain on water resources in states like Nevada, where the industry is already facing criticism for its water usage practices.

Despite these challenges, many see the lithium rush as a chance for economic development and job creation in some of the world's most disadvantaged regions. In Chile, for example, the lithium industry has created thousands of jobs and generated significant revenue for the government. Similarly, in the United States, the lithium rush has the potential to create new economic opportunities in regions that have historically struggled with poverty and underemployment.

As the lithium rush gathers momentum, we're seeing a range of reactions from different stakeholders. Environmental groups are sounding the alarm about the industry's impact on local communities and the environment, while industry insiders are touting the benefits of lithium extraction in meeting global demand for green energy. Governments are also playing a key role, with some states and countries offering incentives to attract lithium projects and create jobs.

As we look to the future, it's clear that the lithium rush will be a defining issue in the global transition to a low-carbon economy. The question is, how will we ensure that this transition is fair and equitable for all, particularly in the most vulnerable communities that are often the most affected by climate change? Our investigation has highlighted the need for greater transparency and accountability in the lithium industry, as well as more robust regulations to protect local communities and the environment. As the lithium rush continues to unfold, it's time for a more nuanced and informed conversation about the role of this critical metal in powering our future.
