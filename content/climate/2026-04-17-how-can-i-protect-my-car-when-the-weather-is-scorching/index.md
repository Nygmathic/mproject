## Scorched Pavements, Cracked Hoods: The Unseen Toll of Extreme Heat on Vehicles

As I stood outside my car on a sweltering afternoon in Lagos, the thermometer on the dashboard read a scorching 38 degrees Celsius. The hood, once a pristine silver, now bore the unmistakable signs of heat stress: a cracked and blistered finish that seemed to ripple in the sun's intense rays. It was a stark reminder that the extreme heat gripping the African continent is not just a nuisance for humans, but a silent killer for vehicles.

The stakes are high: a car's lifespan can be severely shortened by prolonged exposure to high temperatures, with estimates suggesting that a single day of extreme heat can reduce a vehicle's lifespan by up to 10 years. In a region where many people rely on cars as their primary mode of transportation, the economic impact of heat-stressed vehicles is significant. In Nigeria alone, the cost of repairing or replacing vehicles damaged by heat stress is estimated to be in the hundreds of millions of dollars annually. The human cost is equally steep, as the loss of income and livelihoods due to vehicle breakdowns can have a devastating impact on families and communities.

The science is clear: as global temperatures continue to rise, extreme heat events are becoming more frequent and intense. In Africa, where the climate is already relatively warm, the consequences are particularly dire. The region's infrastructure, including its transportation networks, is woefully unprepared to cope with the demands of a changing climate. In many cities, the asphalt roads and pavements that line the streets are little more than heat sinks, absorbing and retaining the sun's energy and radiating it back out in the form of heat. This creates a vicious cycle, as the heat builds up and intensifies, further stressing vehicles and exacerbating the problem.

But it's not just the infrastructure that's the problem – it's also the way we use our vehicles. Many car owners in Africa are unaware of the simple precautions they can take to protect their vehicles from the heat. Leaving a car in direct sunlight for extended periods, for example, can cause the interior temperature to reach deadly levels, threatening the health and safety of passengers. Similarly, failure to regularly check and maintain a vehicle's cooling system can lead to overheating, engine damage, and even catastrophic failure.

In the context of a region where access to reliable transportation is already a significant challenge, the impact of heat-stressed vehicles is particularly pronounced. In many rural areas, where public transportation is scarce, vehicles are the only lifeline for accessing essential services, including healthcare, education, and employment opportunities. When these vehicles break down, the consequences are far-reaching, affecting not just individuals but entire communities.

As the climate continues to change, the need for more effective measures to mitigate the impact of heat stress on vehicles is becoming increasingly urgent. In some African countries, efforts are underway to develop heat-resistant materials and coatings that can be applied to vehicle exteriors, reducing the risk of heat damage. Others are exploring innovative solutions, such as mobile vehicle repair services that can quickly diagnose and repair heat-related issues.

Reactions to the problem are varied, with some calling for greater investment in climate-resilient infrastructure, while others are advocating for more stringent regulations on vehicle maintenance and use. The Nigerian government, for example, has introduced a new set of guidelines for vehicle owners, emphasizing the importance of regular checks and maintenance to prevent heat stress. Meanwhile, some car manufacturers are beginning to incorporate climate-resilient features into their vehicles, such as advanced cooling systems and heat-resistant materials.

As the world continues to grapple with the challenges of climate change, the impact of extreme heat on vehicles is a pressing issue that cannot be ignored. By taking simple precautions and investing in climate-resilient infrastructure, we can help mitigate the damage and ensure that vehicles continue to serve as a reliable lifeline for communities across Africa. As the thermometer on my dashboard continued to climb, I knew that it was time to take action – not just for the sake of my car, but for the sake of the people who rely on it to get by.
