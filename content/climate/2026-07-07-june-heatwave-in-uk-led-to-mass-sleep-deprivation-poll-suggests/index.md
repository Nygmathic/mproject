## The Unrelenting Grip of Heat: A Human and Economic Crisis

The sweltering nights of late June in the UK were a stark reminder of the devastating impact of climate change. As the mercury soared to record-breaking highs, homes became ovens, leaving 86% of residents unable to escape the heat. The consequences were far-reaching, with two in three people struggling to sleep and countless others feeling physically unwell. This is not just a story of individual hardship; it's a symptom of a larger crisis that threatens to upend the very fabric of our society.

The data from a recent opinion poll conducted by YouGov for Greenpeace paints a stark picture of the human cost of climate change. Almost half of respondents reported losing at least three hours of sleep each night, a figure consistent with scientific research showing that global heating is damaging sleep across the world. The effects of sleep deprivation are well-documented, ranging from decreased work performance to increased accidents and lower school test scores. As Dr. Laurence Wainwright at the University of Oxford noted, "When we have 'tropical nights' – where temperatures don't fall below 20C – a good night's sleep is all but impossible for most."

But the impact of heatwaves extends far beyond individual health. Businesses too have felt the strain, with 60% of poll respondents saying their workplaces had been "too hot" and more than a quarter reporting reduced productivity. Nearly one in 10 employees reported working in conditions they felt were unsafe, highlighting the urgent need for government action to set a maximum temperature for indoor and outdoor working, as recommended by the Climate Change Committee.

The economic toll of heatwaves is also significant. With 1 in 5 respondents forced to cancel plans due to high temperatures and 1 in 5 throwing away food that went off or could not be kept cool, the financial burden on households is substantial. The need for retrofit upgrades to homes to cope with future heatwaves is pressing, with more than half of respondents expressing support for a levy on highly polluting companies to fund these essential upgrades. This is not just a moral imperative but also a sound economic decision, with the UK's inadequate infrastructure threatening to exacerbate the crisis.

The UK's failure to address climate change is a tale of missed opportunities and inadequate planning. As the government's official adviser, the Climate Change Committee, has warned for more than a decade, the UK's plans to protect people from rapidly worsening extreme weather are woefully inadequate. In May, the CCC noted that the UK is "built for a climate that no longer exists" and requires urgent changes to survive global heating.

The World Health Organization's director for Europe, Dr. Hans Kluge, has highlighted the critical importance of prevention in mitigating the impact of heatwaves. "Prevention works," he said, "Heat-related deaths in Europe in 2023 would have been around 80% higher without the adaptation measures already in place. We need more of them [as] the summers ahead will be harder."

As the UK faces another heatwave this week, with temperatures predicted to soar to 34C in parts of southern England, the urgency of the situation cannot be overstated. The human and economic costs of inaction will only continue to mount, leaving behind a trail of devastation and loss. It is time for the government to take bold action, prioritizing the needs of the most vulnerable and investing in the infrastructure required to safeguard our future.
