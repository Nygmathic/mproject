## Secret Deals and Shattered Trust: Alabama Data Center Non-Disclosure Agreement Rocks Bessemer

In the heart of rural Alabama, a web of secrecy has been spun around a proposed hyperscale data center, dubbed Project Marvel, that could forever alter the lives of residents in Bessemer. A recently released non-disclosure agreement between city leaders and the project's developers has laid bare a shocking truth: officials were required to destroy records related to the project, effectively eroding the public's trust in their government.

The release of the agreement, which was initially effective in February 2025 and amended in February 2026, has sent shockwaves through the community. Environmental groups, who had threatened to sue the city over its refusal to share public documents, have hailed the move as a crucial step towards transparency. However, the city's reluctance to comply with open records laws has raised eyebrows and sparked concerns about the motivations behind the secrecy agreement.

According to the text of the non-disclosure agreement, city officials were prohibited from releasing information related to Project Marvel in six categories, including details about the project's developers, site plans, and reports or studies. Moreover, the agreement contained a provision that required city officials to destroy all copies of information the developer considered confidential, including notes taken by city officials. This move has been criticized by local residents and environmental groups, who argue that such secrecy agreements undermine the public's trust in their leaders.

Bessemer city council member Cleo King expressed his shock at the discovery, stating, "I was totally shocked that city officials signed such an agreement with the data center developer." King, who has voted against rezoning measures for Project Marvel, emphasized that the secrecy agreement was a clear indication of the city's priorities. "It is absurd that three city officials—two of whom are not elected by the people of Bessemer—can sign a document that makes an entire city government more accountable to out-of-state developers than their own constituents," he said.

The secrecy agreement has been condemned by Charles Miller, policy director for the Alabama Rivers Alliance. Miller argued that such agreements were a clear example of how big-time developers could "weaponize" non-disclosure agreements to hide their actions from public scrutiny. "The only winners here are big-time data center developers who want to hide their actions from public scrutiny," he said.
