## **Mass Die-Off of Seabirds Raises Concerns of El Niño's Impact**

Birds are dying in unprecedented numbers along the Pacific coast, with the latest estimates suggesting that at least 12,000 seabirds have perished in the past month alone. The alarming trend has experts scrambling to identify the cause, with many pointing to the El Niño weather pattern as a potential culprit.

The die-off, which began in late January, has been reported in several coastal states, with the worst-hit areas being California, Oregon, and Washington. The affected birds include species such as the Western gull, the Cassin's auklet, and the Rhinoceros auklet, all of which are known to be sensitive to changes in ocean temperatures and currents.

According to data from the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), the past month has seen a significant increase in the number of dead birds washing up on beaches. While the exact cause of the die-off remains unclear, many experts believe that the El Niño weather pattern, which is characterized by warmer-than-average sea surface temperatures, may be to blame.
