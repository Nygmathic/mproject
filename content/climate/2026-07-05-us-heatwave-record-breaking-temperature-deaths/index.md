## Record-Breaking Heat Brings Death and Devastation to the US

At least 25 people have lost their lives in the US as a record-breaking heatwave scorches swaths of the country, with more than 20 states experiencing temperatures above 100F (38C) in the days leading up to the US's semiquincentennial celebrations. The extreme heat has left millions of people struggling to cope, with over 140 million people remaining under active heat alerts across the US on Sunday.

The situation is most dire in New Jersey, where officials believe the extreme heat was a factor in the deaths of 22 people across 10 counties, mostly in central and northern parts of the state. Many of the deceased were found in homes with no air conditioning, outside their residences, on the street, and in parked cars. Preliminary findings suggest that the deaths are heat-related, although the exact cause of death for each individual will be determined by the chief state medical examiner.

"This is not a typical summer heatwave," the New Jersey department of public health stated. "This type of heat can quickly become life-threatening to humans and to animals of all ages." The National Weather Service (NWS) has warned that the extreme temperatures are expected to persist for several more days, despite cool air from the north moving into the region.

The heatwave has also claimed lives in other parts of the country. In Cook county, Illinois, a heat-related death was reported, with the cause of death recorded as organic cardiovascular disease, with heat stress as a contributing factor. In Mississippi, a 74-year-old man died due to heat exposure, and an 83-year-old woman died after falling in her garden while trying to escape the heat.

As emergency services struggled to cope with the influx of heat-related cases, Donald Trump spoke during rain-dampened celebrations in Washington DC on Saturday, while emergency response officials reported treating 51 people with heat-related issues, with 12 taken to nearby hospitals. Other events scheduled for Saturday, including the Independence Day parade, were cancelled amid the blistering heat.

The heatwave has also had a significant impact on the economy, with the NWS urging the public to avoid heat sickness by drinking plenty of fluids and staying out of the sun and in air-conditioned environments. Officials have also asked people to check on relatives and neighbors, highlighting the human and economic cost of the heatwave.

Scientists warn that heatwaves with extreme temperatures are indications that the world must lower greenhouse gas pollution driving the global climate crisis.
