## A New Era of Leadership at Ibrox

Lawrence Shankland's appointment as Rangers captain marks a significant turning point in the club's history, as the 30-year-old Scotland forward takes the reins from the departed James Tavernier. Shankland, who joined Rangers just two months ago from Hearts, where he also wore the armband and scored 88 goals in 171 games, has spoken of his pride and honour at being chosen for the role.

"It was a dream to be here in the first place, but to get the opportunity to captain the club is a huge honour," Shankland said, reflecting on his journey from being a lifelong Rangers supporter to becoming the club's latest skipper. "I believe since I've come here the only way I've learned how to behave around the place is to act like a captain and be a leader within the group." Shankland's commitment to setting examples and leading by example is a testament to his growing influence at the club, and his appointment is seen as a vote of confidence by manager Derek McInnes.

The Scotland forward's appointment comes ahead of a crucial opening league fixture against Dundee United, and Shankland has acknowledged that scrutiny comes with wearing the armband of such a demanding club. However, he is undaunted by the prospect, insisting that the rewards of being successful at Rangers far outweigh the potential scrutiny. "At times when things are difficult, people do look to the captain to answer for the squad," Shankland said. "That's something I'll need to be responsible for."
