## Rift Within the World's Governing Body: Norway Calls for Infantino's Resignation

Norway's football association has taken a bold step by calling for the resignation of Gianni Infantino, the 56-year-old president of the Fédération Internationale de Football Association (Fifa), amidst controversy surrounding his plan to sell stakes in the World Cup to private investors. This move comes as a significant escalation of pressure on Infantino, following withdrawals of support from several football associations, including England, Wales, Croatia, and Albania.

The Norwegian Football Federation (NFF) president, Lise Klaveness, a qualified lawyer, expressed her disappointment and disillusionment with Infantino's leadership, citing a range of concerns that have been raised with Fifa during her tenure. These include questions surrounding the awarding of tournaments, the Fifa Peace Prize, and the overturning of a ban for American forward Folarin Balogun during the recent World Cup following a phone call from US President Donald Trump.

Norway's stance marks a distinction from other football associations, which have only withdrawn their support for Infantino without explicitly calling for his resignation. However, it is not the only nation to have expressed concerns about Infantino's leadership. The Confederation of African Football (CAF) has uniformly backed Infantino, while European football's governing body, Uefa, has already lost confidence in him, branding his proposal a "shabby, backroom, opaque deal."

Notably, Mexico and Argentina have shown solidarity with Infantino, despite widespread calls for his resignation. Concacaf described the proposal as a "symptom of leadership that has stopped putting football first," and a "unilateral and egregious act of poor governance and leadership." However, unlike Uefa, it did not go as far as to say it had lost confidence in the Fifa president.
