# Prix de l’Arc de Triomphe 2026: Longchamp horse racing updates – live

The autumn showpiece at ParisLongchamp has arrived with its customary blend of supreme tactical drama, elite international competition, and testing storylines across an action-packed card. Leading up to the day’s main event—the Qatar Prix de l’Arc de Triomphe scheduled for 3.05pm BST—the afternoon program has already delivered defining moments for emerging equine talents and rising stars in the saddle. With the ground riding fast around the Bois de Boulogne, early Group One contests have emphasized the critical nature of tactical positioning, starting stalls, and early momentum across the contours of the Parisian venue.

Opening the top-level proceedings, the Qatar Prix Marcel Boussac saw Joseph O’Brien continue a stellar campaign as Green Empress demonstrated determined resilience to claim victory against her own sex. The progressive filly, whose only defeat throughout the season came at the hands of Sun Goddess, held off a sustained challenge from runner-up Data to secure the mile prize. O’Brien praised the filly’s pedigree—being by Saxon Warrior out of a Sea The Stars mare—while noting she profiles as a miler who may stretch out to ten furlongs in due course, with a potential trip to America for the Breeders’ Cup pending discussions with her ownership. The race, however, concluded on a deeply somber note following reports that Spirit Tango had to be put down after the event, marking another tragic loss over the Arc weekend following the demise of British runner Fallen Angel on Saturday.

## Graffard’s Masterstroke in the Lagardère

The juvenile division took a dramatic turn in the Qatar Prix Jean-Luc Lagardère over seven furlongs, where trainer Francis-Henri Graffard was thoroughly rewarded for an unconventional piece of race planning. Graffard made the bold decision to enter the Juddmonte-owned filly Amusingly against the colts rather than targeting the Boussac. Despite a sluggish start that left her trailing early, Amusingly showed her class as Crusherman set the tempo for a long way. Travelling smoothly up the straight, the sole filly in the field displayed exceptional acceleration to sweep past her male rivals and win with authority.

Graffard explained that Amusingly had previously beaten Boussac runner-up Data with ease, prompting the ambitious switch. Discussing future plans alongside Juddmonte racing manager Barry Mahon, the trainer noted that the filly’s blistering speed makes her profile more like a Commonwealth Cup contender rather than a Guineas candidate for next season. While options such as the Breeders’ Cup remain to be deliberated with the connections, the success gave Graffard a major victory on the card ahead of saddling the Arc favorite, providing an emphatic demonstration of the filly’s elite raw ability.

## Nicholls Family Heritage in the Abbaye

The five-furlong Prix de l’Abbaye produced one of the most resonant human interest stories of the afternoon when twenty-year-old jockey Jack Nicholls secured his first career Group One triumph aboard Miss Yechance. Nicholls, riding in his maiden top-level contest, upheld an established sprint legacy. He is the grandson of the late David “Dandy” Nicholls—the master sprint trainer who landed this very contest in 2002 with Continent—and the son of former jockey turned trainer Adrian Nicholls. Handed the mount on the supplemented Karl Burke-trained filly, the young rider timed his run impeccably inside the final furlong.

The sprint unfolded at a ferocious pace, with Czech-trained veteran Ponntos breaking quickly from the rail in stall one. However, the outside draw severely compromised the chances of Naana’s Shadow, who had impressively won the trial last month from stall three for Katie Scott’s 20-horse stable in the Scottish borders, but was stranded out wide from stall 14. In the finish, Miss Yechance asserted her superiority from stall eight, fighting off a determined challenge from Ryan Moore aboard King Charles III Stakes winner Mission Central, who took third as Shiri flashed home from stall four to claim the runner-up spot. The win provided immense emotional solace for Burke following the loss of Fallen Angel the previous day, while a humble Nicholls expressed heartfelt gratitude to Burke and the owners for placing their faith in him.
