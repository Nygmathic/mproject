## A Call to Arms for Scotland's Senior Clubs

In a stark illustration of the dearth of opportunities for young Scottish players, Rangers' Robbie Ure made his debut for the club at the age of 18, only to be limited to a total of three minutes of game time that season. This meager exposure ultimately led to Ure's departure for Anderlecht in Belgium, where he has since flourished, scoring 15 goals in 15 games and attracting the attention of Sevilla.

The Ure case is a poignant example of the challenges facing young Scottish players seeking to break into the top flight. According to recent statistics, only 31% of players participating in the Premiership's opening two rounds were Scottish, with a paltry 5% being Scots aged 21 or under. This stark reality highlights the need for the Scottish Football Association's (SFA) upcoming forum with senior clubs, billed as 'Made In Scotland?'.

Scheduled to take place at St Andrews next month, the summit will be led by SFA chief executive Ian Maxwell and chief football officer Craig Mulholland. Their plea to clubs to give young Scottish players more opportunities is essential, especially given the numerous high-profile examples of talent leaving Scotland for English football. Celtic, for instance, struggled to integrate young Ben Doak, now Gannon-Doak, into their first team, ultimately losing him to English football.

The reasons behind this exodus are multifaceted, with bigger money often cited as a primary motivator. However, this argument oversimplifies the issue. Avarice is indeed a factor, but so too is the lack of opportunity for young players to break into the first team. The SFA will need to appeal to the self-interest of club owners, highlighting the benefits of developing and retaining homegrown talent.

Dundee United, one of the clubs under scrutiny, has invested heavily in developing its own players, only to see one of its promising young talents, Kai Hutchison, lured away to Everton. Celtic, Rangers, Hearts, and Hibs all have their own stories of players leaving for English clubs, with the likes of Dara Jikiemi, Daniel Cummings, Cameron Scott, and Boyd Fraser being just a few examples.

In an attempt to sway the owners and CEOs of Scotland's senior clubs, Maxwell and Mulholland will need to present a compelling case that prioritizes the development of young Scottish players. This won't be an easy task, given the competing interests at play. However, the SFA has a unique opportunity to shape the future of Scottish football and ensure that the country's top clubs prioritize the growth and retention of homegrown talent.
