## A New Era in Youth Recruitment: Premier League Clubs Eye EFL Talent

Manchester City's recent £12.5m signing of Jeremy Monga from Leicester City has sent shockwaves through the English football landscape, not just because of the sizeable fee but also because it highlights a growing trend among Premier League clubs: targeting young talent from the English Football League (EFL) and Scottish academies.

Monga, who joined Leicester at the age of eight and has already broken records in senior football, including becoming the second-youngest Premier League player in April 2025, is the latest example of a teenager who has caught the attention of top-tier clubs. His recruitment, however, is not an isolated incident. BBC Sport research has identified 14 players recruited from EFL or Scottish Premiership academies for Premier League development squads so far this summer, a figure that matches the 14 such transfers made across the whole of 2025-26.

The significance of this trend lies not just in the number of players being recruited but also in their profile. Of the 14 identified, 11 are 18 years old, a stark contrast to last season when only five of the equivalent sample were of the same age. This shift towards targeting younger players is a deliberate strategy, aimed at capitalizing on the potential of players who have already received years of professional coaching but remain young enough to spend time in an under-21 or B-team environment before decisions are made over loans or first-team opportunities.

The economics of academy recruitment have also become a wider issue, with the Premier League considering a potentially capping compensation paid when young players move between academies. This move aims to mitigate the financial burden on clubs that lose talented youngsters to rival academies. The current system allows clubs to receive training compensation alongside future appearance-related payments and sell-on entitlements, while unresolved cases can be referred to the Professional Football Compensation Committee.

Brexit has also had a profound impact on the recruitment of young players. The restrictions imposed by the Governing Body Endorsement system have made it increasingly difficult for clubs to sign European talent, with Analytics FC estimating that the number of players available to British clubs has reduced by around 92%. This has led to a renewed focus on developing homegrown talent, with Premier League clubs targeting EFL and Scottish academies to find the next big star.

However, not all Premier League clubs are adopting this strategy. International Centre for Sports Studies (CIES) figures for 2025-26 show that club-trained players accounted for 16.2% of Manchester City's Premier League minutes, compared to just 0.1% for Tottenham. This discrepancy highlights the varying approaches to youth development among top-tier clubs, with some prioritizing the development of their own talent while others are increasingly looking to external sources to bolster their squads.

As the Premier League continues to evolve, one thing is clear: the recruitment of young talent from EFL and Scottish academies is set to become a defining feature of the top tier. With the economics of academy recruitment under scrutiny and Brexit restrictions limiting the pool of European talent, clubs are turning to homegrown talent to drive their success. The question now is which clubs will be able to identify and nurture the next big stars, and who will reap the rewards of this new era in youth recruitment.

## The Economics of Academy Recruitment

The financial outlay for recruiting young players from EFL and Scottish academies is a significant factor in their appeal to Premier League clubs. Nine of the 14 moves identified this summer are listed as free transfers, with the other five for undisclosed fees. This approach allows clubs to spread relatively modest sums across several promising teenagers, offering obvious appeal to those accustomed to paying tens of millions for established first-team players. Only one needs to develop into a Premier League regular for the strategy to potentially generate significant value.

## A Changing Landscape

Brexit has not only affected the recruitment of European talent but has also had a profound impact on the movement of players between English clubs. Analytics FC found that Premier League 2 produced more British transfers to EU clubs than any other English division before Brexit, but those moves had halved by 2021-22. This shift has led to a renewed focus on developing young players within the English system, with Premier League clubs targeting EFL and Scottish academies to find the next big star.
