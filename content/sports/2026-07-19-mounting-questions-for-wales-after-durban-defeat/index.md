## Questions of Depth and Determination

The Welsh rugby season has drawn to a close, marked by a ninth defeat in 12 Tests, following the 43-0 drubbing handed out by the Springboks in Durban. This latest loss has left many questioning the depth and determination of the Welsh men's side, and the future under coach Steve Tandy.

The Nations Championship, a new competition that began with some encouragement after the 39-21 win against Fiji in Cardiff, has seen Wales struggle to make an impact. A convincing defeat in Argentina was followed by South Africa's latest hammering of Wales, which has quashed any sign of a mini-revival. The team's travels across the world, totaling almost 20,000 miles on six flights for two games, will now be followed by a break and an inquest into what has been achieved this season.

One of the most striking aspects of the Durban defeat was the gulf between South Africa and Wales on the pitch. The Springboks, the number one team on the planet, hammered the side 11 places below them in the world rankings. In 2022, Wales won a Test match in Bloemfontein, but four years on, that was never going to be repeated in this one-sided contest. The bookmakers gave Tandy's men a 42-point head start in the handicap before kick-off, proving to be an accurate prediction.

The tourists showed fight and spirit, but it was only Springboks' inaccuracy that kept the score down in testing humid conditions. At least Wales conceded 30 fewer points and four less tries than the 73-0 loss to South Africa in Cardiff in November 2025. However, they still failed to score a point for the second successive time in this fixture, with the aggregate score standing at 116-0 over the 160 minutes.

Former Wales hooker Scott Baldwin expressed his disappointment, stating, "I feel flatter after that than the 73-0 loss." He noted that South Africa didn't have a strong team as they did at the Principality Stadium, and that several of the Welsh players who went to South Africa four years ago and won a Test match now seem to have gone backwards over the past two games.

Coach Tandy, however, does not believe the latest loss affects what he has learned this season. He stated, "When you come up against the best and most physical sides in the world, these are the experiences you have to go through unfortunately." Tandy has had 12 games and three campaigns to find out his best starting side, but former Wales captains Dan Biggar and Sam Warburton do not believe he does yet.

The selection of the team is one of the main tests for an international head coach, and Tandy is still experimenting at fly-half between Dan Edwards and Sam Costelow, while the centre combination is also not settled. The midfield dilemma seems to have Welsh pundits most riled, with the consensus being that Eddie James needs to be moved to inside centre at the expense of a ball-playing 12 like Hawkins or Thomas.

The Welsh Rugby Union (WRU) director of rugby Dave Reddin, who appointed Tandy, will know the gap to the top eight Test teams remains, despite the claims of improvements made in 2025-26. The team will try and close the gap further when the Nations Championship returns in November in Cardiff, with a must-win opener against Japan followed by fixtures against New Zealand and Australia.
