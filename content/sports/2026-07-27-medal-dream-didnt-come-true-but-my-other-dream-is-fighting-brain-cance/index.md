## A Triumph of the Human Spirit

Scotland's Archie Goodburn walked out to a thunderous ovation at Tollcross International Swimming Centre on Monday, his heart racing with anticipation. The 25-year-old swimmer had qualified for the Commonwealth Games final just 24 hours prior, a feat that would have seemed impossible given his circumstances. Goodburn had been diagnosed with brain cancer two years ago, at the age of 22, and was told he might not see his 40s.

Despite this daunting prognosis, Goodburn has continued to push the boundaries of what is possible. He has undergone new treatments that have slowed the growth of his tumours, allowing him to train and compete at an elite level. In February, he broke the Scottish 50m breaststroke record, clocking a time of 27.12 seconds.

Goodburn's determination to succeed extends far beyond his own personal goal of winning a Commonwealth medal. He is also a passionate advocate for brain cancer research, using his platform to raise awareness and push for increased funding. In an exclusive interview with BBC Sport Scotland, Goodburn spoke about the importance of ensuring that brain cancer research is properly resourced, so that others may benefit from the advances he has received.

"I was thinking today - the goal was to make the final, and the dream was to win a medal," Goodburn said, his voice filled with emotion. "That dream didn't come true, but one of my other dreams is that we see a future where brain cancer isn't the biggest killer of patients under 40." Goodburn's words are a testament to his courage and resilience in the face of adversity.
