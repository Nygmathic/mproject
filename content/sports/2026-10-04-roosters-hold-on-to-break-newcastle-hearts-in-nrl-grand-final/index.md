The Sydney Roosters have captured their 16th premiership in breathless fashion, edging out the Newcastle Knights 19-18 in a dramatic National Rugby League Grand Final on Sunday. Overturning a daunting 12-point deficit at a sold-out Accor Stadium, the foundation club denied Newcastle a fairytale conclusion to their Cinderella season, securing their first competition title since 2019. Guided by inspirational captain James Tedesco and an exceptional showing from dual-code international winger Mark Nawaqanitawase, the Roosters dug deep into their reserves of resilience to survive a ferocious late onslaught and claim their fourth premiership under the stewardship of long-term head coach Trent Robinson.

## Knights Surge Early in Front of Packed Accor Stadium

The underdog Knights stormed out of the blocks with immense intensity, completely unsettling the Sydney defence in the opening exchanges to establish a commanding 12-0 advantage. Francis Manuleleua opened the scoring for Newcastle, crossing over to reward his team’s early dominance and stun the opposition. Newcastle’s momentum reached a crescendo shortly thereafter when star fullback Kalyn Ponga produced a classic Grand Final moment, breaking clear and running the length of the field to touch down. The breathtaking individual effort sent Newcastle supporters into raptures inside a packed Accor Stadium, putting the Knights firmly in the driving seat and threatening to turn their remarkable postseason journey into championship glory.

The emotional charge carried by the Knights came against a distressing backdrop in the build-up to Sunday's decider. Newcastle headed into the showpiece event just a day after chief executive Peter Parr voiced deep concern regarding a tragic incident on Saturday, in which a vehicle drove directly into a crowd of supporters gathering to back the team ahead of their Grand Final appearance. According to New South Wales Police, an 18-year-old male driver was taken into custody following the crash in Wallsend, New South Wales. The collision left 10 individuals injured, including several children; a five-year-old girl and a 67-year-old man remained in critical condition, while eight other victims were reported in stable condition in hospital.

## Roosters Mount Thrilling Comeback Through Nawaqanitawase

Faced with a 12-point mountain to climb, the Roosters leaned on their seasoned leadership and elite outside backs to claw their way back into the contest. Mark Nawaqanitawase ignited the Sydney revival, showcasing superb footwork to slice through the Newcastle line and cut the deficit. Playing the final match of his two-year stint at the club before returning to rugby union, the winger—who holds the unique potential of representing Australia at both league and union World Cups over the next 12 months—proved to be an unstoppable attacking weapon. The momentum swung decisively when Billy Smith demonstrated tremendous strength and composure, piling over the line to score and level the contest at 12-12.

Sydney sustained the pressure and edged ahead when halfback Sam Walker stepped up to convert a crucial penalty goal awarded after a defensive infringement from Newcastle. With the lead in hand, the Roosters doubled down on their advantage through the aerial prowess of Nawaqanitawase. Nicknamed 'Marky Mark', the acrobatic winger flew into the corner to plant down his second try of the afternoon, extending Sydney’s lead to 18-12 and crowning a show-stopping personal display in his rugby league farewell.

## Cherry-Evans Strikes Decisive Blow as Sydney Clings On

With the contest hanging in the balance, it was Hull FC-bound playmaker Daly Cherry-Evans who stepped up to land the decisive blow. Running out as the oldest Grand Finalist in the history of the sport, and featuring on the premiership stage 15 years after claiming his first medallion, Cherry-Evans drew on every ounce of his vast experience to nudge over a simple field goal. The drop goal crucially pushed the Roosters' advantage beyond the margin of a converted try, establishing a 19-12 buffer that would ultimately decide the championship.

The veteran's tactical awareness proved entirely necessary as Newcastle refused to surrender. Knights winger Greg Marzhew broke through multiple tackles to roll over the line and score, setting up a heart-stopping finish. When Sandon Smith coolly slotted a brilliant conversion from the touchline, the deficit was slashed to a single point at 19-18. However, the 'Chooks'—one of only two original clubs still remaining from the inaugural 1908 season of what eventually became the NRL—dug in over the final exchanges, repelling Newcastle's desperate efforts to preserve their narrow lead until the final siren.

## Historic Silverware and Emotional Farewells

Reflecting on his match-winning contribution and the triumph in his final NRL outing, an emotional Cherry-Evans deflected credit to his teammates, noting: "I didn't do anything. I'm so grateful. Thank you so much for the support. It's been an amazing journey. I'm so lucky. I had so much belief in this club, the players, the coach. This is what it's all about. And we did it. And I'm so, so proud to be here, and I just can't wait to go see my family."

After proudly hoisting the Provan-Summons Trophy, Nawaqanitawase paid tribute to the beaten Knights while celebrating the unforgettable conclusion to his two-season tenure with the Red, White, and Blue. "Credit to the Knights, they fought for 80 minutes there," said Nawaqanitawase. "This is going to last me a lifetime, it's sad to go but I love everyone here, I love all the boys, and let's celebrate this win, far out." The hard-fought victory cements a historic milestone for Robinson and his squad, adding another chapter of silverware to the Roosters' illustrious history.
