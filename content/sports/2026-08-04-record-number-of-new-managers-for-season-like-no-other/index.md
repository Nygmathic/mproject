## A New Era for English Football

The 2026-27 Premier League season has kicked off with a record-breaking nine clubs beginning the campaign under new permanent management. This unprecedented level of change has dramatically reshaped the league's managerial landscape, casting a spotlight on the ever-changing nature of football's most demanding role.

From Liverpool to Manchester City, and from Newcastle United to Chelsea, the scale of turnover has been breathtaking. Liverpool appointed Andoni Iraola after Arne Slot's departure, while Manchester City turned to Enzo Maresca following Pep Guardiola's decision to step down. Chelsea, meanwhile, opted for Xabi Alonso after Liam Rosenior's ill-fated tenure came to an end. Bournemouth appointed Marco Rose after Andoni Iraola's contract expired, and Nottingham Forest hired Oliver Glasner following Vitor Pereira's dismissal.

Crystal Palace turned to Pierre Sage after Glasner left at the end of his contract, Fulham appointed Alvaro Arbeloa after Marco Silva's deal expired, while Ipswich Town named Gary O'Neil as successor when Kieran McKenna resigned. Newcastle United are also expected to appoint Matthias Jaissle after Eddie Howe stepped down, taking the total to nine clubs beginning the season with a new permanent boss.

This level of change is not new, however. The Premier League has seen significant turnover over the years. In 2016-17, eight clubs began the campaign under new management, a feat which included Guardiola's arrival at Manchester City and Antonio Conte's appointment at Chelsea, who went on to win the title in their first season. Prior to that, seven clubs changed manager before the 1995-96 season, while six did so in 2012-13.

The league's longest-serving manager, Arsenal's Mikel Arteta, has been in charge for less than seven years. Only Atletico Madrid's Diego Simeone, who has led the Spanish side since December 2011, has been in charge of a club for longer across Europe's five major leagues. Closer to home, Aston Villa's Unai Emery and Leeds United's Daniel Farke have been with their current clubs for more than three seasons, while Sunderland boss Regis le Bris, appointed in July 2024, is already the fourth longest-serving manager in the division.

Manchester United boss Michael Carrick, who became the club's permanent manager in May after taking interim charge in January, is among the few managers who have been in post longer than him. Carrick is also United's seventh permanent manager since Sir Alex Ferguson retired in 2013.

This season will also test the theory of the 'new manager bounce', which suggests teams often enjoy a short-term improvement after appointing a new head coach, helped by renewed motivation, tactical tweaks, and a psychological reset. The concept could be tested like never before, with matchweek one expected to feature three fixtures between clubs led by new managers – Fulham against Chelsea, Manchester City versus Bournemouth, and Liverpool travelling to Newcastle.

Liverpool's start is particularly unusual, with Iraola's first six Premier League matches all coming against teams under new permanent managers. The question on everyone's lips is whether the traditional 'new manager bounce' still exists, and whether the new crop of managers can bring about the desired change.

For the first time since 1985-86, England's top flight will begin without Sir Alex Ferguson, Arsene Wenger, Jose Mourinho, Jurgen Klopp, or Guardiola occupying a dugout. Between them, they have won 26 of the league's 34 titles. More than that, they defined successive generations of English football, setting the standard during the Premier League's formative years, transforming perceptions of preparation, sports science, and nutrition, introducing new levels of tactical pragmatism and psychological warfare, and reshaping the game with high-intensity pressing and positional football.

Time will tell if the next defining figure emerges immediately. But with the managers who shaped more than three decades of Premier League history now gone, the 2026-27 season marks the beginning of a new chapter. The average tenure of a top-flight boss now stands at about 20 months, illustrating just how fleeting life in one of football's most demanding jobs can now be.

The upheaval did not begin this summer, however. The 2025-26 Premier League season featured 11 managerial changes, with Forest among the clubs to make multiple appointments during the campaign. The question remains whether changing manager offers guarantees of success, or whether it is simply a recipe for short-term fixes.

Recent history suggests that changing manager offers no guarantees. During the 2025-26 season, several clubs that switched coaches continued to struggle, while others enjoyed an immediate uplift. The likes of Carrick and Rosenior have shown that it is possible to make an immediate impact, but it remains to be seen whether this season's new managers will follow suit.

As the season gets underway, one thing is clear: the Premier League has entered a new era, and the next chapter of English football's history is about to unfold.
