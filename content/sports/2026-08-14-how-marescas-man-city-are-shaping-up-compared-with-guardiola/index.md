## A Tactical Evolution: Maresca's Manchester City Takes Shape

Manchester City manager Enzo Maresca's tenure has gotten off to a promising start, with the team's pre-season displays suggesting a distinct tactical approach that diverges from his predecessor Pep Guardiola's. Despite initial concerns that Maresca's appointment would signify a continuation of Guardiola's methods, it appears that the Italian coach is charting a new course, one that is rooted in positional play and a more rigid structure.

Guardiola's tenure was marked by a fluid approach, with the Spanish coach frequently adapting his tactics to suit the opposition and the players at his disposal. While he was often associated with a possession-based game, Guardiola's system was not fixed, and he was known to tweak his approach to exploit weaknesses in the opposing team. His emphasis on positional play, as described by long-time assistant Domenec Torrent, was a key component of his strategy, with the goal of creating an advantage through the positions of players on the field.

Maresca's Manchester City, however, appears to be taking a more structured approach, with the 3-2-2-3 shape deployed in pre-season fixtures hinting at a more rigid positional style of play. The system relies on three defenders in the first line, capable of playing with the ball while also covering the width of the pitch defensively. Two midfielders support the defense, progressing the ball and screening the defenders, while also counter-pressing quickly if the ball is lost.

## The Role of the Wingers

One of the defining features of Maresca's system is the emphasis on wide wingers, a throwback to the work of Raheem Sterling and Leroy Sane in 2017. This approach is designed to create space for the wingers to operate, with the goal of isolating them against their full-backs and creating opportunities for them to cut back and create chances for the forwards. Nico Gonzalez, speaking after a grueling game against the K-League All-Stars, provided valuable insight into the team's approach, stating that "we try to arrive to the wingers. We have a lot of quality in wingers."

This approach is not without its challenges, however. As Guardiola himself noted in an interview with journalist Marti Perarnau, convincing the wingers to stay wide and resist the urge to drift into central areas is a difficult process. However, Maresca's system is designed to simplify the game for the players behind the winger, with the goal of creating repeatable situations that can be exploited by the attacking players.

## Changes in the Centre of the Pitch

While the emphasis on wide wingers is a key aspect of Maresca's system, it is not the only factor at play. The coach has also asked Josko Gvardiol to move from left-back to a left attacking-midfield position when City have the ball, helping to form the box shape in midfield. This has allowed players like Phil Foden and Rayan Cherki to make an impact in games, operating in small spaces and creating opportunities for the forwards.

Out of possession, Maresca's Manchester City has adopted a more defensive approach, with a focus on a man-to-man press high up the pitch. This is a marked departure from Guardiola's tactics, which often emphasized a more fluid and adaptable approach. The changes in Maresca's system are evident, and it remains to be seen how the team will adapt to the challenges of the Premier League and international competitions.

## Implications and Reactions

The implications of Maresca's tactical approach are significant, with the coach's emphasis on positional play and rigid structure potentially creating a more solid defensive foundation for the team.
