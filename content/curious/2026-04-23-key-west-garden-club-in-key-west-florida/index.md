## A Secret Garden in the Fort

Tucked away on the southern edge of Key West, Florida, lies a Civil War-era coastal fortification that defies convention. The West Martello Tower, home to the Key West Garden Club, is a testament to the power of preservation and imagination. As one navigates the arched brick passageways and former fort spaces, it becomes clear that this is no ordinary garden. The air is thick with the scent of orchids and bromeliads, while the sounds of tropical birds and buzzing insects create a symphony of life.

The West Martello Tower's history is as fascinating as it is complex. Originally intended to support the nearby Fort Zachary Taylor, the structure was never fully completed. Over time, its military purpose dissolved into a series of different uses, including troop quartering, storage, signaling, radio operations, and even an anti-aircraft battery site. By the late 1940s, the tower had deteriorated to such an extent that there was pressure to demolish it. However, local advocates stepped forward to save the structure, and the Key West Garden Club entered into a land-lease agreement with Monroe County in 1964. This decision not only preserved the tower but also paved the way for its transformation into a unique cultural and horticultural destination.

## A Garden of Contrasts

In 1976, the West Martello Tower was recognized as a historic site and listed in the National Register of Historic Places. Today, visitors can explore the tower's former military spaces, now softened by the lush greenery of tropical plants. The vaulted ceilings, gun mounts, and old military fabric remain visible among the vibrant flowers and foliage, creating a striking contrast between the past and present. The garden's designers have carefully curated a selection of plants that reflect the island's natural beauty, including orchids, bromeliads, and cacti. Prehistoric plants, such as the Titan Arum, are also on display, adding an air of scientific curiosity to the space. The pièce de résistance is the butterfly garden, where visitors can witness the life cycle of these delicate creatures up close.

The garden's emphasis on native wildflowers is a deliberate nod to the island's natural heritage. A small hill provides a great view of the nearby Higgs Beach, while the surrounding fields of wildflowers create a breathtaking backdrop. As one explores the garden, it becomes clear that the designers have carefully balanced the needs of both the plants and the visitors. The result is a space that is both beautiful and functional, a true testament to the power of collaboration and creativity.
