## A Legacy of Contrasts

The Man from the Cave of the Angels has long been an enigma. Buried in the Cueva de los Ángeles during the Middle Ages – a time when the cave was already an ancient burial site, dating back to the Upper Paleolithic period – his presence raised more questions than answers. His remains, later radiocarbon-dated to the 12th or 13th century, were found in a context that seemed incongruous, to say the least: a prehistoric monument, built thousands of years earlier. The initial assumption was that he was a Christian hermit, possibly a recluse or a wandering pilgrim, who had chosen this remote location as his final resting place. However, the subsequent DNA analysis has significantly upended this theory, suggesting a far more complex and intriguing narrative.

## A Genetic Odyssey

Instead, his genetic makeup is a testament to the complex history of human migration and interbreeding in the Iberian Peninsula. The results indicate that he is a descendant of a North African or Middle Eastern migrant population, who arrived in the Iberian Peninsula during the early stages of the Neolithic period, some 6,000 years ago. This startling revelation not only challenges our understanding of the early European population but also raises fundamental questions about the cultural and spiritual practices of this enigmatic individual.
