## The Story Behind the Streets

Michigan Avenue, the iconic stretch of road in Chicago, is a prime example of how a street can evolve over time. Before 1909, the northern portion of the avenue was known as Michigan Boulevard. It wasn't until the 1920s that it became widely recognized as Michigan Avenue. According to the Chicago Public Library, the street was named after Lake Michigan, which comes from a Native American word meaning "Great Water." As the road ran along the shoreline of Lake Michigan in the late 1800s, it's no wonder that the name stuck. Today, Michigan Avenue is a shopper's paradise, boasting a stunning array of shops, historic buildings, and vibrant street art.

Another street that has become synonymous with its city is Fifth Avenue in New York. The naming process for this street is straightforward, dating back to 1811 when the city adopted a grid system that would later include Fifth Avenue. Over time, it evolved into a residential haven for New York City's elite, home to iconic landmarks like Tiffany's, the Plaza Hotel, and Saks Fifth Avenue. Walking along Fifth Avenue today means navigating a veritable treasure trove of historical sites, including St. Patrick's Cathedral, the Empire State Building, and Rockefeller Center.
