## A Timeless Pillory in Braine-le-Château, Belgium

In the quaint town of Braine-le-Château, nestled 20 kilometers south-east of Brussels, a centuries-old pillory stands as a testament to the region's rich history. This ancient structure, with its distinctive roof, has been a focal point of the town's Grand'Place since 1521. The pillory's story is a fascinating tale of power, justice, and preservation, weaving together the threads of the Counts of Hornes, the French Revolution, and the evolution of the town itself.

The pillory's construction is attributed to Maximilien de Hornes, chamberlain to Charles V, who showcased his authority by building a series of high steps supporting a column. The column's capital bears an inscription commemorating Maximilien de Hornes, Knight of the Golden Fleece of the Emperor Charles, in the year 1521. At the top, six columns support a small roof, which was replaced during the 2019 restoration. This restoration effort not only preserved the pillory's integrity but also shed light on its original design, which had been known but not visually represented.

The pillory's history is marked by instances of threatened destruction. In 1794, during the French Revolution, there were talks of abolishing the pillory. The bailiff, Justinien Thienpont, opposed this move, arguing that the pillory was a symbol of the town. History proved him right, as the pillory survived the revolution and went on to be listed as a historical monument in 1936. Its preservation was further secured when it was transformed into a public fountain in 1839, with two stone troughs added in 1849. Although the water never flowed, this transformation ensured the pillory's continued presence in the town.

Today, the pillory is included in the list of exceptional architectural heritage of Wallonia, a testament to the town's commitment to preserving its history.
