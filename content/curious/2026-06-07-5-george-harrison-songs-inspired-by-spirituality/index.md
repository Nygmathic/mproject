## The Music of Transcendence

As the world mourns the passing of a legend, the enduring power of George Harrison's music serves as a testament to the enduring allure of Eastern spirituality. The Beatles' quiet Beatle, known for his introspective nature and spiritual pursuits, left an indelible mark on popular music. A closer examination of his oeuvre reveals a profound influence of Eastern spirituality on his songwriting, with several of his most iconic tracks reflecting his deep fascination with the mystical and the divine.

Harrison's interest in Eastern spirituality was no fleeting infatuation. It was a profound and sustained engagement that would come to shape his worldview and inform his creative output. His 1970 album _All Things Must Pass_ is often cited as a pivotal work in this regard, featuring songs like "My Sweet Lord" and "Awaiting on You All" that showcase his growing preoccupation with the spiritual dimensions of life. These tracks, alongside others like "While My Guitar Gently Weeps" and "Love You To," demonstrate a clear debt to the musical and philosophical traditions of the East.

One of the most striking aspects of Harrison's spiritual odyssey is the degree to which it was shaped by his encounters with Eastern mysticism. His meeting with the Maharishi Mahesh Yogi in the mid-1960s marked a turning point in his life, introducing him to the teachings of Transcendental Meditation and the concept of the divine within. This newfound understanding would find expression in songs like "I Me Mine," which grapples with the tension between individual identity and the universal self. Similarly, "Awaiting on You All" is a paean to the transformative power of spiritual awakening, as Harrison seeks to transcend the limitations of the ego and connect with a higher reality.

Harrison's fascination with Eastern spirituality was not without its challenges, however. His increasing involvement with the Hare Krishna movement in the late 1960s and early 1970s led to a series of high-profile feuds with his bandmates, who were wary of his growing devotion to the sect. The tensions between Harrison's spiritual pursuits and his role within the Beatles are a fascinating aspect of the band's history, one that has been largely overlooked in favor of more sensationalized narratives. As such, it is worth noting that Harrison's spiritual explorations were not simply a quirk or a passing fascination, but a deeply personal and transformative journey that would shape his music and his worldview for decades to come.

The impact of Eastern spirituality on Harrison's music extends far beyond the obvious references to Indian classical music and instrumentation that pepper his songs. Rather, it is a subtle yet pervasive presence that shapes the very tone and texture of his work. Songs like "Tired of the Same Old Scene" and "Beware of Darkness" demonstrate a growing preoccupation with the nature of reality and the human condition, as Harrison grapples with the complexities of existence and the search for meaning in a seemingly chaotic world.

As fans around the world continue to draw inspiration from his music, it is worth remembering that Harrison's greatest gift to us was not his songs themselves, but the profound sense of connection and community that they have fostered.
