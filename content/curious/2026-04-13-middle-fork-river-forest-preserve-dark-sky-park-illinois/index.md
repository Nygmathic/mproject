## A Star-Filled Sanctuary in the Heart of Illinois

As I stood beneath the star-filled canvas of the night sky, the twinkling expanse above Middle Fork River Forest Preserve seemed to stretch on forever. The preserve, nestled in the rolling hills of east-central Illinois, is a place where the boundaries between earth and sky blur, and the rhythms of nature take center stage. It's a place where the darkness is not just a absence of light, but a presence that envelops and envelops, a reminder of the vastness and mystery of the universe.

The preserve's designation as Illinois' first International Dark Sky Park in 2018 was a recognition of its unwavering commitment to protecting natural darkness. This meant implementing strict outdoor lighting policies, using shielded fixtures, and fostering a community-wide effort to keep the glow of development at bay. The result is a rare pocket of true night, a place where the stars shine brightly, and the world is bathed in an ethereal glow. By day, Middle Fork is a classic prairie and river landscape, with the Middle Fork of the Vermilion River winding through woodlands, restored tallgrass prairie, and wetlands. Eight hiking trails loop past wildflowers in spring and thick green canopies in summer, while canoes drift along the river, and campers settle in for the night, far removed from urban distractions. Eleven campsites are designated as "Dark Sky Friendly," a testament to the preserve's dedication to preserving the night sky.

The stakes of this effort are significant. As urbanization and light pollution continue to encroach on natural habitats, the preserve serves as a beacon of hope for those seeking to reconnect with the natural world. By protecting nocturnal wildlife and supporting scientific observation, the preserve provides a unique opportunity for researchers to study the behavior of nocturnal creatures, and for citizens to experience the beauty of the night sky without the artificial glow of human-made lights. The preserve's designation as a Dark Sky Park also acknowledges the importance of preserving cultural heritage. In an age where the night sky is increasingly dominated by artificial light, the preserve offers a glimpse into the past, when the stars shone brightly above, and the world was bathed in a soft, ethereal glow.

## A Legacy of Light-Pollution-Free Observations

The preserve's journey towards Dark Sky Park status began in the early 2000s, when a group of local astronomers and conservationists came together to form the Champaign County Astronomical Society. They recognized the unique potential of the preserve's remote location, and the lack of artificial lighting, to provide a world-class stargazing experience. The society worked closely with the Champaign County Forest Preserve District to develop a comprehensive plan for protecting the night sky, which included the installation of shielded lighting fixtures, the creation of dark trails, and the implementation of strict outdoor lighting policies.

The preserve's dark trails, which were introduced in recent years, allow hikers to experience the landscape with minimal artificial light. On moonless nights, the Milky Way stretches visibly overhead, and meteor showers draw bundled-up spectators to open fields. The preserve's public stargazing programs and night-sky events are organized by the Champaign County Forest Preserve District, and have become a beloved tradition among locals and visitors alike. By offering a unique opportunity to experience the night sky in all its glory, the preserve serves as a reminder of the importance of preserving our natural heritage, and the need to balance human development with the protection of our environment.

## A Global Movement Takes Root

The preserve's commitment to protecting the night sky is part of a global movement to preserve dark skies. DarkSky International, the organization that designated the preserve as an International Dark Sky Park, has certified over 150 sites worldwide, from the Atacama Desert in Chile to the Scottish Highlands. The movement is driven by a growing recognition of the importance of preserving the night sky, not just for its aesthetic value, but for its scientific and cultural significance.
