The Moon Base User's Guide outlines the monumental task that NASA and its partners face in establishing a sustainable human presence on the moon. The guide identifies over 1,000 requirements, ranging from the mundane (e.g., air pressure, temperature control, and power generation) to the extraordinary (e.g., radiation protection, in-situ resource utilization, and lunar regolith excavation). These requirements are grouped into five categories: infrastructure, life support, habitability, operations, and safety. The guide also highlights the need for significant advancements in areas such as lunar surface transportation, communication, and navigation. The sheer scale of the task is daunting, and many experts are questioning whether the deadline can be met.

## A Legacy of Uncertainty

The Moon Base User's Guide is not the first attempt by NASA to establish a human presence on the moon. The Apollo programme, which successfully landed six manned missions on the lunar surface between 1969 and 1972, was a remarkable achievement that captured the imagination of the world. However, the programme was also plagued by technical challenges, budget overruns, and a lack of clear objectives. The Apollo 13 mission, which was aborted due to an explosion on board, is a stark reminder of the risks involved in space exploration. Today, as NASA embarks on a new lunar endeavour, it is clear that the agency is learning from the past, but also that the stakes are higher than ever before.
