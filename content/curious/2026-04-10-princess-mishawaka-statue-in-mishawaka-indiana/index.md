## A Tale of Two Histories

In the heart of Mishawaka, Indiana, a bronze statue stands as a testament to the legend of Princess Mishawaka, a Shawnee Indian maiden who has captured the imagination of locals and tourists alike. The statue, created in 1987 by sculptor Sufi Ahmad, depicts a regal figure with a fierce gaze and an outstretched arm, as if beckoning passersby to explore the rich history behind her name. But scratch beneath the surface, and the tale of Princess Mishawaka reveals itself to be a complex and multifaceted one, with different versions of her life and a lingering question: was she even real?

The plaque accompanying the statue tells a story of a fierce warrior who led her people after her father's injury in a battle with the Potawatomi. Captured and held captive, she fell in love with a white man named Dead Shot, only to be rescued by a Shawnee named Gray Wolf, who was thwarted in his pursuit of her by Dead Shot. But was this a tale of true love or a narrative constructed to promote a romanticized image of the American frontier? The stakes are high, as the story of Princess Mishawaka has become an integral part of Mishawaka's identity, earning the town its nickname, the "Princess City."

The Princess Mishawaka Society, a group dedicated to preserving the legend, insists that Mishawaka was indeed the daughter of Chief Elkhart and was abducted by Gray Wolf, only to be rescued by Dead Shot. However, the History Museum in nearby South Bend, Indiana, shares a more nuanced view, suggesting that the story was likely fictional and invented in the 1930s to promote tourism. This raises questions about the motivations behind the creation of this legend and the impact it has had on the local community.
