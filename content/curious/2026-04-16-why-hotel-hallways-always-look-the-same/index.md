## Design by Repetition

Hotel hallways, those seemingly endless corridors that lead guests to their rooms, are often overlooked as a design feature. Yet, they are ubiquitous, found in hotels of every shape and size, from budget-friendly motels to luxury resorts. But have you ever stopped to think: why do hotel hallways always look the same? The answer lies at the intersection of psychology, economics, and comfort, where a complex interplay of factors has given rise to a design trend that is both familiar and, paradoxically, alienating.

The hallways of hotels are designed to be efficient, navigable, and easy to maintain. The repetition of identical doors, the standardized signage, and the neutral color palette all contribute to a sense of predictability and familiarity. From a visitor's perspective, this consistency can be comforting, particularly in unfamiliar surroundings. It is a reflection of the hotel's commitment to providing a hassle-free experience, where guests can focus on their stay rather than worrying about navigating a labyrinthine layout.

However, this emphasis on efficiency and standardization has also led to a homogenization of hotel design. The result is a visual landscape that is both instantly recognizable and, in its uniformity, unsettling. It is a paradox that has been observed in various industries, where the pursuit of consistency and familiarity can lead to a loss of individuality and creativity. In the case of hotel hallways, this trend is exacerbated by the sheer scale of the industry, with countless hotels around the world adopting similar design strategies in an effort to appeal to a broad range of customers.

## The Comfort of the Familiar

The psychological benefits of repeated design elements in hotel hallways are well-documented. Research has shown that our brains are wired to respond positively to familiar patterns and stimuli, which can increase feelings of comfort and relaxation. This phenomenon is often referred to as the "mere exposure effect," where our brains associate familiar stimuli with positive emotions and decreased anxiety. In the context of hotel design, this means that the repetition of hallways can serve as a form of comfort food for guests, providing a sense of security and predictability in an unfamiliar environment.

However, this emphasis on familiarity can also have a dark side. The homogenization of hotel design can lead to a sense of cultural and creative stagnation, where unique and innovative design elements are sacrificed in favor of a bland, cookie-cutter aesthetic. This is particularly evident in the proliferation of "designer" hotels, where the emphasis on style and luxury often comes at the expense of individuality and creativity. In these cases, the repetition of hallways can become a symbol of the hotel's attempt to project a certain image or aesthetic, rather than providing a genuine and authentic experience for guests.

## Pushing Back Against the Trend

In recent years, a growing number of hotels has begun to push back against the trend of uniform design in hallways. These hotels are opting for more unique and innovative approaches to hallway design, which can range from bold color schemes and patterns to interactive installations and immersive experiences. While these design strategies may not appeal to every guest, they can help to create a more memorable and engaging experience for travelers who are looking for something beyond the familiar.

One notable example is the Hotel Nairi in Yerevan, Armenia, which features a hallway designed by the renowned architect Tigran Tsitoghdzyan. The hallway is a riot of color and patterns, with vibrant textiles and bold graphics that create a sense of energy and movement. While this design may not be to everyone's taste, it is undeniably unique and memorable, and provides a refreshing alternative to the standardization of hotel design.

## The Future of Hotel Hallways

As the hotel industry continues to evolve and adapt to changing consumer preferences, it will be interesting to see how the design of hallways evolves. Will the trend towards uniformity and standardization continue, or will hotels begin to experiment with more innovative and unique design elements? One thing is certain: the design of hotel hallways will remain a critical aspect of the guest experience, and will continue to play a significant role in shaping the way we perceive and interact with these spaces.

In the meantime, travelers can expect to see a continued emphasis on comfort and familiarity in hotel hallways, as well as a growing trend towards more unique and innovative design approaches. Whether or not these design strategies will ultimately lead to a more memorable and engaging experience remains to be seen, but one thing is clear: the design of hotel hallways is about to get a whole lot more interesting.
