## A History of Haste

Some of the most enduring horror movies in cinema history were made with lightning speed, their success a testament to the power of creative intuition and technical know-how. One such film, the 1973 classic _The Texas Chain Saw Massacre_, was shot in just 21 days, on a shoestring budget of $300,000. Director Tobe Hooper's vision for the film was clear from the outset – to create a raw, visceral experience that captured the raw terror of a group of friends stalked by a family of cannibals in rural Texas. To achieve this, Hooper relied on a small, agile crew that worked tirelessly to capture the perfect shot, even if it meant pushing the actors to their limits.

Another example of a film made in record time is the 1976 hit _Carrie_, based on the bestselling novel by Stephen King. Director Brian De Palma shot the film in just 28 days, a feat that was all the more remarkable given its ambitious scope and technical demands. The film's success can be attributed in part to the collaboration between De Palma and his young star, Sissy Spacek, who brought a vulnerability and intensity to the role of the titular character that continues to captivate audiences to this day.

## The Benefits of a Tight Schedule

While it may seem counterintuitive, the constraints of a tight production window can actually prove beneficial for filmmakers. By forcing them to be more creative and resourceful, time pressure can stimulate innovative solutions and new ideas. This was the case with the 1980 film _The Empire Strikes Back_, the second installment in the iconic _Star Wars_ trilogy. Director Irvin Kershner had only 101 days to shoot the film, a challenge that was compounded by the logistical complexities of filming on location in Norway. Despite these challenges, Kershner's team managed to deliver a film that is widely regarded as one of the greatest sequels of all time, with a narrative that expertly builds on the foundation laid by the original.

In contrast, the 1995 film _Apollo 13_ was made in just 73 days, with a budget of $52 million. Director Ron Howard's vision for the film was clear – to create a gripping, authentic portrayal of the Apollo 13 mission, which saw three astronauts narrowly escape disaster after an explosion occurred on board. To achieve this, Howard relied on a talented cast, including Tom Hanks and Bill Paxton, who brought a depth and nuance to their performances that belied the film's tight production schedule.

## When the Clock Ticks On

Not all films made in record time have been so fortunate. The 1975 film _The Apple_, a musical science fiction epic, was shot in just 24 days, with a budget of $1 million. Despite its ambitious scope, the film was ultimately a commercial and critical failure, with many critics accusing its director, Menahem Golan, of prioritizing style over substance. This experience serves as a sobering reminder that even the most talented filmmakers can fall victim to the perils of a tight production schedule, where the pressure to deliver can lead to compromises that ultimately undermine the quality of the final product.
