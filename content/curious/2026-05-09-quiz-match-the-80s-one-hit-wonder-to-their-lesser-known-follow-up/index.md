## The B-Side Band: Unpacking the Curious Case of One-Hit Wonders

In the annals of music history, few phenomena are as fascinating as the one-hit wonder – a band or artist that achieves fleeting success with a single song, only to vanish into obscurity. But what if we told you that these supposed one-hit wonders often had more to offer than meets the eye? A closer examination reveals a trove of hidden gems, lesser-known follow-ups that showcase the artistic range and depth of these oft-maligned musicians.

Take, for instance, the case of Men Without Hats, who burst onto the scene in 1983 with the quirky, new wave-infused hit "The Safety Dance." This jaunty tune, with its catchy chorus and sing-along lyrics, became an unlikely anthem of the era. But few fans know that Men Without Hats had a more nuanced, complex follow-up in "Pop Goes the World," a song that eschewed the frivolity of "The Safety Dance" for a more introspective, synth-heavy sound. Released in 1987, "Pop Goes the World" showcased the band's ability to craft songs that were both catchy and intellectually stimulating.

But Men Without Hats are not alone in this regard. Take, for example, the case of A Flock of Seagulls, whose 1982 hit "I Ran (So Far Away)" became a staple of the early MTV era. While few fans remember the band's follow-up, "Space Age Love Song," this 1982 single revealed a more experimental, new wave-influenced side to the band's sound. With its driving beat and soaring synths, "Space Age Love Song" prefigured the band's later, more commercial success.

## The Psychology of the One-Hit Wonder

So why do one-hit wonders often get relegated to the sidelines, their lesser-known follow-ups lost to the sands of time? One reason may be the human brain's tendency to categorize and compartmentalize artistic output. When we encounter a song that resonates with us, we tend to fixate on it, replaying it ad infinitum and ignoring the rest of the artist's oeuvre. This phenomenon is known as the "mere exposure effect," where our brains become accustomed to a particular sound or style, making it more likely that we'll dismiss anything that doesn't fit into that mold.

Additionally, the music industry itself often contributes to the one-hit wonder phenomenon. Record labels and marketing teams often prioritize commercial success over artistic merit, pushing artists to churn out formulaic, radio-friendly hits rather than exploring more experimental or innovative sounds. This can lead to a situation where an artist's follow-up singles are either ignored or dismissed as inferior to their breakout hit.

## The Legacy of the One-Hit Wonder

Despite their relatively short shelf life, one-hit wonders often leave a lasting impact on the music world. Take, for example, the influence of Men Without Hats on the 1990s alternative rock scene. Bands like Depeche Mode and The Cure owe a debt to the band's innovative blend of new wave and art rock. Similarly, A Flock of Seagulls' "Space Age Love Song" has been cited as an influence by artists as diverse as Nine Inch Nails and The Killers.

But the legacy of the one-hit wonder extends beyond their musical influence. In an era where social media and streaming platforms have created a culture of disposability and instant gratification, the one-hit wonder phenomenon serves as a reminder of the importance of context and history in music appreciation. By excavating the lesser-known follow-ups of these oft-maligned artists, we're forced to confront the complexities and nuances of their artistic output, and to reevaluate our assumptions about the nature of success and failure in the music industry.
