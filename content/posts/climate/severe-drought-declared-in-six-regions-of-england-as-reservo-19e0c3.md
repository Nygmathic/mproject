Six regions across England have entered a severe drought status following the country's hottest summer on record. Parts of Devon, Cornwall, Dorset, Somerset, East Sussex, and West Sussex are facing critically low reservoir levels that jeopardize public water supplies. The climate crisis has driven the south-east and south-west of England into their driest April-to-September stretch in more than a century.

According to the Environment Agency, typical reservoir capacity for this period is around 75 percent. Current reserves fall drastically short, with Ardingly reservoir at just 19 percent capacity, threatening supplies to approximately 95,000 properties in Haywards Heath, Uckfield, and surrounding areas. Wimbleball reservoir, which serves South West Water customers in East Devon, North Devon, Somerset, and West Dorset, as well as Wessex Water customers in West Somerset, stands at 16 percent capacity. Other depleted facilities include Cornwall’s Drift and Stithians reservoirs, which serve 56,000 households, and Devon’s Tottiford and Venford group, supplying 47,000 households.

In response, Environment Secretary Angela Eagle stated that the government is expanding planning and support for conditions the country has "not experienced for decades." However, Eagle has not yet approved requests from South East Water, South West Water, and Southern Water to enforce non-essential water bans on commercial entities such as golf clubs, private schools, and commercial car washes. Meanwhile, Water Minister Emma Hardy instructed the Environment Agency to prioritize public drinking supplies over environmental rules in severe drought zones when trade-offs are unavoidable, acknowledging that nature could suffer.

The National Drought Group confirmed that authorities will transition to daily monitoring of river and reservoir levels. The Environment Agency is also escalating water quality testing because reduced water volumes diminish sewage dilution, heightening environmental pollution risks. Furthermore, officials will conduct increased compliance checks on businesses that abstract water to ensure they remain within legal limits. Environment Agency Chief Executive Philip Duffy urged water utility companies to execute their drought plans to safeguard public supplies, while calling on the public to reduce personal consumption to protect rivers, wildlife, and agriculture.
