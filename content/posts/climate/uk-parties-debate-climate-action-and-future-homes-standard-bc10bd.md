During the recent political party conference season, major UK parties outlined contrasting environmental platforms. At the Conservative conference, Kemi Badenoch proposed eliminating the Future Homes Standard (FHS). The policy would have required roughly six million planned UK homes to feature solar panels, insulation, heat pumps, and batteries. Conservative figures cited building costs of £30,000, but alternative estimates place installation between £5,000 and £8,000, with potential annual energy bill savings exceeding £1,300, compared to retrofitting costs of over £20,000 later. University of Oxford professor Jan Rosenow warned that constructing fossil-fuel-dependent housing creates "a liability for the occupants" and causes market uncertainty. Additionally, the UK Green Building Council noted the construction sector is already prepared for the standard. The Conservatives also pledged to abolish Natural England and the Environment Agency, despite ongoing river pollution and declining wildlife.

Concurrently, the Green Party shifted its policy by dropping its long-standing opposition to nuclear power. Although prioritizing renewable energy, Green MPs may now support new nuclear reactors. Paul Dorfman of the Nuclear Consulting Group criticized the move, cautioning that nuclear projects involve high costs and construction delays exceeding 20 years, which could alienate grassroots voters. The Greens recently increased their vote share in the Holborn and St Pancras byelection, which was won by Labour's Sagal Abdi-Wali.

At Labour's conference, Andy Burnham briefly referenced climate issues while announcing a publicly owned body to invest in the national energy grid alongside the privatised National Grid, as well as a fund to plant 500,000 trees across England. Greenpeace UK urged Labour to deliver its climate commitments and reject new North Sea oil and gas expansion, noting that decisions on the Rosebank and Jackdaw drilling sites were postponed until after the byelection. Meanwhile, Friends of the Earth and other advocacy groups warned that severe weather from an intensifying El Niño system—expected to bring stormier winter conditions to the UK along with global droughts, floods, and higher food prices—makes environmental action imperative for all political parties.
