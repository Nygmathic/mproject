Thousands of homes and businesses across the island of Ireland have been left without electricity, while flight and ferry schedules face widespread disruption following the passage of Storm Boelo.

Electricity operators reported extensive damage to power lines caused by severe weather from Saturday night into early Sunday. Northern Ireland Electricity stated that 7,000 customers experienced outages, while ESB Networks reported approximately 18,000 affected properties in the Republic of Ireland, mainly in the north-western region. ESB Networks indicated that repair crews had been deployed to all impacted locations, with expectations that supply would be restored to most customers by Sunday evening.

Airports across the region reported disrupted operations. Belfast City Airport saw cancellations on routes connecting to London, Aberdeen, Glasgow, Edinburgh, and Liverpool. At Belfast International Airport, 17 morning flights were cancelled. Meanwhile, Graeme McQueen of Dublin Airport operator daa confirmed that airlines cancelled 12 inbound and 12 outbound flights, primarily due to adverse weather in other countries. Sea transport was similarly affected, as Stena Line and P&O Ferries cancelled multiple Sunday morning sailings between Belfast, Larne, Cairnryan, and Liverpool. However, Stena Line’s Dublin to Holyhead service continued without disruption.

On the roads, Northern Ireland's Department for Infrastructure addressed around 350 reported incidents involving flooding and fallen trees. Although Londonderry's Foyle Bridge remained closed, several previously impassable routes, including the Ballyrobin Road and Glenshane Road, were reopened on Sunday morning. Precautionary temporary flood defences also remained active along Lockview Road and Laganbank Road in Belfast.

The storm brought powerful wind gusts and heavy rain over the weekend. On Saturday, wind speeds reached 83 mph at Malin Head in County Donegal and 78 mph at Orlock Head in County Down. Significant rainfall was also recorded, with Castlederg and Lough Fea receiving nearly 40 mm. While weather warnings expired and winds were set to ease on Sunday evening, the Met Office cautioned that another low-pressure system would bring strong winds late Monday into Tuesday, prompting a yellow wind warning for parts of Northern Ireland.
