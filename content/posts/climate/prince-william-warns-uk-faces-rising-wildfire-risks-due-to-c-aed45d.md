The Prince of Wales has warned that the United Kingdom faces a "terrifying prospect" of increased wildfires caused by climate change, stressing the critical need for advanced preparation. Speaking during a visit to Treherbert in Rhondda Cynon Taf—a community endangered by a major mountain blaze in August—Prince William urged the nation to treat wildfire threats with the same urgency as continental Europe. He raised concerns regarding a shortage of specialized firefighting aircraft, reflecting on his past service flying Sea King helicopters and noting that aerial resources remain a key vulnerability for the country. He added that 2026 marked the first summer he grew genuinely worried about the extent of wild blazes across the nation.

The prince’s comments follow an unprecedented surge in fire incidents triggered by months of historic heatwaves and dry weather throughout spring and summer. Data provided by the National Fire Chiefs Council revealed a record 1,350 significant wildfires across England and Wales, representing an increase of more than 300 compared to 2025 and over 1,000 compared to 2024. During the peak in August, the South Wales Fire and Rescue Service was forced to handle 14 simultaneous wildfires, prompting a major incident declaration. Firefighters from 22 outside services and military personnel, including 200 members of the Queen's Own Gurkha Logistic Regiment, were mobilized to dig fire breaks and deliver 2.1 million litres of water. Aled Bound, a crew manager at Maesteg fire station, described the season as the most physically and mentally exhausting of his career due to deep-rooted flames continually reigniting.

During his visit, Prince William commended local volunteers, military members, and emergency services for their immense efforts to protect lives and properties. In Treherbert, charity Valleys Kids kept its local community building open continuously for nearly a week, transforming it into an emergency command post and rest facility for crews. Kath Edwards, the charity's managing director, highlighted how local residents and businesses rallied together by donating food and resources. In response to the growing environmental risks, the Welsh government hosted a wildfire summit aimed at enhancing future readiness, while the UK government announced plans to outfit RAF Chinook helicopters and A400M transport aircraft for future firefighting missions. Adam Openshaw, deputy chief fire officer for South Wales, confirmed that authorities have gained crucial insights from the severe season to better handle upcoming risks.
