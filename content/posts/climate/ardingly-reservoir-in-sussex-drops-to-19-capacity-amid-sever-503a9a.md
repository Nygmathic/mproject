Severe drought conditions continue to strain water supplies across parts of England, leaving the Ardingly reservoir in Sussex operating at just 19 percent capacity. The reservoir, which is managed by South East Water, supplies roughly 95,000 properties located in and around Haywards Heath and Uckfield. A site visit reported by The Guardian revealed an exposed muddy bed that normally sits under six metres of water. While the Environment Agency typically anticipates seasonal storage levels around 75 percent, several facilities remain critically depleted, including South West Water's Wimbleball reservoir at 14.5 percent capacity.

Following the hottest summer on record in the UK, severe drought was officially declared in regions including East Sussex, West Sussex, Devon, Cornwall, Dorset, and Somerset. Almost three-quarters of England and wide areas of Wales remain in drought or severe drought status. According to estimates presented to the national drought group, regions need 120 percent of average rainfall through April to exit drought conditions. Despite recent precipitation from Storm Austen and Boelo, which created what was described as "weather whiplash", officials confirmed the overall drought outlook has not changed. Water Minister Emma Hardy highlighted the dual threat of simultaneous flooding and drought as hard, dry ground struggles to absorb rainwater.

Local residents in Haywards Heath have expressed frustration over water management. Lifelong residents Caroline Allan and Graham Ward, alongside long-time resident Delphine Hazeldone, raised concerns regarding infrastructure decay and expanding housing developments that strain existing resources. Increased demand from a growing population, data centres, and energy infrastructure, combined with unaddressed leaks and climate variability, has exacerbated water scarcity. Frustration is heightened by the nearby Weir Wood reservoir, located less than six miles away and currently 80 percent full. Owned by Southern Water, Weir Wood has been out of service since 2018 because its 1950s treatment facility reached the end of its operational lifespan.

Emergency plans created by South East Water during a drought in 2025 to connect the two reservoirs via an overland pipe were abandoned when rainfall resumed. South East Water is now studying the feasibility of a buried pipeline, while Southern Water plans to construct a replacement treatment facility by 2030. A spokesperson for Southern Water explained that a total rebuild was chosen to ensure long-term resilience, adding that even if the Weir Wood plant were currently functional, it would not resolve the shortage at Ardingly.
