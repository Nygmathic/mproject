Italian military personnel recently collected and disarmed a substantial collection of unexploded First World War weaponry that surfaced from rapidly shrinking glaciers in the Dolomites. The operation, conducted by the army's Julia Alpine brigade, followed reports from summer hikers near Canal San Bovo in the Primiero Valley. A broader sweep across high-altitude sites in the Trentino Alto Adige region led to the recovery and neutralization of 250 explosive devices, including 18 Italian 75mm artillery shells and 37 French-produced Thevetot mortar rounds supplied to Italian forces during the conflict.

These munitions originate from the white war, fought between Austro-Hungarian and Italian troops across frozen, dangerous terrain from 1915 to 1918. According to Stefano Morosini, a University of Bergamo professor who coordinates heritage projects at Stelvio national park, the emergence of these dangerous items is "very much a consequence of global heating." Morosini explained that rising temperatures cause a growing number of preserved explosives to surface at the end of each summer. Hikers and mountaineers are instructed to never touch any uncovered explosives and to report discoveries immediately to the police.

Accelerated glacier retreat over the last decade has progressively uncovered long-frozen relics, a process that first began revealing weapons, sledges, letters, and diaries in the 1990s. In 2015, a wooden cave shelter on the 3,095-meter peak of Mount Scorluzzo reappeared after the surrounding ice melted. Troops had locked up the barracks when the war ended in November 1918, leaving behind straw beds, clothing, lanterns, newspapers, postcards, coins, and canned food. A dedicated white war museum is set to open in Bormio in June 2027 to display such artifacts.

The white war is believed to have killed over a million people, with most casualties resulting from hypothermia, avalanches, or mountain falls. Over the past 15 years, dozens of frozen bodies have emerged from the melting ice. In 2017, the family of an Italian soldier was able to conduct a burial after identifying documents were found alongside his body on the Presena glacier. Heatwaves at high altitudes are also making glacier travel increasingly unsafe. Morosini noted the stark impact of climate change in the region, pointing to a 2022 disaster on the Marmolada glacier where a massive ice collapse killed 11 people.
