British agricultural producers are warning of escalating financial and environmental strains that threaten the country's food supply. According to Jamie Burrows, a Norfolk arable farmer and chair of the National Farmers' Union crops board, recent heatwaves and drought during the hottest UK summer on record contributed to England’s lowest recorded cereal harvest. Simultaneously, operational costs have surged. Following the outbreak of conflict in Iran in late February, the price of red diesel used for agricultural machinery has nearly doubled, while prices for fertilisers and livestock feed have risen by approximately 20 percent over the same timeframe.

These agricultural pressures align with broader concerns raised by researchers, industry leaders, and the National Audit Office (NAO). On Monday, the parliamentary public accounts committee will begin hearings examining food supply resilience. The inquiry will review NAO findings highlighting deficiencies in emergency planning by the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs, which previously omitted key supply chain businesses from catastrophe exercises.

The UK currently produces around 60 percent of its own food, making it significantly more reliant on imports than many European peers. Expert analysis from Professor Tim Lang highlights that domestic distribution is heavily centralized, relying on 131 hubs to supply 12,300 retail stores, while the majority of food imports flow through just four major ports. Furthermore, after disbanding its post-World War II national food stockpile in the 1990s, Britain lacks the strategic reserves maintained by some Scandinavian nations.

Although the government is launching a public campaign urging citizens to store bottled water and nonperishable goods at home, critics argue official measures remain insufficient. A group of over 150 academics and food experts recently issued a letter accusing ministers of being "complacent" regarding emergency food planning. Professor Paul Behrens of the University of Oxford noted that the UK faces simultaneous risks from the climate crisis, El Niño weather patterns, energy disruptions, cyber-attacks, and growing water scarcity in key supply regions like the Mediterranean and North Africa. Meanwhile, some individuals, such as south Wales resident Ana, have independently taken up personal stockpiling to prepare their households for potential future disruptions.
