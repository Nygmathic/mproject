During a Tuesday evening rally in Lubbock, Texas, Donald Trump announced the termination of Biden-administration regulations that required oil and gas producers to limit methane leaks. Trump stated that rolling back these rules would lower consumer costs, save energy producers $45 billion, and release 210 million barrels of oil, an amount equal to roughly 10 days of U.S. petroleum consumption. He characterized the previous mandates as unnecessary burdens that had raised energy prices for smaller operators.

In a statement, Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Administrator Lee Zeldin supported the proposal to revise Clean Air Act rules, describing it as an effort to "end the war on oil and natural gas." The agency estimated the change would unlock 210 million barrels of crude oil alongside 1.41 trillion cubic feet of natural gas. Zeldin had previously noted in March 2025 that the agency was re-evaluating the regulations to promote domestic energy dominance. 

The policy shift targets the landmark 2024 methane standard, which established the first nationwide limits on methane and volatile organic compound emissions from existing fossil fuel infrastructure. That rule mandated that companies curb flaring, venting, and leaks, while also establishing a super-emitter initiative to track major release events identified by third-party sensors.

Health and environmental advocates strongly criticized the administration's plan. Darin Schroeder of the Clean Air Task Force stated that the EPA is failing its legal obligation under the Clean Air Act to safeguard public health and urged the agency to enforce the 2024 regulations. American Lung Association CEO Harold Wimmer warned that unchecked leaks release hazardous substances such as benzene, formaldehyde, and volatile organic compounds, worsening air quality for nearby communities. Furthermore, Environmental Defense Fund representative Rosalie Winn argued the administration is allowing polluters to profit while wasting resources, noting that operators have leaked, flared, or vented $7 billion worth of natural gas since the EPA halted rule enforcement in March 2025. Last year, the United States led the world in oil and gas sector methane emissions, a pollutant responsible for at least a quarter of global warming today.

Watchdog group Fieldnotes revealed through public records requests that oil and gas representatives met with the EPA at least 38 times during the administration's first year to push for revisions, despite some industry executives publicly supporting emissions rules. Fieldnotes researcher Julia Kane noted that these corporations received unprecedented access to shape policy. Additionally, Continental Resources had written to the EPA in April opposing prohibitions on routine gas flaring.
