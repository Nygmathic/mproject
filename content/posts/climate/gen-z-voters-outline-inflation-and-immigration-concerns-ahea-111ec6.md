As the 2026 United States midterm elections approach, young voters across the country are expressing heightened concerns about the nation's political trajectory. Data from the U.S. Census Bureau indicates that nearly 50 million Americans between the ages of 18 and 29 are eligible to participate, though approximately half remain civically disengaged or unregistered.

In responses shared with The Guardian, Generation Z readers highlighted several major issues influencing their political views. Key worries include escalating living costs, federal immigration enforcement, environmental degradation, election integrity, and the erosion of civil rights.

Economic strain is a major factor shaping voter engagement. In Ohio, 22-year-old library worker Charlie stressed the necessity of taxing the ultra-wealthy and advocating for universal healthcare to support working citizens facing severe wealth disparities. In Connecticut, a 25-year-old maintenance worker explained that working extensive hours to cover expenses such as rent, utilities, insurance, student loans, and medical bills limits the time available for civic participation. The worker also expressed frustration with candidates from both major parties, specifically criticizing Connecticut Governor Ned Lamont for favoring corporate interests over the working class.

Broader federal policies and administrative actions are also driving anxiety. Tyler, a 24-year-old church employee in Minnesota, pointed to rising inflation, immigration enforcement, and potential post-election subversion as key considerations. In Illinois, 28-year-old Emily cited artificial intelligence, federal propaganda, immigration enforcement, and the war in Gaza as vital issues, urging voters to support candidates with stronger integrity.

Environmental rollbacks and voting conditions worry voters in southern states. Matthew Doutt, a 26-year-old accountant in Tennessee, criticized the federal government's impact on climate defenses and noted that state partnerships with immigration enforcement could lead to federal agents appearing at polling places to intimidate voters in heavily gerrymandered districts.

Local political dynamics are also mobilizing first-time and student voters. Studying in Ireland, 18-year-old Texas native Niamh Catterall plans to cast a mail-in ballot, prioritizing candidate focus on minority protections, church-state separation, and state-level needs, praising candidates such as Gina Hinojosa and James Talarico. Nathan, a 21-year-old computer science student in Texas, highlighted LGBTQ+ rights and datacenter growth, noting that strong performances by candidates like Talarico bring hope for a "blue Texas" despite historical Democratic losses in southern elections.
