Beginning on Nov. 6, online pornography featuring strangulation, suffocation, real or simulated incest, and adults portraying children under 16 will become illegal across England and Wales. The forthcoming ban comes after ministers announced new legislation earlier this year in response to cross-party pressure.

Although non-fatal strangulation is already recognized as a crime, broadcasting or uploading depictions of it online has remained legal until now. Justice Minister Alex Davies-Jones explained that the target of the law is not harmless sexual preferences, but material that glorifies or normalizes acts of violence against women. She noted that adult content can shape views on healthy relationships—especially for young people—and cautioned that violent material bluring what is considered acceptable in real life.

The upcoming restrictions follow recommendations from a 2025 review into pornography regulation carried out by Conservative peer Gabby Bertin. The investigation pointed to a "total absence of government scrutiny" regarding the porn industry and warned that content depicting choked women leads to devastating real-world outcomes. It also criticized disturbing role-play scenarios involving incest and adults posing as minors. Welcoming the implementation, Lady Bertin stated that stronger oversight and enforcement will signal clearly to hosting services and the public that extreme content promoting harm is unacceptable.

The End Violence Against Women Coalition likewise supported the legislative change, calling it a vital move against the normalisation of violence toward women and girls following years of expert campaigning. Janaya Walker, the group's interim director, stressed that strangulation carries risks of fatal or permanent injury, adding that its visibility in adult material normalizes sexual violence among youth. Walker urged ministers to strictly enforce the ban and revise the Online Safety Act so that digital platforms are legally obligated to remove prohibited content.
