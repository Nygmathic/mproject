Payment giants Visa and Mastercard are facing a potential £5 billion group legal action in the United Kingdom over allegations of charging unfair transaction fees and undermining competition. Law firms Hausfeld and Harcus Parker have filed an application with the Competition Appeal Tribunal seeking compensation for UK organizations allegedly overcharged on card payments since October 2020. The claim focuses on multilateral interchange fees, which businesses pay through their banks whenever customers process transactions using credit, debit, or prepaid cards.

Advocates of the legal action contend that a lack of market competition leaves merchants with little choice but to pay excessive rates. The claim aims to recover fees collected over a six-year period alongside accrued interest, with sources close to the matter estimating its total value at roughly £5 billion. Former travel sector executive Stephen Allen, a British businessman who has targeted the card networks through group litigation since 2021, is leading the case. Financing is provided by Bench Walk Advisors, a litigation funding firm headquartered in London that was launched in 2017.

If the tribunal approves the application, around one million UK-based businesses, charities, and organizations with annual revenues under £100 million will be automatically enrolled in the claim unless they opt out, with potential payouts of several thousand pounds per entity. Larger companies would need to actively opt in to join, with potential recoveries reaching tens of millions of pounds. However, reaching a final resolution could take years. A separate US class action filed against the companies in 2011 over ATM access charges was settled only last year for $167.5 million combined.

Both payment firms strongly disputed the lawsuit's claims. A Mastercard spokesperson stated that interchange fees are essential for a fast, secure payment network and described the case as part of a trend toward "opportunistic funder-backed litigation" that fails to assist UK businesses. The spokesperson added that the claim is the exact type of lawsuit targeted by proposed government reforms regarding opt-out class actions. A Visa spokesperson similarly argued that interchange fees are critical to maintaining a secure digital ecosystem that benefits consumers, merchants, and banks.
