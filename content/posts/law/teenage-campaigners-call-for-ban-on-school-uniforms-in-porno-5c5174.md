Four teenage students aged 14 and 15 have launched a campaign demanding a complete ban on the depiction of school uniforms in pornography. Rose, Mae, Aylin, and Willow—members of a student group called the Little Feminist Book Club—argued that associating school attire with sexual acts normalizes the sexualization of children and puts young people at risk.

A law scheduled to take effect on November 6 will prohibit pornography depicting sexual activity involving individuals pretending to be under the age of 16. However, the campaigners noted that this legislation fails to outlaw depictions of adults dressed as older schoolchildren. The group seeks to eliminate this legal grey area, asserting that children's clothing should never be portrayed sexually regardless of the performer's actual age.

The issue has directly impacted the student organizers and their peers. Members of the group reported feeling uncomfortable wearing their school uniforms in public, with one student choosing to stop wearing skirts altogether. Mae recalled that her discomfort began at age 12 after watching a television show in which a character wore a school uniform for a sexual purpose. Due to privacy and safety concerns surrounding artificial intelligence, the campaigners chose not to wear uniforms when addressing a Parliament event hosted by Tools for Change in July.

Data cited by the group indicates that one in three girls has experienced sexual harassment while wearing a school uniform. Following the launch of their campaign and petition, the group received numerous messages from women detailing personal experiences with catcalling, daily harassment, and assault tied to their school attire.

The initiative has gained backing from several political figures. A local MP and an MSP are assisting the group, with plans to table an early day motion and deliver a handwritten letter to Prime Minister Andy Burnham. Meetings have also been scheduled with victims minister Alex Davies-Jones, Jess Asato MP, and peer Gabby Bertin. To expedite legal protection, the students' MP advised amending existing legislation rather than drafting a new law. The campaigners emphasized that adult leaders and lawmakers must now take responsibility for changing the law to keep children safe.
