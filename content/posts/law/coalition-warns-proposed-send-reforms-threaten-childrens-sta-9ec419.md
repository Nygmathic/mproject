A coalition of public figures, campaigners, and organization leaders has warned that proposed government reforms to special educational needs and disabilities (SEND) services threaten the statutory rights of children and young people. The proposed changes to SEND and education otherwise than at school (EOTAS) frameworks could significantly diminish existing legal entitlements, including enforceable education, health and care plans (EHCPs), parental choice of educational settings, and access to independent appeal processes.

The group highlights that the proposals impact multiple stages of support. These include qualifying criteria for statutory help, the content of EHCPs, who determines provision, school placement decisions, and how families can challenge inadequate rulings. Additionally, the reforms threaten the ability of children unable to attend traditional schools to secure alternative education. According to the signatories, these measures reverse the direction of the Children and Families Act 2014, which put children and families at the heart of decision-making.

While supporting broader goals for a more inclusive system—such as early identification of needs, improved teacher training, specialist expertise, and adequate staffing—the group stresses that these enhancements do not require families to surrender their legal protections. They argue that when educational provision is unavailable or unsuitable, legal rights serve as a crucial mechanism for securing proper education, asserting that system improvement promises cannot replace enforceable guarantees.

To support their stance, the signatories reference the 2019 education select committee SEND inquiry, which featured committee member Lucy Powell. That inquiry concluded the 2014 framework was sound if funding and implementation challenges were met, specifically cautioning the government against "weakening or watering down duties". Ministers are being urged to work alongside SEND organizations and families to retain legal protections, maintain individual provisions, boost system accountability, and preserve independent appeal options. The signatories include broadcaster Chris Packham, Disability Rights UK CEO Kamran Mallick, and National Education Union general secretary Daniel Kebede, alongside various academics, actors, and leaders of disability charities.
