Former Democratic Unionist Party leader Jeffrey Donaldson faces significant safety risks while serving a 15-year sentence for historical sexual crimes, according to a former prison governor. Donaldson received his sentence on Thursday following a conviction in June on 18 counts of sexual abuse, including rape, committed against two young females. His punishment includes 11 years for offences against one victim and four years for the second. Due to provisions regarding crimes committed prior to the Criminal Justice (Northern Ireland) Order 2008, he is eligible for a 50 percent reduction in time served behind bars. Consequently, he will spend half the period in prison and the remaining time on licence under community supervision.

The former Lagan Valley MP is currently held at Maghaberry Prison near Lisburn, an establishment housing approximately 1,600 prisoners. Speaking on BBC Radio Ulster's Talkback programme, former prison governor Ian Acheson stated that protecting Donaldson presents a complex security problem for correctional authorities. Acheson highlighted that sex offenders convicted of crimes against minors occupy the lowest tier of prison hierarchies and encounter severe hostility from other inmates. Furthermore, Donaldson faces potential risks linked to his past political leadership of the DUP and his prior service in the Ulster Defence Regiment.

Donaldson is reportedly being kept in Maghaberry's Moyola Unit, which generally accommodates inmates with medical conditions, to isolate him from physical attacks or verbal harassment. He spends up to 18 hours each day inside his cell, where he is believed to pass time reading library books. Acheson remarked that while Donaldson is entitled to safety, he should not receive preferential treatment. Acheson also argued that long-term solitary confinement would be inappropriate and potentially unlawful, suggesting that authorities might compulsorily relocate Donaldson to a facility in another UK jurisdiction.

In court, Judge Paul Ramsey characterized Donaldson's downfall as a "catastrophic descent into disgrace." Donaldson, who denied all allegations and is seeking an appeal, will also be placed on the Sex Offenders Register for life. Should his appeal fail, his term in custody will end in late 2033. His political career began in 1985 with the Ulster Unionist Party before he joined the DUP in 2003 and became its leader in 2021, a role he held until his charges were brought in March 2024. He was stripped of his knighthood by King Charles in July.
