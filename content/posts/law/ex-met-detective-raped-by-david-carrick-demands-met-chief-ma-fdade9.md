A former Metropolitan Police detective who was raped by fellow officer David Carrick has demanded the resignation of Met Commissioner Mark Rowley, describing her experience during legal proceedings as "like being raped all over again". Linda Williams, a 60-year-old resident of Hertfordshire who gave up her right to anonymity, recently resolved a civil lawsuit against the force for an undisclosed six-figure sum without the Met admitting liability.

Williams was assaulted by Carrick in 2004 but did not report the attack until 2021. She argued that the police force failed to shield her from the serial offender and created an environment where she felt unable to come forward. She stated that the legal process was deeply traumatic, leaving her feeling controlled and powerless, and drove her to consider suicide. Williams noted that despite her background as a sexual offences officer, the complete absence of support from the police means she would no longer report a rape if she were attacked today.

During the civil case, Williams stated that the Met's legal defense team questioned the severity of her post-traumatic stress disorder, using her high performance as an exemplary officer to argue that she showed no psychological trauma. Speaking to the BBC, she attributed 90 percent of her trauma to the Met and 10 percent to Carrick. She asserted that the assault has kept her from entering any relationships for more than two decades, destroying the future she had envisioned. Williams urged for Rowley's replacement and recommended establishing a dedicated support unit managed by sexual assault survivors.

Carrick, a 51-year-old former armed officer, is currently serving 37 life sentences after pleading guilty in 2022 and 2023 to 71 sexual offences, including 48 rapes targeting 12 women. In November, he was also convicted of molesting a child in the late 1980s and repeatedly abusing a former partner.

A spokesperson for the Met expressed deep regret, admitting that serious investigative flaws prevented the force from identifying Carrick's pattern of abusive behavior or taking misconduct action earlier. The Met stated that it aimed to handle Williams' claim quickly and compassionately to minimize distress, adding that Commissioner Rowley has previously met with her, praised her courage, and invited her to meet again to discuss the issues further.
