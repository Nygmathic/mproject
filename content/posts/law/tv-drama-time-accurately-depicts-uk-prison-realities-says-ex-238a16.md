Former prison officer and author Alex South has highlighted how the television drama *Time* reflects the harsh realities inside detention facilities in England and Wales. Writing about the show’s fictional young offender institution, HMYOI Alderwick, South states that the three-episode drama accurately captures an environment defined by severe noise, anger, and overcrowding.

The commentary addresses the broader context of the Sentencing Act 2026, which permits individuals serving standard determinate sentences to be released after spending a third of their sentence in custody. Designed to relieve the ongoing prison capacity crisis, the policy exempts individuals convicted of serious offenses such as murder, manslaughter, and rape. However, South points out that recent recall rates indicate early release provides only temporary relief. Additionally, reoffending accounts for 80 percent of all offending in England and Wales, demonstrating that the system rarely changes individuals for the better.

A significant operational hurdle highlighted in the commentary is the widespread culture of "keep-aparts," particularly in institutions housing young adults. Under this system, prisoners involved in gang or postcode conflicts are strictly separated. Because these individuals cannot share space during visits, gym sessions, or educational classes, staff must manage complex, staggered schedules. As a result, time out of cells for affected groups is cut from three or four hours daily to a single hour.

Systemic issues leave facilities severely understaffed and overstretched, with little access to libraries, training, or socialisation. A report published this year on children in custody found that young people in nearly every youth establishment were locked in their cells for up to 19 hours a day, while other prisoners remain locked up for as long as 23 hours daily. Echoing a concern raised by David Tennant’s character in the show, South stresses that keeping individuals isolated without constructive activities or conflict resolution training ultimately increases long-term violence and fails to prepare prisoners for life after release.
