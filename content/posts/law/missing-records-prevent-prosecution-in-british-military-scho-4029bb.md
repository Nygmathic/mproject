Two women who state they were sexually abused as 10-year-olds at a British military school in Bünde, Germany, during the 1980s cannot see their alleged abuser prosecuted because of missing military records. A joint investigation by The Guardian and openDemocracy revealed that absent employment and tax documents have prevented investigators from establishing legal jurisdiction over the suspect, a British citizen employed by the British army.

The victims, identified as Anne and Jane, remained silent for decades after the staff member allegedly warned them that their fathers would be removed from the military if the truth emerged. Both women, currently in their 50s, reported suffering severe trauma, including relationship struggles, sleep disturbances, and lasting emotional distress from the alleged abuse.

Five years ago, in 2018, Anne disclosed the allegations to the Truth Project, an initiative under the Independent Inquiry into Child Sexual Abuse in England and Wales, after learning the suspect was still alive and her father had retired. The case was transferred to the Royal Military Police, who searched for Jane to verify the claims. Detectives located Jane around the same time the two women reconnected on Facebook, prompting Jane to inform her parents about the abuse for the first time.

Although investigators obtained photos, witness accounts, and staff registers, military police concluded they lacked the authority to proceed with a United Kingdom prosecution. Under NATO regulations, nations can enforce domestic laws on overseas personnel, but authorities could not determine if the suspect was hired under a London Ministry of Defence contract due to destroyed school records and a lack of tax records with British or German authorities. Additionally, German legal time limits had expired, blocking prosecution in Germany. After the women raised concerns, a senior military officer apologized in writing, conceding that the case handling "fell below the professional standards" expected. A subsequent review by the newly created Defence Serious Crime Unit produced identical findings, leading the victims to state that the Ministry of Defence refuses to accept responsibility for their care.
