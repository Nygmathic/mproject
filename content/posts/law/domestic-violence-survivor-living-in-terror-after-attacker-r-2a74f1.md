A domestic violence survivor was left living in fear after her violent former partner was released from prison without an electronic monitoring tag, despite past probation breaches and assurances from police. Michelle, a 38-year-old mother of four, was forced to relocate her family to a static caravan, with relatives sleeping on her floor for protection. Her ex-partner was sentenced to 38 months in November 2024 for affray and assault after punching her and threatening her with a knife.

Although ordered to remain away from Michelle’s town following his initial release in February 2025, the man repeatedly violated his probation terms and was recalled to prison. Following his latest release on October 15, no tracking tag was placed on him, leading Michelle to state that authorities appeared to be waiting for something terrible to happen before offering help. After inquiries were submitted by The Guardian, a government source stated on Monday that the man had been fitted with GPS and alcohol monitors, though the source maintained there was "no causal link" between the media contact and the device installation.

Michelle’s situation mirrors broader systemic failures detailed in a report by the chief inspectors for probation and prisons. The report revealed that nearly half of offenders are not tagged promptly after leaving custody, with one in ten waiting up to five months. Additionally, private contractor Serco fulfilled its contractual targets simply by attempting to fit devices, even though a third of untagged offenders were not present at agreed addresses.

The case sparked criticism in parliament, where Liberal Democrat MP Vikki Slade questioned why domestic violence survivors were not receiving greater protection. Meanwhile, Mark Fairhurst, national chair of the prison officers' union POA, wrote to Justice Secretary Alex Norris suggesting that prison staff be trained to fit tags before inmates are freed. The Ministry of Justice defended its record by stating it is addressing an inherited system in crisis through the expansion of electronic tagging, while Serco noted that it reports all uncompleted fittings to the relevant authorities.
