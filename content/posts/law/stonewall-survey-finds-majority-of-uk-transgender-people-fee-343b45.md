A recent survey conducted by Stonewall reveals growing concerns among transgender people in the United Kingdom regarding social acceptance. While nearly half of the broader British LGBTQ+ population feels more accepted today than a decade ago, 58 percent of trans+ individuals—and 75 percent of trans women—report feeling less accepted. Furthermore, 80 percent of trans respondents expressed pessimism about the future of equality.

The study indicates that safety concerns are prompting many trans people to withdraw from public spaces. Almost half of trans respondents reported avoiding certain restaurants and bars, while 37 percent refrain from using single-sex spaces like toilets and changing rooms. Additionally, 29 percent avoid exercise classes or athletic teams. The survey also highlighted mental health challenges, with 32 percent of trans respondents and 22 percent of LGB respondents reporting that they have attempted suicide at some point in their lives.

The findings follow a landmark UK Supreme Court ruling establishing that sex in law refers to biological sex. Gender-critical campaigners viewed the decision as a clarification of the Equality Act that restores protections for women-only spaces without needing case-by-case justifications. However, Stonewall Chief Executive Simon Blake warned of negative real-world consequences, such as trans individuals avoiding medical care due to updated NHS guidance regarding hospital wards.

Speaking to The Guardian, Blake stated that Stonewall is not seeking to overturn the judicial decision. Instead, he called on the government and official watchdogs to monitor the ruling's consequences and intervene if harm occurs. He noted that discrimination on the basis of gender reassignment remains illegal under the law, and that Supreme Court judges insisted their decision should not be interpreted as a culture war victory.

Blake, who returned to lead Stonewall in 2024 following institutional departures from its workplace diversity initiative, is pursuing a less combative strategy to rebuild trust. Drawing on the approach of early figures like co-founder Ian McKellen, Blake emphasized patience and pragmatic dialogue, noting that "progress takes time" when working to secure protections and bridge societal divides.
