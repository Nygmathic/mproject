The International Criminal Court is continuing its investigative operations despite experiencing early financial and operational pressures from recently announced United States sanctions. Speaking to The Guardian, deputy prosecutor Nazhat Shameem Khan stated that while banking institutions are over-complying with Washington's measures, the tribunal has maintained its output without scaling back its capabilities.

The measures follow actions by US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who labeled the ICC a rogue court and granted a six-month grace period from the October 9 announcement for the tribunal to address American concerns. Rubio alleged that outside experts wanted the court to prosecute American citizens. However, Khan explained that no Americans have been brought before the tribunal, clarifying that the court's work in Afghanistan focuses on crimes against women and girls by the Taliban and Islamic State Khorasan. This month, four arrest warrants were unsealed against Taliban members for gender persecution.

The new US penalties target the institution as a whole, following earlier individual sanctions placed on 13 staff members in February 2025, including Khan. To protect itself, the court is replacing its Microsoft software with European options and discussing alternative banking and health insurance arrangements. Additionally, the court registrar is negotiating with the European Union regarding protective measures.

These external challenges coincide with leadership changes inside the prosecutor's office. Formal inquiries into sexual harassment allegations against former chief prosecutor Karim Khan began in late November 2024. He denied the allegations but was removed in July after a vote by member states. Nazhat Shameem Khan, who is not related to her predecessor, is currently co-running the office while awaiting the appointment of a new chief prosecutor.

Khan affirmed that court investigations remain fully active, reporting "significant progress" on the Gaza war file and other cases. Arrest warrants were issued in November 2024 for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and former defence minister Yoav Galant, with further applications currently kept secret by judicial order. The court continues to receive international backing, recently receiving the Sakharov prize for freedom of thought from the European Parliament alongside funding pledges from member countries.
