Justice Secretary Alex Norris has stated that the court backlog in England and Wales will still decline despite the government scrapping plans to restrict jury trials. Speaking to the BBC, Norris explained that introducing judge-only trials would have been complicated to implement and would have slowed down wider justice reforms. Prime Minister Andy Burnham reversed the policy, which had previously been proposed under Sir Keir Starmer’s administration to allow single judges to decide cases carrying sentences of under three years.

Under the revised approach announced by the Ministry of Justice, judge-only trials will be limited strictly to complex financial and fraud cases. Thousands of medium-level criminal cases will instead stay before volunteer magistrates. The backlog of court cases currently stands at almost 81,000, driven by previous funding cuts and national lockdown closures. Although official forecasts suggested the backlog could reach 100,000 before the next general election and potentially 200,000 by 2035, recent government funding to hire more judges for unused courts has shown signs of stabilizing the figures. Norris affirmed that the backlog would begin to decrease during the current Parliament and drop significantly over the coming decade.

The decision to protect jury trials was welcomed across the legal sector and by opposition parties. Andrew Thomas KC, chairman of the Criminal Bar Association, praised the retreat, remarking that "Justice needs juries." However, Thomas raised alarms regarding separate proposals that would remove a defendant's right to choose a Crown Court trial and double magistrates' sentencing authority to two years. He warned this change would redirect approximately 25,000 cases annually to magistrates' courts, including serious matters such as sexual assault, child abuse, and grievous bodily harm, which volunteer magistrates are ill-equipped to handle due to existing shortages and the complexity of multi-day proceedings.

Opposition politicians, including shadow justice secretary Nick Timothy, Liberal Democrat spokeswoman Jess Brown-Fully, and Green MP Sian Berry, celebrated the reversal as a victory for the justice system. Labour MP Karl Turner, who had previously been suspended from his party for opposing the original reforms, expressed gratitude for the Prime Minister's support. Meanwhile, Claire Waxman, the victims' commissioner for England and Wales, cautioned that victims were being asked to rely on an approach that ministers had previously indicated would not be sufficient on its own.
