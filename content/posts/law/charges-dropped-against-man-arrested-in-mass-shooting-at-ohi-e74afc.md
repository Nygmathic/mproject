Prosecution authorities in Ohio have dismissed all criminal charges against a 20-year-old man arrested in connection with a festival shooting in Toledo that injured 12 people. Eljay Crisp-Carr was released after spending approximately five months in custody. He had originally faced over 50 felony counts, including 25 charges of attempted murder, linked to the June 6 incident at the Old West End festival. During a Friday court hearing, Judge Lori Olender of the Lucas County Court of Common Pleas approved the prosecution's request to drop the charges.

Crisp-Carr’s legal representatives informed local news station WTVG that he was not present at the event when the shooting occurred, citing video footage and mobile phone records supporting his location. After the charges were dropped, Crisp-Carr told WTVG that "God is good" and maintained his innocence, explaining that law enforcement had mistakenly identified him from a photograph. His mother, April Carr, expressed delight to news outlet WKYC regarding his release. However, defense lawyer Alex Bodiford warned WKYC that the actual perpetrator remains uncaptured and could still present a danger.

In a statement issued Friday morning, the Lucas County Prosecutor’s Office clarified that the case was dismissed without prejudice, leaving open the option to refile charges if necessary. The office noted that newly emerging evidence appeared to support the defense, adding that discovery information was shared with Crisp-Carr's attorney. Officials emphasized that the Toledo Police Department’s investigation remains active and that authorities intend to present their case in a courtroom rather than through public opinion.

The violence at the annual festival, held in a historic district of Toledo along Lake Erie, began when a dispute between competing groups escalated into an exchange of gunfire between two individuals, neither of whom suffered injuries. Event organizers called off the second day of the festival following the gunfire.
