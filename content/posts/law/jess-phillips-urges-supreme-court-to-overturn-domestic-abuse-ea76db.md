Labour MP Jess Phillips has called on the UK Supreme Court to reverse a decision that prevents a domestic abuse survivor from bringing legal action against Sussex Police and the Crown Prosecution Service (CPS). The case, which the highest court is set to examine on Wednesday and Thursday, centers on the accidental disclosure of the victim's residential address to her violent former partner.

The survivor, identified in court documents as XGY, was forced to move after a CPS lawyer revealed her location during a magistrates court proceeding. The hearing was intended to obtain an order barring her ex-partner—who was accused of rape, death threats, and threats of an acid attack—from entering her street. Sussex Police had failed to mark her address as confidential before passing the case file to prosecutors. Last year, the Court of Appeal ruled that the police and CPS were protected by core immunity, extending this protection to investigative work outside the courtroom.

Phillips, a former minister for safeguarding and violence against women and girls, argued that sustaining the appeal court's ruling would prevent law enforcement from being held accountable for systemic failures. She emphasized that civil claims have historically forced necessary reforms in handling cases of domestic abuse and sexual violence. The victim, XGY, stated that she still experiences the impact and loss of safety more than six years later, stressing that survivors must be able to rely on authorities to keep sensitive information safe.

In response to the Court of Appeal decision, Phillips, alongside fellow parliamentarians David Davis and Andrew Mitchell, has introduced a bill amendment to overturn the legal precedent if the Supreme Court does not. Phillips warned that granting broad legal immunity harms public confidence in law enforcement, citing previous issues such as the murder of Sarah Everard and misconduct at the Charing Cross police station. Sussex Police and the CPS were contacted for comment.
