A federal judge has ruled that the top United States prosecutor in southern California was "unlawfully serving" in his post and must be disqualified from managing two active criminal cases. Judge Michael Seabright determined on Tuesday that Bill Essayli cannot continue to supervise or take part in those proceedings following legal challenges raised by defense attorneys. However, the directive will not take effect right away, as the Department of Justice announced its plan to appeal the decision.

The decision represents the latest development in a prolonged court dispute surrounding efforts by the Trump administration to retain Essayli as head of the federal prosecutor's office without obtaining approval from the United States Senate. Essayli, a former Republican state legislator and ally of Donald Trump, originally assumed an interim leadership role in April 2025. Because he was never officially nominated by the president, he avoided the traditional Senate confirmation process. After his initial interim term expired in July, officials assigned him an acting title and later designated him as a first assistant and special attorney. Tuesday's ruling cited an August decision by a federal appellate court involving a similar situation with a Nevada justice department official to confirm that Essayli was still holding his post unlawfully.

Responding to the decision, Essayli released a statement expressing respectful disagreement with the judge's conclusion. He argued that the president and the attorney general possess full authority to designate supervisory personnel within the Justice Department, adding that he anticipates remaining in his position until the United States Supreme Court ultimately resolves the dispute. Conversely, defense attorney H Dean Steward, who brought the challenge against Essayli, commended the ruling in comments to the LA Times, describing it as a victory for constitutional law and accusing the administration of repeatedly violating appointment statutes.

Essayli is not the only official to face such a disqualification; in December, former personal attorney to Trump Alina Habba was removed from her role as top prosecutor in New Jersey, though she subsequently remained at the department as a senior adviser. Over the past year, Essayli has drawn attention for pursuing cases aligned with the administration's political priorities, including aggressive deportation efforts against immigrants and the prosecution of protesters. Most recently, he initiated a voter fraud drive alleging that thousands of registered California voters are likely non-citizens and calling for their ballots to be excluded, a move strongly contested by state election representatives.
