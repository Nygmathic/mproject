Dapo Akande, a prominent British legal scholar, has been elected to the United Nations International Court of Justice, marking a significant diplomatic success for the United Kingdom. His election comes nearly ten years after the UK lost its seat on the panel. Akande will serve a minimum term of nine years as one of the tribunal's 15 judges.

Originally qualifying as a lawyer in Nigeria, Akande is a public international law professor, a fellow at All Souls College, Oxford, and a member of the UN International Law Commission. He has extensive experience acting as a counsel, adviser, and expert before international legal bodies, including the ICJ. Reacting to the outcome, Akande expressed gratitude to the states that supported him, promising to decide cases independently and impartially while contributing to the peaceful resolution of international disputes.

The UK previously experienced a major diplomatic defeat in 2017 when Sir Christopher Greenwood unexpectedly lost his seat to an Indian candidate, leaving the court without a British judge for the first time. That loss raised questions about the UK's standing at the UN and its compliance with advisory opinions. A year prior, in 2016, the UN General Assembly voted 94 to 15 to refer the disputed status of the UK-controlled Chagos Islands in the Indian Ocean to the ICJ. The court subsequently ruled in favor of Mauritius. Although the current Labour government negotiated a settlement following talks begun under the previous Conservative administration, the legal approval process in the UK parliament is currently stalled due to opposition from the Trump administration.

The ICJ handles conflicts between states and has recently attracted global attention for its opinions concerning Israel's responsibilities to the population in Gaza as an occupying power. UK Lord Chancellor Alex Norris, who campaigned extensively for Akande, commended the new judge for bringing the "independence, legal rigour, judgment and breadth of perspective" required for the role. Norris reaffirmed the UK's support for the ICJ, noting its acceptance of compulsory jurisdiction and encouraging other nations to do the same.
