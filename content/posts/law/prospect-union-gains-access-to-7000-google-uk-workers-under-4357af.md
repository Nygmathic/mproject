Under newly enacted UK labor laws, the trade union Prospect has reached a deal to recruit and organize staff across Google UK. The agreement, finalized on Monday, grants the union formal access to approximately 7,000 employees across Google’s domestic operations, including YouTube, while excluding Google DeepMind. Prospect will now be permitted to hold sessions inside Google facilities, such as its headquarters in King’s Cross, London, to communicate with non-unionized staff and encourage them to sign up.

This development follows a similar pact Prospect established with Meta, the parent company of Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp, which employs roughly 5,000 people in the UK. Meanwhile, the Communication Workers Union is pursuing a separate recognition agreement with Google DeepMind. In the past, labor groups encountered significant barriers when attempting to organize tech employees, often being forced to reach workers from outside business premises. This was demonstrated two years ago when the GMB union failed in its attempt to gain recognition at an Amazon warehouse near Coventry.

Prospect, which represents more than 160,000 workers across multiple UK sectors, stated that the new agreement signals a growing union presence in major technology corporations. Mike Clancy, the general secretary of Prospect, stated that the deal shows labor groups can successfully bargain with major tech firms, giving employees a stronger voice on matters such as pay, flexible working arrangements, corporate culture, and artificial intelligence deployment. Google explained that it signed the access agreement to comply with updated national legislation, while noting that it maintains strong internal worker relations and plans to preserve direct dialogue alongside existing employee representation groups.

The access rights stem from the Employment Rights Act, which permits unions to enter workplaces or message employees electronically to arrange meetings. Additionally, beginning in January, employers will be required to inform their workforce of the right to join a union. TUC general secretary Paul Nowak recently noted that he anticipates a broader surge of private sector union agreements over the coming year as these "transformative" workplace access regulations come into force.
