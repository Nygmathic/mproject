South Carolina State Representative Brandon Guffey has turned a personal tragedy into a legislative movement for child online safety after his 17-year-old son, Gavin, died by suicide following an online sextortion scheme.

The event took place in July 2022 in Rock Hill, South Carolina, while Guffey was running for the state House of Representatives. Gavin was targeted on Instagram by an account under the name BellaJanet28, which pretended to be a local college student. After participating in a game of truth or dare, Gavin sent intimate photos to the account. The extortionist then demanded money, threatening to distribute the images to all of Gavin's online contacts. Shortly after receiving the threats, Gavin shot himself using a gun his father had left on a shelf after a camping trip.

Following the tragedy, Guffey pushed for legislative changes to combat online abuse. One year later, South Carolina passed Gavin's Law, which classified sextortion as a felony and made education on the threat mandatory in schools. Guffey later advanced further regulations requiring social media companies to implement safety features for users under 16. These measures mandate parental consent to set up accounts and prohibit direct marketing, automatic video playback, and the feature known as "infinite scroll."

Guffey also established Less Than 3, a non-profit organization that advocates for youth protection and assists affected families. He regularly discusses his son's story at schools, churches, and community gatherings, and has authored a book titled *Tomorrow Needs You*. In the book, Guffey openly reflects on his initial feelings of failure, his upbringing, and his changing perspective on mental health. After learning how quickly impulsive decisions to commit suicide occur, Guffey disposed of his firearms.
