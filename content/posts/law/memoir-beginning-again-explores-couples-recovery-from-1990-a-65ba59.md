Thomas Trezise, a professor who served as an assistant professor of French at Princeton in July 1990, has authored a memoir titled *Beginning Again* that details his experience after his wife, Susan Brison, was violently attacked and nearly murdered.

The assault occurred on July 4, 1990, while the couple was visiting a colleague’s remote farmhouse in rural France. Brison, then an assistant professor of philosophy at Dartmouth, went for a walk and was ambushed near a dirt road junction. Her attacker seized her throat from behind, moved her into a muddy ravine, repeatedly struck and strangled her, and smashed her with a rock. Brison ultimately survived by pretending to be dead. The attack left her severely injured, resulting in a fractured trachea, massive facial swelling, and extensive bruising.

After Brison failed to return, a colleague alerted Trezise, and she was transported by ambulance to a hospital in Grenoble. During her 11-day hospital stay, Trezise remained at her side, acting as her guard, voice, and advocate while she was mostly unable to speak. Sedatives were prescribed to both of them. Throughout the ordeal, Trezise concealed his own distress to serve as "the strong, supportive spouse."

Brison began speaking publicly about sexual violence months after the assault, publishing an account in the New York Times in 1993 and later writing her book, *Aftermath*. She eventually urged Trezise to document his own experience. His resulting memoir incorporates the full, unedited police deposition that Brison spent eight hours dictating from her hospital bed. In the statement, Brison revealed that imagining her husband finding her body gave her the strength to endure the attack. Brison noted that reading her deposition within Trezise's book helped her appreciate the emotional toll the event took on him.

Trezise recalled struggling with feelings of complicity as a man and wanting to avoid claiming victimhood when his wife's trauma was far worse. However, he emphasized that sexual violence causes widespread damage and represents a societal issue rather than strictly a woman's problem. Today, Trezise and Brison are tenured professors in their seventies and have an adult son.
