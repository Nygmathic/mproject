Stargazers and astronomy enthusiasts are gathering for the tenth anniversary of the Exmoor Dark Skies Festival, an annual event taking place from 16 October to 1 November. Set across the towns and villages surrounding Exmoor National Park, the two-week event features around 80 individual activities designed to showcase the area's clear, dark night skies. The schedule includes astronomy sessions, planetarium shows, educational talks, moonlit swimming, and guided bioluminescence walks.

The festival highlights the national park's official standing as a Dark Sky Reserve, a title awarded in 2011 by the International Astronomical Union to restrict light pollution and protect the public's right to view the stars. Festival organiser Julie Colman explained that the multi-week program brings together various activities celebrating the local skies, native wildlife, and elements of personal wellbeing. According to Colman, Exmoor experiences minimal light pollution, featuring a central zone where there is "no light at all" across a vast area. This environment allows observers to view clear constellations and the Milky Way, a quality demonstrated in photographs from previous festivals showing crisp star formations against dark backdrops.

In addition to promoting astronomy, the festival serves as a boost for local commerce. The National Park designed the event in 2017 to assist regional businesses as the standard tourism season comes to a close. Antony Brunt, manager of the Yarn Market Hotel in Dunster on the edge of Exmoor, noted that public awareness of the festival has expanded significantly, leading directly to increased bookings. His hotel organizes specialized trips led by a local astronomer and coordinates visits to various park venues and activities. Brunt stated that the event draws visitors from across the United Kingdom, including repeat guests who travel to the hotel twice a year specifically for stargazing weekends. Driven by this expanding interest, many of the festival's scheduled events sold out prior to its opening day.
