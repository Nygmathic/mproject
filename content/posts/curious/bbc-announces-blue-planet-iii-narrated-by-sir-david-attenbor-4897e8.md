Broadcaster Sir David Attenborough will narrate *Blue Planet III*, an upcoming six-part natural history series set to air on the BBC later this autumn. Produced by the BBC Natural History Unit based in Bristol, the project marks 25 years since the original show first premiered. The latest installment took nearly four years to shoot and explores poorly understood ocean environments, ranging from shallow coral reefs and tropical island ecosystems to the deepest regions of the sea.

To capture newly discovered animal behaviors, the production team utilized an array of advanced equipment. This technology included remote underwater cameras, aerial drones, and manned submersible vehicles capable of descending into remote marine habitats.

At a launch event on Tuesday at Piccadilly Circus in London, preview footage from the series was showcased across prominent digital screens. Attenborough, who turned 100 earlier this year, expressed pride in his involvement and noted his surprise at narrating a third series during his centennial year. In a newly released trailer, he described the marine environment as endlessly fascinating, promising that the episodes will highlight surprising and intelligent sea life while emphasizing the critical relationship between humans and the ocean.

The project includes an original score composed by Hans Zimmer alongside Bleeding Fingers Music. Zimmer, who previously wrote the music for *Blue Planet II*, described the franchise as the most significant work of his career, stating that it inspires viewers to protect the planet by showcasing its natural beauty rather than lecturing them.

The series also features an original song titled Into The Deep (Seas Cry), co-written by Jade Thirlwall, a member of the pop group Little Mix. Thirlwall, a long-time admirer of the franchise who referred to Attenborough as "a true hero," expressed deep gratitude for being invited to participate in the production.
