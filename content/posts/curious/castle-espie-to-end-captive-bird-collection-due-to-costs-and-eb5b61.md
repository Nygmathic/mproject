Castle Espie wildlife reserve in County Down is shutting down its captive bird collection and guided educational tours as part of broader cost-saving measures by its managing charity, the Wildfowl and Wetlands Trust. Starting in November, all remaining captive birds at the reserve will be relocated to alternative trust locations across the UK.

Alex Lane, the trust's director of operations, explained that the number of captive birds at the site had dropped from about 500 to under 100. Speaking to BBC News NI, Lane attributed this decline to avian influenza and logistical challenges stemming from Brexit. Under the Windsor Framework, transporting birds from Great Britain to Northern Ireland involves increased bureaucracy, including mandatory health certificates and entry checks, which create substantial administrative and financial burdens.

Lane noted that the organization is dealing with a severe budget deficit and needs surplus funds to carry out its primary conservation goals. Despite the closure of the captive collection, Lane stated that the trust aims to clear space for wild species and re-establish the venue as a "hub for wetland nature conservation." Situated along Strangford Lough near Comber, the reserve contains diverse habitats such as freshwater lagoons and seasonally flooded terrain, providing shelter to native and globally threatened bird species.

The decision to stop providing guided educational tours was similarly driven by financial strains and a shortage of school bookings, which Lane suggested might be linked to rising travel costs for schools. The facility will, however, continue to allow self-guided school trips.

In comments to BBC News NI, Social Democratic and Labour Party politician Cara Hunter, who serves on the Stormont education committee, voiced disappointment regarding the cancellation of guided visits. She expressed concern that removing these structured educational experiences could hinder access to nature learning, particularly for schools with fewer financial resources. Northern Ireland's Department for Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs and the UK's Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs have both been approached for comment.
