Apple's new foldable smartphone, the $1,999 iPhone Duo, rapidly sold out following its release late last month. Chief Executive Officer John Ternus, who replaced Tim Cook in September, expressed enthusiasm regarding the public's initial reaction to the launch. However, Chief Operating Officer Sabhi Kahn admitted during an earnings call with financial analysts that the technology giant cannot predict when inventory will balance with ongoing consumer demand. Retailing for £1,999 in the UK, the device remains highly sought after despite reports from some buyers that their screens cracked along the inner fold. Apple previously pledged to replace affected devices, though executives did not address the issue during the recent financial update.

Despite these manufacturing hurdles, Apple reported record annual global sales for its iPhone line. During the three-month period ending September 26—prior to the release of the Duo—iPhone sales grew by 17 percent to $57 billion. The company's total annual revenue expanded by 15 percent to $479 billion. Quarterly sales for Mac computers also set a record, rising 27 percent to $10.7 billion. In contrast, revenue for iPads fell by 10 percent, while the wearables division, which encompasses items like headphones and the Apple Watch, declined by 7 percent.

Apple continues to navigate broader component bottlenecks that executive leadership expects to persist into next year. According to Kahn, production has been slowed by difficulties in sourcing enough microprocessors with advanced node technology, which are primarily manufactured in Taiwan by TSMC. These constraints have particularly restricted the availability of iPads and Mac computers. 

The company is also struggling with rising memory costs, driven by the expanding popularity of artificial intelligence platforms from developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic. The surge in AI chatbots has increased competition for data center space, raising memory storage prices for device makers. Cook previously characterized this sudden spike in data center expenses as a "thousand year flood." In response to these financial pressures, Apple has implemented price increases of approximately 20 percent across nearly all of its hardware line.
