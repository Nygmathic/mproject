The Met Office has issued an amber weather warning for severe winds as Storm Austen, the first named storm of the 2026/2027 season, approaches the United Kingdom. Driven across the Atlantic by a high-altitude jet stream reaching peak speeds around 200 mph, the developing system is expected to bring hazardous conditions to several regions. The amber alert—the second-highest tier issued by the forecaster—applies to Cornwall and parts of western Devon between 03:00 and 10:00 BST on Friday.

The storm is forecast to deepen as it moves near south-west England on Thursday night, with the strongest winds arriving early Friday. Coastal headlands in north and west Cornwall will face the strongest north-westerly gales, with gusts up to 80 mph along exposed coasts and up to 70 mph further inland. The Met Office indicates a high risk of "significant" disruption, warning that flying debris and large coastal waves create a danger to life. Power cuts, property damage, and delays across road, rail, air, and ferry networks are also expected.

As Storm Austen moves through the English Channel, damaging gusts exceeding 80 mph could hit the Channel Islands, which fall outside the Met Office warning system. Additionally, broader yellow wind warnings cover south Wales, the English Channel coast, and the rest of south-west England, where gusts between 50 and 70 mph are anticipated.

Heavy rain will accompany the storm across much of the country, bringing welcome moisture to southern regions currently experiencing severe drought. However, persistent downpours in southern and eastern Scotland have prompted a yellow rain warning, with 20 to 40 mm of rain expected widely and up to 80 mm in localized spots, raising flooding risks. Snow may also fall over Scottish mountains above an elevation of 600 meters.

Unsettled conditions are expected to persist after Storm Austen passes. Another low-pressure area propelled by the jet stream could bring further gales over the weekend, particularly to northern regions, with western Scotland potentially seeing gusts of 80 mph or more. Further low-pressure systems will maintain periods of wind and rain next week, though forecasters note that high pressure could eventually bring calmer conditions to the south by early November.
