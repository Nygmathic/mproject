On Tuesday night, SpaceX temporarily imposed speed restrictions on Ukrainian drones using its Starlink satellite internet network, capping connectivity at 100 mph (160 km/h). Drones traveling faster than this threshold lost their connections and became unusable, disrupting military missions and hindering Kyiv's defenses against incoming Russian strikes. The restriction made it nearly impossible to intercept jet-powered Shahed drones, which travel above 198 mph (319 km/h). Full connectivity was restored by Wednesday evening, following a night in which Russian attacks cut electricity to 600,000 people and hit Ukrainian military energy and communications facilities.

The satellite network, which relies on over 10,000 active low Earth orbit satellites, has become vital to Ukrainian military operations across air, land, and naval forces. According to George Barros from the Institute for the Study of War, Starlink functions as "the backbone of military communications in Ukraine," allowing drones, command centers, and artillery to operate as a unified real-time system. A Ukrainian drone reconnaissance soldier named Neo told the BBC that the ground terminals require no special preparation and can be deployed rapidly in the field. Compared to standard radio networks, Starlink provides longer-range connectivity for drones while offering greater resistance to electronic jamming.

Reactions to the temporary slowdown highlighted Ukraine's deep reliance on the system. Kurt Volker, a former US ambassador to NATO, told the BBC that cutting off access leads to more deaths and described the slowdown as a cynical decision. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky initially expressed hope during a London visit that the incident was a mistake, but later told the Financial Times that it was an intentional choice rather than a technical glitch.

In statements on X, SpaceX owner Elon Musk suggested that any operational limits on the military-grade Starshield service are determined by the US government and military. Musk added that commercial Starlink usage is restricted to defensive or civilian activities and cannot be used for offensive military actions. The US government permits Ukraine to use both commercial Starlink and military Starshield systems, while Moscow's access was cut off by Musk in February 2022. Although Starlink is unauthorized in Russia, Ukrainian officials have previously reported that Russian forces obtained terminals for use in occupied territory. SpaceX stated in 2024 that it deactivates terminals if confirmed to be used by unauthorized or sanctioned parties.
