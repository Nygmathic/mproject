The Metropolitan Police have announced that no further steps will be taken regarding a social media statement made by Sarah Helm, the wife of Jonathan Powell, who serves as the government's national security adviser. The law enforcement agency concluded its review after specialist officers examined a message she published on the social media platform X referencing the group Palestine Action. Following an assessment of the online post, police established that the published material failed to reach the threshold required for criminal prosecution.

Helm's statement was published on Thursday, 15 October, in direct response to an online discussion concerning the writer Sally Rooney. Helm wrote on X appealing for divine assistance for authors who support Palestine and Palestine Action. Her message followed public debate over Rooney's announcement that she planned to use royalties generated from her books to financially support the group. Rooney is a best-selling novelist whose published works include the novels *Normal People* and *Conversations with Friends*.

In light of Rooney's commitment to donate her book earnings to Palestine Action, her publisher, Faber, concluded that it could not sell her novels inside the United Kingdom. Consequently, booksellers across the UK will not be provided with supplies of her books. Palestine Action declares that its overarching objective is ending global participation in Israel's genocidal and apartheid regime. However, the movement was officially designated as a proscribed terrorist organization in July 2025. Expressing support for such a proscribed group is considered a criminal offense under the law and is punishable by sentences of up to 14 years in prison.

The Metropolitan Police originally acknowledged the situation on Friday, informing the public that authorities were made aware of the Thursday post and were assessing it to decide whether a formal police investigation might be required. By Saturday, specialist officers completed their review of the post and determined that it "did not meet the criminal threshold." The force subsequently confirmed that no further police action would be taken regarding the matter.
