Prominent technology entities are making major financial pledges as the artificial intelligence sector faces heightened scrutiny regarding its broader impact on society. Elon Musk announced on social media that he intends to grant a share of SpaceX to every child in the United States starting next year. Musk's pledge followed a decision by artificial intelligence firm Anthropic to dedicate 5 percent of its shares toward a fund designed to support the "public good". Anthropic's board could also choose to grant an extra 2 percent of shares to the fund, which will not be utilized for lobbying or political contributions.

Anthropic is preparing for a potential stock market listing that might value the business above $1 trillion, having recently reported a 45 percent quarterly revenue increase to $16.8 billion. Like other developers, it aims to show substantial profit potential despite spending billions on infrastructure and model development. Meanwhile, SpaceX established an initial public offering record in June by generating $85.7 billion. Specific mechanics concerning how SpaceX equity will be distributed to American youth have not been revealed.

These promises arrive amid rising concerns over artificial intelligence safety and widespread job losses. Research from the International Monetary Fund projected that AI will affect around 40 percent of jobs globally and up to 60 percent in advanced economies. A former Anthropic researcher, Jacob Coxon, told BBC News that industry workers are alarmed by the speed of development. Anthropic head Dario Amodei, OpenAI chief Sam Altman, and Musk have all backed proposals to slow technological progress, introduce regulation, and establish independent monitoring.

Government leaders are also addressing the risks associated with the technology. US President Donald Trump and major tech executives recently signed a commitment establishing corporate responsibility for safety, though Trump has consistently rejected passing legislative controls. The president subsequently named Jay Clayton, the director of national intelligence, to head an AI taskforce tasked with coordinating federal involvement with businesses, consumers, and religious organizations.
