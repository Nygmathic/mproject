Delhi is confronting a severe drop in air quality as winter sets in, pushing the capital's air quality index into the "very poor" category. On Wednesday morning, the index reached 377, driven by high concentrations of PM2.5 particles that clog human lungs. Prolonged exposure to such conditions presents serious health risks, particularly for children and older people. The sudden surge in pollution comes shortly after the city logged its lowest average October pollution levels in eight years. Experts attribute the change to dropping temperatures, light winds, and smoke carried from crop-residue burning in neighbouring Punjab and Haryana.

To address the deteriorating environment ahead of the Diwali festival, authorities invoked Stage II of the Graded Response Action Plan. This emergency framework mandates intensive road cleaning, increased parking fees to discourage private vehicle use, and extra public transit services, including 20 additional weekday train trips on the Delhi Metro. Officials have also directed government offices to stagger work schedules to ease traffic congestion. During severe pollution episodes, emergency measures can also include hybrid schooling, work-from-home options, and restrictions on certain vehicles.

With Diwali only days away, concerns exist that traditional fireworks could worsen the air quality further by emitting fine particles and harmful gases. On Monday, India's Supreme Court permitted the manufacture, sale, and use of certified green firecrackers in Delhi and surrounding regions for limited hours. The court specified that fireworks can only be set off from 06:00 to 07:00 and from 20:00 to 22:00 local time on November 7 and 8. It also prohibited fireworks within 300 metres of hospitals, care homes, schools, and silence zones.

Green firecrackers feature modified chemical formulations to reduce emissions and must display QR codes for verification. Acknowledging that illegal conventional fireworks had previously been sold under the guise of green alternatives, the Supreme Court directed police to patrol sales areas, conduct random QR-code checks, and confiscate non-compliant products. Air quality issues have also affected Mumbai, where the index hit 322 on October 24 before improving to 89 on Wednesday, while neighbouring Navi Mumbai recorded a reading of 118.
