Severe flooding triggered by Storm Adam has resulted in the deaths of at least four people and injured another in France. The storm inundated streets and caused major travel disruptions across the southern part of the country on Tuesday. Among those killed were two women, aged 86 and 26, who drowned in a cellar in the Bouches-du-Rhône region. In Corsica, floodwaters swept away a four-wheel-drive vehicle on Tuesday; one occupant managed to escape through a window, but a male passenger was later discovered dead. Authorities have not yet disclosed details concerning the fourth casualty.

Dozens of residents required emergency shelter in Mouriès and Saint-Martin-de-Crau, two towns located between Montpellier and Marseille, where officials confirmed one person was injured. Transport Minister Philippe Tabarot posted on X that train lines and roads throughout the area faced severe disruption. Commenting on the crisis, Jacques Witkowski, prefect of the Bouches-du-Rhône department, stated on Wednesday that "the water has risen to unprecedented levels" and affirmed that emergency personnel were extensively mobilized to handle the situation.

France is also preparing for additional severe weather as Storm Austen, a newly named Atlantic system, heads toward the northwestern part of the country. France's meteorological agency, Météo-France, placed five western departments—Côtes-d'Armor, Finistère, Morbihan, Ille-et-Vilaine, and Manche—under an orange-level wind warning effective from Thursday evening through Friday.

Prior to reaching France, Storm Adam caused severe flooding in eastern Spain, resulting in at least three deaths. Floodwaters isolated several towns along the Mediterranean coast, necessitating helicopter rescues for people trapped on rooftops. On Monday, members of the Military Emergencies Unit recovered a woman's body in the Valencian town of Oliva. Two other women died over the weekend, including one found on a beach in Sagunto and another swept away near Barcelona in Cabrera de Mar. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez visited the Valencia region on Tuesday and pledged national government resources for rebuilding, while the Valencian government authorized a €23.5 million (£20 million) aid package for victims.
