Surrey County Council has surpassed its initial tree planting goal by putting approximately 885,000 trees in the ground between 2021 and 2026, according to a recent progress report. The local authority originally aimed to plant 600,000 trees during that five-year span, but overall efforts resulted in more than 800,000 new plantings across the region. In the 2025/26 financial year alone, 116,359 trees were planted. Looking forward, the council has committed to reaching 1.2 million trees by 2030, an amount equivalent to one tree for every county resident.

Despite these figures, local representatives have raised concerns about whether the newly planted trees are surviving. Elmbridge Borough Council recently passed a motion requesting that the future East Surrey council revise its tree strategy to monitor survival rates. Officials noted that many trees planted within the borough die within a year because they do not receive proper aftercare. Raising doubts over the focus on raw totals, Councillor Katerina Lusk argued that there is "little point celebrating" the volume of planted trees if a substantial portion perishes a year later.

The findings were included in a broader report evaluating the performance of the council's climate change delivery programme over the past five years. The document stated that the authority is generally on target to achieve its 2030 net zero objectives, having successfully cut its carbon emissions by roughly 60%. A major accomplishment of the initiative involved assisting low-income households to lower their energy expenses and improve their living conditions. Upgrades including solar installations, insulation, and low-carbon heating measures were delivered to more than 1,438 low-income homes.

However, the report also outlined upcoming challenges in maintaining decarbonisation policies. The council noted that its environmental programme faced significant disruptions stemming from wider economic pressures, supply chain constraints, and external market conditions.
