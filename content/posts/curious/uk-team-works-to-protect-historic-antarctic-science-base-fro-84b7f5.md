A team of specialists from the United Kingdom is traveling 9,000 miles to Antarctica to safeguard a historic British science base endangered by global warming. The UK Antarctic Heritage Trust, a charity located in Cambridge, is currently in the second year of a three-year project aimed at protecting Port Lockroy. Located on Goudier Island, the site is more than 80 years old and houses a museum, a colony of over 1,000 gentoo penguins, and the southernmost post office in the world.

Escalating temperatures and severe weather along the rapidly warming Antarctic Peninsula have led to significant structural deterioration at the base. After initial assessments identified severe structural weaknesses, last year's personnel prepared the location for this season's interventions. The organization described the upcoming efforts as its "biggest ever conservation season" at the site. During a five-month stay at remote Base A, an eight-person group will install support beams and props to help the structures withstand heavier snowfall and increased precipitation, while also monitoring the native penguin population.

The crew working at Port Lockroy is led by former Royal Air Force member Carole McCullough. Her team includes conservation carpenters as well as specialists in museum operations and mountain rescue. Simultaneously, a separate field team composed of six experts will travel farther south to conduct the initial comprehensive assessment of Wordie House, another British heritage location. This secondary site is where one of the longest continuous climate records on the continent originated in 1947.

According to Camilla Nichol, the chief executive officer of the trust, the Antarctic Peninsula has become one of the fastest-warming areas on the planet since measurements began at Wordie House. Nichol stated that the charity observes the effects of these changes on local wildlife and historic sites every year, adding that the chosen specialists bring vital expertise, resilience, and enthusiasm to tackle the rising challenges of climate change.
