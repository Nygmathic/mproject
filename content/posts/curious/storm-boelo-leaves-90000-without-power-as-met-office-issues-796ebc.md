Clean-up operations are under way across parts of the United Kingdom following flooding, property damage, and widespread power cuts caused by Storm Boelo. The Energy Networks Association (ENA) reported that around 90,000 properties remained without power as of Monday. The storm, which followed Storm Austen, claimed two lives on Sunday after falling trees struck a car carrying a man in his 20s in north London and killed a 60-year-old man in Mid Kelton, Dumfries and Galloway.

The Met Office has issued a yellow warning for strong winds across eastern Northern Ireland and the western coastlines of Wales, England, and Scotland, effective from 18:00 on Monday to 03:00 on Tuesday. Forecasters advised people to prepare essentials like torches and batteries, warning that further power outages, travel disruptions, and coastal spray are likely. Rain moving across south-west England, Wales, and Northern Ireland on Monday morning will reach central, eastern, and northern England, along with most of Scotland, by lunchtime. Winds are expected to intensify overnight as low pressure passes near Northern Ireland and north-west Scotland, peaking on Tuesday. A second yellow wind warning is active from 03:00 to 23:59 on Tuesday for much of Scotland and parts of Northern Ireland and northern England.

Restoration efforts have faced severe weather obstacles. ENA chief executive Leo Slade told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme that the sector experienced "a tough weekend" as continuous winds of 50 to 85 mph hampered engineers from replacing damaged power infrastructure safely. Slade noted that while the national grid is resilient, further debate is required regarding future investments to address climate-change-driven storms.

Transport disruptions continue, while several Scottish schools closed due to structural damage or missing heating, power, and water. Business owners have also reported severe losses. Marcus Reed, an operations director at a fragrance manufacturer in Lyndal, Cumbria, stated that power loss would shut his facility until Tuesday evening during his busiest pre-Christmas period. In Largs, North Ayrshire, flooded shop owner Wendy McKinney criticized the council's sandbag response. North Ayrshire Council told the BBC that it handed out nearly 300 sandbags in Largs and Saltcoats, followed established resilience protocols, and will review flooding reports to learn lessons.
