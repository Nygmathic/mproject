A biologist who once tracked migrating birds across Europe has welcomed upcoming legislation banning lead ammunition, warning that the public remains largely unaware of how severely the toxic material harms wildlife. Sacha Dench, a Chagford resident born in Australia, earned the nickname "The Human Swan" after flying a paramotor for 4,300 miles (7,000 km) from Arctic Russia to the UK alongside Bewick's swans during a 2019 expedition.

Dench explained that lead toxicity presents a critical danger to wild birds across the continent. Every discharged shotgun cartridge can disperse as many as 300 miniature lead pellets into natural habitats, resulting in an estimated 7,000 to 13,000 tonnes of lead being released into the UK environment annually. Water birds frequently ingest these metallic bits after confusing them with food or grit, breaking them down inside their stomachs. This internal breakdown causes fatal poisoning, killing up to 100,000 UK water birds each year and around one million throughout Europe, which threatens several species with extinction.

In response to long-standing warnings from scientists and conservation groups, laws prohibiting the sale and use of lead shot across England, Wales, and Scotland are scheduled to take effect in April 2029. Some individuals have already made the transition. Sharif Adams, who oversees deer management in Devon's Teign Valley woodland and serves as chairman of the British Deer Society's South West branch, said switching to non-toxic ammunition made sense for his conservation-centered work. Adams added that game merchants who distribute venison to the human food market are increasingly demanding deer harvested with non-lead alternatives.

Dench's advocacy group, the Lead Ammunition Collective, was recently honored with the RSPB Medal, the charity's top award, which was received by Dr Ruth Cromie and Prof Debbie Pain at the organization's annual general meeting earlier this month. Dench observed that the distinction—previously conferred upon David Attenborough and the King—celebrates an extraordinarily broad coalition of contributors who united to achieve meaningful environmental change.
