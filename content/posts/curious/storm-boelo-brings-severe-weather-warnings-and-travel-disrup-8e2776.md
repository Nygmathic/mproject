Scotland is preparing for severe disruption as Storm Boelo, the second named storm to hit within two days, brings powerful winds and heavy rainfall across the country. Official weather warnings have been issued while transport operators have already cancelled multiple services. The Met Office has placed most of the nation under a yellow weather warning starting at midday on Saturday, followed by an amber alert running for 24 hours from 21:00 on Saturday until 21:00 on Sunday across the mainland and Western Isles. On Friday, the Scottish government’s resilience room met to coordinate severe weather preparations.

Forecasters anticipate widespread gusts of 60 mph to 70 mph, reaching up to 80 mph in exposed coastal regions. The strongest winds will initially affect western Scotland and the far north of Northern Ireland on Saturday evening before moving eastwards overnight and throughout Sunday. Alongside heavy rain, the severe conditions bring risks of flying debris posing a danger to life, power outages, damaged buildings and trees, and impacted mobile phone coverage. Meteorologists noted that while the 24-hour amber warning is not unprecedented, its long duration over such a broad geographical region is unusual.

Public transport networks face widespread disruption. ScotRail confirmed service cancellations on key routes, including lines between Glasgow and Ayr, Glasgow Queen Street to Oban and Mallaig, and Inverness to Kyle of Lochalsh and Wick or Thurso. Network Rail stated that remaining trains will run at reduced speeds. Ferry operator CalMac has cancelled 14 of its 30 west coast services, with some suspended until Monday and all other routes facing disruption. Furthermore, Police Scotland urged drivers to consider whether journeys are necessary, warning that stopping distances can double on wet roads and spray drastically reduces driver visibility.

Safety organizations and local authorities have also issued warnings and altered plans. The Royal National Lifeboat Institution advised "extreme caution" near shorelines, warning that waves could knock people off their feet or wash them into the sea. Community gatherings have been heavily affected, including the cancellation of Saturday's activities at Paisley's Halloween festival and the Enchanted Forest event in Pitlochry. A Sunday family fun day at Dean Park Castle and Country Park in Kilmarnock has been postponed, while a Friday Scottish Cup match between Penicuik Athletic and Clyde was among the first sporting delays. Western Isles Council also advised organisers of Sunday events and church services to plan carefully for strong winds.
