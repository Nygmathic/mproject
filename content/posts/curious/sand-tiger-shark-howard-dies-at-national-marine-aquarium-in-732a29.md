A female sand tiger shark that had been living at the National Marine Aquarium in Plymouth since the facility first opened nearly 30 years ago has passed away. The shark, known formally as Howard and also called Howie, was put to sleep by caretakers after failing to show improvement following medical intervention.

According to staff at the National Marine Aquarium, team members originally decided to provide medical treatment after observing distinct changes in Howard's daily behavior and her overall physical condition. However, when the shark did not respond positively to the medical care, the staff made the decision to end her life. She was estimated to be almost 30 years old.

During her time at the Plymouth aquarium, Howard was housed in more than one area of the facility. She originally resided within the Great Barrier Reef habitat before later being relocated to the Atlantic Ocean habitat. Over the course of her stay, she grew to a final length of nine feet, which measures 2.7 meters.

Members of the aquarium team emphasized the significant educational impact Howard had throughout her decades at the site. The staff explained that she served as an "incredible ambassador" for her species, playing a key role in helping to clear up common myths surrounding sand tiger sharks, which are recognized as a critically endangered species.

Aquarium personnel expressed deep sadness regarding the loss of such a major presence from the venue. In addition to sharing their grief, the staff released a statement expressing immense gratitude to their valued visitors for showing support and understanding during this period.

The facility also reassured the public that other sand tiger sharks remain at the aquarium for guests to see. Two remaining sand tigers, named Ushaka and Mandela, can still be observed by visitors inside the Atlantic Ocean habitat.
