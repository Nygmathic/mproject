The Welsh government has announced plans to prohibit traditional steel lattice pylons for new power lines over which it holds regulatory authority. Plaid Cymru ministers favor placing electrical cables underground instead, fulfilling a commitment made in the party's manifesto ahead of May's Senedd election to restrict large steel pylons on power lines rated up to 132 kilovolts.

A public consultation regarding the policy was launched on Friday and will run until January 15, 2027. Government officials stated that burying electricity lines could protect local landscapes and amenities by minimizing visual impact. Buried infrastructure could also reduce risks to birds and wildlife while leaving power networks less vulnerable to severe weather. However, the consultation document acknowledges that undergrounding generally entails higher installation and maintenance costs, as well as potential construction disruptions. Under the proposed rules, overhead cables supported by wooden poles or similar, less intrusive infrastructure would remain permissible if specific planning criteria are satisfied.

The government initiative comes amid significant local resistance to proposed power line infrastructure, particularly in the Towy Valley. Last year, legal action was brought against Green Gen Cymru by groups representing hundreds of landowners. Green Gen Cymru, alongside sister firm Bute Energy, planned to invest nearly £1bn by 2030 to build three major transmission routes linking planned onshore wind farms to the broader electricity grid across Carmarthenshire, Ceredigion, Powys, and Shropshire.

In an effort to address community concerns, Green Gen unveiled a compact 22-meter pylon designed "with the Welsh landscape in mind". However, the developer maintains that placing cables underground costs an average of 4.5 times more than overhead options. According to the company, burying the lines for its Towy Usk project between Carmarthenshire and Powys would require an additional £500m. Welsh Energy Minister Adam Price stated that while new infrastructure is necessary to accommodate changing energy generation in Wales, the government wants developments that are sensitively designed to minimize local and environmental impacts while providing maximum benefits to the country.
