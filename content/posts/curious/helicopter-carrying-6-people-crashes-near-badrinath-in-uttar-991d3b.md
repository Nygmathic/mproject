Rescue teams in the northern Indian state of Uttarakhand are searching for survivors following a helicopter crash involving six individuals, five of whom were pilgrims. Uttarakhand Chief Minister Pushkar Singh Dhami confirmed that the aircraft went down in a mountainous zone near a village in Chamoli district, close to Joshimath. Emergency responders were dispatched to the site, though the condition of those on board remains uncertain.

The aircraft had taken off on Friday afternoon from Badrinath, a prominent Hindu pilgrimage location, before losing contact. Although the cause of the disaster has not been determined, the event occurred during adverse weather conditions. India's weather department had previously issued an "orange alert" for the district, warning of heavy rain, limited visibility, thunderstorms, hail, and high winds.

Dhami stated that a combined rescue effort is underway involving the district administration, the disaster management department, and the State Disaster Response Force. The Chief Minister noted that he is actively keeping track of the rescue efforts and has commanded that any individuals with severe injuries be airlifted for medical care.

Uttarakhand sits in the Himalayas along the borders with China and Nepal. The state draws millions of devotees each year to its holy Hindu shrines, including the Char Dham pilgrimage locations of Badrinath, Kedarnath, Gangotri, and Yamunotri. Due to harsh winter conditions, access to these remote temple sites in steep mountain valleys is restricted to only part of the year.

Helicopter transport has grown in popularity among visitors seeking to avoid difficult journeys through the mountains. However, a series of fatal accidents has drawn increased attention to flight safety. In June 2025, seven individuals were killed in a helicopter crash near Kedarnath, matching an October 2022 disaster on the same route that also resulted in seven fatalities. After several additional helicopter crashes were recorded during the 2025 pilgrimage period, aviation regulators imposed stricter safety checks and limited certain flight operations.
