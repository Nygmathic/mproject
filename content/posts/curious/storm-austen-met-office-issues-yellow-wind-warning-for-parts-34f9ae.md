The Met Office has announced that Storm Austen, the first named storm of the autumn, is expected to bring heavy rain and severe gales to parts of the UK starting late Thursday night. The weather system, forming as a rapidly deepening low-pressure area over the Atlantic, will advance towards the country over the coming days. In preparation for the severe conditions, weather forecasters have issued a yellow warning for wind that comes into effect at midnight on Friday.

The Met Office yellow wind warning covers south Wales, south-west England, and southern coastal counties, remaining active until 12:00 BST on Friday. Peak wind gusts could reach 70 mph within the warning zone, while wider regions across west Wales and southern England are expected to see speeds between 50 and 60 mph. Forecasters warn that these strong winds are likely to disrupt travel, cause power cuts, and lead to structural damage on buildings.

Leading up to the storm, the UK will face heavy rainfall over the next few days, alongside increasing winds around southern England and west Wales on Wednesday. Intensifying rainfall will move across England and Wales throughout Thursday night. Although the most severe winds will subside from the south-west later on Friday morning, much of England and Wales will experience ongoing blustery weather and periodic heavy rain. Conversely, Northern Ireland and Scotland are expected to avoid the majority of the impacts caused by Storm Austen.

Storm Austen is forecasted to move out into the North Sea later on Friday, granting a brief period of calm and clear skies overnight. However, another deep low-pressure system is set to arrive in time for the weekend. Heading towards Scotland, this second system is unlikely to be named as it is not anticipated to match Austen's severity. Nevertheless, gusts of 70 to 80 mph will hit western Scotland on Saturday, threatening ferry cancellations or delays and bringing restrictions to bridges. Elsewhere, Saturday will see widespread wind and heavy rain moving east, followed by a drier but persistently windy Sunday, especially toward the north-east of Scotland.
