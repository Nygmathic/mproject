More than 2,000 salon proprietors across the UK have submitted an open letter requesting that Chancellor John Healey reduce value-added tax on hair and beauty services from 20% to 10% in the upcoming Budget. Backed by the British Hair Consortium (BHC), the request stems from escalating operational costs, including higher wages, pension contributions, and National Insurance adjustments. The BHC argues that a tax reduction would enable businesses to preserve high street locations, train new personnel, and recruit more workers.

The industry has increasingly transitioned toward self-employment models where stylists rent chairs rather than holding direct employment. Research indicates that while chair rental provides flexibility, autonomy, and increased earnings, traditional employers argue it creates an "unlevel playing field" because standard salons absorb the expenses of educating future hairdressers. Marcus Perkins, who has operated M&M Spa in Quarry Bank near Brierley Hill for 22 years, noted that he has trained over a dozen young workers over the last two decades. This year, Perkins spent approximately £2,000 on travel and course fees to support 21-year-old stylist Trearna Hart.

According to findings from the Future of Hairdressing report, 53% of salon owners have cut back on apprentice training due to financial strain and concerns over inconsistent instruction standards. The BHC warned that apprenticeship numbers have already dropped significantly and could decline further without financial relief. Hart noted that many of her college peers faced difficulties entering the workforce and expressed concern over the diminishing presence of high street salons.

A Treasury spokesperson stated that hair and beauty establishments contribute significantly to high streets, jobs, and apprenticeships, adding that the industry has gained from reforms to business rates. Government ministers have also supported recent increases to minimum wage levels and employers' National Insurance payments, describing them as essential steps to raise pay, enhance public services, and stimulate economic expansion.

Despite these pressures, the sector remains a vital economic fixture. A report by the British Beauty Council revealed that hair and beauty sites comprise nearly a third of all leisure venues in UK retail centres and equal the combined number of pubs, bars, and cafes in smaller commercial areas. Clients also highlight their social value; Dorothy Senior, a customer at M&M Spa for two decades, emphasized that the salon provides a complete well-being experience beyond simple beauty treatments.
