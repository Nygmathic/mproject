The United Kingdom economy registered unexpected growth in August, expanding by 0.1 percent despite analysts anticipating a mild decline, according to data from the Office for National Statistics (ONS). Chancellor John Healey described the output as a "sign of resilience" during an interview with the BBC from the International Monetary Fund (IMF) annual meeting in Bangkok. Healey noted that the positive performance occurs amidst severe global economic disruptions.

The latest ONS report showed that the economy grew by 0.7 percent in the three months through August, following a 0.4 percent expansion in July and a revised 0.4 percent increase in June. August's performance was bolstered by growth in the production sector, which encompasses energy supply, manufacturing, and oil and gas extraction. However, the construction sector contracted, and the services industry experienced no change. ONS director of economic statistics Liz McKeown identified computer programming as the main contributor to quarterly growth, while acknowledging that construction and production shrank over the three-month timeframe.

Looking ahead to his first Budget in two weeks, Healey stated that he intends to grant financial relief to households and businesses, though he stressed that any new measures must remain strictly within existing government spending limits. Addressing technology's role, Healey noted that artificial intelligence is starting to influence economic productivity. PwC senior economist Jake Finney observed that AI infrastructure investment is aiding British economic performance, placing the country ahead of many European peers, though behind the United States.

Independent commentators and political figures offered contrasting views on the economic outlook. Capital Economics chief UK economist Paul Dales warned that expected inflation increases could slow future growth, even though the economy has resisted elevated energy prices and borrowing costs better than expected. IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva noted that Britain is showing slightly improved growth prospects compared to other G7 nations. However, Shadow Chancellor Andrew Griffith criticized the figures, emphasizing four straight months of construction declines and calling for reduced red tape and lower taxes. Liberal Democrat Treasury spokesperson Daisy Cooper also dismissed the 0.1 percent gain, stating that families continue to grapple with living costs while economic momentum remains sluggish.
