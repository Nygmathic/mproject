The British High Street pharmacy chain Boots has been sold to Canada's billionaire Weston family in an $8.9 billion (£6.7 billion) deal. Wittington Investments, the holding company making the purchase alongside partner Fairfax Financial Holdings of Toronto, is buying the 177-year-old retailer from US private equity firm Sycamore Partners and the Pessina family. Operational control will go to Wittington, with the transaction expected to finalize in early 2027.

The acquisition encompasses Boots' retail network in the UK and Ireland, Boots Opticians, the No7 Beauty Company, and operations in Thailand along with franchised outlets. However, Sycamore Partners and Stefano Pessina's family will retain control of Germany's Alliance Healthcare Deutschland and Mexico's Farmacias Benavides. Ranked fifth on this year's Sunday Times Rich List with a combined fortune of nearly £19 billion, the Weston family already owns Canadian grocery business Loblaws and pharmacy operator Shoppers Drug Mart, and previously owned London's Selfridges department store. A separate UK arm of the family holds majority ownership in Associated British Foods, the parent entity of Primark.

Founded in 1849 by John Boot as a Nottingham herbalist store, Boots expanded into a major retail presence selling health, beauty, food, and travel items, alongside offering essential services like optical and hearing care and vaccinations. It also launched the widely copied Advantage Card loyalty program in 1997. Reduced city center footfall due to remote working trends led to hundreds of store closures in recent years, leaving the chain with approximately 1,800 locations and 51,000 employees. Boots generated £7.5 billion in sales during its latest financial year, representing a 3.2 percent growth compared to 2024.

Under the new ownership, Weston will become chairman of Boots and intends to pursue store renovations and expand healthcare offerings. Retail expert Catherine Shuttleworth, chief executive of savvy marketing, noted that frequent changes in ownership—including Sycamore's brief 18-month tenure—had been "an unhelpful distraction." She added that while immediate store changes are unlikely, long-term investment in key growth areas like health and beauty should enhance the overall customer experience.
