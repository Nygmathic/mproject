A Radio 4 broadcast has examined the difficulties corporate executives face when rebuilding their careers after serving time in prison for financial offences. The coverage focused on former business leaders who have drawn on their experiences during and after incarceration to establish new commercial operations.

Among those featured was Riordan Maynard, the former chief executive officer of the mobile phone firm Touchbase. Maynard expanded the organization from a small business selling mobile phones into a multinational company maintaining offices in Europe, Australia, Asia, and the United States. Following the 2008 financial crisis, Touchbase lost deals worth hundreds of millions of pounds. Maynard’s subsequent actions resulted in him serving five and a half years in a United States prison. During his sentence, he witnessed brutal violence and struggled with his mental health, experiences that ultimately inspired an idea for a new business venture.

The feature also highlighted Sushovan Hussain, who previously served as the chief financial officer of Autonomy, a business founded by the late Mike Lynch. Hussain assisted in building Autonomy into one of the largest technology companies in Britain. After the business was sold, Hussain was convicted of fraud in the United States and was sentenced to five years in prison in 2019. Upon leaving prison, he faced obstacles obtaining bank accounts and insurance. These post-incarceration challenges motivated him to launch a new enterprise designed to help former offenders re-enter the workforce.

The programme exploring post-prison business ventures was produced by Sally Abraham, Nick Holland, and Phoebe Keane, with Matt Willis serving as editor. The item was part of Radio 4's broadcast schedule alongside weather reporting and other features.
