Rising fuel costs are creating significant financial strain for charitable organizations and commercial enterprises across the North East. Data from the RAC motoring group shows that the average cost of diesel in the United Kingdom has crossed £2 per litre for the first time. The global price increases follow seven months of severe disruptions to the production and transport of regional oil and refined products caused by the war in Iran.

In Newcastle, the cancer charity Daft as a Brush is among those struggling with the price increases. The organization, which uses 30 vans to drive patients 600,000 miles annually to hospital appointments, is currently spending £15,000 a month on diesel. This represents a £5,000 monthly increase from the previous year. Scott Jobson, the charity's head of income generation, stated that the organization receives no discounts at the pump and must use donation funds to cover the hikes, which is hindering the charity's growth. The extra expense has also forced the group to pause plans to transition its fleet from diesel to electric vehicles. Volunteer driver Davey Hall noted that filling a tank from half full recently cost £94, compared to £40 to £45 for a fill-up prior to the price surges.

Businesses in the region are encountering similar obstacles. Billy Johnson, director of Teesside Minibus Hire in Middlesbrough, explained that long-term contracts signed with businesses and schools were based on lower historical fuel prices, bringing the company close to operating at a point where it is no longer sustainable. While the business attempts to transfer extra costs to day-hire customers, many clients affected by general inflation are turning to cheaper, unlicensed providers that lack the same operational overheads. Johnson expressed doubt about the firm's ability to survive over the long term if fuel prices do not decrease.

In response to high prices, the government announced in May an extension of the fuel duty freeze through the end of the year, maintaining a 5p per litre tax cap. The measure was originally introduced by the former Conservative administration after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine drove fuel costs up in 2022. Government representatives have emphasized that there is no reason to fear diesel shortages stemming from the Iran war, maintaining that the country possesses a "diverse and resilient supply."
