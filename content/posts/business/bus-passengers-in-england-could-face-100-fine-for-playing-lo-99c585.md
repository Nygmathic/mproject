The government is introducing measures to address antisocial behavior on public transport in England, which could see bus passengers fined up to £100 for playing loud music. Local authorities have received updated guidance on exercising these powers, though specific local rules must still be approved by the Transport Secretary. Alongside loud music, the broader initiative aims to tackle issues such as vaping, littering, and aggressive conduct across transport networks.

While playing loud music or singing on trains has been prohibited since 2005, the government plans to reinforce awareness through a national campaign urging rail passengers to "share the carriage, not the noise." On railways, individuals who refuse requests to turn off loud media can face removal from trains or fines reaching £1,000 under current regulations. Transport for London similarly relies on existing byelaws to stop disruptive audio playing, reporting that most individuals comply when asked by enforcement staff before further legal action is required.

Questions remain regarding how the bus penalties will be implemented. According to the government, bus drivers will not be expected to handle enforcement directly. Instead, authorized personnel such as Transport Safety Officers and police will oversee compliance. Science, Research and Innovation Minister Chris McDonald told BBC Breakfast that passengers uncomfortable with confronting offenders can report incidents to transport staff or the British Transport Police. However, figures such as Michael Solomon Williams from the Campaign for Better Transport highlighted that British Transport Police are typically absent from bus routes, raising concerns over how local forces will manage enforcement.

Reactions to the announcement have been mixed. Prime Minister Andy Burnham described loud phone audio as a widespread frustration that ruins journeys. Meanwhile, union leadership and political opposition emphasized broader safety issues. TSSA general secretary Maryam Eslamdoust called the crackdown a positive step but urged increased funding for rail police to combat violence against staff. Shadow transport secretary Richard Holden pointed to a 68% surge in assaults on railway employees, while Liberal Democrat spokesperson Olly Glover warned that the regulations require funding for safety officers to be effective. Additionally, Transport Focus reported that 40% of passengers feel vulnerable because of antisocial conduct.
