Discussing earnings remains a complex topic for people who are dating, leading young adults in New York City to hold differing views on when to reveal their income. While some individuals believe salary information should remain private until a relationship is firmly established, others suggest assessing financial compatibility right from the beginning.

Many young professionals feel that asking about money too early in a relationship is inappropriate. Alexa Ludato, a 25-year-old who works in insurance, believes salary conversations should only happen after becoming an official couple, though she prefers knowing a date's occupation beforehand. Her friend, 22-year-old Vik Sunderaj, agrees that compensation should only be discussed in a serious relationship, adding that bringing up the subject during initial dates is unusual and could signal that the person is not a good match. Bridget Shurdut, a 26-year-old sales worker, views early questions about earnings as a "red flag" that would prevent her from continuing to see someone, noting that she prioritizes ambition and drive over income figures.

In some cases, couples refrain from sharing precise income figures even after dating for a prolonged period. Emily Moretta, a 26-year-old insurance worker, has been with her boyfriend for two years without either of them disclosing their exact compensation, stating they might only share this detail when preparing to move in together. Similarly, 25-year-old tech sales worker Willa Morton believes exact figures are unnecessary because a partner's financial situation can be gauged through their lifestyle and spending patterns. Although Morton knows her boyfriend, a company founder, earns more than she does, she has not requested an exact figure. The couple relies on an unwritten agreement where he pays for their dates, which average around $200, while she covers smaller expenses such as coffee.

However, dating and relationship coach Kate Mansfield advises that discussions regarding money ought to take place far earlier than most people think. According to Mansfield, individuals should discuss topics such as saving, spending, generosity, lifestyle, and financial independence during their initial dates, as these core habits directly influence a couple's compatibility.
