Late on Saturday, central Frankfurt and several other German cities experienced violent unrest and shop looting following rallies protesting the rising cost of living. Although thousands took part in mostly peaceful daytime demonstrations organized by the campaign group Gen Z regelt, evening clashes broke out as rioters targeted emergency personnel with stones, bottles, fireworks, and molotov cocktails. Police utilized water cannons and deployed specialized units to control the crowds across Frankfurt, Berlin, Cologne, and Hamburg.

During the violence in Frankfurt, rioters smashed shop windows, stole items including footwear, and broke into a gun store, where police arrested a suspect and recovered two weapons. Roman Poseck, the interior minister for Hesse state, stated that roughly 100 identified left-wing extremists had infiltrated the Frankfurt rally. Similar disruptions occurred elsewhere; Cologne police chief Johannes Hermanns reported that extremists targeted officers near the central railway station with bottles and a thrown bicycle. Furthermore, Berlin police reported 27 officers injured and up to 56 temporary detentions, while public broadcaster NDR stated that 120 people were arrested in Hamburg after demonstrators in black set fire to barricades.

On Sunday, German Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt informed Bild newspaper that police had successfully prevented further escalation overnight, calling for "harsh punishments" against looters and rioters. Gen Z regelt spokesperson Lina Friedrich emphasized that the peaceful demonstrators were demanding increased support for education and healthcare, opposition to compulsory military service, and a fairer distribution of financial burdens. Official figures indicated up to 18,000 people attended demonstrations across Frankfurt, Munich, and Berlin, though German media reported nationwide turnout in the tens of thousands.

The German demonstrations were part of broader youth-led protests across Europe focused on economic strain, public service cuts, and housing issues. In France, more than 320,000 people joined demonstrations organized by the Yellow Vests, resulting in over 770 arrests and an attempted murder investigation after a police officer was hurt by a molotov cocktail. Clashes between demonstrators and law enforcement were also reported in the Italian cities of Rome and Turin, while Portuguese police cleared a protest camp formed to oppose new housing legislation.
