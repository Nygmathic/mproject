Alphabet, the parent company of Google, lost more than $500 billion in market value on Thursday after revealing that its artificial intelligence search summaries are reducing search advertising revenue. Shares in the firm plummeted over 12 percent to a six-month low, bringing Alphabet's total stock market valuation down to $3.46 trillion from $3.97 trillion. For the three months ending in September, total revenue rose 25 percent compared to the prior year to reach $127.6 billion. However, lower-than-expected advertising income unnerved investors.

Philipp Schindler, Google's chief business officer, stated that the company's conversational AI mode is capturing traffic that previously generated revenue from traditional search ads, though he expressed confidence in the firm's AI search trajectory. Millions of users have shifted toward rival tools such as ChatGPT and Claude. Speaking to the BBC's Today programme, former Google advertising executive Kim Scott explained that the business is prioritizing long-term innovation, stating Google would "rather eat its own lunch than allow someone else to eat their lunch" even if short-term revenue is impacted.

The financial strain of the AI race is affecting other major tech firms as well. Both Amazon and Microsoft detailed plans to continue heavy spending in the sector. Amazon saw its stock fall 6 percent in after-hours trading. Despite an 11 percent increase in quarterly sales to $201 billion, the company reported a negative cash flow of $42 billion after spending increased six-fold in three months. Amazon Chief Executive Andy Jassy defended the outlay, pointing to future growth opportunities in custom computer chips and its Bedrock AI platform.

Microsoft experienced a milder market reaction, with its shares falling by less than 1 percent on Thursday. The company reduced its AI-dedicated investment spending by about $5 billion and previously announced plans to eliminate thousands of jobs to cut costs. Microsoft reported a 19 percent quarterly revenue increase to $92.3 billion, backed by 30 percent growth in its cloud division to $63 billion. Chief Executive Satya Nadella noted that daily users of the Copilot AI tool surged 500 percent year-over-year. Commentator Woo highlighted that while enterprise demand for AI remains strong, Wall Street continues to examine where the technology's ultimate value will be captured.
