The trade association Energy UK is urging the government to immediately intervene to assist households struggling with energy bills this winter. The organisation warned that failing to act promptly could result in a deeper, more costly, and longer-lasting crisis. The call comes after households in England, Scotland, and Wales on variable tariffs saw Ofgem's price cap increase by four percent in October, raising the average annual bill for a typical direct debit household using gas and electricity to £1,723.

Further price increases are anticipated early next year. Data highlighted by the BBC shows forecasters predicting a 16 percent rise in January for 20 million households subject to the price cap. Consultancy firm Cornwall Insight projects that a typical annual bill could reach £1,999. Although the government recently reduced VAT on electricity and previously altered some bill levies, Energy UK stated these savings were negated by surging wholesale gas and electricity prices. These wholesale costs have been pushed up by global events, including conflict in the Middle East and shipping disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz, creating pressure reminiscent of the 2022 energy crisis triggered by Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

Industry figures have voiced serious concerns over escalating customer debt, which currently adds an average of £67 a year to every household's bill. Dhara Vyas, chief executive of Energy UK, argued that urgent intervention is needed before prices jump again, warning that delayed emergency measures could prove poorly targeted and more expensive. Simone Rossi, head of supplier EDF Energy, similarly warned that the country is "walking into a second energy crisis." Supporting this assessment, Adam Scorer, chief executive of the charity National Energy, informed BBC Breakfast that poorer households are plunging into increasingly severe debt.

To cushion the impact, Energy UK is proposing targeted support beyond the existing £150 Warm Home Discount to eventually establish a discounted social tariff. The group is also seeking a debt relief scheme for the hardest-hit households and the removal of additional levies from electricity bills into general taxation. Speaking at the Labour Party conference, Prime Minister Andy Burnham acknowledged to the BBC that home energy expenses are extremely difficult and confirmed that the government is considering all possible measures to ease the pressure on consumers.
