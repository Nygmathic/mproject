Single people are increasingly turning to in-person dating gatherings as frustration grows over digital matching applications. At a speed dating event in Guildford organized by Love at First Sign, attendees voiced dissatisfaction with assessing potential partners based on minimal online information. Data from the communications regulator Ofcom indicates that while 11 percent of adults use digital dating platforms, monthly time spent on these services dropped from nearly four hours in 2023 to three hours last year.

Event organizers attribute part of this transition to widespread remote working since the pandemic, which has reduced spontaneous workplace and commute encounters. Financial pressures are also influencing dating habits. Studies show that monthly dating expenses can reach approximately £130, and a survey by Barclays revealed that rising costs altered dating habits for more than a quarter of respondents. Participants noted that structured singles events, which typically cost between £15 and £30 and include a beverage alongside an activity, offer better value by allowing them to interact with multiple individuals in person.

Social media influencers and entrepreneurs have also highlighted the benefits of meeting offline. Candice Gallagher, who documented attending ten dating events across ten weeks, observed that in-person gatherings prevent conversations from stalling in digital "chat graveyards" and yield a higher return on investment.

In response to shifting user behavior, major digital dating companies are modifying their services. Chine Mmegwa from Match Group, which owns Tinder and Hinge, noted that the company has introduced features like astrology elements, double-dating options, and live events across the United States and the United Kingdom to encourage offline connections.

Research from Match Group involving 3,007 single individuals showed that 21 percent of baby boomers met their most recent partner in a pub, bar, or club, compared to just 4 percent of Gen Z respondents. Additionally, 20 percent met their partner via an app, and another 20 percent met through mutual friends. Attendees at local events, including 28-year-old Olivia Miller-Davis, reported finding prospective partners, demonstrating a growing desire among daters to establish connections outside of digital screens.
