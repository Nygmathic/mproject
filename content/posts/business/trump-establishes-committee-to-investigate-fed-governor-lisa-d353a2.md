President Donald Trump announced the creation of a special committee to look into allegations of mortgage fraud against Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook. The move represents a renewed attempt to oust Cook from the central bank's board, following a Supreme Court ruling in June that determined her prior dismissal was unconstitutional. The panel plans to confer with Justice Department officials to evaluate whether proper cause exists for her removal.

A private White House hearing has been set for November 5, with transcripts to be published after the event. During the hearing, Cook will have the opportunity to submit a written statement, present evidence, and undergo questioning by legal counsel from both the White House and her own team. Defending his actions, Trump stated that his role requires ensuring laws are followed, including dismissing subordinates who lack trustworthiness. In response, Cook's legal representatives argued that the administration is misrepresenting facts based on simple clerical mistakes. Although her lawyers voiced "grave doubts" regarding the fairness of the proceeding, they expressed confidence that an impartial evaluation would demonstrate Cook committed no fraud and clear her to remain on the board.

Cook was named to a 14-year term by Joe Biden in 2022, becoming the first Black governor in the central bank's history. She previously served on Barack Obama's economic advisory council and held teaching positions at Harvard and Stanford universities. The legal battle began in August 2025 when Trump initially fired her over allegations concerning property listings in Georgia and Michigan, as well as unreported rental earnings in Atlanta. Cook sued, leading to a 5-4 Supreme Court vote to reinstate her, followed by the June decision that struck down her firing because she was not given a chance to dispute the charges beforehand.

The conflict unfolds amid broader tension surrounding the central bank, which Congress designed as an independent institution to protect economic stability. Trump previously launched and later dropped a Justice Department inquiry into former Fed Chair Jerome Powell over interest rate disputes. Powell's successor, Kevin Warsh, has also faced questions regarding political influence. Despite previously supporting rate reductions, the Fed recently raised rates to combat Middle East war-driven inflation, angering the White House ahead of the midterm elections.
