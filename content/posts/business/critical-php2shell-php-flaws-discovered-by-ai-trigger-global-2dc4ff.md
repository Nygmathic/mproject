Global security agencies and internet operators are responding to newly exposed flaws in PHP, an open-source programming language that underpins approximately 70 percent of websites worldwide. The security vulnerabilities, referred to as php2shell, went undetected for over a decade before being discovered using artificial intelligence tools by researchers at cyber-security firms Wiz and Calif. Wiz classified the vulnerabilities as the most severe ever identified using AI, while OpenAI characterized the finding as one of the most impactful security issues in recent memory.

If exploited under specific conditions, the flaws allow unauthorized actors to assume control of web servers and compromise sensitive data. Ryan Austin, a Calif researcher who participated in the discovery, told the BBC it was the most critical flaw he had ever found. Security experts estimate the vulnerability could impact millions of sites, with poorly maintained platforms at heightened risk. To minimize potential exposure, organizations worldwide have pulled systems offline. These include government portals in Finland, Edinburgh council, Scottish health boards, the Netherlands' cyber-security agency, pages from the BBC, and services belonging to Wikimedia.

In response to the discovery, the PHP Foundation issued emergency updates on Wednesday and instructed all users to install them immediately. International authorities promptly reinforced the recommendation. The United States National Security Agency urged all organizations relying on PHP to apply the patches right away or disconnect their systems, warning the flaw provides a "direct path" to database exploitation. Similar guidance was distributed by the National Cyber Security Centre in the UK, alongside counterpart agencies across the European Union, Australia, and Singapore. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi also called on site operators to apply patches or suspend vulnerable sites.

Tech companies reported immediate spikes in attack traffic surrounding the public disclosure. Amazon noted it repelled several targeting attempts prior to the announcement while keeping its websites functional. Cloudflare chief executive Matthew Prince stated on X that exploit attempts scaled rapidly from thousands prior to the public alert to millions shortly after. With the technical specifics now public, cyber-security researcher Salil Kanhere from UNSW Sydney warned that further exploitation attempts are likely to continue in the coming weeks.
