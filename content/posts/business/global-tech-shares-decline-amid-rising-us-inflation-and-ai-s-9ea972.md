Global financial markets have experienced an intensifying downturn in artificial intelligence-related shares, driven by anxieties surrounding elevated US inflation, potential interest rate increases, and mounting debt across the tech sector. The market decline began in the United States and rapidly spread across major Asian and European exchanges.

The sell-off was triggered by economic data showing unexpected inflationary pressure in the United States. Consumer prices rose by 3.7% year-on-year in September, marking the fastest rate of price growth since May, while producer prices increased by 6.3% over the same period. Analysts at Deutsche Bank, led by Jim Reid, noted that price increases extended across the broader consumer basket rather than being confined to energy costs, despite heightened fuel prices linked to the US war on Iran. In response to the data, investors now expect the Federal Reserve to implement additional interest rate hikes, with some anticipating a rate increase as early as the end of the month.

Wall Street led the initial slide, with the Nasdaq Composite falling 1.9% and the S&P 500 dropping 1.1% in its worst performance since July. The Nasdaq fell an additional 1.4% as Thursday trading opened, with data center server maker Dell and component producer Coherent leading early losses. Asian exchanges subsequently posted sharp declines. South Korea’s Kospi 200 dropped 8.1% to hit a six-month low, triggering trading halts via circuit breakers after SK Hynix fell nearly 11% and Samsung Electronics lost 9.5%. In Japan, the Nikkei 225 slipped 4.9%, driven by an 11% decline in chipmaker Kioxia and a 10% fall in SoftBank. Meanwhile, China’s Shanghai Shenzhen CSI 300 fell by up to 2.4% to reach its lowest level since August 2025, and Taiwan's TSMC slipped 2% despite posting a sixth consecutive quarter of profits.

European markets also faced downward pressure, with Germany’s DAX losing 0.36% and Italy’s FTSE MIB dropping 1.3%. The broad market decline follows warnings from the International Monetary Fund, which cautioned this week that a crash in AI share prices could threaten global financial stability. The IMF highlighted that while Asian markets face significant exposure alongside the US, European and UK markets may be somewhat more insulated, though investors holding American assets remain vulnerable.
