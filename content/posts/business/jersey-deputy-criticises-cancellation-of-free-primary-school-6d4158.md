A politician in Jersey has criticized the government's decision to discontinue a free fruit scheme in primary schools, arguing that broader cost-of-living pressures facing families were overlooked. Deputy Louise Doublet voiced concerns after the trial, which began in 2024 across four primary schools, was halted by the education minister. Under the pilot scheme, pupils were provided with apples, bananas, and a variety of other fruits. Doublet highlighted that withdrawing this support while living expenses continue to climb creates additional hardship for local households.

The decision to stop the pilot came after Minister for Education and Lifelong Learning Catherine Curtis cited excessive food waste and low consumption rates among pupils. Curtis explained that parents had requested the fruit be integrated into the wider school food initiative instead. Consequently, the government announced in September that fresh fruit would be absorbed into an existing programme that supplies hot lunches to government primary schools. The overall topic was formally discussed during a States Assembly debate on September 29.

Doublet acknowledged the evidence presented by the minister during the assembly debate, accepting that the pilot scheme had failed to noticeably raise children's consumption of fruit and vegetables. However, she maintained that the scheme's potential financial benefits for families struggling with rising costs were never properly measured. Doublet urged officials to increase spending on nutritious food, favoring locally grown produce to help ease household expenses. She stated that expanding funded meal initiatives would alleviate financial burdens for parents while ensuring children receive healthy nutrition.

Addressing the island's broader meal provision, Doublet also discussed the government's arrangement with UK-based company Apetito Limited, which delivers hot lunches to government primary schools throughout Jersey. While acknowledging public worries regarding food freshness, she noted her understanding that the supplied meals are frozen instead of using artificial preservatives. In evaluating the contract, Doublet characterized the arrangement as the "best available option" currently available for delivering hot school meals. The Government of Jersey has been approached for comment.
