A suspected cyber-attack has compromised cooling and freezing systems across dozens of supermarkets in seven European countries, leading to the disposal of food valued at millions of euros. The disruptions hit stores in Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden, Ireland, the Netherlands, Estonia, and Finland. National cyber-security authorities in Belgium and Sweden are observing the situation, while the UK's National Cyber Security Centre stated it is cooperating with international partners and industry representatives to understand the event, noting that no British stores have reported problems.

The perpetrators manipulated system evaporators into defrost mode, causing temperatures inside units to rise significantly, with freezer levels in some locations reported as high as 50 degrees Celsius. The entity behind the incident remains unknown. Stephan Moelans, chief executive of Sabcobel—the Belgian firm operating systems for retail chain Delhaize—told news agency Belga that workers spent the night and morning disconnecting affected units from the network. Moelans noted that resolving the vulnerability requires physical hardware modifications rather than software updates alone, though adopting secure internet links offers protection. The tactic mirrors an early October incident that affected hundreds of French supermarkets.

In Belgium, roughly 40 Delhaize branches were impacted, including a store in Hoeilaart that reported losing 100,000 euros (£83,500) in spoiled stock. French-owned Carrefour stated that 15 of its Belgian stores were hit but remained open, while five Spar stores run by Colruyt Group were targeted. In Sweden, multiple ICA and Coop shops experienced disruptions. One ICA branch in Sjöbo temporarily closed after its equipment was breached overnight, and Coop informed BBC News that seven of its 800 Swedish stores were affected, adding that the issue was contained.

Elsewhere, Finland's Kesko is investigating cooling failures at several K-stores, and Estonian retailer Selver reported similar issues. In Ireland, parent company Musgrave Group confirmed to local media that a cyber incident affected 13 SuperValu stores, though the problem was limited to a specific refrigerator model. Meanwhile, Danish equipment manufacturer Danfoss told Dutch public radio that overnight attacks compromised cooling systems across at least 17 companies in the Netherlands, including butcher shops and small neighborhood stores.
