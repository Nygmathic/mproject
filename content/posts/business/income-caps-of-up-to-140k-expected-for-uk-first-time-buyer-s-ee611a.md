The English government is expected to establish income caps for households applying for its upcoming first-time buyers program, setting maximum earnings at £110,000 generally and £140,000 for London residents. First reported by The Times, the initiative, named the Your First Home scheme, is designed to provide state-backed equity loans covering 20% of the price of newly built properties sold by participating developers. Full specifics regarding these salary limits and additional program guidelines are expected to be officially confirmed in the upcoming Budget next week.

The scheme is structured to assist purchasers by permitting down payments as low as 2.5%, alongside an initial interest-free period on the government loans. Unlike previous purchasing programs that lacked income restrictions and drew criticism for inflating housing prices by allowing higher earners to borrow greater sums, this initiative establishes financial boundaries. When introducing the program at last month's Labour conference, Prime Minister Andy Burnham noted that the focus would be directed toward applicants who "really need support". According to The Times, ministers initially considered setting lower household income thresholds of £90,000 outside the capital and £110,000 within London, but subsequently adjusted these upward to ensure middle-earning professionals, including nurses and teachers, were not excluded.

In addition to aiding prospective buyers, the government argues that the initiative will help boost home construction across England. This comes after ministers conceded that they are unlikely to meet their target of building 1.5 million homes before the next general election. Labour has indicated that funding for the program will come from existing state budgets, though the government has yet to reveal which specific areas will undergo expenditure reductions. Furthermore, developers who participate in the program will be required to contribute to its operational expenses.

The framework relies on equity loans tied to changes in home values, resembling the Help to Buy program originally introduced by the Conservative-Liberal Democrat coalition. That earlier effort initially operated from 2013 to 2021, offering home buyers five years of interest-free funding for up to 20% of a property's price on newly constructed residences valued up to £600,000, requiring a 5% deposit. A subsequent iteration from 2021 to 2023 limited participation exclusively to first-time buyers and introduced regional price limits on eligible properties.
