Kellogg's has altered its classic UK recipes for Rice Krispies and Coco Pops, replacing whole rice grains with a blend of corn flour and rice flour. The updated formulation also introduces wheat gluten as an ingredient. The food manufacturer stated that unpredictable weather patterns disrupting global cereal and grain supplies prompted the adjustment to preserve product consistency as crop costs escalate. Kellogg's confirmed that no recipe modifications are being made to its other cereals or to Rice Krispies distributed across the United States and Canada.

The modification has triggered widespread complaints from consumers online, who report that the cereal no longer generates its signature crackling sound when mixed with milk. This popping effect has been central to the brand's promotion since the 1950s. Disappointed buyers posting on TikTok, including users named Dani and Lauren, expressed dismay over alterations to the taste and texture, noting that young children are rejecting the cereal. In response, Kellogg's maintained that the new recipes were "carefully assessed for taste, appearance and texture" before release.

Due to the inclusion of wheat gluten, updated packaging will feature a clear warning stating the product contains the allergen, replacing previous warnings that only highlighted potential factory cross-contamination. Kellogg's stressed that the nutritional profile—such as levels of iron, fibre, and Vitamin D—remains identical, with no added artificial colours, sweeteners, or flavours. The cereals also continue to meet UK guidelines on sugar, salt, and fat, building on previous reformulations that reduced salt in the 1990s and sugar in 2018.

The supply chain pressures reflect broader environmental challenges in Asia, which produces the majority of the global rice supply. Extreme weather events linked to El Niño have threatened agricultural output in South East Asia, a region accounting for more than a quarter of world rice production. According to the World Meteorological Agency, this El Niño event is expected to continue until at least 2027. Meanwhile, Kellogg's split into two entities in 2023: Kellanova, which oversees global cereals and snacks and is now owned by Mars, and North American operator WK Kellogg's, which was acquired by Ferrero.
