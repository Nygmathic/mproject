Navigating conversations about personal finances remains one of the most challenging aspects of modern dating, with young adults holding distinct views on when to reveal their earnings. Conversations with 20-somethings in New York City highlight a wide spectrum of approaches, ranging from discussing money early on to avoiding explicit salary numbers altogether.

For several young adults, becoming an official couple serves as the primary milestone for opening up about earnings, spending habits, and debt. Alexa Ludato, a 25-year-old insurance worker, states that while she wants to know a date's line of work early on, she would only discuss specific earnings after establishing an official relationship. Her friend, 22-year-old Vik Sunderaj, agrees that salary discussions belong strictly in serious relationships, adding that being asked about income on the first few dates is unusual and might indicate that a partner is not the right match. Similarly, 26-year-old sales professional Bridget Shurdut considers early inquiries about earnings a "red flag" that would prevent her from continuing to see someone, noting that she cares more about ambition and drive than an exact figure.

Offering a contrasting perspective, dating and relationship coach Kate Mansfield suggests that conversations surrounding money should happen much sooner than most people expect. According to Mansfield, partners ought to discuss spending habits, saving, generosity, lifestyle expectations, and financial independence within the initial dates, as these fundamental values influence overall compatibility.

Despite expert recommendations, some couples navigate long relationships without ever exchanging specific figures. Willa Morton, a 25-year-old tech sales worker, believes that exact numbers are unnecessary because a person's income can be gauged by their lifestyle and spending patterns. Morton knows her boyfriend, a company founder, earns more than her, and the two follow an unspoken arrangement where he pays for dates costing around $200 while she covers smaller expenses like coffees. Similarly, Emily Moretta, a 26-year-old insurance worker, has been in a two-year relationship without either partner revealing their exact pay. Moretta notes that neither has felt the need to ask, suggesting that such a conversation would only become necessary if they reach the stage of moving in together.
