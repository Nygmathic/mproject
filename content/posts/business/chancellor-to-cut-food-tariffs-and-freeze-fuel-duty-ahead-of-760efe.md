Ahead of Wednesday's Budget, Chancellor John Healey is announcing a series of economic measures designed to provide what he described as "breathing space" for households struggling with rising costs. Healey attributed elevated living expenses to ongoing international conflicts in Ukraine and Iran, emphasizing that the government would act where possible to ease financial pressures.

According to reporting by BBC News, the government plans to freeze fuel duty once again, maintaining a five-penny discount originally established in 2022. Additionally, import tariffs will be removed from 100 specific items, including baked beans, garlic, gherkins, plant-based drinks, kerosene, and fertilisers. Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds defended the policy, explaining that these cuts, alongside earlier summer tariff suspensions on products like pasta, tuna, and fruit juice, would generate around £500 million in annual savings and reduce household costs by up to £21 per year.

The upcoming Budget will also feature several targeted relief measures. Drivers using personal vehicles for business will receive a permanent tax-free rate of 55p per mile for up to 10,000 miles, with the rate for additional miles increasing from 25p to 35p. For carer’s allowance recipients, the government plans to replace the existing income limit cliff-edge with a taper mechanism so working carers do not lose their full allowance. Self-employed fathers will become eligible for two weeks of paternity pay worth up to £194 weekly, and hundreds of new Post Office drop boxes will be introduced.

Wage adjustments will also be outlined on Wednesday. Although Labour’s manifesto pledged to eliminate age-based minimum wage tiers, Reynolds indicated the pace of pay increases for younger workers on the National Minimum Wage would slow to balance labor market conditions. The Times reported that the rate for workers aged 18 to 20 is expected to increase by 3.9 percent to around £11.27 per hour, while the National Living Wage for those aged 21 and over is set to rise by 3.9 percent to £13.20. Unison general secretary Andrea Egan criticized the continued pay gap between younger and older staff performing identical jobs. Meanwhile, Conservative shadow chancellor Andrew Griffith argued that the small measures fail to offset the wider pressures of Labour's tax increases, inflation, and higher borrowing costs.
