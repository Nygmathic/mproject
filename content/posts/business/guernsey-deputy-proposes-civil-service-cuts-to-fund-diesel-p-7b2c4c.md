A Guernsey politician has suggested that reducing civil service posts could help fund lower fuel costs for motorists. Deputy Neil Inder announced plans to submit two amendments regarding fuel prices to the upcoming States budget, pointing to extraordinary global circumstances affecting living costs.

The proposed amendments involve freezing fuel duty and lowering the cost of white diesel at the pump by 5p per litre. White diesel is used by standard vehicles like cars, trucks, and buses, and is subject to standard tax rates. This differs from red diesel, which is used in industries such as farming and fishing and receives duty-free or heavily reduced rates.

To pay for the reduction, Inder urged the government to shrink the civil service. Emphasizing that personnel costs represent the largest expense for the States, he estimated that the revenue lost through the lower duty equals the cost of four or five civil servants. He advocated for a policy of non-replacement as current staff retire, rather than continuing to fund those positions.

Inder's measures contrast with proposals from Policy and Resources (P&R). In the 2027 budget, fuel duty is scheduled to rise in line with inflation at 4.3% starting January 1, 2027, bringing the rate to 94.4p per litre. Inder noted that while he would normally accept the P&R plan to raise diesel duty under regular conditions, international events require a change in approach.

Fuel prices have recently reached record levels in Guernsey, following broader international trends. According to the RAC motoring organisation, the ongoing war in the Middle East has driven fuel costs up across Europe, with UK diesel hitting £2.00 per litre. Inder warned that diesel prices have spiked sharply over the past three to four months and are expected to worsen, threatening the broader economy. Stating that "diesel really does make the world go round" and drives local activity, he emphasized the need to address the issue during the annual budget process. The BBC reported that it reached out to the States for comment.
