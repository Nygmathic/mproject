According to the latest quarterly Reputation Risk Index published by the Global Risk Advisory Council, the improper use of artificial intelligence has emerged as the primary threat to corporate reputations. The survey, which gathered insights from more than 150 public affairs leaders across over 20 countries, found that concerns over AI misuse surpassed other major brand risks. These include accusations of endangering children's well-being and fallouts related to the ongoing conflict involving the US, Israel, and Iran.

Behind AI misuse, the survey identified the top corporate risks as actions or allegations that expose children to physical, mental, or emotional harm, alongside breaches of sensitive personal information. Other leading concerns include illegal or unethical practices carried out for financial or strategic gain, as well as flawed operational decisions that damage a company's standing or financial performance. While respondents recognized that AI can boost efficiency, they highlighted rising issues such as data security risks, fraud, cyberattacks, privacy erosion, labor disruptions, and legal challenges tying AI products to real-world harm.

The Global Risk Advisory Council, founded by the Washington D.C.-based firm Global Situation Room, noted that AI misuse has consistently ranked as a top worry since the survey's first edition in April 2025. However, the latest report included an advisory warning that the technology has become a "clear and present danger to public confidence" for many corporations. Isabel Casillas Guzman, the council's chair, explained that while businesses are rapidly adopting AI to boost productivity, public trust in the technology is declining. To protect their standing, the Global Situation Room suggested companies consider scaling back AI-generated content and relying on human employees for customer service.

The focus on AI follows the index's previous report in July, which identified supply chain disruptions and political controversies surrounding the war launched in Iran by the Trump administration alongside Israel as the top corporate risk. Guzman stated that although the conflict launched in February continues, its initial reputational blow has faded. The findings arrive alongside new political developments, as the Trump administration recently named Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton as a new AI czar to coordinate federal involvement across consumer groups, tech firms, and critical infrastructure providers.
