Green Party members voted to adopt a policy motion defining Zionism as a form of racism during the party's conference in Brighton. Passed by 1,022 votes to 813, the resolution describes Zionism as supporting a Jewish state based on ethnic nationalism and calls for a single democratic Palestinian state across all historic Palestine. Under Green Party rules, approved motions become official policy, though they are not guaranteed inclusion in future general election manifestos. The decision has exposed significant internal divisions and drawn widespread external backlash.

The vote highlighted key disagreements among senior party figures. Joint deputy leader Mothin Ali supported the proposal, describing it as a call for a democratic state that guarantees rights for all residents. Conversely, fellow deputy leader Rachel Millward labeled the wording problematic, and four of the party's five MPs—Carla Denyer, Ellie Chowns, Adrian Ramsay, and Sian Berry—publicly opposed it. Former leader Caroline Lucas also argued against the motion during the debate, warning that its over-simplification was making Jewish members feel scared and unwelcome. Following the result, Vivien Lichtenstein, chair of Jewish Greens, told BBC News she might reconsider her future in the party.

Party leader Zack Polanski did not vote after leaving the conference early to campaign. Polanski had supported two potential amendments—one removing the call for a single Palestinian state and another protecting Jewish members in Zionist synagogues—but both were rejected by members. A party spokeswoman subsequently stated that Jewish members would be protected and would not face discipline for attending synagogues identifying as Zionist. Polanski signed a joint statement with party MPs and deputy leaders affirming the Greens as an anti-racist party, while motion proposer Lubna Speitan defended the measure as a stand against oppressive systems.

External groups strongly condemned the motion. Ahead of the vote, the Board of Deputies and the Jewish Leadership Council sent a letter describing Zionism as an anti-racist movement that provided a refuge from persecution, expressing concern for the safety of Jewish members. Israel's Foreign Ministry declared the motion went beyond acceptable political debate and called it "a stain on the history of the United Kingdom". Criticism also came from major rival political parties, with representatives from Labour, the Conservatives, and the Liberal Democrats describing the vote as harmful, divisive, and unacceptable.
