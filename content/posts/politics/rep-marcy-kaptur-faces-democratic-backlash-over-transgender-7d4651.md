Representative Marcy Kaptur, the longest-serving female member in the history of the U.S. Congress, is facing pushback from within her own party after releasing a campaign video on transgender policies. In the advertisement, the Ohio lawmaker stated that she voted alongside Republicans to keep "transgender ideology" out of schools and voiced support for excluding transgender individuals from female sports. Following the ad's release, two county Democratic organizations in her district called for its removal, a former intern published an essay expressing embarrassment over his past work for her, and the president of the country's largest LGBTQ+ advocacy organization confirmed it would not endorse her re-election.

The controversy occurs as the 80-year-old congresswoman prepares for a difficult re-election effort in north-west Ohio. Following a legally mandated map redraw that shifted her district roughly four percentage points to the right, Kaptur is set for a November 3 midterm rematch against Republican Derek Merrin, whom she narrowly defeated in 2024. In an interview, Kaptur explained that she wanted voters to know her exact position on the issue before her opponent attempted to use it against her, citing similar political strategies used in past campaigns.

Kaptur is not alone among Democrats in taking this position. Several party candidates in competitive state and federal races, as well as a small group of House Democrats who joined Kaptur in voting for a school parental consent bill, have publicly favored restrictions concerning transgender youth.

Throughout her 43 years in office, Kaptur has established a tradition of policy independence. First elected in 1982 following a role in Jimmy Carter's presidential administration, she previously broke with party leadership to oppose major international trade agreements and financial bailouts. Based in Toledo, where she still lives in her childhood home, Kaptur maintains a prominent presence in her community. Tony Totty, president of the regional political branch of the United Auto Workers, emphasized that Kaptur remains heavily engaged with her constituents rather than becoming detached in Washington.
