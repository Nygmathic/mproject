Reform UK has announced a plan to halt the United Kingdom's asylum process for an entire parliamentary term, which typically spans five years. Under the proposal, the party would deny all incoming asylum requests, cancel existing applications, and designate all current claimants for deportation. The policy would also end refugee status grants, legal protection pathways, and resettlement schemes, while abolishing the asylum tribunal system and expelling 90,000 individuals with ongoing appeal cases.

To block legal challenges, Reform UK intends to withdraw the country from the European Convention on Human Rights, repeal the Human Rights Act 1998, and disapply the 1951 Refugee Convention. The party plans to render individuals ineligible for asylum if they arrived unlawfully, overstayed visas, or originated from safe nations. Additionally, visa bans could be imposed on nations that refuse deportation flights—such as Pakistan, Somalia, Eritrea, Syria, Afghanistan, and Sudan—with asset freezes and tariffs considered for other non-compliant countries. The party would create a dedicated detention and deportation agency with 24,000 to 28,000 spaces, though it remains unclear if this capacity is adequate. Temporary exception schemes would remain for Ukrainians fleeing war, alongside EU settled status subject to benefit cuts.

Announcing the initiative during a speech in Hythe, Kent, party leader Nigel Farage described the current situation as a "national security emergency" and claimed the measures would save significant funds. Home affairs spokesman Zia Yusuf stated that he would oversee the country's largest mass deportation program, asserting that the party would prioritize British citizens facing healthcare delays and rising housing costs.

The proposals drew sharp criticism from political opponents and advocacy groups. Labour Chair Bridget Phillipson dismissed the strategy as a sign of weakness and desperation, arguing that immigration should be managed by properly enforcing existing rules rather than dismantling the asylum framework. Jon Featonby of the Refugee Council warned that shutting down the system would trigger chaos and push vulnerable arrivals underground. Meanwhile, Conservative shadow home secretary Chris Philp claimed Reform UK was copying Tory ideas, noting his own party's plan to deport 750,000 illegal immigrants over five years. Liberal Democrat home affairs spokesperson Max Wilkinson argued that stability could instead be achieved by joining the European Union's migration and asylum pact.
