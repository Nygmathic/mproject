Undercover footage broadcast by ITV News has revealed several Reform UK councillors and political candidates using racist, violent, and sexist language. The report, gathered over a year during election campaigns across England and Wales, involved undercover journalists trailing figures within the political party.

Among those recorded was Solihull councillor and deputy mayor Alan Feeney, who targeted Asian residents with derogatory remarks and suggested limiting the non-white British population to one percent. Feeney, along with party candidates Jamie Campbell and Chris Whelan, was filmed discussing ways to block a local mosque by burying pork in the ground, as well as making crude comments about a female candidate. In another instance, Bromley councillor Dean Miller made disparaging remarks about Black residents, made anti-Muslim statements, and targeted King Charles with accusations of treason.

Following the broadcast, Reform UK stated that "there is no room for racism" in the party. A spokesperson noted that internal investigations and suspensions were underway, stating that vetting procedures had been enhanced even if some individuals still managed to clear them. Leader Nigel Farage has repeatedly maintained that he intends to remove improper members from the organization. The individuals featured in the footage have since resigned or been suspended, with Feeney, Whelan, and Miller transitioning to independent council seats. Miller stated that a chronic pain condition requiring heavy medication influenced his comments.

Opposing political parties quickly condemned the behavior shown in the documentary. Labour described the findings as deeply disturbing and rejected Farage's attempts to downplay the issue, while the Conservatives argued the incident proved Reform UK is unprepared for government. Liberal Democrat leader Sir Ed Davey expressed alarm over the hatred displayed, and a Green Party spokesperson asserted that the comments align with Reform UK's underlying political platform.
