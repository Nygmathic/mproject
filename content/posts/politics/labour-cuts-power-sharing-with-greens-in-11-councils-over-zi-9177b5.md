The Labour Party has ended local power-sharing arrangements with the Green Party across 11 councils after the Greens adopted a national policy defining Zionism as racism. Local authority areas impacted by the decision include Sheffield, Warwick, and Lewes. Labour Party chair Bridget Phillipson stated that the policy "crosses a line" and asserted that Labour cannot partner with any party supporting it at any level of government.

The dispute follows an October motion approved by Green Party members that described Zionism as an ideology favoring an ethnonationalist state and called for a single democratic Palestinian state. The resolution drew condemnation from Jewish organizations and opposition parties, while leaving senior Green figures divided. Prime Minister Andy Burnham labeled the policy antisemitic, and Labour headquarters subsequently instructed local Labour councillors to demand that Green partners disown the resolution and urge their national leadership to reconsider. Out of 18 existing local agreements, Labour confirmed that 11 failed to meet its criteria.

In response, the Green Party expressed disappointment, issuing a joint statement with the Local Government Association Green Group arguing that national directives disrupt local services and ignore resident interests. Green Party leader Zack Polanski acknowledged the motion was imperfect but defended its underlying stance regarding Israeli policy. Labour noted that six council areas met its requirements to maintain joint arrangements, while cooperation will also continue in the West of England mayoralty.

The breakdown in local alliances is expected to delay budget approvals and policy decisions, forcing councillors in affected areas to establish minority governments or seek new partners. In Sheffield, where a joint administration was formed following the May 2026 elections, Labour council leader Councillor Fran Belbin stated the party would propose operating as a minority administration, claiming trust was broken by the local Greens' failure to support Jewish residents. Sheffield Green leader Angela Argenzio denied allegations of antisemitism, stating local councillors were acting under instructions from Labour's national leader. In Warwick, local Greens refused to condemn the policy, citing political intimidation.
