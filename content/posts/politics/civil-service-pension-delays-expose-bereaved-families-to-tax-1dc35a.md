Delays in paying out civil service pensions could leave bereaved families facing unexpected tax bills, prompting calls for government action. According to tax regulations, lump sum death benefit payments remain tax-free only if settled within two years of the pension scheme being informed of a member's death. Because of severe processing backlogs, some beneficiaries risk missing this window, which would make the payments subject to income tax.

The Civil Service Pension scheme serves 1.7 million UK public sector workers. Administration was transferred to the outsourcing firm Capita from MyCSP in December 2025, with Capita inheriting over 86,000 unresolved cases. By February, that figure expanded to 120,000. Data up to September 30 shows 1,643 death-in-service or bereavement cases have dragged on past 100 days, with only 301 expected to be paid within a fortnight.

Sir Geoffrey Clifton-Brown, chair of Parliament's Public Accounts Committee, has written to Cabinet Office Minister Louise Haigh, warning that current delays make it improbable that payments will meet the two-year target. He urged the government to ensure families are not subjected to an "unjust financial penalty" due to administrative chaos. Fran Heathcote, head of the PCS union, similarly criticized the situation, stating that families should not suffer financially for mistakes outside their control.

The delays have caused real-world hardship. For example, 58-year-old Rob Wassell has spent almost two years awaiting his late brother's payout. Reaching the two-year deadline on October 22, he risks having his lump sum taxed while needing the funds to offset estate costs and depleted savings.

Responding to the criticism, a Cabinet Office spokesperson acknowledged that performance following the transition to Capita has been unacceptable. The government is holding back almost £10 million in payments to the firm while holding it to account. The spokesperson highlighted that families incurring tax charges can pursue compensation through established channels, such as the Pensions Ombudsman. However, the ombudsman currently expects standard cases to take about 18 months to resolve. Capita issued an apology for the delays, stating that its workforce is prioritizing long-overdue cases and individuals experiencing financial hardship.
