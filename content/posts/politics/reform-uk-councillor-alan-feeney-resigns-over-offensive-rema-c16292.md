A former Reform UK councillor has stepped down from two local councils and left his political party after being secretly filmed making offensive remarks about Asian communities. Alan Feeney resigned from Solihull Council and Birmingham City Council following an undercover investigation by ITV News, during which he targeted Asian homeowners with derogatory language.

The footage, gathered over a year in the lead-up to May's council elections, recorded Feeney describing an area as being overrun with Asian families and calling them "absolute parasites." He was also documented stating that non-white individuals should account for no more than one percent of the United Kingdom's population. Feeney, who joined Reform UK last year after defecting from the Conservative Party, was elected to represent the Shard End ward on Birmingham City Council in May.

In a Facebook post on Friday, Feeney stated that his remarks were wrong, offensive, and unacceptable. He offered full apologies and maintained that the recorded statements did not reflect his true beliefs or his more than ten years of public service. Reform UK confirmed to the BBC on Thursday that Feeney had mutually agreed to step down from the party. A spokesperson for Reform Solihull noted that Feeney had spent most of his time as a Conservative councillor and was only briefly affiliated with Reform.

In addition to relinquishing his political posts, Feeney has been suspended from his position as a custodial manager within the prison service. The Ministry of Justice described his remarks as despicable and confirmed an investigation is underway according to its guidelines.

The undercover investigation also included two other Reform candidates in Solihull, Jamie Campbell and Chris Whelan. On Friday, Samantha Gethen, Reform's opposition leader on Solihull Council, announced that all three councillors had stepped down, triggering upcoming by-elections. Speaking on the BBC's Today programme, Reform MP Robert Jenrick defended the party's handling of the situation, contending that leaders acted quickly to remove or suspend those involved and stating that all political parties encounter bad apples.
