Prime Minister Andy Burnham has urged Green Party leader Zack Polanski to overturn a recently passed conference motion that designated Zionism as a form of racism. The controversial measure, approved by party members on Sunday in Brighton, defined Zionism as an ideology supporting "an ethnonationalist Jewish state" and advocated for a single democratic Palestinian state across all historic Palestine, including modern-day Israel.

Speaking during a visit to Berlin on Thursday, Burnham criticized the resolution, stating that it was entirely incorrect and demonstrated terrible judgment. He argued that the policy effectively rules out a two-state solution between Israel and Palestine, insisting that the Green leadership must clarify that the motion will be reversed and will not remain official policy. Asked whether he would rule out a future coalition with the Greens, Burnham declined to comment on prospective political arrangements.

The dispute coincided with the final days of campaigning for the Holborn and St Pancras by-election, where Polanski is a candidate attempting to win the seat formerly held by former Prime Minister Sir Keir Starmer. Polanski missed the debate and vote on the motion after leaving the conference early to return to London for his campaign. Former Green leader Caroline Lucas strongly criticized his absence, telling the BBC that he should have attended to oppose the measure. She characterized the motion as flawed and divisive, adding that it remained to be seen whether Polanski would demonstrate the necessary leadership moving forward.

Prior to the vote, Polanski had supported a failed amendment aiming to protect Jewish members attending Zionist synagogues from discipline, and he had expressed a desire to alter the text regarding a single Palestinian state. While Green Party policy is determined through internal member votes, these decisions are not automatically included in general election manifestos. The motion has provoked widespread internal backlash, with four of the party's five MPs opposing it, its co-deputy leaders divided, the deputy leader in Wales resigning, and condemnation coming from European Green counterparts.

Polanski, who has declined media interviews regarding the matter, issued a statement indicating he would not permit members to be excluded based on their religious or communal affiliations. On Thursday, a Green Party spokesperson described the issue as complex and emotive, framing it around allegations of genocide and ethnic cleansing by Israeli forces. The spokesperson also accused the political establishment of providing support to Israel. Israel has rejected such genocide allegations in an ongoing case at the International Court of Justice, calling them completely unfounded.
