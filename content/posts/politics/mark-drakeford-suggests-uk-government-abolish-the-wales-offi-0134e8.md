Former Labour First Minister Mark Drakeford has suggested that the UK government consider dismantling the Wales Office. Speaking on the S4C program Y Byd yn ei Le, Drakeford indicated that the time has arrived to evaluate closing the department. He pointed out that discussions regarding this possibility have circulated in Westminster for more than ten years, noting that former Labour Prime Minister Tony Blair had previously entertained the same idea.

The role and future of the department have been the subject of frequent speculation since devolution in 1999. At that time, many of its primary responsibilities—such as healthcare, transport, education, agriculture, and the environment—were handed over to the Welsh Assembly, which is now known as the Senedd. The remaining functions of the UK administration were subsequently organized under the title of the Wales Office. Historically, the position of Welsh secretary was established in October 1964 by Harold Wilson's Labour administration, and the corresponding government department opened the following April.

Following the advent of devolution, successive UK governments have contemplated whether the department's functions should be integrated into a unified office that also manages Scottish and Northern Irish affairs. Such structural changes have partial precedent; under a previous Labour government, Peter Hain held combined cabinet oversight for both Wales and Northern Ireland between 2005 and 2007. Earlier this year, then-First Minister Eluned Morgan also commented on the matter, arguing that the Welsh secretary still retained a valid role, provided the official functioned as a voice for Wales within the cabinet rather than serving as London's advocate in Wales.

However, Drakeford's comments met opposition from Labour MP Ruth Jones during an interview on BBC Radio Wales Breakfast on Friday. Rejecting the notion of abolishing the department, Jones characterized the proposal as foolish and argued that Welsh Secretary Stephen Kinnock is actively "fighting the corner for Wales" at the cabinet table, where he remains the only representative from Wales. While acknowledging her respect for the former first minister, Jones highlighted that Drakeford is now retired and suggested that active politicians currently holding office should be permitted to carry out their duties.
