The Conservative Party has announced a pledge to invest £10 billion into a defense network designed to protect Britain from long-range missiles and drones, contingent on winning the next general election. Known as Britannia Shield, the proposed initiative would feature an integrated setup combining advanced radar, upgraded command and control infrastructure, interceptor missiles, and counter-drone capabilities.

Party leader Kemi Badenoch clarified in broadcast interviews that the concept differs from Israel's Iron Dome system, explaining that the UK faces distinct security challenges and does not require that specific scale of defense. Badenoch stated that the project would be financed over a ten-year period, with £1 billion allocated annually. Funding is planned through the party's broader commitment to raise defense spending to 3% of gross domestic product by 2030, supported by welfare budget adjustments, housing benefit restrictions, reinstating the two-child benefit cap, and reallocating funds from net-zero research.

The proposal comes amid warnings from NATO allies regarding Russian drone incursions and a report from a parliamentary defense committee indicating that the UK falls behind European peers in its readiness to defend domestic territory and overseas assets. Conservative leadership pointed to shifting conflict dynamics, including developments observed in Ukraine and the Middle East, highlighting potential vulnerabilities around the English Channel and key national infrastructure.

Defense experts offered varying assessments of the budget. Sidharth Kaushal of the Royal United Services Institute noted that £10 billion would likely meet the UK's practical requirements, such as horizon radar and additional E7 aircraft, though it would fall short of funding a fully multi-layered shield. Prof Steven Barrett of Cambridge Aerospace described the sum as a significant commitment, adding that a realistic setup would focus on high-density population areas and critical facilities like data centers rather than covering the entire landmass.

Inside the party, Shadow Defence Secretary James Cartlidge framed the initiative as one of the country's largest military programs since the Cold War. However, Labour Defence Secretary Wes Streeting rejected the plan, arguing that past Conservative governance left the armed forces "hollowed out and underfunded" while creating a £17 billion equipment deficit. Meanwhile, Prime Minister Andy Burnham faces pressure over targets to increase defense spending to 3.5% of GDP by 2035, while the current government's plan allocates over £350 million to double the British Army's existing Sky Sabre air defense systems.
