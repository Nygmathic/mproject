Television presenter Jeremy Clarkson addressed the difficulties facing the British agricultural sector during an unannounced appearance at the Conservative Party conference in Birmingham. Victoria Atkins, the Conservative shadow environment secretary, introduced the former Top Gear host and star of Clarkson's Farm, who joined land agent Charlie Ireland for a question and answer session. Clarkson described the past twelve months as a "terrible" year for farming, attributing the problems largely to hot weather that turned his own agricultural output into a complete failure despite extensive effort.

Speaking to conference delegates, Clarkson stated that farmers are under relentless pressure from multiple factors, including high energy costs, taxation, adverse weather, and foreign policy developments involving Iran and Ukraine. He explained that while his personal finances are cushioned by alternative work—including hosting ITV's Who Wants to be a Millionaire, writing newspaper columns, and running other projects—typical farmers lack such options. Clarkson also expressed frustration with the national economic climate for business owners, questioning why individuals would establish a business in the country at present. Nevertheless, he maintained that he remains positive about the sector's long-term prospects.

Clarkson, a long-time supporter of the Conservatives, previously participated in a campaign against the Labour government's proposed inheritance tax on family farms. Announced by former chancellor Rachel Reeves during the administration's initial budget, the tax measure was later scaled back following resistance from agricultural campaigners and rural Labour lawmakers. In response to Clarkson's remarks, a government source stated that ministers are collaborating with agricultural workers to boost local food production, strengthen supply chain resilience, and develop a more profitable sector.

During his stage appearance, Clarkson also made light of the venue's attendance, joking about vacant chairs around the room. Additionally, he poked fun at recent comments made by Labour MP Jenny Riddell-Carpenter at a conference fringe event. Riddell-Carpenter had argued that traditional imagery of country pubs, black Labradors, and warm ale represented a false, far-right nostalgic vision of rural life. Clarkson quipped that he must be a socialist because he drinks lager and owns two fox red Labradors.
