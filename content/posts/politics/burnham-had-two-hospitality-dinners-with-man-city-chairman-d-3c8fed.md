Prime Minister Andy Burnham attended two hospitality dinners with Manchester City chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak while serving as Mayor of Greater Manchester, according to deleted records. These engagements, which took place at matches in 2017 and 2018, were omitted from a statement released on Monday by the Greater Manchester Combined Authority (GMCA) detailing his past official interactions with the football club.

A previous GMCA declarations log seen by BBC News revealed a £300 hospitality dinner in August 2017 involving Burnham, Al Mubarak, and former council leader Richard Leese, followed by a second dinner valued at roughly £699 during an April 2018 derby match. Although Burnham originally registered these events, the record was subsequently removed from the GMCA website. The Sun first reported the deletion, which the GMCA attributed to a policy of publishing only current-term entries. However, BBC News noted that entries from 2022 remain online despite the current term starting in 2024.

The disclosures come amid growing political pressure regarding Burnham's former dealings with Manchester City. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has requested that Downing Street publish full details of all encounters between Burnham and high-ranking club officials. Shadow sports minister Louie French also called for complete transparency following Burnham's "inappropriate intervention into football." In response, Downing Street defended the prime minister, stating the dinners represented previously declared matchday hospitality rather than formal meetings.

During an appearance on The Rest Is Politics podcast, Burnham stated he was willing to disclose his past dealings with the club, adding that his only major meeting with the chairman occurred in 2021. The controversy follows an independent commission finding Manchester City guilty in September of violating Premier League financial rules from 2009 to 2018 by using deceptive contracts to hide over £830 million in funding. The club denies wrongdoing and is appealing the decision. Meanwhile, former council leader Sir Richard Leese recently defended an older arrangement in which municipal land was leased below comparable market rates to the team's owners.
