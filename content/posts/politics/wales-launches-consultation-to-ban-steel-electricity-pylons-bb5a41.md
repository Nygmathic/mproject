The Welsh government has initiated a public consultation on proposals to prohibit the installation of traditional steel lattice pylons for electricity transmission lines under its control. Supported by Plaid Cymru ministers, the policy favors placing cables underground instead of using heavy overhead structures for new routes up to 132 kilovolts, fulfilling a Plaid Cymru manifesto commitment made prior to May's Senedd election.

As detailed in the consultation document launched on Friday, placing electrical lines underground could lessen the visual impact of grid infrastructure, helping to protect landscapes and local amenities. Subterranean circuits are also less susceptible to severe weather and reduce potential harm to birds and wildlife. However, the government acknowledges that burying cables generally involves higher installation and maintenance expenses, alongside potential construction disruptions. Where specific planning criteria are met, the proposed policy would allow wooden poles or equivalent, less intrusive alternatives for overhead lines. The consultation will accept feedback until January 15, 2027.

Welsh Energy Minister Adam Price stated that while evolving energy generation and consumption in Wales require updated grid networks, new infrastructure inevitably affects host communities and the environment. He noted that the government aims to encourage sensitively designed installations that minimize local disruption while offering maximum advantages for Wales.

The government's proposal follows significant local opposition to overhead power schemes, such as a project planned in the Towy Valley where local Plaid politicians raised objections. Developer Green Gen Cymru plans to invest nearly £1 billion by 2030 across Carmarthenshire, Ceredigion, Powys, and Shropshire to construct three major transmission routes connecting onshore wind farms designed by sister firm Bute Energy to the electricity grid. Last year, legal challenges were brought against Green Gen Cymru by groups representing hundreds of landowners.

To address local resistance, Green Gen Cymru recently presented a 22-meter (72-foot) compact pylon designed "with the Welsh landscape in mind." Nevertheless, the developer maintains that placing cables underground costs an average of 4.5 times more than overhead lines. According to the company, adopting underground cabling for the Towy Usk project between Carmarthenshire and Powys would add £500 million to overall costs.
