The Conservative Party has concluded its annual conference in Birmingham, demonstrating a unified front behind leader Kemi Badenoch. This level of internal party cohesion marks a clear contrast to the previous year's event, which was marked by speculation regarding a potential leadership challenge by Robert Jenrick. Echoing a tactic from her speech a year ago—when a missing page from distributed media scripts revealed a plan to abolish stamp duty in England and Northern Ireland—Badenoch's latest address similarly omitted a page outlining a new proposal on inheritance tax.

Although inheritance tax is paid by fewer than one in 20 people, it is considered the nation's most unpopular levy. Proponents of reducing or scrapping it contend that it acts as a secondary tax on income that has already been taxed, while appealing to voters who aspire to become wealthy enough to pay it. Opponents, however, have criticized the plan. Reform UK characterized the proposal as a tax cut aimed at multi-millionaires, arguing instead that raising the income tax threshold would benefit significantly more people.

In her address, Badenoch framed the high-profile defections of Jenrick and Suella Braverman as the removal of negative distractions for the party. She directed sharp criticism at Reform UK, ruling out any post-election or pre-election working relationship with them. Badenoch also expressed anger regarding recent comments by Braverman, who had stated on social media that she was not English because of her Asian heritage and asserted that Englishness is based on ethnicity. Referring to her own mixed-race children, Badenoch stated she did not want to tell her son that Black football players on the national team were not considered English.

Rather than focusing extensively on immigration—leaving that subject and the party's policy to leave the European Convention on Human Rights to Shadow Home Secretary Chris Philp—Badenoch focused her speech on economic trust between Labour and the Conservatives. She described the broader political contest as a "battle for the soul of this nation." Both Badenoch and Andy Burnham presented a contrast in enthusiasm compared to predecessors Rishi Sunak and Sir Keir Starmer. While the Conservatives currently enjoy internal unity, opinion polls indicate their support remains extremely low, even below levels seen during their recent general election defeat. Nevertheless, the party hopes to attract voters in southern England from the Liberal Democrats through policies on childcare, council tax, and inheritance tax, while aiming to overtake Reform UK in the polls.
