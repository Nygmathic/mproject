Motions of no confidence against First Minister Michelle O'Neill and three other Sinn Féin ministers have failed in the Northern Ireland Assembly after failing to secure necessary cross-community support. The debates followed a major political row surrounding a planned Orange Order parade in Portadown, County Armagh, where senior Sinn Féin politicians joined protests that blocked the procession along Garvaghy Road. Police are currently conducting a large-scale criminal investigation into the demonstrations.

To automatically force a minister from office or pass a binding no-confidence motion, approval is required from a majority of both unionists and nationalists within the assembly. Although the motions received the necessary 30 signatures from Democratic Unionist Party (DUP) and Ulster Unionist Party (UUP) members, they ultimately fell short. Traditional Unionist Voice (TUV) member Timothy Gaston, who tabled the motions, questioned public confidence in the impartial administration of justice. DUP Deputy First Minister Emma Little-Pengelly stated that Sinn Féin had damaged executive institutions and shattered public trust, while UUP Leader Jon Burrows criticized the party over accountability. DUP assembly member Jonathan Buckley also accused Sinn Féin of a failure in political leadership regarding adherence to the rule of law.

Defending her actions during the debate, O'Neill described the move by unionist parties as pointless, arguing that they have failed to accept equality or respect rights. She defended her decision to block the Garvaghy Road, characterizing the planned march as triumphalist and sectarian. Other Sinn Féin ministers facing exclusion motions—Finance Minister John O'Dowd, Economy Minister Caoimhe Archibald, and Infrastructure Minister Liz Kimmins—also defended their involvement. O'Dowd expressed pride in supporting local residents, Archibald stated that "the days of Orange domination are done," and Kimmins praised community efforts in support of residents while criticizing regressive loyalist elements.

The controversy has spurred calls from TUV leaders Jim Allister and Timothy Gaston for the DUP to pull Little-Pengelly from her role as deputy first minister, a step that would trigger O'Neill's removal from office. While DUP representative Carla Lockhart noted her party has the power to remove O'Neill, DUP Leader Gavin Robinson told BBC Radio Ulster that he was not in the territory to destabilize the government. Instead, Robinson expressed plans to discuss potential legal sanctions against lawbreakers with the secretary of state on Monday evening, emphasizing that nothing remains off the table as his party works toward consensus.
