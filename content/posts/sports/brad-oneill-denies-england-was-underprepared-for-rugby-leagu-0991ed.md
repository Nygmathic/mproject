Brad O'Neill has dismissed assertions that England entered the Rugby League World Cup underprepared following their 30-12 defeat to Tonga last weekend. Despite the opening loss, head coach Brian McDermott has selected an unchanged 19-man squad for Saturday's Group B-C match against France in Perth. Speaking to BBC Sport, O'Neill, who won the 2026 Super League Man of Steel award, stated that the squad had a great week of preparation and felt physically ready, adding that Tonga was simply the superior side on the day.

The defeat to Tonga marked England's first-ever loss to the nation and served as McDermott’s first fixture in charge since taking over in April. England had flown to Australia ten days prior to their opener, which took place just a fortnight after the 2026 Super League Grand Final. Having played no warm-up games, the squad had not featured in an international match since losing to Australia at Headingley in November of the previous year. McDermott indicated that only minor adjustments rather than a tactical overhaul are required. O'Neill emphasized that England are a "new team" needing time to build combinations through practice. To keep their semi-final hopes alive in the combined six-team pool, England must secure decisive victories against both France and Papua New Guinea on October 30.

In contrast to the men's side, England women's head coach Stuart Barrow has altered his starting lineup ahead of Saturday's match against reigning champions Australia, which kicks off in Perth immediately following the men's game. Super League Woman of Steel Jenna Foubister, aged 19, will start at half-back in place of Georgia Roche, partnering Izzy Rowe. In England's 70-6 opening victory against Wales, Rowe equalled a national individual record for points in a Women's World Cup match by scoring two tries and kicking nine conversions. Additional changes see Molly Jones start at centre and Liv Wood at prop, with Lucie Sams added to the interchanges. The fixture marks the team's first meeting with Australia since suffering a 90-4 defeat in Las Vegas in March 2025. Wing Anna Davies acknowledged the difficulty of the task but noted that the squad approaches the match having grown since their previous encounter.
