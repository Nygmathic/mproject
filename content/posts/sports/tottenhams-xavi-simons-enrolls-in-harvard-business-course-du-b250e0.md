Tottenham Hotspur midfielder Xavi Simons has enrolled in an educational course at Harvard University while he continues his recovery from a long-term knee injury. The 23-year-old Dutch playmaker has been out of action since rupturing his anterior cruciate ligament in April, a physical setback that also prevented him from competing in the World Cup.

Simons has joined Harvard’s Crossover into Business initiative, an online program tailored to help professional athletes enhance their commercial skills. The course pairs participating athletes with Harvard MBA students, who act as mentors to guide them through practical case studies and enterprise management.

As part of his broader rehabilitation process, Simons recently traveled to the United States to progress his physical recovery and visited Harvard’s campus located in Boston. Sharing the news on LinkedIn, Simons explained that while returning to match action remains his primary focus, he wanted to make the most effective use of his time away from the pitch. He highlighted a strong desire to gain a better understanding of the business mechanics behind sports, learn from individuals in different sectors, and focus on personal growth beyond his football career.

Harvard’s Crossover into Business program has previously included participants from professional leagues such as the NBA and NFL, as well as elite tennis figures like two-time major champion and former world number one Victoria Azarenka.

Simons is the latest high-profile figure from European football to pursue studies at the American institution. In June, Real Madrid midfielder Eduardo Camavinga took a short course at Harvard focused on the business of entertainment, media, and sports after being left out of the French World Cup squad. Additionally, following his retirement in 2014, former Manchester United manager Sir Alex Ferguson accepted a "long-term teaching position" at the university.
