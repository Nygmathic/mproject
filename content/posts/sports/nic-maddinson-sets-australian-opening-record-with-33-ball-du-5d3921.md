Australian batsman Nic Maddinson endured a difficult return to Test cricket on the opening day of the first Test against South Africa in Durban. Playing his first Test in nearly a decade, the 34-year-old left-handed opener was dismissed for a 33-ball duck, setting a record for the longest scoreless innings by an Australian opening batter in Test history and the third-longest overall. Maddinson missed 13 deliveries before dragging an attempted cut shot off Marco Jansen onto his wickets. South Africa had also missed an earlier opportunity to dismiss him on his 15th ball, failing to review an lbw decision that would have been overturned.

Maddinson’s selection came just a year after he resumed playing cricket following an April 2025 diagnosis of testicular cancer, which had spread to his lung and lymph nodes. He missed most of the previous year undergoing surgery and chemotherapy. Because of his recovery, his domestic appearances since March 2025 were restricted strictly to limited-overs matches. Former Australian opening batter Matthew Hayden criticized the decision to recall him, stating that selectors were "disrespecting first-class cricket." Speaking to SEN during the lunch break, Cricket Australia chief executive Todd Greenberg acknowledged the debate, stating that selectors viewed Maddinson as a talent while declining to further discuss selection decisions.

Chosen over 23-year-old Cooper Connolly as a replacement for injured all-rounder Cameron Green, Maddinson’s inclusion resulted in Australia fielding an entire lineup aged over 30 for the first time in team history. By the middle of the first day, Australia had fallen to 127-6. Aside from 30-year-old Matt Renshaw, the top order faltered, with 37-year-old Steve Smith, 35-year-old Alex Carey, and 32-year-olds Travis Head, Beau Webster, and Marnus Labuschagne all being dismissed. Labuschagne scored 37 before falling lbw to Wiaan Mulder on the final ball before lunch, extending his streak without a Test century to 44 innings.

The fixture against the world Test champions marks the third in a grueling run of up to 21 Tests for Australia before the end of the next Ashes series. That schedule includes a four-Test home series against New Zealand, a five-Test series in India, a potential World Test Championship final, and another Ashes contest. The heavy workload comes as Australia relies on an experienced bowling attack made up of 38-year-old Nathan Lyon, 36-year-old Mitchell Starc, 35-year-old Josh Hazlewood, and 33-year-old Pat Cummins.
