Former Manchester United captain Roy Keane has described the club as "like a circus" following public criticisms made by defender Lisandro Martinez against the team's leadership. Martinez voiced his dissatisfaction after Tuesday's 2-0 Champions League defeat against Atletico Madrid, a result that extended United's winless run to five matches.

The 28-year-old Argentina international questioned the club's summer transfer activity, stating that United should have invested more heavily in the squad after securing qualification for the Champions League. He specifically called for greater contributions from technical director Jason Wilcox. On Wednesday, Martinez addressed the situation on social media, explaining that he had made the remarks out of "anger and frustration." The defender's contract is set to expire at the conclusion of the current season, although United retains an option to extend the deal by an additional year.

Discussing the matter on The Overlap podcast, Keane argued that Martinez's comments exposed the ongoing turmoil inside the club. The former captain stated that players facing difficult periods should focus on self-examination rather than pointing fingers at executive management. Keane questioned Martinez's standing as a senior figure at the club, describing him as a central defender who has made numerous errors, while also doubting whether the current squad possesses enough strong leadership characters.

Former United defender Gary Neville also criticized the Argentine's outburst, drawing comparisons to statements made by former teammate Wayne Rooney in 2010. Neville contended that Martinez's remarks represented an attack on his fellow squad members, suggesting that senior leaders like Harry Maguire or Bruno Fernandes would be furious to hear an implicit message that the existing roster is inadequate. 

The internal friction arrives at a challenging moment for Manchester United, as they prepare to travel to face Leeds United in a Premier League fixture on Sunday.
