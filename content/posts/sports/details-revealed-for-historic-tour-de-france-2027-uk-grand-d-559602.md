Details for the six British stages launching both the men's and women's 2027 Tour de France have been revealed by event organizer ASO at a ceremony in Paris. The joint hosting schedule marks a historic first, as both editions of the race will commence in the same nation outside of France within the same calendar year.

The three-week men's event begins on July 2 with a 184-kilometer leg from Edinburgh to Carlisle. Starting beneath Edinburgh Castle, the northernmost departure point in the competition's history, the route includes a 2.2-kilometer climb at the Cote de Melrose near the birthplace of rider Oscar Onley. The second stage covers 223 kilometers from Keswick to Liverpool, featuring 2,800 meters of elevation across five climbs. The men's British leg concludes on July 4 with a 223-kilometer journey from Welshpool to Cardiff, incorporating eight categorized hills and roughly 3,000 meters of climbing.

The nine-stage women's event, the Tour de France Femmes avec Zwift, launches on July 30 with an 85.7-kilometer stage from Leeds to Manchester. The following day, riders will tackle a 154.4-kilometer route from Manchester to Sheffield that features seven climbs, including Winnats Pass in the Peak District. The UK host section concludes on August 1 with an 18-kilometer team time trial through central London. Starting on Whitehall, the circuit leads past Trafalgar Square and Tower Bridge before ending on The Mall.

The event's organizers and British officials expressed strong enthusiasm for the announcement. Paul Bush, the managing director of Grand Depart GB 2027, called the routes an "extraordinary, and historic moment" for Scotland, England, and Wales. Additionally, UK sports minister Stephanie Peacock confirmed that £32 million in government backing will support the events to build a lasting cycling legacy for the public.

The UK previously hosted Tour stages in 1974, 1994, 2007, and 2014, with the 2014 Yorkshire start attracting over 2.5 million spectators along the roadside. The news follows a strong 2026 season for British cycling, which saw a record 49 British competitors in the men's and women's World Tour ranks, including five stage victories by Matt Brennan at September's Vuelta a Espana.
