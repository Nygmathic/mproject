Cyclists participating in the Giro di Lombardia delayed the start of the race in Bergamo, Italy, by remaining stationary for five minutes to protest growing safety concerns following recent vehicle incidents. When organizers completed the countdown and waved the initial flag, the peloton refused to move, leaving spectators confused. A second starting flag was displayed five minutes later, permitting the riders to begin their course toward Como.

During the demonstration, Belgian rider and double Olympic champion Remco Evenepoel addressed the crowd about the recurring dangers involving vehicles during races. Evenepoel spoke of British cyclist Fin Tarling, who died in August after colliding with a vehicle at the Volta a Portugal, stating that riders refused to continue accepting injuries or fatalities while competing. The protest followed another safety breach earlier that week at the Tre Valli Varesine race, where cyclists Thomas Gloag and Marcel Camprubi were hit by a police motorbike riding in the opposite direction.

The professional cyclists' union, the CPA, issued a statement that was re-posted on Instagram by competitors, including Evenepoel and Great Britain's Tom Pidcock. According to the CPA, riders compete at high speeds with minimal protection and rely heavily on cycling's governing body, the UCI, alongside event organizers to maintain safe road conditions. The union declared that these authorities are failing in their duty and demanded immediate action to improve safety standards. They explained that the brief protest was intended to demonstrate unity without disrupting the event, emphasizing that their primary goal is to "come home safe" to their families.

The Giro di Lombardia is the last of the five prestigious, long-standing Monument races held each season, as well as the penultimate competition on the UCI World Tour calendar. Slovenian rider Tadej Pogacar, who won the event in each of the past five years, did not participate this year after incurring a season-ending injury during the Vuelta a Espana.
