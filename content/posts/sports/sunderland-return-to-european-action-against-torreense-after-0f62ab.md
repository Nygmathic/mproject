Sunderland return to European action on Thursday night as they travel to Portugal to face Torreense in the Europa League. The match represents the club’s first competitive fixture in European football in 53 years, dating back to a tie against Sporting in Lisbon when Sunderland were a second-tier side holding the FA Cup. Torreense, who currently occupy 15th place in Portugal's second division, qualified for the competition after unexpectedly defeating Sporting in last season's domestic cup final. They enter the match following a 2-1 victory over Lillestrom in Norway last month.

Because Torreense's home ground, the Estádio Manuel Marques, seats only 2,431 people and fails to satisfy UEFA requirements, the match will take place at the 23,888-capacity Estádio Dr Magalhães Pessoa in Leiria. Around 4,000 visiting fans are making the journey, with many securing lodging in Lisbon, Porto, and the pilgrimage destination of Fatima. Meanwhile, Sunderland's squad is based almost two hours away at the Logic hotel. To reduce travel fatigue with an upcoming trip to Poland to play Lech Poznan next week, Sunderland will fly directly from Lisbon to England on Friday ahead of a Sunday fixture against Bournemouth.

Sunderland enter the game seeking momentum after an underwhelming start to their domestic season under manager Régis Le Bris, having secured just one win against Fulham. The team has dealt with notable personnel challenges, including hamstring injuries to Brian Brobbey and Dan Ballard, alongside the absence of a natural right-winger, which has forced defenders Trai Hume and Thomas Meunier to play together. Additionally, £30m deadline-day arrival Malick Fofana has found it difficult to displace Nilson Angulo on the left flank. Off the pitch, Sunderland recently announced that 34-year-old Thor Herdal from Nordsjælland has taken over as set-piece coach, replacing James Brayne.

Torreense present a potential obstacle as local supporters prepare for an October 20 national day commemorating the 1809–10 Lines of Torres Vedras military defences. The Portuguese side features key figures such as 38-year-old captain Stopira, who represented Cape Verde at the recent World Cup, playmaker João Marques, and Ghanaian forward Malik Abubakari.
