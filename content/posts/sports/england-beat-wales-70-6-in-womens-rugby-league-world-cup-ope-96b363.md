England opened their Women's Rugby League World Cup campaign in Group A with a decisive 70-6 victory over debutants Wales in Perth. Head coach Stu Barrow’s side scored 13 tries in total to launch their bid to reach the tournament final for the first time.

The Welsh side briefly drew level early in the opening ten minutes when Paige Travis, who represented England at the World Cup four years ago, scored her country's first ever World Cup try, followed by a conversion from Preston. This effort temporarily negated an early try from England's Amelia Brown. However, the setback prompted a sharp response from England, who ran in seven first-half tries to establish a commanding 40-6 cushion by the interval.

Wing Anna Davies and debutant Connie Boyd both secured hat-tricks for England, while Jasmin Strange, a heritage player born outside England, also scored on her debut. Izzy Rowe recorded 26 individual points through two tries and nine conversions, equalling the record for the most points scored by an England player in a single Women's World Cup fixture. Tries were also added by Georgia Wilson and Hollie-Mae Dodd, alongside a second for Brown, who gathered a Welsh kick behind her own try line and ran 109 metres to score one of the longest tries in the sport's history.

England’s attack continued to penetrate the Welsh defense as the match wore on. Wales, led by captain Bethan Dainton, entered the match having played just 13 international fixtures as a nation and having suffered heavy defeats in their previous two meetings with England. Although Wales displayed promise in attack, fatigue became evident, enabling England to close the match with three late tries in the final five minutes.

Despite the heavy result, Wales head coach Tom Brindle will look to take positive experience from competing at the World Cup level. Wales will turn their attention to their next fixture against Samoa on Friday, which offers their best opportunity for a result in a challenging group that also includes reigning world champions Australia.
