According to the latest Guardian Essential poll, public enthusiasm for the Melbourne Cup has dropped to a record low, with more than two-thirds of surveyed Australians expressing little to zero interest in the annual horse race. The survey of 1,003 Australians conducted in late October shows a continuous decline in support, with only 9% of respondents reporting high interest in the event. This represents nearly half the proportion recorded in 2025 and marks a seven percentage point drop from that year.

The poll reveals that combined enthusiasm—covering both high and moderate interest—has fallen to 32%, the lowest figure since Essential began tracking the metric in November 2022. Conversely, the total proportion of respondents claiming low or no interest reached a record 68%, reflecting a 12-point surge from last year. Historical polling shows the combined low and zero interest group stood at 52% in November 2022, 62% in 2023, 52% in 2024, and 56% in 2025. In the latest survey, 23% indicated moderate interest (down three points), 29% reported low interest (up five points), and 39% expressed no interest at all (up eight points).

Essential Media chair Peter Lewis noted that the historic race has become "white noise for many" citizens. Lewis identified negative perceptions surrounding gambling as the primary driver of declining interest, supported by survey data showing that half of Australians believe the event promotes unhealthy gambling habits—an increase of three percentage points from 2025. Animal welfare concerns also factor in, with 36% stating the Cup normalises animal cruelty, though this decreased three points from 2025. Lewis added that the trend reflects a broader reduction in shared national experiences influenced by targeted advertising algorithms. The "nup to the Cup" movement, supported by high-profile politicians and celebrities, has also contributed to decreasing engagement.

Despite the drop in engagement, 62% of Australians still consider the Melbourne Cup a unique element of national identity, though this is down from 78% in 2019. Overall, 48% of participants stated they are less interested in the Cup than in the past. Betting habits further reflect this shift: only 12% of voters said they regularly wager on horse races and plan to bet on Tuesday's race, down five points from 2025. In contrast, 46% said they have no interest in the event and will not place a bet, up six points. Meanwhile, 25% plan to watch and bet despite rarely wagering on horses, and 17% intend to watch without betting.
