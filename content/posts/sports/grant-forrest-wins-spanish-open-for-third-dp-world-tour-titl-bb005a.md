Scottish golfer Grant Forrest earned the largest victory of his career on Sunday after securing a last-hole birdie to win the Spanish Open. The 33-year-old captured a first prize of £480,000 along with qualifying spots for next year's Masters and The Open. The result also elevated him 156 spots in the world rankings, moving him up to 140th place. The triumph marks Forrest's third DP World Tour title and his first professional win outside of Scotland.

The path to victory opened up after his playing partner, Victor Perez, missed an opportunity to pass Eugenio Chacarra, who held the lead in the clubhouse. Speaking to BBC Scotland, Forrest revealed that he felt extreme nerves while preparing for the decisive putt, noting that his hands were shaking before he sank it. He attributed his ability to stay composed throughout the day to recent work on his breathing routines. Reflecting on his professional career, which began more than a decade ago, he described the win as providing significant relief and validation.

Following the tournament, Forrest experienced what he called "a surreal 24 hours" with little sleep as he traveled from Madrid to New Delhi for the DP World India Championship. Looking ahead, he expressed excitement about making his Masters debut in April, having previously attended the event in 2019 solely as a spectator. The victory also grants him his third appearance at The Open, following past participation at Muirfield in 2013 and Carnoustie in 2018, with his next outing set for St Andrews.

Forrest originally earned his DP World Tour card at the conclusion of the 2018 season, stepping up from the European Challenge Tour alongside fellow Scottish players Robert MacIntyre and David Law. Discussing the current form of Scottish golf, Forrest noted that recent tournament wins by MacIntyre, Connor Syme, and Scott Jamieson give positive energy to the nation's players, reinforcing the belief that another victory is always within reach.
