Former tennis star Rafael Nadal is set to join the board of directors at Spanish football club Real Madrid. Club members will be asked to officially approve the 40-year-old's appointment during a meeting scheduled for later this month.

Nadal retired from professional sport in 2024 following an accomplished career that featured 22 Grand Slam titles, two Olympic gold medals, and five Davis Cup victories with Spain. A lifelong supporter of Real Madrid, he holds official club member number 67,778. In response to the news, he posted a photograph of himself as a child wearing the team's kit on social media platform X.

In his post, Nadal expressed gratitude to club president Florentino Perez and the rest of the board for proposing his inclusion. He noted that he had accepted the opportunity with excitement, an eagerness to learn, and a commitment to helping the organization in any way possible. In its announcement, Real Madrid praised Nadal as "one of the greatest legends in the history of sport" and highlighted the pride of having an athlete of his global recognition join their executive team to contribute to the club's present and future direction.

At present, the precise responsibilities Nadal will assume on the board remain unspecified. His upcoming appointment comes during a period of leadership adjustments under Perez, who has served as president since 2009 and earned re-election to a new four-year term in June. Perez recently hired Jose Mourinho for a second spell as head coach in an effort to revitalize the club after two consecutive seasons without winning a trophy across La Liga, the Champions League, or the Copa del Rey. Real Madrid currently sits in third place in the league standings after 11 matches, three points behind defending champions Barcelona.
