Derry City captain Michael Duffy stressed that winning trophies remains the team's main focus following their 2-0 victory over Galway United in the FAI Cup semi-final at Eamonn Deacy Park on Saturday. Duffy scored both goals to secure Derry's spot in the final against Waterford on 8 November at the Aviva Stadium. Bohemians' 2-0 loss to Waterford on Friday meant an extra European place via the league was unavailable, but Duffy indicated that securing European football was merely a secondary benefit compared to winning silverware.

Speaking to BBC Sport NI, Duffy explained that the squad was advised to ignore the previous night's result and focus strictly on reaching the final. The victory comes amid a turbulent season for Derry City, who appointed interim boss Mark Connolly in August following the dismissal of Tiernan Lynch. Since the former defender took charge, the club has eliminated relegation concerns while advancing to within 90 minutes of its seventh cup title and its first since 2022.

Duffy highlighted Connolly's positive impact on the squad, noting that the manager has encouraged the team to play "free-flowing football" and express themselves. According to Duffy, Connolly urges defenders to play from the back and take risks in training and matches, reassuring players that he will take responsibility if they lose possession. The captain said this supportive environment has made playing enjoyable, fostering dominant performances and strong effort across the team over recent weeks.

Looking ahead to the upcoming final, Duffy anticipated a challenging match against Graham Coughlan's Waterford side, pointing out that all prior contests between the two teams this year have been difficult. Leading his hometown club out at the Aviva Stadium will mark a proud moment for Duffy, who expressed hope that he will walk onto the field with his young son before taking the cup back to Derry.
