Cardiff City and Wales defender Dylan Lawlor has highlighted the tactical similarities between his club manager, Brian Barry-Murphy, and national team head coach Craig Bellamy. Speaking on the BBC's Welsh language podcast Y Coridor Ansicrwydd, the 20-year-old explained that both coaches possess a detailed approach and share a matching vision for the game. Lawlor noted that this alignment makes transitioning between club and international duties straightforward. He compared their managerial philosophy to that of former Liverpool manager Jurgen Klopp, stating that both leaders concentrate on their own team's strengths rather than worrying about the opposition.

Lawlor has established himself as a key figure in Barry-Murphy's side, which earned promotion to the Championship last season. Barry-Murphy took charge of Cardiff in June 2025 after the club suffered relegation at the end of the 2024–25 season. Lawlor made his league debut in the final game of that relegation campaign, entering as a second-half substitute against Norwich City under interim manager Aaron Ramsey. Lawlor shared that Ramsey, a fellow former pupil of Ysgol Cwm Rhymni in Caerphilly County, served as an inspiration and provided valuable guidance when starting his coaching career.

At the international level, Lawlor received his first senior Wales cap from Bellamy in September 2025 and has made six appearances over the past year. However, a knee injury sustained while playing for Wales against Ghana in June forced him to miss pre-season training and the start of Cardiff's Championship campaign. Although he recently returned as a substitute during Cardiff's win over Charlton prior to the recent international break, the comeback arrived too late for inclusion in Wales' four recent Nations League fixtures. Lawlor admitted feeling gutted to miss out on facing high-profile opponents such as Portugal's Cristiano Ronaldo and Norway's Erling Haaland. He is now targeting full fitness for November, hoping to feature in Wales' upcoming matches against Portugal and Norway to test himself against top competition.
