FIFA President Gianni Infantino told BBC Sport that he retains the backing of the overwhelming majority of global football governing bodies, dismissing questions regarding his potential resignation following the collapse of a controversial commercial strategy. The FIFA chief struck a confident stance despite recently facing intense scrutiny over the FIFA Forward Enterprise proposal, a short-lived initiative that would have transferred a share of the organization's commercial rights to a private investment entity.

The abandoned plan triggered severe opposition within the sport. Two months ago, leaders from UEFA, Concacaf, and the Asian Football Confederation submitted a letter accusing Infantino of undermining trust "through deception". Last week, UEFA general secretary Theodore Theodoridis called on FIFA to publish all key materials concerning the plan by November 2. Although Infantino apologized to the FIFA Council for deficiencies in consultation and communication, Council members cautioned that he would be considered unfit for office if he failed to provide the requested records. Infantino declined to give a precise timeline for releasing the paperwork, stating that the matter must follow a legal process.

Despite these tensions, Infantino has effectively consolidated his political standing through international outreach, visiting locations such as Japan, Morocco, Cape Verde, and the Dominican Republic. He secured endorsements from all African member associations, while Asian Football Confederation president Sheikh Salman reversed his earlier position to offer full backing. With no alternative candidates yet coming forward before the November 18 deadline, Infantino appears set to run unopposed for a fourth term in the upcoming March presidential election. Addressing the criticism, Infantino stated that while differing viewpoints are a natural part of democracy, the final consensus of member associations must be honored.

Infantino offered his comments from Baku, Azerbaijan, where he was attending the first FIFA Under-15 World Cup and Festival. The youth tournament features teams from 189 member nations, including major sides like England, France, Brazil, Argentina, and Spain. The event also marks the return of Russia to a FIFA-sanctioned competition, representing the nation's first participation since FIFA and UEFA suspended its teams in February 2022 after the invasion of Ukraine.
