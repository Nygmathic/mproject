Scottish golfer Michael Stewart ended the Alfred Dunhill Links Championship tied for fourth place alongside England's Tommy Fleetwood at 17 under par, two strokes behind tournament winner Adrian Meronk of Poland. The performance by the 36-year-old world number 979 has created a major scheduling conflict. By finishing in the top 10 at the event, which took place across Kingsbarns, Carnoustie, and St Andrews, Stewart secured entry into the lucrative Spanish Open. However, that tournament directly conflicts with the Gleneagles Masters, the final event of the Tartan Pro Tour season.

The contrast in Stewart's earnings highlights his rapid shift in fortunes. Just days before collecting nearly 100 times as much at the Dunhill Links, the Ayrshire-based former Walker Cup player earned £2,500 for a runner-up finish at a Tartan Pro Tour event at Duddingston Golf Club near Edinburgh. Despite the higher payout available in Spain, Stewart stated his intention to play at Gleneagles to protect his second-place standing in the Tartan Pro Tour rankings. Securing that position guarantees a card for next season's second-tier Challenge Tour, which he confirmed was his primary target at the start of the year. He noted that multiple players behind him could win and overtake his position if he were to skip the final event.

Stewart described his play as "without doubt" the best week of his career, providing a significant boost to his self-belief. It was only his sixth appearance on the DP World Tour, having missed the cut in all five of his prior starts. While he previously tied for 52nd at the 2023 Open Championship at Royal Liverpool, he recalled feeling disheartened after his final round there, contrasting it with his positive result at the Dunhill Links.

Defending champion Robert MacIntyre, who finished a stroke behind Stewart tied for sixth at 16 under par alongside Brooks Koepka, praised his fellow Scot's achievements. MacIntyre noted that he occasionally plays rounds with Stewart and highlighted his strong short game.
