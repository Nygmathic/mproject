England began the defence of their Wheelchair World Cup title with an emphatic 98-18 victory against Wales in Wollongong. The two-time world champions scored 18 tries while limiting Wales to just three. Despite the dominant result, Rob Hawkins admitted that England "were not playing to our standards" after committing minor errors and giving away points throughout the match.

Hawkins delivered a remarkable individual performance for England, tallying 52 points through 11 tries and four conversions. Teammate Nathan Collins added four tries and successfully converted nine of his 11 attempts, while Joe Coyd, Jack Brown, and Mason Billington also registered tries. Wales remained competitive in the opening 20 minutes, using tries from Stuart Williams and Sid Ramsey to pull within six points at 18-12. However, England responded by scoring 28 unanswered points to build a 46-12 lead by half-time before adding 10 more tries in the second half. Ramsey scored a late consolation try in the 71st minute, bringing his personal total to two tries for the match.

Following the game, Hawkins noted that the performance served as a stepping stone as the squad works toward peaking at the appropriate moment in the tournament. He framed the match as a decent beginning while emphasizing the need to eliminate careless mistakes in future outings.

In the other Group A match, Ireland established a new international points record by defeating the United States 110-10. Ireland scored 20 tries in total, surpassing their previous highest match total of 92 points set against Scotland during last year's Celtic Cup. Joseph Calcott led Ireland's scoring with seven tries, while Cian Horgan crossed the line six times.

Group A play continues on 3 November. Ireland will take on Wales at 05:00 GMT, followed by England facing the United States at 07:30 GMT.
