Four-time Grand Slam champion Naomi Osaka has stated that she believes her game is close to its previous elite level, even as she routinely contemplates how long her professional tennis career will continue. Preparing for the Pan Pacific Open in Tokyo, the 29-year-old Japanese player explained that her personal priorities have shifted following the birth of her daughter, Shai.

Osaka admitted that she would consider retiring from tennis if her three-year-old requested that she reduce her travel, stating that her child is her primary focus. While she invests as much energy as possible into her sport, she noted that parenting responsibilities prevent her from dedicating her entire schedule to tennis. Despite this, she expressed pride in her current achievements and mentioned that she is working on relearning certain instincts while establishing a strong foundation to advance further in tournaments.

The former world number one built her early career by winning the US Open and Australian Open twice each between 2018 and 2021. She subsequently took time away from the sport in 2021 to focus on her mental health, followed by a 15-month absence due to pregnancy. Osaka returned to competitive tennis in 2023 after giving birth.

Since her return, Osaka has moved up to 19th in the world rankings. She is competing as the seventh seed at this week's tournament in Tokyo, where she meets Italy's Jasmine Paolini in the opening round. Her recent path includes reaching the quarter-finals at Wimbledon, during which she defeated top seed Aryna Sabalenka, and securing a runner-up finish at Bad Homburg. She also reached the last 16 at the French Open and the US Open, where her run ended against eventual champion Elena Rybakina. Earlier in the North American hardcourt swing, she made the semi-finals in Washington and the last eight in Toronto.

Reflecting on her capacity to contend for major trophies once again, Osaka indicated that she is "not far off" from her peak level and remains excited to keep building on her progress.
