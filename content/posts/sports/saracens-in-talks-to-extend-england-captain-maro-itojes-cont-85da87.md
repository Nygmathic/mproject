Saracens are actively working to finalize a contract extension with England captain Maro Itoje beyond his current deal, which is set to expire after the next Rugby World Cup. The second-row forward will be 33 by the end of the tournament, making this potentially his last opportunity to pursue a long-standing interest in playing across the Channel.

According to the French newspaper Midi-Olympique, European champions Bordeaux-Begles and Perpignan are among the Top 14 clubs alerted to Itoje's potential availability. Racing 92 have also shown interest in him in the past. While Saracens are optimistic about securing a new agreement, they face stiff financial competition. The French top flight boasts an €11m (£9.2m) salary cap—strengthened by a major five-year broadcast deal with Canal Plus starting next season—which easily exceeds the English Premiership's £6.4m cap.

However, English regulations allow each club to exclude one player from the salary cap under a "marquee spot" designation, giving Saracens additional leverage to match foreign offers. Furthermore, staying in England keeps Itoje eligible for a central national contract worth roughly £150,000 a year. Itoje has spent his entire senior career at Saracens since his teenage years, remaining with the team even during their single season in the Championship following breaches of the salary cap.

A move to France would halt Itoje's international career because the Rugby Football Union maintains a policy against selecting players based abroad. This rule, designed to protect the domestic league, has faced growing scrutiny. Players such as Ellis Genge and former captain Courtney Lawes have argued for the policy to be altered or scrapped entirely. 

Departing for France at the end of a World Cup cycle has become a common path for English stars. Former teammates including Owen Farrell, Manu Tuilagi, Kyle Sinckler, Lewis Ludlam, and Jack Willis have all recently joined Top 14 clubs. While moving abroad would end Itoje's Test commitments, the French domestic season remains demanding, featuring 26 regular-season fixtures alongside European matches and play-offs for the top six teams.
