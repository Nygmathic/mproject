Katie Boulter has earned a spot in the Japan Open semi-finals and regained her status as the British number one woman after coming from behind to defeat Antonia Ruzic in Osaka. The 30-year-old, who entered the tournament as the eighth seed, overcame a set and break deficit to beat the 23-year-old Croatian world number 91 by a score of 3-6, 6-3, 6-3 in a two-hour match.

The victory ensures that Boulter will climb back into the top 50 of the world rankings and move past Sonay Kartal when the official WTA standings are published on Monday. Kartal had taken the top British spot just days earlier after advancing to the final in Wuhan, creating a 55-point lead over Boulter. Reaching the last four in Osaka was required to erase that gap, marking Boulter's first semi-final appearance since competing at Queen's in June.

Boulter found herself under early pressure, struggling on serve throughout the first two sets by winning only 60 percent of her first-serve points, recording four double faults, and giving up seven break points. However, she managed to gain momentum by stepping closer to the baseline to apply pressure. After avoiding a double-break deficit in the third game of the second set, Boulter held serve in the seventh game to spark an eight-game winning run, building a 4-0 advantage in the final set. Although she was unable to serve out the match at 5-2, she ended the contest by breaking Ruzic's serve without losing a point.

Speaking after the victory, Boulter praised her opponent's performance and noted that she tried to stay resilient despite needing some luck in the second set. Preparing to face Czech player Katerina Siniakova for a spot in the final, Boulter called her opponent "one hell of a player" and stated she had nothing to lose.
