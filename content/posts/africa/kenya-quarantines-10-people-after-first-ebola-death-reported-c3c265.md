Health officials in Kenya have quarantined 10 individuals and are searching for 57 additional contacts after a man died from Ebola in Nairobi, marking the country's first recorded case of the disease. The patient, who passed away in a hospital on Monday and was buried on Tuesday following health safety guidelines, had traveled to Kenya from the Democratic Republic of Congo.

The incident has raised serious questions about screening measures across the region. Jean Kaseya, head of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, told BBC News that the man spent roughly a month being ill while traveling through multiple cities in the Democratic Republic of Congo, driving into Uganda, and flying into Nairobi without detection. Kenyan health official Patrick Amoth suggested the patient may have taken medication that concealed his symptoms, while Kaseya emphasized that border points lack rapid diagnostic tests and rely on temperature checks, which fail when fever-reducing drugs are used.

Concerns over screening were echoed by Dr. Dennis Miskellah from Kenya's main doctors' union, who told the BBC that border lapses were evident at major entry points, including borders with Uganda and Tanzania. He criticized relying on travelers to voluntarily report symptoms, noting that stigma encourages people to hide their illness. In response, Kenyan officials urged the public not to panic, assuring heightened surveillance at border crossings and airports. President William Ruto stated that rapid response teams were active, while his spokesman noted that over 650,000 travelers had been screened and 267 samples tested.

Four major hospitals, including Nairobi Hospital, have set aside facilities for Ebola care. Additionally, Health Minister Aden Duale told television station NTV that the government is considering converting a Nanyuki facility—originally planned as a U.S. military treatment center before being halted by courts—into an infectious disease hospital. The ongoing outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo involves the rare Bundibugyo strain, which has no approved vaccine or treatment and has killed more than 4,000 people this year. Neighboring Uganda, which was declared free of Ebola in August, stated that the patient spent less than 24 hours in the country and registered a normal temperature before departure.
