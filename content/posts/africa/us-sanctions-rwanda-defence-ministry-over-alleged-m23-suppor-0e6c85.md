The United States has imposed sanctions on Rwanda's defence ministry over allegations that Kigali is providing military support to the M23 rebel group in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo. Announcing the measures, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent stated that Rwanda must cease backing M23 and pull its forces out of the area. According to the US Treasury, the Rwandan defence ministry instructed thousands of soldiers to fight alongside M23 while supplying weapons, equipment, logistics, and training. Rwanda has denied supporting the rebels.

Washington also targeted Zigama Credit and Savings Society, a major financial institution serving Rwandan security forces. American authorities accuse the bank of facilitating tens of millions of dollars in transactions for M23 and its political wing, the Congo River Alliance, funding Chinese military equipment for Rwandan troops, and aiding the transfer of gold from rebel-controlled territory to Kigali. The sanctions freeze designated assets under US jurisdiction and prohibit US individuals and businesses from conducting transactions with the sanctioned bodies.

The actions are intended to reinforce the Washington Accords, a peace agreement brokered by the US and signed by the Rwandan and Congolese presidents in December 2025. However, fighting has persisted since early 2025, when M23 captured Goma and Bukavu. The rebel group recently joined a new coalition called the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation, headed by former Congolese President Joseph Kabila, which aims to depose the current administration. These latest penalties follow earlier American sanctions against Rwanda's military and four commanders in March 2026, as well as a gold refinery and three mining companies in June 2026.

The foreign ministry of the Democratic Republic of Congo welcomed the penalties, urging Rwanda to abide by its commitments under the peace agreement and withdraw its troops. Conversely, Rwandan government spokesperson Yolande Makolo told the BBC that security commitments in the Washington Accords—specifically neutralizing the FDLR, a militia tied to the 1994 genocide—remain unfulfilled. Makolo added that Rwanda is prepared to honor the accords if the Congolese government fulfills its obligations concurrently and the mediator holds all sides accountable.

In addition to financial pressure, Kigali faces questions over its international sports partnerships. US Senators Jeff Merkley and Marsha Blackburn sent a letter to NBA commissioner Adam Silver asking the league to reconsider its commercial relationship with Rwanda due to human rights concerns.
