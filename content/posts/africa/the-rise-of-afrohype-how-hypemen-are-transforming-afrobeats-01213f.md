A sub-genre of Afrobeats known as Afrohype or lamba is transforming the live entertainment scene across West and Central Africa. Performing energetic ad-libbed vocals and rhythmic sounds over popular instrumental tracks, hypemen and hypewomen have evolved from background motivators into major event attractions. The rise in booking costs for main musical artists following the COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated this trend, with promoters increasingly turning to hype performers. Top figures in the field reportedly earn between 5 million and 8 million naira (£2,844 to £4,550) for sets lasting 30 to 60 minutes.

One prominent practitioner is Toby Shang, born Tobi Michael Adeyeye, who launched his career after gaining attention during his history degree studies at the University of Lagos. In addition to releasing music EPs and the hit song Shedibalabala, Shang founded the School of Hype masterclass to train emerging talent. Although the sub-genre is predominantly male, several women have achieved prominence, including Yaoundé-based artist Lady Ponce, the late Deborah Francis, and Lagos radio presenter Melody Adigo.

The art form stems from centuries-old praise-singing traditions, including West African oral historians known as griots and American negro spirituals. These practices laid the groundwork for the 1980s atalaku tradition in Kinshasa, which subsequently influenced Côte d’Ivoire’s coupé-décalé scene and modern African nightclub culture. Customary practices such as spraying money on performers during celebrations remain integral to the hype experience, and the genre has gained formal recognition through events like the Ghana DJ awards.

Despite its widespread presence at weddings, raves, and corporate events, the sub-genre has drawn criticism. Some observers view the performances as a noise nuisance or accuse them of encouraging fraudulent lifestyles. Cultural critic Dami Ajayi noted that while hype performers display "fine mic control" and sharp humor, their ad-libs can sometimes be offensive or misogynistic. However, practitioners like Shang argue that hypemen perform a vital service by breaking down social inhibitions and encouraging event guests of all ages to dance.
