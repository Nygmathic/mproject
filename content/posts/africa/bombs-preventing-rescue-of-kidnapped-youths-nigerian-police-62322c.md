Nigerian police have discovered a camp where 19 prospective National Youth Service Corps members are being held hostage in the south-eastern state of Imo, but explosives surrounding the facility are stalling a direct assault. According to Police Commissioner Audu Bosso, surveillance drones helped locate the hideout after the recruits were abducted last week. Bosso stated that while officials tracked down the kidnappers, intelligence indicates the perimeter is rigged with homemade bombs, creating a dangerous obstacle for security forces.

The hostages were seized on Thursday near Umunoha in Imo state while traveling in buses from Ibadan in the south-west to orientation camps in Abia and Akwa Ibom states. The exact coordinates of the hideout have not been made public, and police have yet to identify the perpetrators. To prepare for an eventual rescue mission, law enforcement agencies are deploying heavy assets, including armoured vehicles and bulldozers.

The kidnappers are demanding a ransom of 50 million naira ($37,680; £28,440) for the hostages' release. Omotosho Adewale, whose child was taken, told BBC News that he cannot afford the sum and appealed to the government for intervention. Adewale said he had not eaten in three days due to distress. Although Nigerian law prohibits ransom payments and officials repeatedly advise families against giving in to demands, relatives often experience immense pressure to secure their family members' freedom.

Kidnapping for ransom by criminal gangs and armed groups remains a critical security challenge across Nigeria, though large-scale abductions are less frequent in the south than in northern regions. The NYSC program requires most university and polytechnic graduates to spend a year working and performing community service across the nation to foster unity. Although the agency avoids assigning graduates to areas designated as high-risk due to violence, most parts of the south-east were not previously listed as dangerous. Past abductions in the south-east have sometimes been tied to separatist organizations, including the Indigenous People of Biafra, but authorities have not linked this kidnapping to any specific group. The incident has renewed calls for structural reforms to safeguard traveling youths.
