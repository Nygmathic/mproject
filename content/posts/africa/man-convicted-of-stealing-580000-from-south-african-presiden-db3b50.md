A South African court has found Imanuwela David guilty of stealing more than $500,000 from a property owned by President Cyril Ramaphosa. On Monday, David was convicted of theft and conspiracy to commit housebreaking with intent to steal. The incident took place in February 2020 at the Phala Phala cattle farm in Limpopo province. Froliana Joseph, who worked as a cleaner at the location, was also convicted of conspiracy to commit housebreaking with intent to steal, while her brother, Ndilinasho Joseph, was cleared of all charges.

David was charged with taking $580,000 in cash that had been kept inside a sofa on the farm. Following the break-in, he spent the money on a house, diamond-encrusted Rolex watches, two Volkswagens, a Porsche, and three Mercedes vehicles, one of which was intended for his girlfriend. Although David asserted in court that his wealth was generated from businesses involving Bitcoin and diamonds, the court dismissed his claims. Judges further noted that David was the only one of the two convicted individuals who had stolen money remaining in his bank account. Pre-sentencing in the case is scheduled for 13 November.

The theft led to widespread political repercussions in a controversy referred to as the "Farmgate scandal." The crime remained largely secret until 2022, when Arthur Fraser, a former South African intelligence chief allied with former President Jacob Zuma, disclosed the matter. Fraser alleged that Ramaphosa had concealed the burglary from both tax authorities and law enforcement. The president acknowledged that the theft occurred but maintained he was innocent of any wrongdoing.

An independent panel determined in 2022 that the president might have questions to answer over the incident, though members of parliament voted against establishing an inquiry into his impeachment. Legal proceedings surrounding the matter continued into this year. In May, the constitutional court determined that parliament acted unlawfully when it set aside the panel's report. Ramaphosa later used a provincial court in July to block the parliamentary investigation.
