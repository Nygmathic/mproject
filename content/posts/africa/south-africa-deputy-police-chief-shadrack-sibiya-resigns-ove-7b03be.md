Lt Gen Shadrack Sibiya, the 59-year-old deputy chief of South Africa's police force, has resigned from his post three weeks after his arrest on charges relating to sexual offences. According to a spokesperson for the police, a lawyer representing Sibiya submitted his formal resignation letter on Monday during his disciplinary hearing. The former deputy chief has been kept in custody continuously since his arrest three weeks ago.

Sibiya faces serious criminal charges, including human trafficking, sexually grooming a 16-year-old girl, and raping an 18-year-old woman. He denies all allegations of human trafficking, rape, and grooming. Although Sibiya admitted to having sexual relations with the 18-year-old woman, he maintained that the encounter was entirely consensual. In a statement posted on X, police spokesperson Athlenda Mathe clarified that because Sibiya tendered his resignation while a disciplinary case was actively pending against him, his departure from the police force is officially treated as a "dishonourable discharge."

In addition to the sexual offence charges, Sibiya was already placed on suspension last year over allegations linking him to leaders in organised crime, which he denies. These accusations against him surfaced during the Madlanga Commission, an extensive public inquiry examining allegations of police corruption and cooperation with criminal syndicates. Within the commission's proceedings, Sibiya was accused of interfering with official investigations regarding political killings in the province of KwaZulu-Natal. He was also accused of taking a cash gift and 20 impalas from an alleged criminal kingpin to facilitate a police contract, an assertion he rejected.

Over the past year, the Madlanga Commission has transfixed South Africa as it exposed widespread connections between police officials and organized crime figures. President Cyril Ramaphosa established the public inquiry after the KwaZulu-Natal police chief held a press conference where he publicly accused the then Police Minister, Senzo Mchunu, of associating and working with criminal gangs. While Mchunu denies all the accusations brought against him, he has nevertheless been suspended from his post.
