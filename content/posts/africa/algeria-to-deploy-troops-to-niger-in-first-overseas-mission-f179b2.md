Algeria has resolved to send military forces into Niger, marking the country's first foreign deployment since it dispatched troops to Egypt during the 1973 Arab-Israeli war. The decision utilizes a 2020 constitutional provision permitting overseas military operations for the first time, representing a notable departure from Algeria's historical policy against sending forces abroad. The move follows a visit to Algiers by Niger's defence minister seeking assistance to counter armed groups, several of which maintain ties to al-Qaeda or Islamic State.

The deployment secured approval after an emergency session in Algeria's upper house of parliament on Sunday. According to the AFP news agency, 148 of the 174 senators voted in favor of the proposal, which was subsequently supported by the lower house. Official details regarding the exact number of soldiers involved and the timing of their deployment have not been disclosed. Speaking to reporters following the vote, Algerian senator Belkacem Boulghiti stated that troops were not being deployed to wage war, but rather "for a mission to secure economic infrastructure."

Algerian forces are expected to guard energy installations located in northern Niger close to the border with Libya. Algeria maintains key economic interests in the region, including a project to build a pipeline to transport gas from Nigeria through Niger into Algeria. Furthermore, Algeria's state-owned energy enterprise, Sonatrach, signed an agreement with Niger's petroleum ministry for oil exploration in the Kafra region. Reuters reported that two exploration wells in the area are estimated to hold 168 million and 100 million barrels of oil.

This military support comes as the two neighboring states work to repair their diplomatic relationship following recent strain. Algeria previously denounced the 2023 coup that ousted Niger's elected president, Mohamed Bazoum, and elevated General Abdourahamane Tchiani to power. Tensions mounted further in 2025 when Algeria shot down a Malian drone near its border, prompting Niger to recall its ambassador in solidarity with Mali's military leadership. However, bilateral relations have since recovered. Tchiani visited Algiers in February 2026, and in late August, Algerian President Abdelmadjid Tebboune dispatched fighter jets to help quell a coup attempt in Niger, alongside donating four military helicopters.
