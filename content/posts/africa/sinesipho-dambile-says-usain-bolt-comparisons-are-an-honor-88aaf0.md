South African sprinter Sinesipho Dambile considers comparisons between himself and Jamaican legend Usain Bolt to be an honor, stating that "pressure is a privilege" as he works to establish himself among the world's elite runners. Speaking on the Warm Up Track podcast, the 24-year-old Commonwealth 200m champion revealed that he received encouraging advice from Bolt during the 2025 World Athletics Championships in Tokyo, where the eight-time Olympic gold medallist urged him to enjoy his career.

Dambile's standout season was capped by winning Commonwealth gold in Birmingham in July, becoming the first South African athlete to claim the 200m title at the event. He previously finished eighth in the 200m final in Tokyo after running a personal best of 19.97 seconds in the semi-finals. He lowered his personal record further to 19.74 seconds at the Doha Diamond League meeting in June, recording the fourth-fastest time globally in 2026. Dambile also finished second to American Kenny Bednarek at the Diamond League final in Brussels—ahead of Botswana's 2024 Olympic champion Letsile Tebogo—and placed fourth in the inaugural Ultimate Championships.

While the 200m remains his primary discipline, Dambile intends to expand into the 100m to emulate Bolt's career path. He contrasts his relaxed, fan-friendly approach on the track with the bolder showmanship of Olympic 100m champion Noah Lyles. Dambile credits his progress to a strong team spirit among South African sprinters, highlighting the mentorship of veteran athlete Akani Simbine. Together with Simbine, Bayanda Walaza, and Bradley Nkoana, Dambile helped win 4x100m gold at the World Relays in Guangzhou last year.

Like Simbine, Dambile trained under the TuksSport program at the University of Pretoria, alongside sprinter Gift Leotlela and 2025 world indoor 800m champion Prudence Sekgodiso. Following a challenging 2024 season marred by injuries, Dambile has bounced back from his early success as a double medallist at the 2021 Under-20 World Championships in Nairobi. Looking forward, the South African champion is preparing for the upcoming World Championships in Beijing and the 2028 Olympic Games in Los Angeles, aiming to capture both titles and become the greatest 200m runner in track history.
