Two European women who were held captive for more than a year in Niger have been freed and handed over to authorities in the Libyan city of Benghazi. The individuals were identified as 76-year-old Austrian aid worker Eva Gretzmacher and 69-year-old Swiss community worker Claudia Abbt. Both women had lived in Niger for several years prior to being kidnapped separately in the northern city of Agadez in January and April of last year.

The Austrian and Swiss governments have both confirmed the release of their citizens. Swiss administration officials met Abbt in Benghazi to provide medical care, reporting that she was doing well under the circumstances before starting her journey back to Switzerland. Video broadcast by Libya's al-Masar TV showed the two women entering an ambulance and being examined by a health worker. Speaking in French, both women expressed gratitude to eastern Libya’s military commander, Khalifa Haftar, and his son, Saddam Haftar, for securing their release. Privately owned outlet Air Info Agadez reported that a plane was expected to depart Benghazi on Thursday to drop Gretzmacher in Vienna before carrying Abbt home.

Authorities have not disclosed the circumstances surrounding the release, the captors' identities, or any demands, though earlier reports connected the abductions to Islamic State militants. Austrian Foreign Minister Beate Meinl-Reisinger stated that Gretzmacher’s freedom was achieved after "intensive diplomatic efforts" involving close cooperation with regional and international partners over 21 months. Swiss Federal Councillor Ignazio Cassis also expressed relief, noting that Switzerland established a multi-agency task force following the abductions and maintained regular communication with Austria.

The abductions occurred amidst ongoing security turmoil across the Sahel, where Niger and nearby nations have experienced years of jihadist violence and kidnappings carried out by groups affiliated with Islamic State and al-Qaeda. Niger has been governed by a military regime under General Abdourahmane Tchiani since he ousted elected President Mohamed Bazoum in 2023. Meanwhile, Libya remains split between two competing governments following the 2011 NATO-backed overthrow of Muammar Gaddafi, with Haftar backing the eastern administration and Prime Minister Abdulhamid Dbeibah leading the western government in Tripoli.
