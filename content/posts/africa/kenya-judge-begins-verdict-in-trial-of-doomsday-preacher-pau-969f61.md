A judge in Kenya has begun issuing a verdict in the mass murder trial of Paul Mackenzie, a doomsday preacher accused alongside 29 of his followers of killing 191 people. According to court documents spanning 298 pages, all but one of the victims in the case were children. Mackenzie, who established the Good News International Ministries in 2003, allegedly instructed his followers to starve their children so they could "meet Jesus". He and his co-defendants have all pleaded not guilty, maintaining that no one was coerced into dying.

The preacher’s doctrine strictly prohibited modern medicine, formal education, and hair styling, while promoting conspiracy theories that labeled healthcare and digital identity cards as evil or satanic. Mackenzie initially broadcasted his sermons on a television station called Times TV from his church in Malindi, drawing thousands of devotees across Kenya over a sixteen-year period. After closing his main church in 2019, he relocated his followers to a community of scattered mud houses in the Shakahola forest near the Kenyan coast.

In March 2023, local herders uncovered the first human remains in the forest, where authorities eventually discovered more than 400 bodies buried in shallow graves. Seventeen men from a nearby town were hired for $8 a day to exhume the site. Gravediggers described finding multiple corpses per grave, including tied and wrapped remains of infants and small children stacked together. Investigations revealed that victims died from starvation, strangulation, and being bludgeoned to death. Many bodies remain unidentified due to advanced decomposition.

While Mackenzie and other cult leaders face separate legal cases involving radicalization, terrorism, and withholding education, this proceeding marks his first verdict on murder charges. The prosecution’s case was aided by Enos Amanya Ngala, Mackenzie’s former head of security, who accepted a plea bargain to serve as a witness. Relatives described immense grief to BBC News, including Stephen Mwiti, whose former wife is among the co-defendants and who lost all six of his children in the forest. However, some survivors rescued from the forest told BBC News that they still support Mackenzie, insisting that followers fasted voluntarily.
