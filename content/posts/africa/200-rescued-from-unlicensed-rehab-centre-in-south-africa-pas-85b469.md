South African authorities have arrested a pastor and his wife after discovering roughly 200 individuals, some bound in chains, at a suspected unlicensed rehabilitation facility. The centre was being operated out of a church located in the north-eastern province of Mpumalanga. Local officials uncovered the site during an operation on Thursday, reporting that they encountered "gross inhumane conditions" inside the building.

Inspectors found occupants residing in crowded rooms lacking working bathrooms or running water. Multiple patients had sores across their bodies, while others were chained to one another. Although the couple managing the site told authorities they were housing 60 people, officials discovered more than triple that amount. The group included over 30 women, as well as a 16-year-old girl and an 11-year-old boy who were kept in the same room as adults. Most of those held were believed to be suffering from drug addiction, and social development officials noted that some had been kept at the location for an extended period.

Police have charged the operators with kidnapping, though additional charges could follow. The couple charged a monthly fee of 3,000 rand ($182) per patient, with nearly half of the money going to the church. The pair has not yet commented publicly and is scheduled to appear in a local court on Monday. In an interview with local broadcaster Newzroom Afrika, Gift Skhosana, a former drug user held at the facility, said chaining was a frequent practice upon arrival and as punishment, though he added that the facility helped him overcome his addiction. Another former resident stated that his time at the centre left him needing mental health support.

Police stated that 14 patients were transferred to a hospital for further treatment, while 132 individuals have been reunited with their families. The raid comes as illegal rehabilitation centres continue to open across South Africa to meet growing drug abuse. Last year, the department of social development reported uncovering more than 100 unlawful facilities nationwide, with the majority located in Gauteng province, home to Johannesburg.
