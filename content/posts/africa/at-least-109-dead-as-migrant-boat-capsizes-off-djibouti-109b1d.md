A maritime disaster off the coast of Djibouti has claimed the lives of at least 109 people, including 15 children, after a vessel transporting 305 migrants overturned. According to the International Organization for Migration (IOM), the incident marks the country's deadliest shipwreck on record. The disaster occurred overnight between October 8 and October 9 near Guhere, located north of the fishing town of Obock, as the boat attempted to travel from Djibouti to Yemen. Search operations continue as more than 116 passengers remain missing.

The IOM confirmed that the recovered bodies comprise 52 men, 42 women, and 15 children. More than 80 individuals survived the tragedy, all of whom are Ethiopian nationals. Djiboutian officials are conducting search and rescue missions, rendering medical care, and arranging burials for the victims. Meanwhile, the UN agency is working alongside local authorities to deliver emergency support, supplying healthcare, food, and psychosocial assistance to 64 survivors at its Obock response center. Alexandre Coissac, the IOM's chief of mission in Djibouti, called the event a "devastating loss of life" and noted that it underscores the dangers migrants face along this passage.

The migratory path linking the Horn of Africa to the Arabian Peninsula is among the busiest globally. Hundreds of thousands of people, mostly from Ethiopia and Somalia, use the corridor each year to reach Gulf states, particularly Saudi Arabia. Individuals make the journey due to economic motives or displacement driven by regional conflicts and natural disasters. Beyond the perils of traveling long distances on foot in intense heat, migrants face severe hazards at sea, where human smugglers frequently pack people onto crowded vessels traversing the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden.

Figures from the IOM’s Missing Migrants Project indicate that over 900 people died or disappeared along the route in 2025, even as overall movement rose by 18 percent. The corridor previously saw a March shipwreck that left nine people dead and 45 missing. Continued movement along the route occurs amid fighting between Iran-aligned Houthi rebels and Saudi-backed government forces over key coastal heights along the Red Sea. According to Onars, Djibouti's office for assistance to refugees and disaster victims, that conflict has driven more than 3,800 people to flee into Djibouti for safety.
