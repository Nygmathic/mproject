Senegal is preparing to host the Youth Olympic Games across Dakar, Diamniadio, and Saly, marking the first time an event featuring the Olympic rings will take place on the African continent. Spanning 15 days of competition from October 31 to November 13, the multi-sport event is set to bring together approximately 2,700 athletes, primarily aged 15 to 17. The program includes 153 medal events across 25 sports, alongside 10 non-medal engagement sports designed to boost local participation.

Speaking to BBC Sport Africa, International Olympic Committee official Christophe Dubi called the occasion "monumental" for Africa. Local Organising Committee deputy chief operating officer Thierno Cisse noted that Senegal feels the weight of the moment and aims to proudly represent the continent by demonstrating high standards. Organisers also hope the Games will offer immediate and long-term economic benefits, helping address widespread youth unemployment through training and job creation. More than 400 young Africans have already received instruction in event operations, logistics, technology, venue management, and accreditation.

First organized by the IOC in Singapore in 2010, the Youth Olympics subsequently took place in Nanjing and Buenos Aires before Dakar's scheduled 2022 hosting was delayed due to the Covid-19 pandemic. The upcoming gathering will achieve complete gender equality across its competition events for the first time. The lineup features 19 sports from the Paris 2024 Games—including athletics, swimming, breaking, and skateboarding—alongside debuting sports such as Baseball5 and Wushu Taolu.

To ensure long-term community benefits, organisers chose to renovate and repurpose existing facilities rather than build costly new venues. Diamniadio will serve as the operational hub, with the Abdoulaye Wade Stadium holding archery and the opening ceremony. Dakar will host events at complexes including Iba Mar Diop and Tour de l'Oeuf, while Saly Plage Ouest will stage coastal and water sports. The event also highlights local culture through the student-designed lion mascot named Ayo, medal artwork featuring a lion and a baobab tree, and the official song Teranga, performed by Senegalese artist Mamy Mbaye.
