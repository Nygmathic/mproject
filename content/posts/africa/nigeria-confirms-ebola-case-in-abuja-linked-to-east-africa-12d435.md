Nigeria has confirmed an Ebola infection in its capital, Abuja, involving a female patient who traveled from East Africa. Health Minister Muhammad Ali Pate confirmed that laboratory testing verified the diagnosis. Health sources informed BBC News that the patient is not a Nigerian citizen. According to the World Health Organization, she developed symptoms on Wednesday, October 7, four days after arriving in the country. The woman is currently in isolation receiving medical care in an Abuja hospital, while health officials have traced 44 close contacts to contain potential transmission.

This event marks Nigeria as the first West African country to report a case connected to the current Ebola crisis in East Africa. Ugandan health officials reported that the woman showed no symptoms while transiting through their country. Charles Olaro, Uganda’s Director General of Health Services, stated on X that the patient had traveled to eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo for business before transiting through Uganda in early October. Olaro noted that her exposure site remains under investigation, adding that the disease has an incubation period of two to 21 days. Uganda, which cleared its last Ebola patient and declared itself free of the virus in August after reporting at least two deaths, currently has no active confirmed cases.

The wider regional outbreak originated in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in May. Data from the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control indicates that the DRC outbreak, caused by the relatively rare Bundibugyo strain, has resulted in over 9,500 cases and at least 4,600 deaths. There is currently no approved vaccine or treatment for this strain, though clinical trials are in progress. The virus has also spread to Kenya, which has recorded at least nine infections, prompting the United States on Monday to raise its travel advisory level and recommend that citizens reconsider travel there.

Ebola is a rare, deadly viral infection that spreads between humans through direct contact with infected bodily fluids such as vomit or blood. Symptoms include vomiting, diarrhea, organ failure, and in some cases internal and external bleeding. To prevent further spread, the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control has increased disease surveillance, requiring travellers from countries with active transmission to complete health declaration forms. Nigeria previously controlled an Ebola outbreak in 2014 that resulted in at least eight deaths.
