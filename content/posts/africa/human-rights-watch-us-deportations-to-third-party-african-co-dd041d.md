A recent report by Human Rights Watch claims that the United States is violating international human rights law by transferring migrants to third-party African nations. The advocacy group states that the practice exploits human suffering to discourage immigration, leaving many deportees with little choice but to go back to their native countries despite facing significant risks of harm, torture, or persecution.

According to official US data cited by Human Rights Watch, over 500 individuals have been deported to African countries where they do not hold citizenship since Donald Trump re-entered office in 2025. The organization based its findings on interviews with 24 third-country nationals across six African states. Migrants reported severe mistreatment during transport, including being restrained in chains for roughly 18 hours without knowledge of their destination, while also being denied necessary medical care and adequate food or water.

Human Rights Watch documented arbitrary detention and ill-treatment of deportees in Eswatini, Ghana, and South Sudan. Additionally, migrants sent to Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, and Ghana were subsequently returned to their home nations, which the group argues breaches international protections against returning people to danger. The report also highlighted that at least 12 individuals identifying as LGBT were sent to African countries that criminalize same-sex relations, including refugees previously granted US protection.

Specific cases detailed in the report include 15 individuals from Colombia, Peru, and Ecuador sent to the Democratic Republic of Congo in April, most of whom had returned home by late September under conditions the group questioned as truly voluntary. Equatorial Guinea received 65 deportees and rapidly sent at least two home, while Burundi agreed late last month to accept migrants from the US. In Eswatini, officials noted that three deportees voluntarily departed to their home countries, adding that the nation remains committed to safeguarding the rights and dignity of third-country nationals.

Defending the policy, the Department of Homeland Security told the BBC that third-country deportations serve as "an essential public safety tool" to remove dangerous illegal aliens, adding that destination countries offer diplomatic assurances regarding safety. White House spokesperson Lauren Bis explained that transfers involve individuals whose home countries refuse to accept them or who have court orders barring direct return. She added that the administration provides free flights and a $3,000 payment to any illegal alien wishing to return home.
