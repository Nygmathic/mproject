Opposition figures in Malawi are demanding clarity over President Peter Mutharika's extended stay in South Africa, arguing that his absence comes at a time when the nation is experiencing severe economic and logistical crises. The 86-year-old leader departed the country on October 6 for what official channels described as a personal journey. This marks his fifth visit to South Africa this year, with few details made public regarding the purpose of these travels.

The demands for transparency follow widespread domestic difficulties in Malawi. A critical deficit of foreign currency has severely disrupted fuel imports, resulting in long lines at service stations and driving up transportation expenses. In addition to fuel instability, the country is grappling with persistent power blackouts and a scarcity of vital medical supplies across public health facilities. According to the opposition Malawi Congress Party, the state has reached a pivotal juncture requiring firm, decisive leadership.

Speaking to reporters in Lilongwe, campaign director Moses Kunkuyu clarified that the opposition is not contesting the president's right to travel privately. However, he urged Vice-President Jane Ansah to speak directly to the public to provide reassurance regarding who is steering government operations during these mounting challenges. Kunkuyu called on officials to outline explicit strategies to resolve the ongoing forex shortages, blackouts, and healthcare deficits.

In response, Information Minister Shadric Namalomba rejected claims of a political leadership void, asserting that the head of state remains in control at all times regardless of location. Defending the confidentiality of the travel, Namalomba maintained that a private journey should remain private. He further accused the opposition of trying to capitalize on economic hardships that he claimed originated under their prior administration, asking them to let the current government address the problems it was elected to fix.

Amid his ongoing absence, Mutharika appointed his trade minister to represent Malawi at the Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa summit held in Zimbabwe. Meanwhile, Chief Secretary to the Government Justin Saidi recently refuted online rumors claiming the president had traveled to India for medical care, confirming he remained in South Africa. Mutharika, who previously governed from 2014 to 2020, regained the presidency last October after defeating Lazarus Chakwera, and has previously brushed off health speculation as "silly and laughable."
