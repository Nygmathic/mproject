Former Democratic Republic of Congo President Joseph Kabila has re-emerged as a rebel leader, taking command of a newly formed alliance aimed at unseating President Félix Tshisekedi. Kabila, 55, was named leader of the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation (FRN) earlier this month. The announcement confirmed his ties to the M23 rebel group, which seized key eastern cities including Goma and Bukavu last year. The move pits Kabila against the leader he previously handed power to and formed a coalition with following his own 18-year presidency.

Kabila’s career began in 1997 as a commander in the rebel alliance that brought his father, Laurent, to power. Kabila later assumed the presidency in 2001 following his father's assassination. Although leaked election data from Catholic Church observers indicated Martin Fayulu won the 2018 presidential vote, former electoral commission head Corneille Nangaa—who now serves as an FRN deputy—claimed a pre-election agreement between Kabila and Tshisekedi ensured the transition of power.

Facing US sanctions over alleged M23 support as well as a domestic death sentence in absentia for treason, Kabila’s decision to join the insurgency comes amid ongoing conflict in the mineral-rich nation. Policy analyst Henry-Pacifique Mayala told the BBC that Kabila had limited options to regain political standing and likely sought assurances before aligning with M23. Mayala noted that M23’s military strength could grant Kabila greater leverage in future negotiations with the government. However, International Crisis Group analyst Richard Moncrieff stated that President Tshisekedi retains significant advantages, including broad control over the political and military systems, substantial financial resources, and strong support from the United States.

Kabila recently accused Tshisekedi of acting like a tyrant and invoked Section 64 of the constitution, which urges citizens to resist those who exercise power unlawfully. He also criticized the government's reinstatement of the death penalty, declaring, "This is personal." In his first public response, Tshisekedi stated that Kabila’s leadership of the FRN proved the former president never loved the country or its citizens. Meanwhile, the C64 opposition coalition refused to endorse Kabila’s armed movement, maintaining that the nation's security crises cannot be resolved through military action alone and calling instead for inclusive national dialogue.
