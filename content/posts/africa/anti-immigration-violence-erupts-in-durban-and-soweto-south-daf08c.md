Violent anti-immigration demonstrations recently erupted across two major South African hubs, resulting in the destruction of at least 24 vehicles and the looting of foreign-owned businesses. The unrest broke out on Thursday in Durban and in Soweto, a large township near Johannesburg. In Soweto's Orlando West neighbourhood, demonstrators initially gathered to demand the closure of small convenience stores operated by foreign nationals, known locally as spaza shops. Protesters subsequently forced foreign traders out, attacked businesses, and set a building on fire. Police reported that 10 vehicles were set on fire in Soweto alongside reports of gunfire, while 14 vehicles were torched in Durban.

The recent clashes were sparked by public anger following a Constitutional Court decision in July. The ruling struck down restrictions that previously barred undocumented migrants or those who missed immigration deadlines from seeking asylum, leading to massive queues at refugee centers. Government spokesman William Baloyi told Reuters that misinformation had been deliberately spread about the judgment, creating a false impression that undocumented migrants would automatically gain legal status. Baloyi stressed that applications will still be evaluated individually and candidates remain subject to checks. Facing long queues and public safety concerns, the Department of Home Affairs stated its staff had been "overwhelmed" and announced plans to adjust its compliance process while still adhering to the ruling.

In Durban, violence escalated following allegations that a foreign national assaulted a demonstrator, leading to the arrest of three foreign nationals. Protesters assembled outside a refugee reception facility to demand a halt to asylum processing, leading police to discharge rubber bullets to disperse the crowd as local businesses closed. Senior police officer Xolani Fihla noted clashes between South Africans and foreign nationals, though no injuries were initially reported. Acting national police commissioner Lt Gen Puleng Dimpane condemned the property damage, and additional police forces were deployed to prevent further incidents.

South Africa has faced recurring outbreaks of xenophobic violence for nearly two decades, including widespread attacks in 2008 that killed over 60 people. The recent friction follows earlier demonstrations this year that pressured undocumented migrants to leave by June 30, displacing tens of thousands. Tensions persist against a backdrop of widespread poverty, inequality, and an unemployment rate affecting about a third of the workforce, with migrants frequently blamed for economic strains despite researchers finding no evidence for such claims.
