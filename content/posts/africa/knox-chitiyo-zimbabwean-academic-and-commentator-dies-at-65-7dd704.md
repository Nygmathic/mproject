Knox Chitiyo, a Zimbabwean academic and key commentator in Britain on his native country, has died at the age of 65 from bowel cancer. Over the course of his career, Chitiyo developed an international standing for his analysis of Zimbabwe, defence, and security matters, serving as a frequent consultant for diplomats, business figures, journalists, scholars, and activists.

Born in Salisbury—now Harare—in Southern Rhodesia, Chitiyo was the son of Wynona, a nurse, and McLeod Chitiyo, a pathologist. Raised during the closing decades of white minority rule in the Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland, he was educated at St George’s, an integrated private Jesuit secondary school in Salisbury. That experience helped shape his enduring interest in identity, race, and political reform. He later attended the University of Zimbabwe, earning a general degree and a doctorate before embarking on his teaching career there.

Between 1992 and 2003, Chitiyo was a senior lecturer in war studies and military history at the University of Zimbabwe. While there, he co-founded the institution’s Centre for Defence Studies, acting as its deputy director beginning in 1995. He also taught strategic thinking to military officers at the National Army Staff College and the Gweru Military Academy.

As political and intellectual freedom diminished in Zimbabwe under Robert Mugabe, Chitiyo moved to the United Kingdom in 2005. He took a role with the Royal United Services Institute, serving as the head of its Africa programme between 2007 and 2011. In 2012, he became an associate fellow at Chatham House, a position he retained until his death.

Chitiyo worked to ensure members of the Zimbabwean diaspora could contribute to public policy discussions in the UK. He served as president of the Britain Zimbabwe Society, held a seat on the board of the Business Council for Africa as acting chair, and was a fellow at the Brenthurst Foundation. He was also a trustee for the landmine clearance charity Mines Advisory Group. Outside his professional work, he wrote poetry and short stories and was a saxophone player.

He is survived by his wife, Nhamburiko, whom he married in 2002; his son, Kyle, from his previous marriage to Nhmabu; and his sister, Cathy.
