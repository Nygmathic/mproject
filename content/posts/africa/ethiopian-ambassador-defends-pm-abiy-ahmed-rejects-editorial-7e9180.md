Ethiopia’s ambassador to the United Kingdom, Biruk Mekonnen, has challenged an October 8 editorial addressing the crisis in Ethiopia, arguing that the piece presented an incomplete narrative that unfairly framed Ethiopian national interests as a source of regional instability while ignoring the aggressive actions of other regional powers.

In his response, Mekonnen defended Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed against criticism surrounding his 2019 Nobel Peace Prize. The editorial had implied the Nobel committee would hope its future selections would age better, referencing the outbreak of war a year after the honor was awarded. Mekonnen countered that Ahmed earned the prize specifically for establishing peace with Eritrea, asserting that the later war in Tigray does not discredit that achievement or demonstrate that the award was unmerited. He further contested claims that the diplomatic rapprochement with Eritrea directly caused the war with the Tigray People’s Liberation Front, and disputed assertions that Ahmed’s political coalition is maintained solely through personal patronage.

Mekonnen emphasized that the situation must be evaluated within the context of Eritrean foreign policy, which he stated treats a weakened Ethiopia as advantageous to Eritrea's own interests. He argued that even if Eritrea holds strategic significance for Washington, those diplomatic considerations should not overshadow the impact of Eritrean actions on Ethiopian sovereignty and security.

Addressing calls for a truce, the ambassador warned that diplomatic dialogue must not grant political leverage to armed aggression or force Ethiopia into making concessions without establishing accountability for the renewed fighting. Additionally, Mekonnen defended Ethiopia's pursuit of access to the Red Sea and its right to utilize resources from the Nile River. He stated that these initiatives represent legitimate rights to national development and should not be categorized as regional security threats merely because they conflict with the preferences of neighboring nations.
