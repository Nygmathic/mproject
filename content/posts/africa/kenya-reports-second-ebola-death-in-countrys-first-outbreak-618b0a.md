Kenya has registered its second fatal case of Ebola amidst the country's first recorded outbreak of the virus. According to the health ministry, the deceased was a 65-year-old woman who had been placed in quarantine as a close relative of the nation's initial victim. The first patient was a man who had traveled from the Democratic Republic of Congo, became sick in September, received care in several Congolese hospitals, and subsequently died in Nairobi on October 5. The woman developed symptoms on October 13 prior to her death.

Currently, seven additional patients are receiving medical care across two isolation facilities. These individuals include three other relatives of the first deceased patient, three healthcare staff members, and one hospital cleaner. Health officials emphasized that all confirmed cases belong to a single cluster, with no evidence that the virus has spread into the general community. The outbreak involves the Bundibugyo strain of the virus, a relatively rare species that has no approved vaccine or cure, although experimental trials are in progress. Transmitted through direct contact with infected bodily fluids like blood or vomit, Ebola can cause severe illness including vomiting, diarrhea, organ failure, and internal or external bleeding.

Since May, when the virus was confirmed in the Democratic Republic of Congo, more than 4,000 people have died across Africa in what has become the continent's second deadliest Ebola outbreak. The International Rescue Committee noted that Kenya faces an ongoing risk of imported infections due to more than 9,500 cases in the Democratic Republic of Congo. In response, Kenyan authorities have instituted heightened screening protocols for travelers coming from ten designated high-risk nations, requiring incoming passengers to submit digital health surveillance forms and present QR codes upon arrival. Aircraft from these locations are also subject to intensified public health inspections. Additionally, the United States has issued a travel warning regarding Kenya, while neighboring states such as Ethiopia, Somalia, and Uganda remain on alert.

Meanwhile, international investigations are examining potential links between the outbreak in Kenya and a case in West Africa. Nigerian Health Minister Prof Ali Pate reported that his nation's single confirmed Ebola case appears genetically connected to the strain detected in Kenya, though no direct travel between the two countries has been established. Health officials are investigating whether both patients passed through Uganda's Entebbe airport earlier this month. To help curb further transmission, Uganda's health ministry announced that individuals who have traveled to the Democratic Republic of Congo will no longer be permitted to transit through Ugandan territory.
