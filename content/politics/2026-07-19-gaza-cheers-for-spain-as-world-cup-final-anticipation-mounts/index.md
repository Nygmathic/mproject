## A Unlikely Alliance on the World Stage

As the global sporting community prepares for the World Cup final, a sense of excitement and anticipation is sweeping across the Gaza Strip. Amidst the devastation of war and a deepening humanitarian crisis, football has emerged as a beacon of hope and solidarity, with many Palestinians rallying behind Spain, the team set to face off against Argentina on Sunday.

For Ahmed al-Bozm, a 33-year-old Palestinian, the decision to support Spain is a clear one. "I'm completely behind Spain because they stood up for Palestine," he said, referencing the Spanish government's recognition of the State of Palestine in May 2024. This move, coupled with the public expressions of support by Spanish sporting figures, including Barcelona star Lamine Yamal, has earned the team a special place in the hearts of many Palestinians.

The sentiment is not unique to al-Bozm. Across Gaza, people are eagerly anticipating the final, with some even going so far as to organise World Cup-themed events and exhibition matches to revive a sense of sporting life that has largely disappeared during the war. Adnan al-Afifi, a former footballer and stadium manager, has been instrumental in organising these events at the Palestine Sports Club in Gaza City, the only football club still operating and accessible to players in the Strip.

The World Cup has provided a rare opportunity for Palestinians to escape the harsh realities of war, even if only for 90 minutes. "Everyone wants to watch the big matches, especially the final," al-Afifi said, expressing hope that local organisations and community initiatives will organise public screenings for Sunday's match across Gaza. "Spain stood with the Palestinian cause and raised awareness internationally. We hope they will be rewarded by winning the World Cup," he added.

The bond between Spain and Palestine goes beyond mere sporting affinity. The Spanish government's recognition of the State of Palestine has been seen as a significant step towards a two-state solution in the Israel-Palestine conflict. Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez's call for a ceasefire and the delivery of humanitarian aid has been echoed by several athletes and coaches for Spain's national team, including Yamal and former Manchester City coach Pep Guardiola. These gestures have come to symbolise more than football to Palestinians, offering a glimmer of hope in a region ravaged by conflict.

As the final approaches, Palestinians are not only cheering on Spain but also expressing solidarity with the team. Hatem al-Maghribi, the head coach of Gaza's amputee football team, Gaza al-Irada, or Gaza Determination, has praised Spain's support for the Palestinian cause. "These players lost their legs and their arms, but they have not lost their words of gratitude, love and appreciation for those who stood with Palestine," he said. The sentiment is echoed by al-Afifi, who sees these gestures as a powerful reminder that "someone sees what we are going through."

Despite the excitement surrounding the final, the reality of life in Gaza remains harsh. With major stadiums out of service due to displacement and the security situation, football has been limited to five-a-side pitches, where local tournaments are organised to keep players connected to the game. The struggle to revive football in Gaza is a testament to the resilience of the Palestinian people, who have managed to find hope and solidarity in the midst of war and devastation.

As the final approaches, Palestinians are urging local organisations and community initiatives to organise public screenings of the match across Gaza. "We respect every national team, regardless of where they come from," said al-Afifi, expressing the hope that Spain will be rewarded with a World Cup victory for its support of the Palestinian cause.

For now, the outcome of the final remains uncertain. While some, like al-Bozm, are predicting a 3-1 victory for Spain in extra time, others, like al-Maghribi, are cautioning that Argentina may have the better chances due to Lionel Messi's presence on the team. Regardless of the outcome, one thing is clear: the bond between Spain and Palestine has been forged on the world stage, offering a glimmer of hope in a region ravaged by conflict.
