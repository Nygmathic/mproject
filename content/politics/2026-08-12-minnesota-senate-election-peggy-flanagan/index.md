## Progressive Momentum in the North Star State

[Peggy Flanagan](/tags/peggy-flanagan/), the progressive lieutenant governor of Minnesota, has secured the Democratic nomination for the U.S. Senate seat from her home state, capping off a series of victories for the party's left flank in the lead-up to the midterm elections. Flanagan's triumph over centrist Rep. Angie Craig marks a significant milestone in the ongoing struggle for control within the Democratic Party, with the progressive wing asserting its dominance over more moderate elements.

Flanagan's campaign was characterized by a fierce advocacy for the programs and services that she credits with helping her own family escape poverty, including government-assisted food programs, Medicaid, and childcare services. Echoing her own experiences, Flanagan has pledged to vigorously defend these initiatives, which she believes are essential for maintaining social mobility and economic stability. Her campaign platform, replete with proposals for "radical change," promises to upend the status quo on issues such as immigration and healthcare.

Craig, a more establishment-oriented candidate, had the backing of several high-profile Democrats, including former transportation secretary Pete Buttigieg and Sen. Tammy Baldwin of Wisconsin. Craig's campaign, bolstered by a significant fundraising advantage and external expenditures, attempted to capitalize on concerns about Flanagan's ties to the state government. However, Flanagan skillfully exploited Craig's past support for the Laken Riley Act, a measure that preceded the Trump administration's immigration enforcement surge in Minnesota, to mobilize opposition among progressive voters.

This primary victory is the latest in a string of successes for [progressive Democrats](/tags/progressive-democrats/), who have been gaining momentum in the Midwest. Last week, epidemiologist Abdul El-Sayed won the Democratic nomination for the U.S. Senate seat in Michigan, further underscoring the party's shift towards the left. Flanagan's win is particularly noteworthy, given her status as a sitting lieutenant governor and the fact that she ran a spirited campaign against a well-funded opponent.

The Republican primary in Minnesota, meanwhile, saw former sports broadcaster Michele Tafoya emerge victorious. While the Republican contest received relatively less attention than the [Democratic primary](/tags/democratic-primary/), it nonetheless underscores the ongoing competition within the party for the nomination.

Flanagan's path forward is now clear: she will face off against the Republican nominee in the general election, with the stakes for the Democratic Party's progressive wing hanging in the balance. If Flanagan can translate her primary victory into a win in November, it will be a testament to the enduring power of progressive politics in the Midwest and a significant milestone in the ongoing struggle for control within the Democratic Party.

As the party gears up for the general election, Flanagan's campaign will undoubtedly face intense scrutiny from her opponents and the media. However, her victory in the primary serves as a reminder that the progressive wing of the party remains a force to be reckoned with, and that its momentum shows no signs of abating. The question now is whether Flanagan can sustain this energy and translate it into a winning campaign in the fall.
