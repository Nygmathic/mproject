## Favourites and Foes: Ex-Players Weigh in on the FIFA World Cup 2026 Final

As the FIFA [World Cup](/tags/world-cup/) 2026 final draws near, the talk of the town is which team will emerge victorious: reigning European champions Spain, or defending champions Argentina. The predictions are flying in, and some of football's most iconic players are weighing in with their opinions.

For many, the choice is clear: Spain are the team to beat. Former England striker Michael Owen has backed La Roja, calling them the "deserved" winners of the tournament. "Spain haven't got the greatest individual players, I think France probably have," Owen said, "but Spain as a team are everything England are not – they are brave, courageous with the ball." Owen's sentiments are echoed by Zlatan Ibrahimovic, who expects Spain to dominate the final. "Spain are going to do their thing," Ibrahimovic said. "They're going to play collectively... I believe Spain are going to dominate that game."

John Terry, the former England captain, has also come out in support of Spain, saying that Luis de la Fuente's side have "got too much for Argentina". Even Iker Casillas, the Spain captain during their maiden [World Cup](/tags/world-cup/) triumph in 2010, is confident in the team's ability to bring home a second title.

But not everyone is convinced that Spain are the clear favourites. Alan Shearer, one of England's greatest strikers of all time, has backed the reigning champions, citing their mentality and togetherness as key factors. "If there's one team that can do it in terms of stopping them [Spain], it's Argentina because of their mentality and togetherness," Shearer said. "They have been down so many times and in trouble, and somehow managed to get a win. That's what you've got to admire about them."

Micah Richards, an English football pundit, has suggested that Argentina's best chance of beating Spain lies in adopting a low-block transition strategy. "Get the ball to Messi, whereas [Julian] Alvarez or [Lisandro] Martinez just make it horrible, make it how they did with England," Richards said. "I think Argentina need to stay in the game as they did against England and turn it on when they see a shift in momentum."

Gary Lineker, another English great, has stuck with his pre-tournament pick of Spain, but acknowledged that Argentina are "so gritty", making the predictions a very tough choice. "You cannot go against that little fella [Messi]," Lineker said. "He's so great; it's going to be really, really close. It's genuinely 50-50."

In a departure from the consensus, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani refused to pick a side, citing his propensity for backing losing teams. "Everyone I root for has lost," Mamdani said with a laugh. "So I feel like whoever I say is going to lose at this point. I'm gonna say the classic politician's answer – I'm excited for a good game."

The stats seem to back up Spain's favouritism, with Opta predicting a 59.56 percent probability of them lifting the trophy in New Jersey. But with so many experts weighing in on the team's chances, it's clear that this will be a [World Cup](/tags/world-cup/) final for the ages.
