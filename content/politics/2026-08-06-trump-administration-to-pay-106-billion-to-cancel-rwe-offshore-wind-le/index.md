## Billions Paid to Cancel Offshore Wind Leases: A Setback for Renewable Energy

The Trump administration has agreed to pay a staggering €1.06 billion to German energy firm RWE to cancel the company's offshore wind leases off the coasts of New York, California, and Louisiana. This move comes as part of a broader campaign by President Donald Trump to halt wind energy projects across the US, with him repeatedly dismissing wind turbines as unsightly and labeling them an "economic and environmental disaster."

According to the agreement, Washington will fork out $1.22 billion (€1.06 billion) to RWE to relinquish the leases, which had been the subject of prolonged negotiations between the company and the US Department of the Interior (DOI). In a statement, RWE revealed that the decision was made after a thorough assessment, which concluded that there was no feasible path forward for permitting these projects in the US for the foreseeable future.

This settlement marks a significant blow to the renewable energy sector, which had been growing rapidly in the US in recent years. The move is part of a broader trend, with French energy giant TotalEnergies also announcing an agreement with the US Interior Department to cut its leases at Carolina Long Bay and New York Bight. As part of this deal, the DOI agreed to pay the French firm around $1 billion to instead invest in natural gas projects.

RWE has also committed to investing in gas projects across the US, with a particular focus on meeting the rapidly growing energy demand. The company has announced a $900 million investment to acquire a 16% stake in a Louisiana liquified natural gas project, a move that reflects the administration's emphasis on fossil fuels. In a separate development, RWE Americas has revealed plans to invest approximately €17 billion in the US over the next six years, with the aim of growing its generation capacity from approximately 13 GW across 27 states today to 22 GW by 2031.

The agreement has been welcomed by Doug Burgum, the US Interior Secretary, who praised RWE's decision to invest in projects that strengthen the nation's energy security, provide dependable baseload power, and help keep electricity affordable for American citizens. However, critics have expressed concerns that this move will undermine the country's efforts to transition towards a cleaner and more sustainable energy mix.

The implications of this deal are far-reaching, with the cancellation of offshore wind leases set to have significant consequences for the US's renewable energy sector.
