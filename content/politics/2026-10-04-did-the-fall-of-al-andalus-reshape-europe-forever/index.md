# Did the fall of al-Andalus reshape Europe forever?

A foundational inquiry into European political identity has emerged at the center of the Al Jazeera digital series *Deja Vu*, which examines the continent’s long-standing historical trajectories and their modern ramifications. The production asks a profound geopolitical question: Did the fall of al-Andalus reshape Europe forever? Across a comprehensive broadcast spanning forty-nine minutes and four seconds, the program traces Europe’s Muslim history directly from the era of al-Andalus to the modern day. In doing so, it confronts an enduring political and societal contradiction: why, despite having helped shape Europe for centuries from Spain to Sicily, do Muslims still seem to be cast and treated as outsiders in contemporary European life?

To address these themes, the episode features analysis from Tariq Ali, the prominent author and editor of the *New Left Review*. Ali traces the overarching historical arc from the rise of al-Andalus to its eventual fall, exploring how a civilization defined by profound contributions gave way to the burning of books and the painful loss of a shared past. Through this lens, the discussion interrogates the mechanisms through which historical rupture continues to influence the continent’s contemporary political landscape, evaluating the legacy of an era whose erasure continues to reverberate across modern political discourse.

## Historical Erasure and the Modern Politics of Exclusion

The scope of Islamic civilizational influence documented within the program was not confined to a single geographic outpost but extended across significant swathes of the continent. From Spain to Sicily, Muslims actively helped shape Europe for centuries, leaving an imprint on the broader cultural and institutional development of the region. Yet, despite centuries of deep integration and cultural contribution, European political dynamics continue to grapple with an entrenched narrative of alienation. The program asks why Muslims still seem to be cast as outsiders today, exploring the mechanisms that sustain this political estrangement despite a documented historical presence that spanned multiple regions of Southern Europe.

In tracing this dynamic, Tariq Ali focuses on the decisive historical turning point that marked the collapse of al-Andalus. The episode emphasizes that the fall of this society was not merely a military or administrative transition, but an era defined amid the burning of books and the consequent loss of a shared past. By focusing on the destruction of texts and intellectual heritage, the analysis illustrates how the physical obliteration of literature served to sever modern European consciousness from its historical ties to Islamic culture. This deliberate severance of memory, Ali demonstrates, established a long-term precedent that continues to inform how Europe frames its borders, its collective heritage, and the populations it embraces or excludes.

## Tracing the Contemporary Narrative in 'Deja Vu'

Running for an extensive duration of 49 minutes and 04 seconds, this installment of *Deja Vu* provides a dedicated, long-form platform to evaluate how these historical events directly shape Europe’s politics today. Rather than treating the history of al-Andalus as an isolated relic of the past, the broadcast methodically connects the rise and fall of that era with the current treatment of Muslim communities. The structural focus remains tethered to understanding the persistent treatment of Muslims as outsiders, investigating whether modern European political attitudes are recurring echoes—a literal sense of deja vu—stemming from the systematic dissolution of the shared Andalusian past.

As editor of the *New Left Review* and an established author, Tariq Ali provides the critical framework for linking these historical episodes to ongoing institutional and political behaviors. Ali’s commentary scrutinizes how the elimination of the shared past created deep fissures in Europe's understanding of its own development. By examining the rise of al-Andalus alongside its ultimate decline, the episode explores the political consequences of constructing a regional identity that ignores its own Muslim heritage. The program asks whether the political structures of Europe today continue to reflect the cultural divisions formalized centuries ago when that shared past was dismantled.

## Production, Media Strategy, and Broadcast Distribution

The detailed production of this investigative digital series relies on a specialized team assembled by Al Jazeera. This episode was produced by Mohamed Hassan, Marcos Bartolome, and Sonia Bhagat, who structured the inquiry into the enduring impacts of the Andalusian legacy. Editorial oversight for the project was provided by editor Alexandra Locke, ensuring that the 49-minute and 04-second examination remained closely focused on the intersection of historical documentation and modern political critique.

The technical and digital rollout of the episode was supported by sound designer Alex Roldan, alongside engagement producer Adam Abou-Gad. To ensure the program’s central questions reach a wide audience, the broadcast has been distributed across multiple digital networks under the handle @AJEPodcasts, engaging viewers and listeners across X, Instagram, Facebook, and YouTube. Through this multi-platform digital reach, *Deja Vu* brings Tariq Ali’s evaluation of al-Andalus, the burning of books, and the centuries-long presence of Muslims from Spain to Sicily directly into the modern debate over European identity, asking whether the historical fall of al-Andalus permanently altered the continent’s political fabric.
