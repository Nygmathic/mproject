The current conflict is merely the latest iteration of a long-standing struggle between Israel and its neighbors, one that has its roots in the tumultuous events of the early 20th century. The establishment of the State of Israel in 1948 sparked a wave of Arab nationalism, as neighboring countries rejected the new Jewish state and vowed to reclaim their lost territories. The subsequent wars – the 1967 Six-Day War, the 1973 Yom Kippur War, and the 1982 Lebanon War – saw the conflict escalate, with both sides suffering heavy losses. The 2006 Lebanon War, sparked by Hezbollah's abduction of Israeli soldiers, saw Israel launch a devastating 34-day campaign of airstrikes and ground operations, leaving hundreds dead and thousands displaced.

The 2011 Arab Spring, which swept across the Middle East, brought new hope for a lasting peace in the region. However, the subsequent rise of extremist groups, including ISIS and Al-Qaeda, further destabilized the region, as Israel found itself drawn into a broader conflict. The 2019 Israeli election saw the rise of hardline nationalist Benjamin Netanyahu, who has since pursued a policy of aggressive military action against Iran and its proxies, including Hezbollah. The current conflict, therefore, is part of a larger pattern of escalation, driven by competing national interests and a deep-seated mistrust between Israel and its neighbors.
