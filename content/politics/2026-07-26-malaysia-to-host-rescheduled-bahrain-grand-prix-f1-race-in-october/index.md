## Formula One Returns to Malaysia, but Under a Different Name

Malaysia's Sepang circuit will host a rescheduled Bahrain Grand Prix in October, marking a significant development in the Formula One calendar. The Liberty Media-owned sport announced the news on Sunday, with Formula One Chief Executive Stefano Domenicali revealing that an agreement had been signed between the governments of Malaysia and Bahrain, as well as the governing FIA and Formula One.

The decision to reschedule the Bahrain Grand Prix, initially set for April at the Sakhir circuit in Bahrain, was necessitated by the United States and Israel's war on Iran and its spillover in the Middle East. The cancellation of the April race, along with the Saudi Arabian Grand Prix in Jeddah, was a significant blow to the Formula One calendar, which now features 23 races. However, the addition of Sepang takes the championship back to its full capacity, pending the outcome of the final two rounds of the season in Qatar and Abu Dhabi, which are still subject to speculation.

Sepang, a circuit that last hosted Formula One in 2017, will provide a spectacular setting for racing and an unforgettable experience for fans at the circuit and watching around the world, according to Domenicali. The rescheduled Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia will slot in between the Azerbaijan Grand Prix on September 26 and Singapore on October 11. This move allows Formula One to maintain its full calendar while also returning to a great venue, which holds a special place in the sport's history.

The decision to host a Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia is not unprecedented. There are several precedents for one country holding a race with the name of another, including Germany's Nurburgring hosting a Luxembourg Grand Prix and the San Marino Grand Prix being held at Imola for many years. This development highlights the flexibility and adaptability of the Formula One calendar, which has had to navigate unprecedented circumstances in recent years.
