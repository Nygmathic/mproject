## Death Toll Rises in Gulf as Regional Tensions Escalate

A 25-year-old Bangladeshi migrant, Abdul Rashid, was among the scores of workers killed in a devastating missile strike on an oil refinery in the United Arab Emirates. His family, who had sent him abroad to escape poverty, now faces the daunting prospect of repaying the substantial debt they incurred to send him to the Gulf. "We have been paying off the debt for years, and now we are faced with the prospect of paying for his funeral too," his tearful mother said in a WhatsApp message. The story of Abdul Rashid's family is a microcosm of the human cost of the ongoing conflict in the Gulf region, where millions of South Asian migrant workers like him are caught in the middle.

The missile strikes by Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps on Abu Dhabi and Dubai have led to a significant increase in the death toll, with the majority of the victims hailing from countries such as Bangladesh, India, and Pakistan. According to estimates, over 50% of the migrant workers employed in the UAE are from South Asia, making them the most vulnerable demographic in the conflict. The precarious nature of their employment, coupled with the massive debt they accumulate to secure jobs abroad, has left them with little to fall back on in case of an emergency. In Bangladesh, for instance, thousands of families are struggling to cope with the loss of their loved ones, with many more on the brink of financial ruin.

### Economic Consequences of the Conflict

The ongoing conflict in the Gulf has far-reaching economic implications, not only for the South Asian countries but also for the entire region. The UAE, a major economic hub and a significant trading partner for many countries in the region, has seen its economy take a devastating blow. The missile strikes have disrupted oil production, causing a significant spike in global oil prices, and have also led to a decline in trade and commerce. The knock-on effects of this crisis are being felt across the region, from Saudi Arabia to India, with many economies on the edge of a precipice.

The economic consequences of the conflict are further exacerbated by the fact that many South Asian countries are heavily reliant on remittances from migrant workers abroad. In Bangladesh, for example, remittances account for a significant proportion of the country's GDP, and any decline in remittances can have far-reaching consequences for the economy. The Bangladeshi government has estimated that the country stands to lose around $1 billion in remittances due to the ongoing conflict in the Gulf.

The current conflict in the Gulf has echoes of the 1990-1991 Gulf War, which saw a similar escalation of tensions between Iran and the United States. However, the stakes are much higher this time around, with the involvement of multiple regional and global powers. The conflict has also raised concerns about the safety and security of migrant workers in the Gulf, a demographic that is often overlooked in regional and global politics. As the conflict continues to escalate, it is essential to consider the global implications of this crisis, from the potential collapse of the global economy to the rise of extremist groups in the region.

Meanwhile, the Indian government has issued a statement expressing its concern over the escalation of tensions in the Gulf and has called for restraint from all parties involved.
