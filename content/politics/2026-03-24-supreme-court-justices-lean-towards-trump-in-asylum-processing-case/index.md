## The Border's Endgame: A Supreme Court Showdown

A tense silence fell over the Supreme Court on Tuesday as justices leaned in to consider the fate of hundreds of thousands of asylum seekers, their fate hanging precariously in the balance. In a high-stakes case that pits the Biden administration against the Trump-era policy of "metering," the court's conservative majority appeared poised to uphold the government's authority to turn away those seeking refuge at the US-Mexico border.

At the heart of the case lies the Trump administration's policy to cap the number of asylum claims at US-Mexico border crossings, effectively limiting the flow of migrants seeking protection from persecution, violence, or other forms of harm. Dubbed "metering," the policy allowed officials to deem certain border crossings too overburdened to handle additional claims, effectively turning away those who had managed to reach the US border. The Biden administration, having inherited this policy from its predecessor, dropped it in 2021, arguing that it was unconstitutional and contravened the Refugee Act of 1980.

However, the Trump administration's lawyers are now arguing that the policy is not only constitutional but also essential to maintaining national security and preventing what they term "uncontrolled" border crossings. The court's conservative justices, who have been vocal in their support for stricter immigration policies, appear to be sympathetic to this argument. In a series of pointed questions during Tuesday's hearing, they pressed the Biden administration's lawyers to justify the policy's rejection, casting doubt on the government's ability to manage its borders and uphold the rule of law.

The stakes in this case are high, not only for the hundreds of thousands of asylum seekers who have been turned away but also for the broader implications for US immigration policy. If the court upholds the "metering" policy, it would effectively give the government a green light to continue turning away asylum seekers, potentially undermining the very principles of international refugee law. Conversely, a ruling in favor of the Biden administration would affirm the government's duty to provide safe haven to those fleeing persecution and violence, a cornerstone of the refugee compact.

## A History of Asylum: The US's Shifting Stance

The US has a long and complex history with asylum, dating back to the Refugee Act of 1980, which committed the country to providing safe haven to those fleeing persecution. Over the years, the US has taken in hundreds of thousands of refugees, from Cubans fleeing the Castro regime to Vietnamese boat people escaping the Vietnam War. However, the country's stance on asylum has also been marked by periods of retrenchment and restriction, particularly in times of heightened national security concerns.

The Trump administration's "metering" policy was part of a broader crackdown on immigration, aimed at reducing the flow of migrants into the US. Critics argued that the policy was designed to circumvent international law and deny asylum to those who had a legitimate claim. The Biden administration's decision to drop the policy in 2021 was seen as a welcome step towards restoring the US's commitment to international refugee law.

However, the present case highlights the ongoing tension between the US government's duty to provide safe haven and its desire to control its borders. The court's conservative justices have pressed the Biden administration to justify its decision to drop the policy, casting doubt on the government's ability to manage its borders and uphold the rule of law. The outcome of this case will have far-reaching implications for US immigration policy, potentially setting a precedent for the government to turn away asylum seekers on grounds of national security or border congestion.

## Perspectives from the Global South

The US's treatment of asylum seekers has far-reaching implications for countries in the Global South, where many migrants originate. In countries like Guatemala, Honduras, and El Salvador, the US's "metering" policy has been seen as a de facto denial of asylum, leaving many without a safe haven. The policy has also been criticized by human rights groups, who argue that it contravenes international law and undermines the principles of refugee protection.

In an interview with Veridus, a senior official from the Guatemalan government expressed concern about the potential implications of the US court's ruling. "If the US upholds the 'metering' policy, it will only serve to further exacerbate the humanitarian crisis in our region," the official said. "We urge the US to reconsider its stance on asylum and to reaffirm its commitment to international refugee law."

## Reactions and Implications

The prospect of the US Supreme Court upholding the "metering" policy has sent shockwaves through the international community, with many human rights groups and governments condemning the move. The United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) has called on the US to uphold its international obligations and provide safe haven to those fleeing persecution.

The Biden administration, meanwhile, has reiterated its commitment to upholding international refugee law and providing safe haven to those in need. In a statement, a senior administration official said: "We will continue to work tirelessly to protect the rights of asylum seekers and uphold our international obligations."
