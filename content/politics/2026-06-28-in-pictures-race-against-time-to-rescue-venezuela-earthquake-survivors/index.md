## Tragedy Unfolds: Time is Running Out to Save Venezuela's Earthquake Victims

As the sun rises over Venezuela's ravaged coastal towns, rescue crews and volunteers are racing against the clock to locate survivors buried beneath the rubble of collapsed buildings. The catastrophic twin earthquakes that struck on Thursday, measuring 7.2 and 7.5 on the Richter scale, have claimed at least 1,430 lives and left tens of thousands missing, with the United Nations warning that up to 6.76 million people may require shelter, safe water, and medical care.

The first three days after such disasters are a critical window to locate people alive beneath the debris, and that window is rapidly closing. "It's just very chaotic, hot and unorganised," said Australian firefighter Craig Demeillon, who has travelled alone from Miami, Florida, to La Guaira, the hardest hit area, to help with the rescue efforts. "Hopefully, there's more people to find."

One of the most heartening rescues so far has been the recovery of a newborn baby from the ruins in La Guaira. The baby, miraculously pulled alive from the rubble, has brought hope to the devastated community. A man was seen cradling the baby in a widely shared social media video, overcome with emotion.

However, the search effort has been hampered by restricted access to La Guaira state and the requirement that volunteers obtain safe-entry passes. Would-be rescuer Carlos Itriago, 27, described the bureaucratic hurdles as frustrating and potentially life-threatening. "You need a permit to save lives. Just imagine," he said while waiting in line for permission to enter the disaster zone. "How many lives have we already lost by now?"

As the international community rallies to support Venezuela's response efforts, foreign search and rescue teams are arriving in the country with dogs and specialised equipment.
