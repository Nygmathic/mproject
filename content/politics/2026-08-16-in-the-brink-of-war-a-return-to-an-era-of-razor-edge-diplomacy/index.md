## A Glimpse into the Past: The Reykjavik Summit Revisited

In 'The Brink of War,' a Return to an Era of Razor-Edge Diplomacy

The recent film highlighting the 1986 nuclear arms summit in Reykjavik, Iceland, has reignited interest in the dramatic negotiations between Ronald Reagan and Mikhail Gorbachev. This pivotal moment in history showcased the razor-edge diplomacy characteristic of the Cold War era, as the two superpowers teetered on the brink of war while attempting to forge a path towards disarmament.

At the heart of the summit was a proposed deal to eliminate all intermediate-range nuclear missiles from Europe, a move that would have significantly reduced the threat of nuclear war. However, the negotiations ultimately collapsed due to disagreements over verification procedures and the US Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI). The failure of the summit sent shockwaves through the international community, exposing the deep-seated mistrust and animosity between the two great powers.

## The Tensions of the Time

The Reykjavik summit took place against a backdrop of heightened tensions between the United States and the Soviet Union. The Cold War had been simmering for decades, but the 1980s saw a significant escalation in rhetoric and military build-up on both sides. The Soviet Union's invasion of Afghanistan in 1979 and the US's deployment of cruise missiles in Europe had already heightened tensions, but the Reykjavik summit brought the world to the edge of nuclear war.

The stakes were high, with both sides aware that a breakdown in negotiations could have catastrophic consequences. The proposed deal would have required significant concessions from both parties, including the dismantling of entire missile systems and the provision of unprecedented levels of transparency and verification. However, the US's insistence on SDI, a proposed system to defend against incoming missiles, proved a major sticking point for the Soviets, who saw it as a threat to their own strategic capabilities.

## A Glimpse into the Minds of the Leaders

The Reykjavik summit was a defining moment for both Reagan and Gorbachev, showcasing their unique personalities and approaches to diplomacy. Reagan, the self-proclaimed "Great Communicator," was known for his charm and charisma, but also for his unwavering commitment to his ideological convictions. Gorbachev, on the other hand, was a more cerebral and analytical leader, who sought to reform and open up the Soviet system. Their contrasting styles and approaches to negotiations played out on the world stage, as each side attempted to outmaneuver the other.

Despite the tensions and disagreements, there were moments of levity and humor during the summit, as the two leaders engaged in witty repartee and personal anecdotes. However, beneath the surface, the negotiations were marked by a deep-seated distrust and fear of the unknown. The Reykjavik summit was a stark reminder of the risks and uncertainties of nuclear diplomacy, where one false move could have catastrophic consequences.
