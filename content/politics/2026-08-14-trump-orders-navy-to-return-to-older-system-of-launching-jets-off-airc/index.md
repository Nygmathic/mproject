## Trump's Directive Puts the Navy's Advanced Fighters in Jeopardy

US President Donald Trump has issued a directive ordering the Navy to remove the advanced Electromagnetic Aircraft Launch System (EMALS) from its newest aircraft carriers, a move that could cost billions of dollars and undermine the fleet's capabilities.

According to a memo issued on Thursday, the Navy must present Trump with plans to remove EMALS from the Ford-class carriers within two months. The directive also calls for the replacement of the electromagnetic weapons elevators on these ships. This would be a significant setback for the Navy, which has invested heavily in the advanced technology that enables faster and more efficient launch of fighter jets.

The EMALS system, which uses magnetic fields to propel aircraft off the carrier's deck, has been touted as a major improvement over the older steam catapults. It reduces the number of sailors required to operate the catapults, freeing up space on the ship and reducing maintenance needs. However, Trump has long been critical of the new system, claiming it lacks the power of the older steam systems.

Trump's memo directs the fourth Ford-class carrier, the USS Doris Miller, to be the first ship to include the new changes. This will leave the USS Gerald R. Ford, as well as the forthcoming USS John F. Kennedy and USS Enterprise, untouched. The move is likely to cause significant disruptions to the Navy's shipbuilding program, particularly given the complexity of redesigning an already finalized ship.

The decision to revert to older technology may also have implications for the Navy's ability to maintain its fleet. With the US military struggling to staff its ships with enough sailors, the shift away from EMALS could exacerbate existing personnel shortages.

The move also comes at a time when the Navy is facing financial pressures, with the Pentagon seeking increased funding from Congress to support its operations. The House has narrowly adopted a $95 billion package to fund the military, but the Senate still must act when it returns in September.

General Atomics, the company that makes EMALS, has expressed concerns about the decision to remove the system from the USS Doris Miller.
