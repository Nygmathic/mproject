## British Drones Leave Trail of Destruction in Russia

British-made drones have struck targets inside Russia for the first time, marking a significant escalation in the ongoing conflict. The drones, manufactured by two separate British companies, have been deployed in mainland Russia, targeting military and industrial installations with devastating effect.

According to reports, the strikes have caused more than £35bn in economic damage, with widespread and successful targeting of refineries and warehouses. The extent of the destruction is a stark reminder of the UK's increased involvement in the conflict. This latest development comes on the heels of the British government's announcement that it will supply Ukraine with tens of thousands of drones, including long-range strike devices, in its biggest ever package.

The use of British-made drones in Russia has been confirmed by a well-placed military source, who wished to remain anonymous. The source stated that the UK drones had been employed in the conflict, although the exact details of their deployment remain unclear. This new phase of the conflict is having a far more decisive impact than the earlier use of British-manufactured Storm Shadow missiles.

Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova has previously spoken out about the use of drones equipped with UK components and configurations in attacks on Russian territory. In April, the Ministry of Defence (MoD) announced its intention to supply Ukraine with equipment to defend itself against Russia's "illegal invasion." The MoD stated that its support for Ukraine is "unwavering" and that it will stand by its ally "until it wins."

The UK's commitment to Ukraine is underscored by its pledge to provide over 150,000 drones this year, as well as cutting-edge Stone Cloak devices that hinder Russian air defences and innovative low-cost cruise missiles. The provision of such advanced technology underscores the UK's willingness to take a more proactive role in the conflict.

In recent days, Moscow's mayor reported that approximately 600 Ukrainian drones were launched towards the city overnight, with roughly a third of the drones being destroyed. Three people were injured, and a warehouse belonging to Russian online retailer Wildberries was hit. Wildberries, often referred to as Russia's version of Amazon, has been repeatedly targeted since July.
