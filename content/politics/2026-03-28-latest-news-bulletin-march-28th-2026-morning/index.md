From the ancient Greeks and Romans to the Ottoman Empire and the modern-day superpowers, the Mediterranean has been a battleground for competing interests and ideologies.

In recent years, the region has seen a number of significant developments, including the Arab Spring, the rise of Islamist movements, and the growing influence of external powers such as Russia and China. These shifts have created a volatile and unpredictable environment, in which regional players are forced to navigate a complex web of alliances, rivalries, and interests.

As the Mediterranean crisis deepens, many are drawing parallels with the early 20th century, when the region was ravaged by the Great War and the subsequent rise of fascist and nationalist movements.
