## Centuries of Silence Broken

Ivory Coast's 'talking drum' returns to home village after 110 years

In a poignant homecoming, the Djidji Ayokwe, a sacred "talking drum" looted by French troops in 1916, has finally been returned to its ancestral home in Ivory Coast. The revered drum, which played a vital role in the Ebrie community's communication and warning systems for generations, was formally handed over by French authorities in February, marking the end of a 110-year odyssey.

The Djidji Ayokwe was more than a mere instrument of music; it was an integral part of the Ebrie people's way of life. The drum's rhythmic beats conveyed vital information, from announcements of important events to warnings of potential dangers, such as approaching enemies or impending natural disasters. Its return to the village is a testament to the enduring cultural heritage of the Ebrie community, who have long considered the drum a sacred symbol of their identity and shared history.

The drum's theft by French troops during the colonial era was a devastating blow to the Ebrie people, silencing a vital means of communication and severing their connection to their ancestors. The loss was compounded by the subsequent erasure of the drum from the collective memory of the community, leaving a void that would be felt for generations to come. The restoration of the Djidji Ayokwe is, therefore, a momentous occasion, marking not only the return of a cherished cultural artifact but also the reclamation of a vital part of the Ebrie people's heritage.

## The Drum's Long Journey

The Djidji Ayokwe's journey back to Ivory Coast is a complex narrative of colonialism, cultural suppression, and ultimately, redemption. The drum was initially taken by French troops as a trophy, a symbol of their conquest and dominance over the indigenous population. It was subsequently moved to France, where it was displayed as a curiosity in museums and private collections, serving as a reminder of European cultural superiority. The drum's presence in the West was a stark contrast to its original purpose, which was to facilitate communication and community among the Ebrie people.

However, in recent years, there has been a growing movement to repatriate cultural artifacts looted during the colonial era. This trend has led to the return of numerous significant artifacts to their countries of origin, including the Djidji Ayokwe. The drum's return to Ivory Coast is a significant milestone in this effort, highlighting the importance of preserving cultural heritage and acknowledging the historical injustices committed during the colonial period.
