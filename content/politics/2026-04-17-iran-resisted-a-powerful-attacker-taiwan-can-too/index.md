## The Lessons of Iran's Resistance

A sudden, unexpected turn in the Iran-US proxy war has sparked a seismic debate about the limits of military power and the resilience of smaller nations. When Iranian forces repelled a US drone attack on an airbase in central Iran, it marked a rare instance of a weaker military successfully fending off a more powerful attacker. While the implications of this development are still being parsed, one thing is clear: the Iranian example holds a crucial lesson for Taiwan, which is confronting a similar threat from China.

The stakes in both conflicts are high, but the context differs significantly. The Iran-US proxy war is an extension of a broader struggle for regional influence, with the US seeking to contain Iranian expansion and the Iranian regime pushing back against what it sees as foreign encroachment. In contrast, the Taiwan-China conflict is a core aspect of a far more profound issue: the reassertion of national sovereignty in the face of a more powerful neighbour. Both nations have legitimate claims to their territory, but the question is which one will prevail.

This is where the Iranian example comes in – a powerful reminder that conventional military superiority is not the only determinant of success. When US forces launched a barrage of missiles and drone strikes at an Iranian airbase in January, it was seen as a decisive blow that would cripple the Iranian military. Instead, the Iranian air defence systems proved surprisingly effective, downing several drones and intercepting dozens of missiles. The attack was a tactical failure, and it exposed a critical vulnerability in the US military's reliance on precision-guided munitions.

This development has far-reaching implications for Taiwan, which is facing an even more formidable opponent in China. While the Chinese military is significantly more powerful than the Iranian forces, the lessons from Iran are just as relevant. Taiwan's military is smaller and less well-equipped than China's, but it has a crucial advantage: the determination to defend its sovereignty. By leveraging its geography, terrain, and existing military capabilities, Taiwan can create a formidable obstacle for China, even if it cannot match the scale of China's military might.

The historical context of the Taiwan-China conflict is also instructive. The parallels with the Korean War, which pitted South Korea against North Korea and its Chinese backers, are striking. Like the Korean War, the Taiwan-China conflict is a proxy war fought between two global powers, with a smaller nation caught in the middle. However, unlike the Korean War, Taiwan has the potential to adopt a more effective strategy, leveraging its terrain and existing military capabilities to create a buffer zone against Chinese aggression. By doing so, Taiwan can force China to pay a high price for any attempt to take the island by force.

## Regional Perspectives and International Implications

The implications of the Iranian example extend far beyond the Middle East and the Taiwan-China conflict. The international community is watching with great interest as smaller nations begin to push back against more powerful neighbours. From Kosovo to Kashmir, the world is witnessing a new era of resistance and sovereignty, where smaller nations are refusing to be intimidated by the might of their more powerful counterparts. This is a significant shift in the global balance of power, and it has profound implications for the future of international relations.

In this context, the European Union has a crucial role to play. As a major global player, the EU has a vested interest in promoting the sovereignty of smaller nations and preventing the reassertion of national power in the face of globalisation. The EU's policy on Taiwan is a case in point, with the bloc insisting that Beijing should engage in dialogue with Taipei and respect the island's sovereignty. By taking a firmer stance on Taiwan, the EU is signalling its commitment to upholding the principles of sovereignty and self-determination.

The international community is watching with great interest as the situation unfolds, with many analysts predicting a long and bloody conflict.

The island's leaders are taking a more assertive stance, leveraging international support and military modernisation to create a formidable obstacle for China. In the EU, there is a growing recognition of the need to strengthen ties with Taiwan and provide greater support for the island's sovereignty.
