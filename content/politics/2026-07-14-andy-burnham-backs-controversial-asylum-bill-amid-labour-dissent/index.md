## A Divided Labour: Andy Burnham Backs Controversial Asylum Bill Amid Rebellion

Tensions within the Labour party have been laid bare as Andy Burnham, the prime minister-in-waiting, voted in favour of the government's contentious asylum bill, despite a significant rebellion by 14 Labour MPs. The legislation, which aims to tighten the immigration system and reshape the appeals process, has sparked intense debate within the party, with some members arguing that it risks creating a more restrictive and expensive asylum system.

The immigration and asylum bill, which passed its second reading in the House of Commons with a margin of 264 votes to 90, has been met with both praise and criticism from various quarters. While the government has hailed the bill as a crucial step towards reducing the number of people crossing the Channel in small boats, Labour dissenters have voiced concerns that it may undermine the country's commitment to providing sanctuary to those fleeing war and persecution.

For Burnham, the decision to support the bill marks a significant departure from his party's traditional stance on immigration and asylum. The Labour MP for Makerfield has long been a vocal advocate for a more compassionate approach to immigration, but his vote on Monday evening suggests that he is willing to compromise in order to maintain a working relationship with the government.

The bill itself is a complex and multifaceted piece of legislation, which seeks to address several key issues within the asylum system. One of the most contentious aspects of the bill is its proposal to tighten the application of article 8 of the European convention on human rights, which protects the right to private and family life. This change would potentially reduce the number of successful asylum appeals, but critics argue that it may also lead to a more restrictive and bureaucratic system.

The government has also announced plans to replace immigration judges with a new system of independent adjudicators, which ministers claim will speed up decision-making and reduce backlogs. However, Labour backbenchers have questioned the wisdom of this move, arguing that it may lead to a more expensive and complex system.

The debate surrounding the asylum bill has been marked by intense rhetoric from both sides, with the home secretary, Shabana Mahmood, accusing Labour of being out of touch with public opinion. Mahmood argued that the bill was necessary to restore public confidence in the asylum system, while also preserving Britain's commitment to those fleeing war and persecution.

However, Labour rebels have countered that the bill risks exacerbating the very problems it claims to address. Nadia Whittome, the Labour MP for Nottingham East, voted against the bill, citing a lack of evidence that it would solve the issues it claimed to address. Stella Creasy questioned the government's intentions, suggesting that the new "core protection" route may be little more than a "Diet Coke version of refugee status".

Tony Vaughan, the Labour MP for Folkestone and Hythe, warned that poor Home Office decision-making, rather than the tribunal system, was driving the volume of appeals. These concerns have been echoed by some Conservative MPs, who have argued that the bill does not go far enough in addressing the root causes of irregular migration.

As the debate surrounding the asylum bill continues to unfold, it remains to be seen how Labour will navigate its internal divisions. Burnham's decision to back the bill has sparked criticism from some quarters, but it also reflects a growing recognition within the party that compromise is necessary in order to maintain a working relationship with the government.

Ultimately, the passage of the asylum bill marks a significant shift in the country's approach to immigration and asylum. While it remains to be seen whether the bill will achieve its stated goals, one thing is clear: the debate surrounding immigration and asylum is far from over.
