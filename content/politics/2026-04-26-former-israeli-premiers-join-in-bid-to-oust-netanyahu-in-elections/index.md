## A Brewing Storm in Israeli Politics: Former Premiers Join Forces to Challenge Netanyahu

Tensions are running high in Israel, where the country's long-serving Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is facing a mounting challenge from an unlikely duo: former premiers Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid. The two right-wing and centrist politicians, once sworn enemies, have set aside their differences to form an alliance in a desperate bid to oust Netanyahu and bring an end to his 12-year tenure in power.

The stakes are high, with the Israeli elections scheduled for later this year. Bennett, a hardline right-winger who has been a vocal supporter of annexation in the West Bank, has long been at odds with Netanyahu, whom he accuses of betraying the country's right-wing values. Lapid, on the other hand, is a centrist who has been critical of Netanyahu's handling of the economy and his relationship with the country's ultra-Orthodox parties. Together, the two men hope to form a viable alternative to Netanyahu's Likud party, which has dominated Israeli politics for decades.

The alliance is a significant development, one that highlights the deep divisions within the Israeli right. Bennett's Yamina party and Lapid's Yesh Atid party have traditionally been seen as rivals, with Bennett's hardline stance on issues like annexation and settlement expansion putting him at odds with Lapid's more moderate views. However, the two men have come together in a last-ditch effort to challenge Netanyahu, who is facing corruption charges and growing unpopularity.

The challenges facing the alliance are significant. Netanyahu's Likud party has a strong grassroots base, and his ability to mobilize support among the country's right-wing voters has been a key factor in his success. Bennett and Lapid, on the other hand, will need to convince voters that they have the vision and the leadership skills to take the country forward. The alliance will also need to navigate complex relationships with other parties in the Israeli parliament, the Knesset, where they will need to form a coalition in order to govern.
