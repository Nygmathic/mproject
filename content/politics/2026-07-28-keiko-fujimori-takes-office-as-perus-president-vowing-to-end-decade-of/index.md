## A New Era of Stability in Peru?

Conservative leader Keiko Fujimori has taken office as Peru's president, vowing to restore political stability and tackle rising crime after a decade of turmoil. In a ceremony attended by King Felipe VI in Lima, Fujimori, the 51-year-old leader of the Popular Force party, was sworn in as the country's first female head of state, following a narrow victory in June's election by fewer than 50,000 votes.

Fujimori's ascension to the presidency marks a significant shift in Peru's politics, as she becomes the first female head of state in the country's history. Her return to power after 26 years is a testament to the enduring legacy of her father, Alberto Fujimori, who ruled Peru from 1990 to 2000. Fujimori's Popular Force party and its main ally, Popular Renovation, control half the seats in the newly installed Senate, granting her strong congressional backing and making it more difficult for lawmakers to remove her from office.

The decade of instability that Fujimori has pledged to end has been marked by nine presidents, with only three elected by popular vote. Many of these leaders took office after their predecessors resigned or were removed by Congress, often amid corruption scandals. This tumultuous period has taken a toll on Peru's economy, with rising crime and extortion becoming increasingly pressing concerns.

Fujimori's campaign focused heavily on security, with promises to restore order and tackle organised crime. She has pledged to govern with an "iron fist," including plans to build a mega-prison modelled on El Salvador's Cecot facility and allow judges to conceal their identities during criminal trials for security reasons. These measures have been met with caution by opposition leaders, who have urged Fujimori's incoming administration to repeal laws passed by the previous Congress that critics say have weakened efforts to combat organised crime.

Fujimori has also pledged to boost infrastructure investment by making it easier for government agencies to partner with private investors. This strategy aims to revive economic growth and create jobs, a crucial challenge for the new administration. With a strong congressional backing and a clear mandate to restore stability, Fujimori's presidency will be closely watched by domestic and international observers alike.

## A Decade of Turmoil: Context and Consequences

Peru's recent history has been marked by a series of short-lived governments, often resulting from corruption scandals and internal power struggles. This instability has had far-reaching consequences, including a decline in economic growth and a rise in crime. The country's fragile institutions and weak governance have created an environment in which corruption and organised crime can thrive.

Fujimori's predecessor, Pedro Castillo, was ousted in December 2022 after a tumultuous tenure marked by controversy and corruption allegations. His removal was a turning point in Peru's politics, paving the way for Fujimori's return to power. While Fujimori's policies aim to address the country's security concerns, many question the feasibility of her plans, particularly in light of the country's complex history of corruption and governance issues.
