A sudden and unannounced delay in a planned military operation against Iran has sent shockwaves through the diplomatic corridors of the Middle East, with Gulf states breathing a collective sigh of relief. The stakes were high, with the potential for a devastating conflict that would have far-reaching consequences for the region and the world. The prospect of a broader conflict threatened to engulf the entire region, drawing in other major powers and destabilizing the already volatile Middle East. The Gulf states, in particular, were caught in the middle, with Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates (UAE) walking a delicate tightrope between their close ties to the US and their shared historical and cultural heritage with Iran.

To understand the sudden delay in the planned attack, it is essential to consider the intricate web of alliances and rivalries that underpins the region's politics. The Gulf states have long been concerned about the potential consequences of a US-led attack on Iran, which could lead to a catastrophic escalation of the conflict and destabilize the entire region. In recent months, they have been quietly lobbying the Trump administration to reconsider its approach, advocating for a more measured and diplomatic approach to resolving the crisis.

The Gulf states are not alone in their concerns. Other major powers, including China and Russia, have also been pressing for a more cautious approach to the crisis, highlighting the need for dialogue and diplomacy to resolve the disputes between the US and Iran. The European Union, too, has been vocal in its opposition to a US-led attack, warning of the potential consequences for regional stability and global security.
