## A Shift in Allegiance

In a dimly lit community center in Omaha, Nebraska, a group of young Latino men gathered around a makeshift table, their eyes fixed on a worn map of the state. The faded outline of the state's borders seemed to symbolize the vast distances they had traversed in search of work and a better life. They were the quintessential products of globalization, their lives intertwined with the rhythms of the meatpacking industry. Yet, it was the closure of a local meat plant that would set in motion a chain of events that would upend their politics and redefine their relationship with power.

The closure of the meat plant, a major employer in the region, had sent shockwaves through the community, leaving hundreds of workers without a steady income. As the weeks turned into months, the impact was felt far beyond the plant's walls. Local businesses began to struggle, and the once-thriving community started to fray at the edges. It was in this context that the young men, many of whom had been previously disengaged from politics, began to take notice of a new face in town: Dan Osborn. A local Democrat with a reputation for being fiercely independent, Osborn had been quietly building a coalition of supporters among the Latino community.

The stakes were high. As the 2024 presidential election loomed large, the dynamics of the US electoral landscape were changing. Demographics were shifting, and the Latino vote, once a reliable bastion of support for the Democratic Party, was becoming increasingly fragmented. President Trump's victory in 2016 had been fueled, in part, by the disaffection of young, working-class men, particularly Latinos. But in Nebraska, a state that had traditionally voted Republican, the economic winds were blowing in a different direction. As the meat plant closure underscored the fragility of the local economy, the young men began to see politics through a new lens.

Osborn's campaign was built on a simple yet powerful promise: to put the needs of the community first. By advocating for policies that benefited small businesses and workers, he was able to tap into a deep well of anger and frustration that had been building among the Latino community. For many, the closure of the meat plant had been a wake-up call, a stark reminder of the precarious nature of their lives. As they listened to Osborn's message, they began to see the connection between their economic struggles and the broader political landscape. It was a eureka moment, one that would propel Osborn to victory in the 2024 primary.

## A New Era of Politics

The Osborn victory was more than just a local anomaly; it represented a seismic shift in the national conversation. For too long, the Democratic Party had taken the Latino vote for granted, relying on a narrow coalition of established leaders and interest groups. But in Nebraska, the closure of the meat plant had created a new kind of politics, one that was more inclusive, more participatory, and more attuned to the needs of the community. As Osborn's campaign demonstrated, the key to winning over young, working-class men lay not in pandering to their fears or anxieties but in listening to their concerns and addressing their needs.

This new era of politics was not limited to Nebraska or the Latino community. Across the country, a quiet revolution was underway, as young people, particularly men of color, began to take an active role in shaping the national conversation. From the streets of Los Angeles to the factories of Ohio, a new generation of leaders was emerging, one that was less interested in traditional party affiliations and more focused on addressing the pressing issues of the day. Climate change, income inequality, and access to healthcare were just a few of the topics that were driving this new wave of activism.

The shift in politics was not unique to the United States. In countries as far-flung as Brazil, South Africa, and India, a similar dynamic was playing out. The closure of the meat plant in Nebraska was just one small part of a much larger phenomenon, one that was driven by the same global forces that had brought about the rise of globalization.
