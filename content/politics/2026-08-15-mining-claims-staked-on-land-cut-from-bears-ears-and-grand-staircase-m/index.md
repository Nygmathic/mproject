Prospectors are staking claims on land that was severed from the Bears Ears and Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monuments in Utah, setting the stage for a contentious battle over the future of these protected areas. The move comes as the federal government continues to grapple with the implications of a 2017 executive order that significantly reduced the size of these national monuments, sparking heated debate over the role of public lands in Utah.

The Bears Ears and Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monuments were established in 2016 to protect the region's rich cultural and natural heritage, including Ancestral Puebloan ruins, ancient petroglyphs, and fragile ecosystems. However, in 2017, President Donald Trump signed a proclamation reducing the size of both monuments by nearly 2 million acres, sparking widespread criticism from environmental groups, Native American tribes, and local communities. The decision was widely seen as a giveaway to the fossil fuel and extractive industries, which have long sought to access the region's mineral resources.

Since then, prospectors have been quietly staking claims on land that was once part of these national monuments. According to reports, several mining companies have filed applications with the Bureau of Land Management (BLM) to explore for mineral deposits, including gold, copper, and uranium. While the BLM has yet to approve any mining claims, the move has already sparked concerns among environmental groups and Native American tribes, who argue that the region's unique cultural and natural resources are too valuable to be sacrificed for the sake of economic development.
