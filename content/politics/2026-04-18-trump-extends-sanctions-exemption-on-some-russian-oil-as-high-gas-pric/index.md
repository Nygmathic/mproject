## Energy Markets in Flux

As global oil prices continue to soar, reaching record highs in the wake of the ongoing conflict in the Middle East, the Trump administration has quietly extended an exemption to some Russian oil exports from its sweeping sanctions regime. The move, which has sent shockwaves through the international energy community, marks the latest twist in a delicate dance between Washington and Moscow as they navigate a volatile global energy landscape.

## A Calculated Gamble

The stakes are high, with the global economy teetering on the brink of recession as the price of crude oil has breached $140 per barrel. The US, along with its allies, had initially imposed crippling sanctions on Russian oil in response to Moscow's aggression in Ukraine, aiming to strangle the country's ability to fund its military campaigns. However, as the conflict in the Middle East escalated and energy markets began to destabilize, the Trump administration has reassessed its stance, opting to selectively exempt certain Russian oil exports from the sanctions regime.

The exemption, which has been extended until the end of the month, will allow a select group of Russian oil companies to continue exporting crude oil to countries that have signed on to the US-led sanctions regime, in exchange for a significant reduction in the volume of oil they are permitted to export. The move has been widely interpreted as a calculated gamble by the Trump administration, designed to alleviate pressure on global energy markets and mitigate the impact of the escalating conflict in the Middle East on the US economy.
