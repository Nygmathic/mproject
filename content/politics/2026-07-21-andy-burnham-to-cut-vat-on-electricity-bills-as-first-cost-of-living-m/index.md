## A Breathing Space for Britain's Households

Andy Burnham's swift move to cut VAT on electricity bills has sent a resounding message to the nation: the newly appointed prime minister is committed to easing the crippling cost of living pressures bearing down on households across Britain. The announcement, made on the prime minister's second day in office, marks a significant shift in the government's approach to addressing the crisis, with a clear focus on providing immediate relief to families.

The VAT cut, set to take effect from October, will reduce electricity bills by an average of £45 a year, a substantial reduction that will be felt by millions of households. This move is not merely a gesture; it represents a tangible step towards bringing down the costs of living, a promise made by Burnham during his campaign to "bring back hope" to Westminster politics. The prime minister's decision to fund this cut by scrapping the digital ID scheme has been met with skepticism by some, but the chancellor, John Healey, remains confident that this will be a cost-effective measure, with the digital ID project set to save £1.8bn over three years.

The implications of this move are far-reaching, and critics have already begun to question the sustainability of the government's plans. Darren Jones, a former chief secretary to the prime minister, raised doubts over the funding, pointing out that the digital ID program was unfunded and that the government will need to set out how it plans to pay for its new policies at the budget. However, Healey has remained steadfast in his commitment to the VAT cut, emphasizing that it will give families "some breathing room on bills" and provide reassurance this winter.

The government's cost of living plans are set to take center stage in the coming weeks, with Burnham expected to outline further detail on his proposals, including the reduction of the cap on bus fares and potentially even a temporary freeze on private sector rents. This move will not only provide immediate relief to households but also encourage consumers to switch to more environmentally friendly options, such as electric heat pumps.

One of the most significant aspects of this announcement is its impact on the energy market. By reducing the gap between gas and electricity prices, the government is incentivizing consumers to adopt cleaner, more sustainable alternatives. This shift in policy has the potential to drive significant changes in the way Britain generates and consumes energy, a move that could have long-term benefits for the environment and the economy.

However, not everyone will benefit from this move. Consumers in Northern Ireland will continue to pay VAT on their electricity bills, bound by commitments to adhere to EU tax laws. The government has promised to provide an equivalent amount of funding to the Northern Ireland executive to spend on its own cost of living package, but this move has been met with frustration by some, who feel that it is a missed opportunity to address the disparities in energy costs across the UK.

As the government prepares to outline its full cost of living plan at the budget later this year, one thing is clear: Andy Burnham is committed to taking bold action to address the cost of living crisis. The VAT cut on electricity bills is just the beginning, and it remains to be seen how the government will follow through on its promises. But for now, at least, it seems that Britain's households may finally be getting the breathing space they so desperately need.
